# Ventilatory Limitation to Exercise in Obese HFpEF

> Obese HFpEF patients exercise to a mechanical ventilatory ceiling imposed by dynamic hyperinflation and expiratory flow limitation; reducing filling pressures (PCWP) does not relieve this ceiling, challenging the cardiac-centric model of exercise intolerance in obese HFpEF.

## Evidence

### Babb 2026 — Key Finding

**N=42 obese HFpEF (BMI 38±7); randomized crossover: nitroglycerin (NTG) vs. placebo:**

- NTG **reduced PCWP** at rest (P<0.01) and at 20W (P<0.01) — confirmed cardiac unloading
- NTG had **no effect on exercise capacity** (peak work rate unchanged; P=NS)
- NTG had **no effect on breathing mechanics** (EELV, EILV, FVC unchanged; R²=0.96 for EELV before vs. after NTG)
- Patients stopped at the same ventilatory ceiling before and after cardiac unloading (source: [[babb2026ventilatorylimit]])

**Conclusion:** Exercise capacity is ventilatory-limited, not primarily cardiac-limited, in obese HFpEF — lowering PCWP alone is insufficient.

### Leahy 2025 — Dynamic Hyperinflation Prevalence

**N=55 obese HFpEF (BMI 38±7):**
- 20W exercise: **62% had dynamic hyperinflation** (ΔEELV ≥150 mL)
- Peak exercise: **85% had dynamic hyperinflation**
- EFL (expiratory flow limitation) at rest: **75%; at peak: 87%**
- ΔEELV correlated with ΔPCWP at peak (r²=0.167; P=0.002) — DH elevates measured PCWP via intrathoracic pressure (source: [[leahy2025heartlung]])

## Mechanism: Three Converging Constraints

1. **Dynamic hyperinflation:** EELV increases with exercise → EILV approaches TLC → tidal volume expansion limited → VE can only increase via ↑ respiratory rate (↑ dead space ratio) → ventilatory inefficiency
2. **Expiratory flow limitation (EFL):** ~75–87% of obese HFpEF patients have EFL; constrains expiratory flow even at high intrathoracic pressures; worsens dynamic hyperinflation
3. **Obesity-related lung mechanics:** ↑ work of breathing (chest wall mass), ↓ respiratory muscle efficiency, ↓ FVC at baseline → smaller ventilatory reserve throughout exercise

## Clinical Implications

### Paradigm Challenge
- Elevated PCWP at exercise is a diagnostic criterion and treatment target for HFpEF
- In obese HFpEF, PCWP elevation during exercise is **partly driven by dynamic hyperinflation** raising intrathoracic pressure — not purely cardiac diastolic dysfunction
- Transmural PCWP (corrected for pleural pressure) would be lower (source: [[leahy2025heartlung]])
- Implication: **Exercise PCWP may overestimate true cardiac filling pressures** in obese HFpEF

### Treatment Implications
- Diuretics and haemodynamic interventions that lower PCWP may not improve exercise capacity in obese HFpEF
- **Respiratory-focused interventions** (weight loss → reduced EFL/DH; OSA treatment → reduced respiratory inefficiency; breathing training) may be more effective for exercise tolerance
- GLP-1 RA (semaglutide) through weight loss may improve ventilatory mechanics as part of its mechanism in STEP-HFpEF/SUMMIT

## Distinction from Non-Obese HFpEF

- Ventilatory limitation is most pronounced in obese HFpEF (BMI >30, especially class II–III)
- Non-obese HFpEF: exercise limitation primarily cardiac (reduced CO reserve + peripheral factors)
