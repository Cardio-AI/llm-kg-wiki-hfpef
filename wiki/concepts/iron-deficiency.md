# Iron Deficiency in HFpEF

> Iron deficiency is the most prevalent treatable comorbidity in HFpEF, present in 59% of patients; impairs exercise capacity through skeletal muscle dysfunction independent of haemia; IV iron improves 6MWT in the only HFpEF-specific RCT to date, but evidence is preliminary.

## Aliases
| Alias | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ID | abbreviation | used interchangeably in trials |
| Iron-deficiency anaemia | alternate-name | subset only — majority of HFpEF ID is non-anaemic |

## Epidemiology

- **Overall prevalence in HFpEF:** 59% (95% CI 52–65%; 15 studies, N=1,877) (source: [[beale2019iron]])
- **Absolute iron deficiency** (ferritin <100 ng/mL): 30%
- **Functional iron deficiency** (ferritin 100–299 ng/mL + TSAT <20%): 34%
- Prevalence comparable to HFrEF; substantially higher than general population
- High methodological heterogeneity in ID definition across studies (source: [[beale2019iron]])

## Definition

Standard criteria used in HF trials:
- **Absolute ID:** Serum ferritin <100 ng/mL
- **Functional ID:** Ferritin 100–299 ng/mL **AND** TSAT <20%
- Both criteria are used in FAIR-HFpEF, AFFIRM-AHF, and ESC guidelines

## Mechanism: Why Iron Deficiency Worsens HFpEF

Iron deficiency impairs exercise capacity via:
1. **Mitochondrial dysfunction:** Iron is essential for oxidative phosphorylation and electron transport chain; ID → impaired mitochondrial ATP production → skeletal muscle fatigue independent of haemoglobin level
2. **Reduced O₂ transport:** Anaemia component (subset of ID) reduces arterial O₂ content → lower A-VO₂ difference peak
3. **Worsened peripheral muscle function:** Reduced myoglobin, impaired energetics → accelerated peripheral fatigue during exercise

Mechanism is distinct from and additive to cardiac mechanisms of exercise intolerance in HFpEF (source: [[beale2019iron]], [[haykowsky2011exercise]])

## Impact on Exercise Capacity

- 3/4 studies showed ID associated with worse peak VO₂ (source: [[beale2019iron]])
- ID associated with worse 6MWT and poorer KCCQ/MLHFQ quality of life scores in observational studies
- Effect is independent of haemoglobin level — non-anaemic ID impairs exercise capacity

## Treatment Evidence

### FAIR-HFpEF (von Haehling 2024) — Only HFpEF-Specific RCT

| Outcome | FCM | Placebo | Difference | P |
|---------|-----|---------|------------|---|
| 6MWT change (week 24) | +49m | −8m | **+57m** | **0.029** |
| KCCQ overall summary | +6.5 pts | — | NS | 0.21 |
| NYHA class improvement | — | — | NS | NS |
| Serious adverse events | 5 | 19 | Rate ratio 0.27 | 0.043 |

**Critical limitations:** Stopped early after N=40 (planned N=200); severely underpowered for all endpoints; results preliminary (source: [[vonhaehling2024fair]])

### AFFIRM-AHF (Ponikowski 2020) — HFrEF Context

- LVEF <50% population (predominantly HFrEF); FCM vs. placebo after acute HF
- Primary composite (total HFH + CV death): RR 0.79 (P=0.059) — narrowly missed significance due to COVID disruption
- Total HF hospitalisations: RR 0.74 (P=0.013) — significant
- CV mortality: HR 0.96 — not reduced
- HFpEF patients excluded (LVEF <50% required); limited generalisability (source: [[ponikowski2020affirm]])

### Evidence Gap

No adequately powered RCT of IV iron in HFpEF. FAIR-HFpEF established proof-of-concept; a larger trial is needed. [knowledge gap — needs future ingest]

## Clinical Approach

- Screen all HFpEF patients for ID using ferritin + TSAT
- IV FCM is the preferred repletion route in HFrEF trials; oral iron ineffective due to hepcidin-mediated absorption block in HF
- Low-grade recommendation in current guidelines (Class 2b or quality measure level) for HFpEF
