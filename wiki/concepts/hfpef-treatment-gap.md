# HFpEF Treatment Gap

> Despite HFpEF accounting for ~50% of HF patients, no large RCT has demonstrated convincing reduction in mortality or morbidity across the full population; this is the central unresolved problem in heart failure medicine.

---

## Mechanism

Despite [[hfpef]] accounting for ~50% of heart failure patients, **no large RCT has demonstrated a convincing reduction in mortality or morbidity across the full HFpEF population.** This is the central unresolved problem in heart failure medicine and the primary motivation for ongoing HFpEF research. (source: 2021-ESC-Guidelines-Heart-Failure.pdf)

**Why do [[hfref]] therapies fail in HFpEF? Proposed explanations:**
- **Pathophysiological mismatch:** [[diastolic-dysfunction]] and myocardial stiffness — not neurohormonal activation — are the dominant drivers in HFpEF; the RAAS/SNS axis plays a smaller role
- **Phenotypic heterogeneity:** "HFpEF" is an umbrella containing multiple distinct syndromes (hypertensive, obese/metabolic, amyloid, etc.); a single therapy cannot target all
- **Non-CV comorbidity dilution:** High non-CV mortality and hospitalization in HFpEF patients dilutes CV-specific treatment effects
- **Trial enrollment issues:** Some trials enrolled patients without true HFpEF (TOPCAT non-Americas), contaminating the study population

## Clinical Relevance

### Current Guideline Recommendations for HFpEF

**ESC 2021 — Two Class I recommendations only:**

| Recommendation | Class | Level |
|---|---|---|
| Screening for and treatment of aetiologies and CV/non-CV comorbidities | I | C |
| Diuretics for congested patients to alleviate symptoms | I | C |

(source: 2021-ESC-Guidelines-Heart-Failure.pdf)

**AHA 2022 — Partial closure of the gap:**

| Agent | Class | Level | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| SGLT2 inhibitors | **2a** | B-R | EMPEROR-Preserved + DELIVER; reduces HF hospitalization + CV death |
| MRA (spironolactone) | 2b | B-R | TOPCAT Americas subgroup; inadequate for full recommendation |
| Sacubitril/valsartan | 2b | B-R | PARAGON-HF LVEF <57% subgroup |
| Diuretics | I | C | Symptom relief |
| Comorbidity treatment | I | C | Hypertension, AF, T2DM, obesity |

(source: 2022-CirculationsAHA-Heidenreich-AHA-ACC-HFSA-Guideline-HF.pdf)

**SGLT2i Class 2a represents partial closure** of the treatment gap: the first agent with guideline endorsement above Class 2b for HFpEF. However, Class 2a reflects "benefit >> risk, reasonable to perform" — it is not equivalent to a mortality-proven Class I recommendation. The primary composite benefit in EMPEROR-Preserved was driven by HF hospitalizations, not CV mortality. **The mortality gap in HFpEF remains open.**

### Comorbidity-Centred Approach
Current best strategy: aggressive treatment of underlying drivers — hypertension (antihypertensives Class I, Level A for HF prevention), [[atrial-fibrillation|AF]], diabetes, obesity, iron deficiency, and CAD. Treatment of the specific underlying phenotype may improve outcomes even in the absence of a universal HFpEF therapy. (source: 2021-ESC-Guidelines-Heart-Failure.pdf)

## Evidence

### Emerging Signals (as of ESC 2021) — Now Partially Resolved

1. **[[sglt2-inhibitors]]**: EMPEROR-Preserved (2021) and DELIVER (2022) — both positive; AHA 2022 issued Class 2a recommendation for HFpEF. Benefit driven by hospitalisation reduction, not CV mortality. (source: 2022-CirculationsAHA-Heidenreich-AHA-ACC-HFSA-Guideline-HF.pdf)
2. **[[sacubitril-valsartan]]**: PARAGON-HF LVEF <57% subgroup + PARADIGM-HF/PARAGON-HF meta-analysis → FDA endorsed for LVEF "below normal." AHA 2022 Class 2b for HFpEF. ESC 2021 noted without European recommendation.
3. **[[spironolactone]]**: TOPCAT Americas subgroup + post-hoc EF <55% analysis positive. AHA 2022 Class 2b for HFpEF. ESC 2021 did not translate to recommendation.

(source: 2021-ESC-Guidelines-Heart-Failure.pdf; source: 2022-CirculationsAHA-Heidenreich-AHA-ACC-HFSA-Guideline-HF.pdf)

### Haemodynamic Monitoring — CHAMPION Trial

The CHAMPION trial (CardioMEMS implantable PA pressure sensor) showed that management guided by physician knowledge of central haemodynamics significantly reduced HF hospitalisations in NYHA III HF. This finding was confirmed in a subgroup restricted to HFpEF patients and in an analysis of Medicare beneficiaries. While not a pharmacological therapy, haemodynamic-guided management partially fills the gap for managing congestion when drug options are limited. (source: 2019-CIRCRESAHA-Pfeffer-HFpEF_perspective.pdf)

### Exercise Training — Functional Gap Filled

[[Supervised-exercise-training|Supervised exercise training (SET)]] is the most consistently positive HFpEF intervention for exercise capacity:

| Outcome | Effect (8 RCTs, n=503) | CI |
|---|---|---|
| Peak VO2 | +2.8 mL/kg/min | 1.6–4.0 |
| Exercise time | +21% | — |
| 6MWD | +40 m | — |
| Safety | No major adverse events | — |

**2022 AHA/ACC:** Class I, Level A recommendation for exercise training in HF (EF-agnostic). (source: 2023-CirculationAHA-Sachdev-hfpef-exercise.pdf)

**Critical caveat:** Effects on mortality and HF hospitalisation are unknown — individual trials are underpowered for hard outcomes. SET fills the functional gap but not the mortality gap. Mirzai 2025 (state-of-the-art review, ~30 RCTs): MICT has the largest evidence base; HIIT not superior to MICT for ΔpVO₂; ~1/3 of patients are non-responders. (source: raw/2025-HFR-Mirzai-Review_physical_activity_hfpef.pdf)

**SGLT2i symptom gap — partially resolved:** DELIVER (Solomon 2022) showed dapagliflozin improved KCCQ-TSS at 8 months (win ratio 1.11, P=0.009; mean +2.4 pts). DETERMINE-Preserved (McMurray 2024) was neutral at 16 weeks (P=0.079). Reconciliation: DELIVER used win-ratio approach at 8 months; DETERMINE used change score at 16 weeks. SGLT2i symptom benefit in HFpEF is likely real but may require ≥8 months or specific analytical sensitivity to detect. See [[contradictions]] #16. (source: raw/2022-NEJM-Solomon-Deliver_study.pdf; source: raw/2023-CirculationsAHA-McMurry-DETERMINE_study.pdf)

### Phenotype-Based Approach — Proposed Operational Solution (2023)

The 2023 HFA/ESC consensus (Anker 2023) and the ACC 2023 ECDP (Kittleson 2023) propose a two-layer treatment model to partially close the gap:
1. **Universal layer:** SGLT2i (dapagliflozin/empagliflozin) for all HFpEF patients without contraindication — only pharmacotherapy with proven benefit across the spectrum.
2. **Phenotype-guided add-on:** Targeted therapy for the dominant comorbidity — 7 phenotype categories (hypertension, iron deficiency, obesity, T2DM, AF, ischaemic disease, COPD), each with specific agents.

This does **not** close the mortality gap — SGLT2i benefit is driven by HHF reduction — but provides the most operationally advanced framework for practice as of 2023. See [[hfpef-phenotype-profiling]]. (source: 2023-ESC-Anker_HFpEF_phenotyping.pdf)

## Open Questions
- Does SGLT2i benefit in HFpEF extend to CV mortality reduction (not just hospitalisations)?
- Does SET reduce HF hospitalisation and mortality, or only functional status?
- Does the TOPCAT Americas subgroup represent a genuine MRA benefit that was diluted by enrollment contamination?
- Can HFpEF be sub-phenotyped (obese, hypertensive, amyloid) to identify treatment-responsive subgroups?
- Is the sacubitril/valsartan benefit in lower-EF HFpEF real, or driven by the HFmrEF subgroup within PARAGON-HF?
- Does Mg supplementation enhance SGLT2i benefit in HFpEF? (see [[ferreira2026emperor]])
- Why did DETERMINE-Preserved not show KCCQ benefit despite EMPEROR-Preserved/DELIVER showing event reduction? Duration? Mechanism?
- Can phenotype-targeted exercise regimens (HIIT for select subgroups, MICT for majority) improve responder rates beyond the current ~2/3?

## Contradictions
- FDA endorsement vs. ESC non-recommendation for sacubitril/valsartan in HFpEF: different evidentiary thresholds and timing.
- TOPCAT Americas positive subgroup vs. neutral overall result: regional data integrity issues unresolved.
- SGLT2i: Class I in HFrEF; Class 2a for HFpEF (AHA 2022); No ESC HFpEF recommendation (ESC 2021) — publication-timing discordance. See [[guideline-comparison]].
- SET improves peak VO2 consistently but hard outcome data absent — functional benefit ≠ mortality benefit.
- SGLT2i Class 2a for HFpEF is driven by HF hospitalization reduction, not CV mortality — a partial gap-closure at best.

See [[contradictions]].
