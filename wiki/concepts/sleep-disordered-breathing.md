# Sleep-Disordered Breathing in HFpEF

> SDB is among the most common and mechanistically relevant comorbidities in HFpEF; predominantly obstructive (OSA) in the obese HFpEF phenotype, it compounds diastolic dysfunction through sympathetic activation, inflammation, CaMKII overactivation, and arterial stiffening.

## Aliases
| Alias | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| SDB | abbreviation | clinical shorthand |
| OSA | abbreviation | obstructive sleep apnea — dominant subtype in HFpEF |
| CSA | abbreviation | central sleep apnea — dominant in HFrEF |
| Sleep apnea in HF | alternate-name | non-specific term |

## Epidemiology

- SDB prevalence in HF (combined): **50–75%** (source: [[cowie2017sdb]])
- SDB prevalence in HFpEF specifically: **58–80%** (source: [[wester2023sdb]])
- **OSA predominates in HFpEF**, especially obese phenotype (BMI >30); CSA predominates in HFrEF
- SDB prevalence by HFpEF phenotype (source: [[wester2023sdb]]):
  - Metabolic/obese HFpEF: **40–60%**
  - Older/vascular HFpEF: variable
  - Younger, NP-deficient HFpEF: **10–20%**
- AHI ≥15 events/h defines clinically significant SDB (regardless of symptoms)
- SDB often unrecognized in HFpEF — symptoms attributed to cardiac disease

## Subtypes

| Type | Mechanism | Predominance | Treatment |
|------|-----------|--------------|-----------|
| **OSA** | Upper airway collapse ≥10s; partial or complete | HFpEF (obese) | CPAP, weight loss, GLP-1 RA |
| **CSA** | Central respiratory signal pauses; Cheyne-Stokes | HFrEF | ASV (contraindicated in HFrEF+CSA) |
| **Mixed** | Both coexist | Both subtypes | Individualized |

## Mechanism: SDB → HFpEF Progression

Five converging pathways (source: [[wester2023sdb]]):

1. **Increased cardiac afterload:** Intermittent hypoxia → ↑ sympathetic tone + ↑ ROS → arterial hypertension → LV hypertrophy → diastolic dysfunction; OSA intrathoracic pressure swings → ↑ LV wall stress → fibrosis

2. **Arterial stiffening:** Severe SDB (AHI >30) independently predicts higher pulse wave velocity (PWV) in HFpEF (β=0.234; P=0.005) but NOT in HFrEF — HFpEF-specific interaction (source: [[suzuki2018sdb]])

3. **Systemic inflammation:** Intermittent hypoxia → NF-κB activation → TNF-α, IL-6 → myocardial fibrosis, hypertrophy; CaMKII-mediated MMP2 upregulation → ↑ passive LV stiffness

4. **CaMKII overactivation** (novel mechanism): Intermittent hypoxia/reoxygenation → ↑ ROS → CaMKII oxidation → dysregulated Na⁺/Ca²⁺ homeostasis → impaired diastolic SR Ca²⁺ reuptake → diastolic dysfunction + atrial arrhythmias (source: [[wester2023sdb]])

5. **Insulin resistance:** OSA strongly associated with T2DM; insulin resistance → RAAS activation + inflammation → HFpEF development

## Treatment Evidence

### SERVE-HF: Critical Safety Finding

SERVE-HF (N=1,325 HFrEF, LVEF ≤45%, AHI ≥15, predominantly CSA):
- ASV significantly **increased CV mortality** (HR 1.28; P=0.01) and all-cause mortality (HR 1.17; P=0.048)
- **Contraindication established: ASV is contraindicated in HFrEF + predominantly CSA** (source: [[cowie2017sdb]])
- Mechanism unknown: suppression of CSA may eliminate compensatory respiratory drive; elevated mean airway pressure reduces LV preload

### HFpEF-Specific Evidence (Limited)

- Small study (N=36 HFpEF+OSA): ASV improved diastolic function, reduced BNP, reduced composite HF endpoint — but sample size insufficient for conclusions (source: [[wester2023sdb]])
- **No large-scale RCT of CPAP or ASV specifically in HFpEF has been completed**
- CPAP in OSA: reduces AHI, improves sleepiness and BP; modest effects on cardiac function

### Indirect Treatment via Weight Loss

- GLP-1 RA (semaglutide): weight loss → OSA severity reduction; STEP-HFpEF data supports metabolic/obese HFpEF phenotype where SDB burden is highest (source: [[wester2023sdb]])
- SGLT2 inhibitors: reduce visceral/subcutaneous fat + CaMKII activity → may reduce SDB severity indirectly

## Therapeutic Implications

- Screening for SDB should be considered in all obese HFpEF patients
- OSA treatment (CPAP/weight loss) may reduce BP, cardiac remodelling, and inflammatory burden
- **CaMKII inhibition:** preclinically promising, clinically limited by specificity/bioavailability issues [knowledge gap — needs future ingest of CaMKII clinical data]

## Open Questions

- Does PAP therapy for OSA reduce HFpEF hospitalisation or mortality? (No large RCT completed)
- Is SDB-related arterial stiffening reversible with CPAP? (source: [[suzuki2018sdb]] establishes the association; intervention data lacking)
- Does treatment of SDB reduce AF burden in HFpEF? (CaMKII pathway suggests plausibility)
