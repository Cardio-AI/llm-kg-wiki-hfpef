# Chronotropic Incompetence

> Blunted heart rate response to exercise, present in roughly 30–50% of HFpEF patients, that limits cardiac output augmentation during exertion; a distinct comorbidity phenotype in which beta-blockers — the drug class most likely to worsen it — may do more harm than good.

## Mechanism

Chronotropic incompetence is the failure to augment heart rate appropriately in response to increasing exercise workload. It is one of the four components of the multimechanistic model of exercise intolerance in HFpEF, alongside skeletal muscle myopathy, pulmonary vascular limitation, and peripheral vascular dysfunction. (source: Sachdev2023Exercise)

**Quantitative importance:** in invasive cardiopulmonary exercise testing of HFpEF patients, the change in heart rate from rest to peak (ΔHR) is the **second strongest independent predictor of peak VO₂** after the arteriovenous oxygen difference (A-VO₂ Diff) reserve — partial correlate 0.53 (standardized β 0.43; P=0.0007), ahead of the change in stroke volume (partial correlate 0.35; P=0.04). Chronotropic incompetence therefore limits both cardiac output reserve directly and, secondarily, peripheral perfusion. (source: Haykowsky2011Exercise)

**Proposed mechanisms:** autonomic dysfunction, beta-blocker use, and diastolic dysfunction itself — at high heart rates, diastolic filling time is reduced, which can paradoxically worsen filling pressures even as heart rate rises. (source: Haykowsky2011Exercise)

**Prevalence:** estimates converge around 30–50% of HFpEF patients (Anker2023HFpEFPhenotype: 30–50%; Sachdev2023Exercise: ~50%). In the RELAX trial cohort specifically, chronotropic incompetence was present in **77%** of patients at baseline — the highest prevalence figure recorded in the wiki for this phenotype, in a population selected for exertional limitation with reduced peak VO₂/6MWD. (source: Redfield2013RELAX; source: Anker2023HFpEFPhenotype)

A related but distinct phenotype — high resting heart rate (>80 bpm) — is present in 20–30% of HFpEF patients and is associated with adverse outcomes in secondary analyses of CHARM and I-PRESERVE. (source: Anker2023HFpEFPhenotype)

## Clinical Relevance

### Beta-Blockers: A Central, Unresolved Tension

Beta-blockers reduce heart rate and are hypothesised to worsen chronotropic incompetence — one of the field's central, unresolved treatment questions, because beta-blockers are simultaneously among the most commonly prescribed drugs in HFpEF (60–80% of patients in registries), largely for comorbid indications (AF rate control, hypertension, ischaemic heart disease) rather than for HFpEF itself. (source: Kaddoura2024BetaBlocker; source: Anker2023HFpEFPhenotype)

**Evidence that beta-blockers may be harmful in the chronotropic incompetence phenotype:** beta-blocker **withdrawal** significantly increased functional capacity and VO₂ uptake in HFpEF patients with chronotropic incompetence — directly suggesting that these drugs are harmful in this specific subphenotype, even though they may be warranted when hypertension or ischaemic heart disease dominate the clinical picture. (source: Anker2023HFpEFPhenotype)

**Evidence on beta-blockers overall:** a systematic review and meta-analysis (16 studies, 27,188 HFpEF patients) found a statistically significant 19% reduction in all-cause mortality with beta-blocker therapy in observational cohort data (pooled OR 0.81, 95% CI 0.65–0.99, P=0.044), but this association lost significance after removing the two shortest follow-up studies (OR 0.77, 95% CI 0.59–1.02, P=0.068), and there was no significant effect on HF rehospitalisation (OR 1.13) or the composite outcome (OR 1.01). Fifteen of sixteen included studies were observational; the single available RCT (metoprolol succinate) was not powered for clinical outcomes. The apparent mortality benefit likely reflects confounding by indication — beta-blockers tend to be prescribed to healthier, more haemodynamically stable patients — rather than a causal effect, and the chronotropic incompetence mechanism means a net-harm scenario cannot be excluded. (source: Kaddoura2024BetaBlocker)

### Rate-Modulation Therapies Tested

- **Ivabradine (EDIFY trial):** no anti-hypertrophic/anti-remodelling effect and no improvement in exercise tolerance or NT-proBNP in HFpEF. (source: Anker2023HFpEFPhenotype)
- **myPACE RCT:** a personalised, accelerated heart-rate pacemaker setting improved NT-proBNP, physical activity, and KCCQ. (source: Anker2023HFpEFPhenotype)
- **Rate-adaptive atrial pacing:** did not improve exercise capacity. (source: Anker2023HFpEFPhenotype)

The divergent results (device-based accelerated pacing positive; rate-adaptive atrial pacing and ivabradine neutral) indicate that simply raising heart rate is not sufficient — the specific means of chronotropic modulation matters, and mechanisms are incompletely understood.

## Open Questions

- Can chronotropic incompetence be pharmacologically corrected in HFpEF without net harm, given that beta-blockers — the most heart-rate–active drug class in routine use — may worsen it?
- In patients where chronotropic incompetence coexists with a competing indication for beta-blockade (AF, hypertension, ischaemic heart disease), how should the conflicting phenotype-specific guidance be resolved?
- Why did myPACE (accelerated pacing) succeed while rate-adaptive atrial pacing and ivabradine failed to improve exercise capacity — is the benefit specific to pacing device physiology, or to patient selection?
- Does the very high chronotropic incompetence prevalence in RELAX (77%) reflect a genuinely more exercise-limited trial population, or ascertainment differences from the 30–50% general HFpEF estimates?

## Contradictions

Beta-blockers are recommended as phenotype-specific add-on therapy for the hypertension and ischaemic heart disease phenotypes, yet withdrawal of beta-blockers improves functional capacity in the chronotropic incompetence phenotype — the same drug class is beneficial or harmful depending on which HFpEF phenotype dominates in a given patient, and many patients have overlapping phenotypes.
