# HFpEF Mimics and Differential Diagnosis

> A defined set of non-HFpEF diseases reproduce the clinical phenotype of preserved LVEF with elevated filling pressures ("secondary HFpEF" / "masqueraders") but carry disease-specific, often disease-modifying, treatments — active exclusion is a precondition for a valid HFpEF diagnosis, particularly at LVEF extremes.

## Mechanism

HFpEF is diagnosed on a clinical/haemodynamic phenotype — preserved LVEF (≥50%) with signs of elevated LV filling pressure — but this phenotype is not specific to the comorbidity-driven, diastolic-dysfunction mechanism that defines "primary" HFpEF (see [[diastolic-dysfunction]]). A distinct set of diseases produce the same functional picture through entirely different pathophysiology (infiltration, hypertrophic sarcomere mutation, pericardial restriction, or high-flow states), and are therefore classified as "secondary HFpEF" or "masqueraders" rather than HFpEF proper. Because their treatments diverge sharply from standard HFpEF management, misclassification has direct therapeutic consequences. (source: Anker2023HFpEFPhenotype; source: borlaug2023statement)

## The Mimic Categories

The Borlaug 2023 JACC Scientific Statement lists non-HFpEF "masqueraders" that must be excluded before a HFpEF diagnosis is accepted (Table 2): ATTR amyloidosis, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM), cardiac sarcoidosis, Fabry disease, restrictive cardiomyopathy, and high-output HF (source: borlaug2023statement). The HFA/ESC phenotype profiling statement (Anker 2023) frames the same set as "secondary HFpEF," requiring specific management rather than standard HFpEF treatment: restrictive cardiomyopathy (ATTR, AL amyloidosis, Fabry disease), hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, constrictive pericarditis, and valvular heart disease (source: Anker2023HFpEFPhenotype).

| Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) | Sarcomeric gene mutation → myocyte hypertrophy/disarray | ~5% | Genetic; different prognosis and treatment algorithm (beta-blockers, disopyramide) vs. standard HFpEF |
| Cardiac sarcoidosis | Granulomatous infiltration | Not quantified in wiki sources | Higher prevalence reported in Black patients |
| Restrictive cardiomyopathy (idiopathic/infiltrative) | Amyloidosis, sarcoidosis, or haemochromatosis-driven infiltration; idiopathic forms also occur | <1% | Distinguished from primary HFpEF by restrictive filling pattern out of proportion to wall thickness |
| Constrictive pericarditis | Pericardial fibrosis/calcification restricting diastolic filling | Not quantified in wiki sources | Right heart catheterisation recommended to differentiate in ambiguous cases |
| Fabry disease | Lysosomal glycosphingolipid accumulation → LV hypertrophy | Not quantified in wiki sources | Enzyme replacement/chaperone therapy available — disease-specific |
| Haemochromatosis | Iron deposition-driven restrictive/infiltrative cardiomyopathy | Not quantified in wiki sources | Treatable with iron chelation/phlebotomy |

## Clinical Relevance

**Triggers for extended workup:** Very high LVEF (>65–70%) shows U-shaped mortality (lowest at 60–65%) and mandates secondary HFpEF workup for ATTR, HCM, paradoxical low-gradient aortic stenosis, and Fabry disease — each with a specific treatment (source: Anker2023HFpEFPhenotype). ESC 2021 guidance similarly states that restrictive cardiomyopathies, cardiac amyloidosis, and constrictive pericarditis can produce haemodynamics indistinguishable from HFpEF and must be excluded before attribution; right heart catheterisation is recommended to differentiate these in clinically ambiguous cases (source: McDonagh2021ESC).

**Extended diagnostic workup:** CMR, technetium-pyrophosphate (Tc-PYP) scintigraphy, endomyocardial biopsy, and genetic testing (source: Anker2023HFpEFPhenotype).

**Race-specific risk:** Black patients carry a disproportionate burden of several mimics — HCM, cardiac sarcoidosis, constrictive pericarditis, high-output HF, Fabry disease, and haemochromatosis, in addition to the ATTR V122I variant (3.43% prevalence in Black Americans ≥60 years) — requiring comprehensive diagnostic workup before attributing an unexplained HFpEF phenotype to primary disease (source: Ilonze2024Disparities). See [[hfpef-disparities]].

## Open Questions

- What is the true population prevalence of each individual mimic (cardiac sarcoidosis, Fabry disease, haemochromatosis, high-output HF, constrictive pericarditis) within diagnosed HFpEF cohorts? — not quantified in current wiki sources.
- Should extended workup (CMR, Tc-PYP, genetic testing) be systematically applied at first HFpEF diagnosis, or reserved for patients meeting specific triggers (LVEF >65–70%, unexplained LVH, Black race)?
- How much of the reported HFpEF treatment-trial heterogeneity reflects unexcluded mimics within enrolled cohorts?

## Contradictions
None identified — the HFA/ESC (Anker 2023) and Borlaug 2023 frameworks converge on largely the same mimic list (restrictive cardiomyopathy/ATTR/AL amyloidosis, HCM, Fabry, constrictive pericarditis, high-output HF), differing only in emphasis (comorbidity wheel vs. pathophysiological phenogroup framing).
