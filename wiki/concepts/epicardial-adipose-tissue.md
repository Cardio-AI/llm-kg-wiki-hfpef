# Epicardial Adipose Tissue in HFpEF

> EAT is a metabolically active fat depot with direct access to the myocardium; in obese HFpEF, enlarged EAT exerts pericardial restraint and secretes proinflammatory adipokines, contributing to diastolic dysfunction, AF, and microvascular dysfunction through mechanisms independent of systemic obesity.

## Anatomy and Assessment

- EAT: fat between visceral pericardium and myocardium; ~75% lies over RV; ~20% of total heart weight in non-obese individuals
- Shares microcirculation with myocardium — **no fascial barrier** between EAT and adjacent myocardium → direct paracrine signalling
- Strongly associated with VAT, obesity, metabolic syndrome — but has **independent effects** beyond systemic adiposity (source: [[timoteo2024eat]])
- **Assessment:**
  - CT: preferred for EAT volume (3D reconstruction, Hounsfield units)
  - Echo: measures thickness (parasternal long/short axis)
  - CMR: volume + functional characterisation
  - EAT density (HU) inversely correlated with volume; lower density (≤−76.2 HU) = more severe metabolic syndrome + higher mortality in HFpEF

## Mechanism 1: Pericardial Restraint

EAT accumulation → pericardium becomes taut → physical compression of LV → ↑ LVEDP → ↑ PCWP:
- **20–40%** of LVEDP relates to extrinsic pericardial restraint at rest; increases with volume loading (source: [[timoteo2024eat]])
- RV cannot dilate freely → septal shift → biventricular interdependence
- Modest ↑ in venous return → disproportionate ↑ filling pressures

**Imaging evidence (Zamani 2023, N=28 obese HFpEF):**
- Epicardial fat vs. LV eccentricity index: **r=0.88; P<0.001**
- Paracardial fat vs. LV eccentricity: **r=0.91; P<0.001**
- BMI, subcutaneous fat, visceral fat: **NOT correlated** with LV eccentricity
- 13/28 patients (46%) had abnormal cardio-mechanical interaction (LV eccentricity >1.0)
- **EAT specifically** (not systemic obesity) drives cardiac compression (source: [[zamani2023pericardialfat]])

**Pericardiotomy pilot (4 obese HFpEF women; thoracoscopic):** pericardiectomy improved LVEDV at similar filling pressures + improved peak VO₂ at 3 months; proof-of-concept for pericardial restraint hypothesis (source: [[timoteo2024eat]])

## Mechanism 2: Paracrine/Endocrine Dysfunction

In obesity, EAT transitions from protective to pathological adipokine profile:
- **↓ Anti-inflammatory:** ↓ adiponectin
- **↑ Pro-inflammatory:** ↑ leptin, resistin, TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, MCP-1, PAI-1
- Direct diffusion via shared microcirculation → myocardial inflammation, fibrosis, microvascular rarefaction
- Atrial EAT → ↑ AF recurrence after catheter ablation (3-fold ↑ subclinical AF in monitoring studies)
- Leptinaldosterone-neprilysin axis: leptin → ↑ aldosterone → ↑ sodium retention + neprilysin activity → HFpEF development (source: [[timoteo2024eat]])

## Treatment Targets

| Intervention | EAT Effect | Mechanism | Evidence Quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| **GLP-1 RA** | Largest reduction (~2× SGLT2i) | Weight loss; dose-dependent, rapid | STEP-HFpEF supportive |
| **SGLT2 inhibitors** | ↓ EAT volume and density | Metabolic + anti-inflammatory | Observational + trial data |
| **Statins** | ↓ EAT + anti-inflammatory | Atorvastatin > simvastatin/ezetimibe for EAT | [[ortegahernandez2024statins]] |
| **MRA/ARNi** | Indirect (blocks EAT paracrine pathway) | Mineralocorticoid receptor blockade | Insufficient EAT data |
| **Pericardiotomy** | Relieves pericardial restraint (not reduces EAT) | Mechanical | N=4 pilot only |

## Clinical Relevance

- EAT is a **modifiable target** in obese HFpEF amenable to GLP-1 RA and SGLT2i
- EAT reduction likely contributes to the exercise capacity benefit seen in STEP-HFpEF/SUMMIT with semaglutide
- EAT imaging (CT/CMR) may help identify patients most likely to benefit from obesity-targeted treatments [knowledge gap — prospective data needed]
