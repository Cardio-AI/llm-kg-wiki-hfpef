# Magnesium in HFpEF

> Serum magnesium is both a prognostic marker and an SGLT2-inhibitor-modulated biomarker in HFmrEF/HFpEF: in EMPEROR-Preserved, higher (not lower) baseline magnesium predicted worse outcomes on placebo, yet patients with higher baseline magnesium derived the greatest benefit from empagliflozin — the opposite pattern from HFrEF, where low magnesium is the adverse marker.

## Biology

Magnesium is an abundant intracellular cation essential for energy-dependent cellular processes; only ~1% circulates extracellularly, measured as serum magnesium (normal adult range 0.7–1.0 mmol/L, i.e. 1.7–2.4 mg/dL). It is essential for cardiac contractility and rhythm, as well as for endothelial and vascular function. (source: Ferreira2026Emperor)

Two mechanistic hypotheses have been proposed for a potentially detrimental role of elevated magnesium in HFmrEF/HFpEF specifically:
1. **Calcium competition:** at the cardiomyocyte level, magnesium competes with calcium for key calcium-handling proteins (RyR2, SERCA2a, NCX), potentially impairing calcium cycling, delaying relaxation, and contributing to elevated ventricular filling pressures.
2. **Sympathetic and conduction effects:** magnesium inhibits norepinephrine release from sympathetic nerve terminals by blocking presynaptic voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels, leading to reduced sympathetic tone, negative chronotropic effects, and slowed electrical conduction (e.g., at the atrioventricular node) — which may compromise cardiac performance particularly under stress or exertion.

(source: Ferreira2026Emperor)

## Evidence: EMPEROR-Preserved Secondary Analysis (Ferreira 2026)

Pre-specified secondary analysis of EMPEROR-Preserved (N=5,988; LVEF >40%, i.e. combined HFmrEF/HFpEF; median follow-up 26.2 months; empagliflozin 10 mg/day vs. placebo).

### Baseline Magnesium Quintiles

| Quintile | Serum Mg (mmol/L) | N |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | 0.67±0.07 | 1,291 |
| Q2 | 0.78±0.02 | 1,189 |
| Q3 (referent) | 0.83±0.01 | 1,378 |
| Q4 | 0.88±0.01 | 1,078 |
| Q5 | 0.96±0.05 | 1,052 |

Patients with higher serum magnesium were older, had more atrial fibrillation, higher NT-proBNP, lower eGFR, and used loop diuretics more often; patients with lower magnesium had a higher prevalence of type 2 diabetes and used thiazide-type diuretics more often. (source: Ferreira2026Emperor)

### Magnesium as a Prognostic Marker (Placebo Arm)

Relative to Q3, the highest magnesium quintile (Q5) carried a significantly higher risk of the primary composite (cardiovascular death or HF hospitalization): fully adjusted HR 1.44 (95% CI 1.09–1.90; P=0.010), driven by both cardiovascular death (adjusted HR 1.54, 95% CI 1.14–2.11) and HF hospitalization (adjusted HR 1.55, 95% CI 1.14–2.11). The lowest quintile (Q1, hypomagnesemia) showed no significant difference in risk versus Q3 (fully adjusted HR 0.93; P=0.62) — the risk gradient was specific to elevated, not reduced, magnesium. Associations limited to the HFpEF subgroup (LVEF ≥50%) overlapped those seen in the full cohort, and there was no significant magnesium-by-LVEF interaction (P>0.10 for all outcomes). (source: Ferreira2026Emperor)

### Empagliflozin's Effect on Serum Magnesium

Empagliflozin increased serum magnesium by an average of 0.05 mmol/L (95% CI 0.044–0.049; P<0.001) versus placebo, with the increase apparent as early as week 4 and sustained at a steady state thereafter, regardless of baseline magnesium level (slightly more pronounced in the lowest quintile: 0.06 mmol/L vs. 0.04–0.05 mmol/L in other quintiles). Empagliflozin reduced the odds of hypomagnesemia (serum Mg <0.75 mmol/L) by an estimated 69% (OR 0.31, 95% CI 0.26–0.36; P<0.001) while increasing the odds of hypermagnesemia (serum Mg >0.90 mmol/L) more than three-fold (OR 3.34, 95% CI 2.93–3.81; P<0.001). (source: Ferreira2026Emperor)

### Treatment Interaction by Baseline Magnesium

The empagliflozin (vs. placebo) treatment effect on the primary composite was **more pronounced at higher baseline magnesium levels**: Q1 HR 1.05 (95% CI 0.77–1.39); Q2 HR 0.85 (0.63–1.14); Q3 HR 0.88 (0.66–1.17); Q4 HR 0.62 (0.45–0.86); Q5 HR 0.60 (0.45–0.79) — interaction P-trend=0.030 (treatment-by-magnesium as a continuous variable, P=0.012). A similar pattern of greater apparent benefit at higher magnesium was observed for both cardiovascular death and HF hospitalization individually, despite nonsignificant interaction tests for those individual components (attributed to lower statistical power). (source: Ferreira2026Emperor)

## Clinical Relevance

This is presented as the first large study to explore associations of serum magnesium with cardiovascular and renal outcomes specifically in HFmrEF/HFpEF. The practical implication drawn by the authors: higher serum magnesium does not appear problematic in HFmrEF/HFpEF as long as patients are treated with an SGLT2 inhibitor, because the empagliflozin benefit was, if anything, greater among higher-magnesium patients — more than offsetting the placebo-arm risk gradient associated with elevated magnesium. (source: Ferreira2026Emperor)

The authors note that the placebo-arm association between higher magnesium and worse outcomes may be confounded: impaired kidney function, older age, and atrial fibrillation were all more prevalent among patients with higher magnesium levels in this cohort, and it cannot be fully ascertained whether magnesium has a true direct influence on HFmrEF/HFpEF outcomes or is instead a marker of these more prevalent comorbidities. (source: Ferreira2026Emperor)

## Contrast with HFrEF

The direction of the magnesium–SGLT2i interaction in EMPEROR-Preserved is the **opposite** of that previously reported in EMPEROR-Reduced (HFrEF): in HFrEF, **low** (not high) baseline serum magnesium was associated with worse HF hospitalization, cardiovascular death, and adverse kidney outcomes, and empagliflozin's benefit appeared **more pronounced among patients with lower baseline magnesium**. In EMPEROR-Preserved, it is patients with **higher** baseline magnesium who both fare worse on placebo and derive the greatest empagliflozin benefit — a directionally reversed pattern across the two HF phenotypes. Whether this reflects genuinely different magnesium-related biology in HFmrEF/HFpEF versus HFrEF, or confounding by each population's differing comorbidity profile, is unresolved. (source: Ferreira2026Emperor)

## Limitations

- Secondary, exploratory (hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory) analysis of a trial not designed to test this specific hypothesis
- Serum magnesium is a crude surrogate for intracellular and total-body magnesium stores; no direct causal/mechanistic pathway linking magnesium elevation to SGLT2 inhibitor benefit has been established
- Analysis pools HFmrEF (LVEF 41–49%) and HFpEF (LVEF ≥50%); the HFpEF-restricted associations overlapped those for the whole cohort but were not independently powered
- P values and confidence intervals were not adjusted for multiplicity, given the exploratory nature of the analysis

(source: Ferreira2026Emperor)

## Open Questions

- Is the reversal of the magnesium–SGLT2i treatment interaction between HFrEF and HFmrEF/HFpEF a true biological phenomenon (distinct magnesium handling or myocardial calcium biology across phenotypes), or an artifact of differing comorbidity confounding (age, AF, kidney function) between the two trial populations?
- Does correcting or avoiding iatrogenic hypermagnesemia in HFpEF patients already on SGLT2i therapy meaningfully change outcomes, or is elevated magnesium purely a marker of confounding comorbidity (CKD, AF, older age) rather than a modifiable risk factor?
- What is the mechanistic basis, if any, for magnesium's proposed calcium-competitive effect on RyR2/SERCA2a/NCX translating into the specific diastolic/hemodynamic phenotype of HFpEF?

## Contradictions

Direction reversal of the magnesium–SGLT2i interaction across HF phenotypes: in HFrEF (EMPEROR-Reduced), lower baseline magnesium predicts greater SGLT2i benefit; in HFmrEF/HFpEF (EMPEROR-Preserved), higher baseline magnesium predicts greater empagliflozin benefit. This opposite direction suggests either genuinely different magnesium-related biology between HF phenotypes or a confounded/statistical artefact — currently unresolved. See [[contradictions]].
