# HFpEF Diagnostic Definitions — Heterogeneity and Consequences

> Competing guideline HFpEF definitions share the LVEF ≥50% criterion but diverge dramatically on additional requirements, enrolling 12–90% of the same cohort and capturing different sensitivity/specificity trade-offs for invasively confirmed physiologic HFpEF.

---

## Mechanism

### Why Definitions Diverge

All HFpEF definitions require LVEF ≥50% and HF symptoms, but differ in the additional criteria required to confirm elevated filling pressures:

| Definition | Additional Criteria | Typical Restrictiveness |
|---|---|---|
| ACC/AHA | Symptoms + LVEF ≥50%; structural/functional evidence (flexible) | Broad |
| ESC 2021 | Symptoms + LVEF ≥50% + ≥1 abnormal echocardiographic/NP marker (Table 9) | Moderate |
| HFSA | Strict echocardiographic diastolic dysfunction criteria | Narrow |
| TOPCAT trial | LVEF ≥45% + either: hospitalisation for HF or elevated NPs | Trial-specific |
| PARAGON trial | LVEF ≥45% + structural evidence | Trial-specific |
| I-PRESERVE trial | LVEF ≥45% + hospitalisation or clinical signs | Trial-specific |
| RELAX trial | LVEF ≥50% + elevated NPs or structural abnormality | Trial-specific |

The practical consequence: applying all seven definitions to the same 461-patient cohort enrolled 12% (HFSA) to 90% (ACC/AHA) of patients. (source: 2019-CirculationAHA-Ho-exercise-response.pdf)

### Sensitivity vs. Specificity Trade-off

**Reference standard:** Invasive CPET — physiologic HFpEF (HFpEF_phys) defined as PCWP ≥15 mmHg at rest or ≥25 mmHg during exercise. 53% of a referred HF cohort met this criterion. (source: 2019-CirculationAHA-Ho-exercise-response.pdf)

| Criterion | Sensitivity for HFpEF_phys | Specificity for HFpEF_phys |
|---|---|---|
| ACC/AHA definition | 91% | 11% |
| HFSA definition | 17% | 94% |
| Echo structural markers | 71% | 51% |
| NT-proBNP ≥125 pg/mL | 48% | 74% |
| E/e' >9 | 78% | 59% |
| E/e' >13 | 46% | 86% |

Broad definitions (ACC/AHA) are sensitive but enroll many patients without elevated filling pressures. Restrictive definitions (HFSA) exclude most such patients but miss the majority of true physiologic HFpEF. (source: 2019-CirculationAHA-Ho-exercise-response.pdf; source: 2021-ESC-Guidelines-Heart-Failure.pdf)

## Clinical Relevance

### Prognostic Weight of Physiologic HFpEF

HFpEF_phys (invasive PCWP elevation) independently predicts CV events (HR 1.62, p=0.01) even in patients not meeting any guideline definition. This means:
1. The physiologic abnormality carries prognostic risk regardless of label
2. Some true HFpEF patients are excluded by every guideline definition
3. CV event rates in trial cohorts vary 75–298 per 1000 person-years depending on definition applied — the same patient may be "low-risk" or "high-risk" depending on which definition labels them

(source: 2019-CirculationAHA-Ho-exercise-response.pdf)

### Implications for Trial Interpretation

**Why trials disagree or fail:** The populations enrolled by [[topcat]], [[paragon-hf]], [[i-preserve]], and [[charm-preserved]] are not equivalent populations despite all carrying the "HFpEF" label. Broad-enrollment trials (ACC/AHA-like criteria) include many patients without elevated filling pressures who have no pathophysiological target for filling-pressure-directed therapy. Restrictive-enrollment trials may have genuine treatment effect diluted by power limitations.

The [[topcat]] enrollment controversy (non-Americas suspected contamination) is an extreme case of the general principle: definitional heterogeneity and enrollment quality determine whether trial results are interpretable for any specific HFpEF subpopulation.

### The Physiologic Definition as a Reframe

HFpEF_phys — defined invasively by [[cardiopulmonary-exercise-testing|invasive CPET]] — may be a more biologically coherent target for therapy than any guideline definition. However, invasive testing is impractical at scale. Non-invasive proxies (E/e', NT-proBNP, LA volume index) have only moderate accuracy and cannot substitute for invasive confirmation in ambiguous cases.

## History

- **Pre-2019** — Multiple trial-specific definitions used in parallel; no systematic comparison of their diagnostic accuracy; tacit assumption that "HFpEF" was a shared population across trials
- **2019** — Ho et al. (MGH, n=461) first systematic comparison with invasive CPET reference; demonstrated enrollment heterogeneity 12–90% and showed HFpEF_phys is prognostically independent (source: 2019-CirculationAHA-Ho-exercise-response.pdf)
- **2021** — ESC Guidelines endorsed simplified three-criterion approach for clinical use; HFA-PEFF reserved for expert centres; acknowledged limitation that non-invasive markers have only moderate accuracy (source: 2021-ESC-Guidelines-Heart-Failure.pdf)

## Open Questions

- Can a single non-invasive screening algorithm reliably identify HFpEF_phys without invasive confirmation?
- Should future HFpEF trials use invasive CPET-confirmed enrollment to reduce heterogeneity?

- Which definition best predicts treatment response to SGLT2i, exercise training, or other emerging therapies?
