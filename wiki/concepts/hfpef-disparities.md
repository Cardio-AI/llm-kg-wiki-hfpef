# Racial and Ethnic Disparities in HFpEF

> Racial and ethnic disparities in HFpEF are compounded across the care continuum — from a disproportionate burden of disease in younger Black patients, to diagnostic under-performance of natriuretic peptide-based tools, to trial underrepresentation — requiring disease-specific and culturally-aware clinical approaches.

## Epidemiology

**First HFpEF hospitalisation rates (per 1,000 patient-years):**
| Group | Rate | Trend |
|-------|------|-------|
| Black women | **7.4** (highest) | Fastest annual increase |
| Black men | 6.2 | — |
| White women | 5.9 | — |
| White men | 4.9 | — |

- Black young adults (39±6y): **20-fold higher** cumulative HF incidence vs. White peers (CARDIA study)
- Higher HTN burden (earlier onset, more severe), higher obesity, higher diabetes prevalence in Black patients → earlier HFpEF onset
(source: [[ilonze2024disparities]])

## Diagnostic Barriers in Minority Populations

### 1. Low Natriuretic Peptide Levels in Black Patients

- ~20–35% of HFpEF outpatients with **confirmed elevated PCWP** have low BNP (<100 pg/mL) — predominantly Black patients
- **Causes:**
  - Lower LV diastolic wall stress per filling pressure (higher LV wall thickness relative to cavity)
  - NPPB gene polymorphisms (affecting natriuretic peptide production)
  - Insulin resistance → reduced NP secretion
  - Increased androgenicity
- **Clinical impact:** Normal NP levels cannot exclude HFpEF in Black patients → risk of under-diagnosis
(source: [[ilonze2024disparities]])

### 2. Diagnostic Scoring System Bias

- H2FPEF score and HFA-PEFF criteria validated in predominantly White cohorts
- Systematic **underdiagnosis of HFpEF in Black patients** confirmed in ML study (scoring systems calibrated to White population NP levels)
- [knowledge gap — race-adjusted HFpEF scoring algorithms needed]

## ATTR V122I Amyloid Cardiomyopathy

- Isoleucine 122 (V122I) TTR variant in **3.43% of Black Americans ≥60 years**
- Autosomal dominant; male predominance 6:1; poor survival; West African origin
- Clinically mimics HFpEF — diastolic dysfunction, preserved EF, exertional dyspnea
- Must be **actively excluded** in Black patients with unexplained HFpEF phenotype (source: [[ilonze2024disparities]])
- Also relevant: QM-6 in the 2024 ACC/AHA performance measures (source: [[kittleson2024accaha]])

## HFpEF Phenocopies in Black Patients

Beyond ATTR V122I, Black patients have higher prevalence of:
- Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM), cardiac sarcoidosis, constrictive pericarditis
- High-output HF, Fabry disease, haemochromatosis
- These require differentiation from non-obstructive HFpEF — diagnostic workup should be comprehensive

## Treatment Disparities

| Treatment | Finding |
|----------|---------|
| SGLT2 inhibitors | Evidence of effectiveness in Black patients; underutilised in this population |
| Semaglutide (STEP-HFpEF) | Benefit in Black patients; underrepresented in trial |
| Spironolactone (TOPCAT) | Effective in Americas subgroup (includes Black patients); underutilised |
| Guideline-directed therapy overall | Lower rates of prescription and uptake in minority populations |

## Social Determinants of Health

- 2024 ACC/AHA update (source: [[kittleson2024accaha]]): new quality measure (QM-2) for SDOH screening in all HF patients
- Food insecurity, housing instability, transportation barriers → medication non-adherence, missed follow-up
- [knowledge gap — SDOH-targeted interventions with HFpEF outcomes data are lacking]

## Open Questions

- Should NP diagnostic thresholds be race-adjusted? (raises equity concerns vs. medical accuracy)
- How should ATTR V122I screening be integrated into HFpEF workup in Black patients? (cost-effectiveness unknown)
- Are there HFpEF-specific disparities in exercise rehabilitation uptake and outcomes?
