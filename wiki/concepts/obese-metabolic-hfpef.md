# Obese-Metabolic HFpEF Phenotype

> In obese HFpEF, metabolic and inflammatory dysregulation — driven primarily by adipose tissue — creates a systemic substrate for HFpEF that extends well beyond the heart; weight loss and metabolic correction (GLP-1 RA, SGLT2i) produce clinically meaningful symptom improvement in this phenotype.

## Aliases
| Alias | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Obese HFpEF | alternate-name | most common clinical shorthand |
| Metabolic HFpEF | alternate-name | emphasises metabolic mechanism |
| Adipogenic HFpEF | research-name | adipose tissue-driven subtype |
| Obesity-dominant phenotype | alternate-name | used in phenomapping literature |

## Definition and Epidemiology

**Clinical definition:** BMI ≥30 kg/m² plus HFpEF (LVEF ≥45–50%); often accompanied by T2DM, metabolic syndrome, OSA
**Prevalence:** ~30% of HFpEF (Shah 2014 phenomapping; [[shah2015phenomapping]]); obesity prevalence 30–40% in contemporary HFpEF cohorts (Anker 2023; source: [[anker2023hfpefphenotype]])
**Demographics:** Younger age at HFpEF onset; higher representation of women; Black patients over-represented relative to overall HFpEF (source: [[ilonze2024disparities]])

## Pathophysiology

### Epicardial and Pericardial Adipose Tissue
- **EAT** infiltrates the myocardium and coronary adventitia; paracrine inflammation impairs microvascular function (source: [[timoteo2024eat]])
- Pericardial fat → pericardial restraint → impairs LV filling and diastolic compliance (source: [[timoteo2024eat]])
- EAT volume correlates strongly with LV eccentricity ratio (r=0.88; source: [[timoteo2024eat]])

### Systemic Metabolic-Inflammatory Axis
- **Lipotoxicity:** Excess free fatty acids → mitochondrial ROS overproduction → cardiomyocyte dysfunction
- **Insulin resistance:** RAAS activation + ↓ NO signalling → vascular stiffness + LV hypertrophy
- **Adipokine imbalance:** ↑ leptin (pro-inflammatory), ↓ adiponectin (cardioprotective)
- **Systemic low-grade inflammation:** IL-6, CRP, TNF-α elevated → microvascular endothelial dysfunction (source: [[paulus2013novelparadigm]])

### Sleep-Disordered Breathing
Obese HFpEF: OSA prevalence 50–80% (source: [[wester2023sdb]]):
- Intermittent hypoxia → ROS → CaMKII oxidation → Ca²⁺ dysregulation → AF substrate
- Dynamic hyperinflation → confounds measured PCWP (overestimates transmural filling pressure)
- SERVE-HF showed that ASV is contraindicated in HFrEF with central sleep apnoea; currently unknown for obese HFpEF

### Peripheral and Ventilatory Limitation
- Peak VO₂ impairment is disproportionately due to peripheral (skeletal muscle) dysfunction, not purely cardiac limitation (source: [[haykowsky2011exercise]])
- A-VO₂ difference reserve is the strongest independent predictor of peak VO₂ (β=0.66; source: [[peripheral-mechanisms-hfpef]])
- Dynamic hyperinflation further limits exercise tolerance in obese HFpEF (source: [[babb2026ventilatorylimit]])

## Key Clinical Features

| Feature | Obese HFpEF | Non-obese HFpEF |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|
| BMI | ≥30 kg/m² | <30 kg/m² |
| NT-proBNP | **Lower** (diluted by obesity) | Higher |
| CRP, IL-6 | Higher | Lower |
| Exercise intolerance | Severe | Moderate |
| OSA prevalence | 50–80% | 20–30% |
| Age at onset | Younger | Older |
| Primary mechanism | Metabolic-inflammatory | Fibrotic-hypertensive |

**Diagnostic challenge:** NT-proBNP under-estimates filling pressure elevation in obese HFpEF — requires echocardiographic and haemodynamic confirmation (source: [[ilonze2024disparities]])

## Treatment Evidence

### GLP-1 Receptor Agonists

**STEP-HFpEF (N=529; non-DM; semaglutide 2.4 mg vs. placebo; 52 weeks):**

| Outcome | Sema | Placebo | Difference | P |
|---------|------|---------|-----------|---|
| KCCQ-CSS (pts) | +16.6 | +8.7 | **+7.8** | <0.001 |
| Body weight (%) | −13.3% | −2.6% | **−10.7 pp** | <0.001 |
| 6MWD (m) | +21.5 | +1.2 | **+20.3** | <0.001 |
| CRP ratio | — | — | 0.61 | <0.001 |
| Win ratio (composite) | — | — | **1.72** | <0.001 |

(source: [[kosiborod2023stephfpef]])

**Limitation:** Not powered for CV mortality/HF hospitalisation; 95.8% White; low baseline SGLT2i use (3.6%)

**STEP-HFpEF DM (N=616; T2DM; semaglutide 2.4 mg):** KCCQ-CSS +7.3 pts, 6MWD +14.3 m, weight −6.4% (source: [[kosiborod2024stephfpefdm]])

**SUMMIT (NCT04847557; Packer 2025, NEJM; published):** Tirzepatide (GLP-1/GIP dual agonist) vs. placebo in obese HFpEF (BMI ≥30, LVEF ≥50%, no T2DM required); N=731. Co-primary endpoints both met: KCCQ-CSS +6.9 pts (P<0.001); CV death/worsening HF HR 0.62 (95% CI 0.41–0.95; P=0.026) — **first GLP-1RA trial with hard event reduction in HFpEF.** CMR substudy: pericardial AT −43 mL (P<0.001); LV mass −11 g (P=0.004). (source: [[packer2025summit]]; source: [[kramer2025summit-cmr]])

### SGLT2 Inhibitors (All HFpEF Including Obese Phenotype)

- EMPEROR-Preserved: HR 0.79 (21% reduction in HHF/CV death); benefit consistent in obese subgroup (source: [[anker2023hfpefphenotype]])
- DELIVER: Similar benefit; consistent across LVEF spectrum (source: [[anker2023hfpefphenotype]])
- SGLT2i reduce EAT, systemic inflammation, and mitochondrial dysfunction (source: [[albulushi2025sglt2fibrosis]])

### Phenotype-Based Treatment Wheel (Anker 2023)

Recommended add-ons for obese HFpEF beyond SGLT2i:
1. Semaglutide / Tirzepatide (GLP-1 RA / dual agonist)
2. Aggressive management of OSA (CPAP or weight loss)
3. Structured aerobic exercise training (source: [[sachdev2023exercise]])
(source: [[anker2023hfpefphenotype]])

## Open Questions

- Does weight loss with GLP-1 RA reduce CV mortality independently in obese HFpEF? (SUMMIT showed HR 0.62 for CV death/worsening HF composite; CV mortality alone not independently significant — partial gap closure)
- Is there additive benefit of GLP-1 RA + SGLT2i in obese HFpEF? (not tested in any RCT)
- Does sarcopenic obesity (lean mass loss during GLP-1 RA weight loss) worsen functional capacity long-term?
- Should NT-proBNP thresholds be obesity-adjusted for HFpEF diagnosis?
