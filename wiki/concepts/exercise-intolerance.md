# Exercise Intolerance in HFpEF

> Reduced peak VO2 and exercise capacity are the dominant symptoms of HFpEF; the primary mechanism is peripheral skeletal muscle myopathy (A-VO2 difference accounts for >50% of VO2 reduction), with cardiac, pulmonary vascular, and peripheral vascular contributions operating in parallel.

## Mechanism

### Four-Component Model

Exercise intolerance in [[hfpef]] is multimechanistic. Invasive [[cardiopulmonary-exercise-testing|CPET]] with simultaneous measurement of cardiac output (CO), pulmonary artery wedge pressure (PCWP), and arteriovenous O2 difference allows mechanistic dissection:

**1. Skeletal muscle myopathy (primary contributor)**
- A-VO2 difference (peripheral O2 extraction) accounts for >50% of the VO2 reduction vs. healthy age-matched controls
- Myopathic changes: fibre-type shift (type I → type II), mitochondrial dysfunction, reduced capillary density, impaired oxidative phosphorylation
- Mechanism is partially reversible with [[supervised-exercise-training|exercise training]]
- This is the single largest correctable target for exercise-based interventions

**2. Cardiac limitation**
- Blunted stroke volume augmentation: stiff LV (increased afterload, reduced diastolic compliance) limits Frank-Starling reserve during exercise
- Chronotropic incompetence: present in ~50% of HFpEF patients; attenuated heart rate response to exercise limits cardiac output reserve
- PCWP rises excessively during exercise (>25 mmHg) in >50% of HFpEF patients, reflecting [[diastolic-dysfunction]]

**3. Pulmonary vascular**
- Chronically elevated PCWP → secondary pulmonary hypertension (TR velocity >2.8 m/s in a substantial fraction)
- Elevated RV afterload + V/Q mismatch limits ventilatory efficiency (elevated VE/VCO2 slope)
- Contributes to dyspnoea disproportionate to exercise load

**4. Peripheral vascular**
- Impaired arteriolar vasodilation and reduced skeletal muscle perfusion during exercise
- Endothelial dysfunction; reduced NO-mediated vasodilation (mechanistic target of nitrate trials [[redfield2015neat]], [[zamani2015indie]] — both neutral)
- Systemic microvascular dysfunction → impaired reactive hyperaemia and abnormal regional vasodilation during exercise (source: Pfeffer2019HFpEF)
- **Sympathetically-mediated vasoconstriction (dynamic exercise):** MSNA (muscle sympathetic nerve activity) increases paradoxically during dynamic cycling in HFpEF — opposite to healthy controls, which show MSNA suppression during exercise. This drives excessive SVR elevation, reduced leg blood flow (LBF), and reduced leg vascular conductance (LVC). HFpEF patients require a higher perfusion pressure to maintain a given LBF than both HFrEF and controls. SVRI reduction with exercise is negatively correlated with VO₂peak. This sympathetic excess is specific to dynamic exercise — during static (isometric) handgrip exercise HFpEF MSNA responses resemble healthy controls, implying a distinct mechanism for static vs. dynamic exercise intolerance. (source: manabe2023sympathetic; source: borlaug2010exercise)
- **Functional sympatholysis gap:** In healthy muscle, locally released metabolites override sympathetic vasoconstriction to ensure O2 delivery ("functional sympatholysis"). Whether this mechanism is impaired specifically in HFpEF — and thus fails to counteract excessive MSNA — is a major unresolved knowledge gap. (source: manabe2023sympathetic)

(source: Sachdev2023Exercise)

### Respiratory Muscle Oxygen Competition (IMT mechanism)
A less-recognised peripheral contributor: during high-intensity exertion, respiratory muscles (diaphragm, intercostals) compete with locomotor muscles for limited cardiac output. In HFpEF — with reduced cardiac output reserve — this competition is proportionally more costly. Inspiratory muscle training (IMT) specifically reduces the metabolic demand of respiratory muscles, effectively redirecting O2 delivery to exercising limb muscles. This mechanism is distinct from all four components above and explains why IMT achieves VO₂ improvement (~+2.9 mL/kg/min; Palau 2014) without changing echocardiographic parameters. (source: Bohmke2022Nonpharm)

### Caloric Restriction and Skeletal Muscle Fat Infiltration
Obesity-related intermuscular adipose tissue (IMAT) infiltration directly impairs skeletal muscle oxidative capacity and O2 utilisation. Caloric restriction (−7% body weight in SECRET trial) reduced fat mass and LV mass, yielding VO₂peak +1.3 mL/kg/min — effect was additive with MCT exercise training. Mechanism is peripheral: reduction in IMAT → improved skeletal muscle metabolic efficiency. This links the obesity HFpEF phenotype specifically to the peripheral component of exercise intolerance. (source: Bohmke2022Nonpharm)

### Why the Hierarchy Matters

Earlier conceptual models centred [[diastolic-dysfunction]] (cardiac filling pressure) as the primary exercise limiter. The invasive CPET evidence establishes that peripheral skeletal muscle is the dominant contributor — cardiac filling pressure elevation is real but accounts for a minority of the VO2 deficit. This reframes therapy: interventions targeting filling pressures alone (diuretics, nitrates, RAAS) are insufficient; restoring skeletal muscle function requires exercise training.

## Clinical Relevance

**Measurement:** Peak VO2 is the primary metric. Normal predicted peak VO2 by age/sex; HFpEF patients typically 40–60% of predicted. Exercise time and 6MWD are secondary but clinically meaningful.

**Prognostic weight:** Lower peak VO2 is associated with worse outcomes in HFpEF. Peak VO2 improvement with [[supervised-exercise-training]] (+2.8 mL/kg/min) is clinically meaningful; effects on hard outcomes are not yet established.

**Therapeutic implication:** SET is currently the only intervention with consistent, replicated positive effects on exercise capacity in HFpEF. Cardiac- and vascular-targeted interventions ([[sacubitril-valsartan]], nitrates, sGC stimulators) have not consistently improved exercise capacity in RCTs.

## History

- **Pre-2010s** — Exercise intolerance in HFpEF attributed primarily to cardiac filling pressure elevation ([[diastolic-dysfunction]]); peripheral muscle not systematically characterised
- **2010** — Kitzman et al. published the first randomised exercise training trial in HFpEF, demonstrating substantial improvement in cardiorespiratory fitness (source: Pfeffer2019HFpEF)
- **2010** — Borlaug et al. (invasive supine exercise hemodynamics, n=55): 58% of patients with exertional dyspnea, normal BNP, and normal resting hemodynamics had HFpEF by exercise PCWP ≥25 mmHg; hemodynamic gap emerged within 1.5 min at 20W; exercise PASP ≥45 mmHg identified HFpEF with 96% sensitivity and 95% specificity. All noninvasive markers (BNP, E/e', ESC algorithm) had AUC <0.70. Established invasive exercise hemodynamics as the reference standard for early, exercise-unmasked HFpEF. (source: borlaug2010exercise)
- **2010–2020** — Invasive CPET studies at specialist centres (including MGH, Wake Forest) demonstrated that peripheral A-VO2 difference, not cardiac output, accounts for the majority of VO2 reduction in HFpEF; chronotropic incompetence quantified in ~50%
- **SECRET trial (Kitzman)** — Exercise training combined with caloric restriction → significantly improved aerobic capacity; combination was **additive** — both interventions contributed independently; CR mechanism: fat mass / IMAT reduction → skeletal muscle metabolic efficiency (source: Pfeffer2019HFpEF; source: Bohmke2022Nonpharm)
- **2014** — Palau 2014 crossover RCT: IMT at 25–30% MIP for 12 weeks → VO₂peak +2.9 mL/kg/min (P=0.02) without echocardiographic change; establishes respiratory muscle O2 competition as a tractable exercise intolerance contributor (source: Bohmke2022Nonpharm)
- **2020** — Kinugasa 2020 at-home IMT (30% MIP × 24 weeks): improved anaerobic threshold and % predicted MIP; demonstrates home delivery feasibility (source: Bohmke2022Nonpharm)
- **2022** — Bohmke review synthesises all 4 exercise modalities (MCT, HIIT, combined, IMT) + dietary strategies; OPTIMEX-CLIN (N=180, HIIT vs. MCT) confirms no HIIT superiority at 3 and 12 months
- **2023** — Sachdev AHA Scientific Statement formalised the mechanistic hierarchy; SET meta-analysis (8 RCTs, n=503) established exercise training as the most effective available intervention (source: Sachdev2023Exercise)
- **2023** — Borlaug JACC Scientific Statement: noncardiac contributors to exercise intolerance quantified — skeletal muscle (fibre shift, sarcopenia, mitochondrial loss), autonomic (chronotropic incompetence ~50%, baroreflex insensitivity), lung (diffusion abnormality), kidney. Confirmed 5-component disease spectrum. (source: borlaug2023statement)
- **2023** — Manabe et al. mini-review: sympathetic neural responses to exercise in HFpEF characterised — MSNA case data document paradoxical sympathoexcitation during dynamic cycling; static exercise MSNA resembles controls; functional sympatholysis identified as major knowledge gap. (source: manabe2023sympathetic)
- **2025** — Landsteiner et al. (N=872, MGH): exercise PCWP/CO slope >2 mmHg/L/min predicts CV outcomes independent of resting hemodynamics (HR 1.42, 95% CI 1.08–1.86, P=0.012); exercise-unmasked HFpEF is a distinct prognostic category. (source: landsteiner2025hemodynamics)
- **2024** — Sharif et al. (JCF, pilot RCT n=22): 12.5-week progressive resistance training in HFpEF; VO₂peak 17.1→19.4 mL/kg/min; leg press 92→129 kg; LF% 32.6→31.6% (unchanged — fat-selective improvement); lean mass increased — confirms resistance training reverses muscle mass loss without introducing fat mass gain; peripheral mechanism reinforced (source: sharif2024locomotor)
- **2024** — Obaya et al. (Physiol Res Int, RCT n=40): lower-limb aerobic cycling (21.51 mL/kg/min post) superior to upper-limb arm ergometry (19.26 mL/kg/min; P<0.001); LVEF unchanged in both arms — VO₂ improvement is limb-specific, not cardiac, reinforcing peripheral skeletal muscle as the exercise intolerance mechanism (source: obaya2024aerobic)
- **2023** — SECRET-II (Brubaker, Circ Heart Fail 2023; NCT02636439; N=88): CR+AT vs. RT+CR+AT in obese HFpEF; both improved VO₂peak ~5–7% and KCCQ ~15–20 pts; RT added leg strength (+4.9 Nm; P=0.05) and muscle quality (+0.07 Nm/cm²; P=0.043) but did NOT add VO₂ benefit (P=0.21) and did NOT prevent skeletal muscle mass loss — the primary hypothesis that RT offsets CR-induced lean mass loss was refuted. Confirms aerobic mechanism dominates; resistance and aerobic pathways are separable. (source: Brubaker2023SECRET2)
- **2024** — INABLE-Training (Borlaug, Mayo Clin Proc 2024; NCT02713126; N=73; 75% NYHA III; 63% rural): inorganic sodium nitrite 40 mg TID vs. placebo added to 12-week supervised exercise training; exercise improved VO₂ +0.79 mL/kg/min (P<0.001), KCCQ, 6MWD; nitrite added no benefit on any endpoint (ΔVO₂ −0.13; P=0.77) — fifth major NO/cGMP negative trial in HFpEF; confirms exercise benefit extends to high-acuity rural NYHA III population. (source: Borlaug2024INABLE)
- **2022** — HEART Camp HFpEF subgroup (Alonso, J Card Fail 2022; NCT01658670; N=59 HFpEF): 18-month behavioral coaching vs. enhanced usual care; adherence 42% vs. 14% at 12 months (P=0.025), 56% vs. 0% at 18 months (P<0.001); 6MWT +63 m vs. +13 m (P=0.048); all KCCQ domains improved significantly. HFrEF subgroup showed no benefit — identifies adherence as the dominant implementation gap for exercise benefit in HFpEF specifically. (source: Alonso2022HEARTcamp)
- **2025** — Ex-DHF (Nat Med 2025, n=322, Edelmann): largest 12-month combined endurance+resistance training RCT in HFpEF; primary Packer composite NOT MET (tau-b=−0.073; P=0.17); VO₂ +1.3 mL/kg/min (P=0.003); NYHA class improved OR 5.89 (P<0.001); adherence ~53% — demonstrates VO₂ and functional improvement despite missing the composite primary endpoint; adherence remains a critical trial design constraint (source: edelmann2025exdhf)

## Open Questions

- Does improving skeletal muscle function (via exercise training) translate to reduced HF hospitalisation and mortality, or only to functional improvement?
- What is the contribution of each mechanism in distinct HFpEF phenotypes (obese, hypertensive, amyloid)?
- Can chronotropic incompetence be pharmacologically corrected in HFpEF without harm (beta-blockers are neutral or potentially harmful in HFpEF)?
- Does the peripheral A-VO2 deficit persist after SGLT2i treatment, or does metabolic improvement from SGLT2i partially reverse skeletal muscle myopathy?
- Is IMT effective across HFpEF phenotypes, or specifically in those with disproportionate inspiratory muscle weakness (e.g., obese HFpEF with increased ventilatory load)?
- Does caloric restriction benefit extend to non-obese HFpEF (cachexia phenotype at risk)? Malnutrition correction trials (PICNIC, EFFORT) suggest harm from undernutrition but these are not EF-stratified.
- Is functional sympatholysis impaired in HFpEF during dynamic exercise, and if so, does it fully explain the excess sympathetic vasoconstriction? MSNA data come from single case reports; population-level evidence is absent.
- Does the static vs. dynamic exercise distinction in sympathetic response have therapeutic implications (e.g., resistance training as a phenotype-specific intervention for those with predominant dynamic sympathoexcitation)?
- Why did Ex-DHF miss its primary endpoint despite improving VO₂ and NYHA? Was the Packer composite insensitive to VO₂ and symptom gains, or was adherence (~53%) the limiting factor? Would a co-primary endpoint design (VO₂ + Packer composite) have reached significance? (source: edelmann2025exdhf)
- Does resistance training's fat-selective effect (Sharif 2024) specifically benefit the cardiometabolic/obesity HFpEF phenotype, and does it add to aerobic training when combined? The Ex-DHF combined protocol achieved +1.3 mL/kg/min — less than the ~+2.8 mL/kg/min meta-analysis average — possibly reflecting the combined training dilution or patient mix. (source: sharif2024locomotor, edelmann2025exdhf)
- Why did resistance training fail to prevent skeletal muscle mass loss in SECRET-II despite 20 weeks of supervised training? Is caloric restriction–induced catabolism dominant over anabolic RT stimulus at the doses used, or was protein intake inadequate? Does this finding apply to the pharmacological weight loss context (GLP-1RA trials SUMMIT, STEP-HFpEF) where weight loss rates are faster? (source: brubaker2023secret2)
- Why did the HEART Camp behavioral coaching intervention improve exercise adherence and outcomes specifically in HFpEF but not HFrEF patients? Is this related to the peripheral (skeletal muscle) vs. cardiac mechanism distinction — HFpEF patients have a larger exercise capacity reserve that can be unlocked by motivation, whereas HFrEF patients are cardiac-limited? (source: alonso2022heartcamp)
- Does the consistent NO/cGMP pathway failure across organic nitrate (NEAT-HFpEF), inorganic nitrate (INDIE-HFpEF), inorganic nitrite (INABLE-Training), PDE5i (RELAX), and sGC stimulators (SOCRATES, VITALITY) reflect fundamental biology — that skeletal muscle myopathy in HFpEF is not NO-mediated — or insufficient drug exposure during peak exercise states? (source: borlaug2024inable)

## Contradictions

- Nitrate trials (NEAT-HFpEF, INDIE-HFpEF) were designed on the assumption that vascular/NO-mediated impairment was a primary exercise limiter — both were neutral. Consistent with skeletal muscle being the dominant mechanism (vascular is secondary). See [[contradictions]].
