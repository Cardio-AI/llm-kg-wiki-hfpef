# HF Phenotype Classification

> Heart failure is classified into four EF-based phenotypes (HFrEF/HFmrEF/HFpEF/HFimpEF) with distinct pathophysiology and evidence bases; AHA 2022 added HFimpEF; all cut-offs are pragmatic, not mechanistic.

## Mechanism

Heart failure is classified into phenotypes based on LVEF, reflecting different pathophysiology, epidemiology, and evidence bases for treatment. This classification was introduced in the 2016 ESC Guidelines, refined in 2021 (HFmrEF renamed, SGLT2i added as HFrEF pillar), and extended in AHA 2022 with the addition of HFimpEF. (source: McDonagh2021ESC; source: Heidenreich2022AHA)

| Phenotype | LVEF | Core feature |
|---|---|---|
| [[hfref]] | ≤40% | Systolic dysfunction; four proven mortality-reducing pillars |
| [[hfmref]] | 41–49% | Mildly reduced; heterogeneous, weak evidence base (Class IIb, Level C) |
| [[hfpef]] | ≥50% | [[diastolic-dysfunction]]/elevated filling pressures; SGLT2i Class 2a (AHA 2022) |
| **HFimpEF** | **≥40% (prior ≤40%)** | **EF recovery; AHA 2022 addition; continue HFrEF therapy to prevent relapse** |

**HFimpEF** (AHA 2022): Patients with prior LVEF ≤40% who subsequently improve to ≥40%. Key distinction from HFpEF: these patients have a history of systolic HF and should **continue** HFrEF disease-modifying therapy (RAAS blockade, beta-blockers, SGLT2i) even after EF normalisation — discontinuation risks relapse. ESC 2021 has no equivalent named category but does not contradict this management approach. (source: Heidenreich2022AHA)

The rationale is historical and empirical, originating from landmark HFrEF trials. The threshold is **pragmatic, not mechanistic** — LVEF is a continuous variable; any cut-off is arbitrary. LVEF measurement by [[echocardiography]] has substantial inter-observer and inter-modality variability. (source: McDonagh2021ESC)

## Clinical Relevance

The three-way classification matters because therapies proven in [[hfref]] have largely failed in [[hfpef]] — see [[hfpef-treatment-gap]]. [[hfpef]] is the most heterogeneous category, and there is active scientific debate about whether it should be sub-classified by comorbidity phenotype, haemodynamic profile, or biomarker pattern to enable targeted therapy.

## History

- **2016** — ESC Guidelines introduced three-way EF classification; intermediate group named 'HF with mid-range EF' (HFmrEF)
- **2021** — ESC Guidelines renamed intermediate group to 'HF with mildly reduced EF' (HFmrEF); SGLT2i added as fourth HFrEF pillar; SGLT2i in HFpEF listed as knowledge gap (source: McDonagh2021ESC)
- **2022** — AHA/ACC/HFSA Guideline introduced **HFimpEF** as a fourth EF category; formalised A–D staging as primary organisational framework; added SGLT2i Class 2a for HFpEF (source: Heidenreich2022AHA)

## EF Phenotype Distribution Across Major Registries

The proportion of each EF phenotype varies substantially by clinical setting and geography (Savarese 2022):

| Registry | Setting | Region | HFrEF (≤40%) | HFmrEF (41–49%) | HFpEF (≥50%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ESC-HF-LT | Outpatient | Europe | 60% | 24% | 16% |
| Swedish HF Registry | National | Sweden | 56% | 21% | 23% |
| OPTIMIZE-HF | Hospitalised | USA | 49% | 17% | 24% |
| GWTG-HF | Hospitalised | USA | 39% | 14% | 47% |
| G-CHF | Mixed | Global (40 countries) | 54% | 21% | 24% |
| ASIAN-HF | Mixed | Asia | 81% | 6% | 13% |
| China-HF Registry | Hospitalised | China | 40% | 23% | 36% |

Key interpretation: outpatient registries show lower HFpEF fractions than inpatient registries; US inpatient registries show highest HFpEF fractions (~47%), reflecting the obese, comorbid US HFpEF phenotype and inclusive EF cut-offs. The wide range (16–47%) has direct implications for trial generalisability and is partly explained by [[hfpef-diagnostic-definitions]] heterogeneity. (source: Savarese2022GlobalBurden)

## Evidence

### Diagnostic Pathway (ESC 2021)

```
Suspected HF (symptoms/signs, risk factors, abnormal ECG)
     ↓
Measure [[natriuretic-peptides]]
     ├── BNP <35 pg/mL AND NT-proBNP <125 pg/mL → HF unlikely
     └── BNP ≥35 or NT-proBNP ≥125 pg/mL (or strong clinical suspicion)
          ↓
     [[echocardiography]] → assess for structural/functional abnormalities
          ├── Normal → HF unlikely (consider alternative diagnosis)
          └── Abnormal findings → HF confirmed
               ↓
          LVEF measurement
               ├── LVEF ≤40%   → [[hfref]]
               ├── LVEF 41–49% → [[hfmref]]
               └── LVEF ≥50%   → [[hfpef]]
                    ↓
          Determine aetiology and commence treatment
```

(source: McDonagh2021ESC)

### Key Caveats
- LVEF cut-offs are arbitrary; EACVI defines LV systolic dysfunction as <52% (male)/<54% (female), overlapping with the HFmrEF range
- Patients transition between categories over time, particularly HFmrEF
- A supra-normal LVEF (>65–70%) should prompt investigation for cardiac amyloidosis or hypertrophic cardiomyopathy
- **HFimpEF vs. HFpEF distinction** (AHA 2022): Prior LVEF ≤40% → recovered LVEF = HFimpEF, NOT HFpEF. Treatment differs: HFimpEF requires continued HFrEF therapy. (source: Heidenreich2022AHA)
- ESC 2021 uses "HF with improved LVEF" informally but without a formal fourth category; AHA 2022 formalised this as HFimpEF. (source: McDonagh2021ESC)

## Open Questions
- Will sex-specific or continuous EF thresholds replace the current pragmatic cut-offs?
- Should HFpEF be sub-classified by phenotype (obese/metabolic, hypertensive, amyloid) for targeted therapy?
- Is HFmrEF a genuinely distinct phenotype or always recovering HFrEF or declining HFpEF?

## Contradictions
- LVEF ≥50% cut-off for HFpEF is acknowledged as arbitrary; a future redefinition using sex-specific or continuous EF thresholds is plausible. (source: McDonagh2021ESC)
- **HFimpEF ambiguity across guidelines**: AHA 2022 defines HFimpEF as prior LVEF ≤40% now ≥40%; borderline patients (e.g., prior LVEF 38%, now 42%) overlap with HFmrEF in ESC 2021 — no equivalent ESC 2021 category. (source: Heidenreich2022AHA)
