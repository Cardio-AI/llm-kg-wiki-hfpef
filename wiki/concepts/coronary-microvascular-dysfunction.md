# Coronary Microvascular Dysfunction

> CMD is proposed as a key intermediate mechanistic link between systemic comorbidity-driven inflammation and the myocardial changes (fibrosis, stiffness, diastolic dysfunction) that define HFpEF — though whether it is cause or consequence remains debated.

## Mechanism

The coronary microcirculation (arterioles <500 µm) regulates myocardial perfusion during demand states. CMD is characterised by:
- **Impaired vasodilation:** reduced endothelium-dependent (NO/eNOS) and independent dilation
- **Increased vasoconstriction:** enhanced adrenergic/endothelin-mediated tone
- **Structural rarefaction:** reduced microvascular density; perivascular fibrosis

Functional manifestation: impaired coronary flow reserve (CFR), typically defined as CFR <2.0–2.5.

### Paulus–Tschöpe Paradigm (2013)

The dominant mechanistic model for CMD in HFpEF (source: raw/2013-JACC-Paulus-Tschoeppe-HFpEF_novel_paradigm.pdf):

> Comorbidities (hypertension, T2DM, obesity, COPD, CKD) → **systemic inflammation** (↑TNF-α, IL-6) → **coronary microvascular endothelial inflammation** → ↓NO bioavailability → ↓cyclic GMP → ↓PKG activity → **titin hypophosphorylation** (N2BA→N2B isoform shift) + **cardiomyocyte hypertrophy** + **interstitial fibrosis** → diastolic stiffness → HFpEF

This pathway positions CMD as the mechanistic interface between systemic metabolic disease and cardiomyocyte-level diastolic dysfunction.

### D'Amario Extension (2019)

D'Amario 2019 (Front. Physiol.) elaborated the "common soil" concept: CMD is not merely a downstream effector but an active driver shared across multiple HFpEF comorbidities. Additional mechanisms: endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EndoMT); calcium overload; OSA-associated oxidative stress (OSA prevalence in HFpEF: RR 2.2 for event prediction). (source: raw/2019-FrontPhysiol-DAmario-CMD_in_HFpEF.pdf)

## Clinical Significance

- CMD is prevalent in HFpEF (~40–75% in series using invasive coronary physiology testing)
- Predicts worse prognosis in HFpEF independent of obstructive CAD
- Provides mechanistic rationale for anti-inflammatory and cGMP-targeting therapies (which have largely failed — see [[hfpef-treatment-gap]])
- Statins (anti-inflammatory effect) and PDE-9 inhibitors identified as potential CMD-targeting agents in D'Amario 2019; no large RCT evidence in HFpEF

## History

Pre-2013: CMD studied primarily in the context of microvascular angina and non-obstructive CAD. Applied to HFpEF by the Paulus–Tschöpe paradigm paper (JACC 2013). D'Amario 2019 synthesised CMD evidence in HFpEF specifically. The paradigm remains influential but its therapeutic predictions have not been validated — trials targeting the NO/cGMP axis (RELAX, NEAT-HFpEF, INDIE-HFpEF, VITALITY-HFpEF) were all neutral in HFpEF. ## Open Questions

- Is CMD a cause or consequence of diastolic dysfunction in HFpEF?
- Does treating CMD improve HFpEF outcomes, or is it a biomarker of severity?
- Which diagnostic threshold (CFR, IMR, FFR) best identifies CMD relevant to HFpEF prognosis?

## Contradictions

The Paulus–Tschöpe paradigm predicts benefit from NO/cGMP pathway enhancement (↑PKG → ↓diastolic stiffness). Multiple RCTs targeting this exact pathway (RELAX, NEAT-HFpEF, INDIE-HFpEF, VITALITY-HFpEF, CAPACITY-HFpEF) were neutral. Either the CMD model is incomplete, the drugs tested did not reach the relevant target adequately, or the therapeutic window for this mechanism is narrow. See [[contradictions]].
