# Pericardial Restraint

## Aliases
| Alias | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Pericardial constraint | alternate-name | used interchangeably in haemodynamic literature |
| Pericardial compression | alternate-name | mechanical framing |
| Cardiac compression syndrome | research-name | older terminology in surgical literature |
| Paracardiac adipose compression | research-name | obesity-specific mechanism framing |

> The pericardium is a non-compliant sac; when total cardiac volume expands (exercise, obesity, fluid overload), the pericardial constraint raises filling pressure in both ventricles simultaneously via ventricular interdependence — in obesity-related HFpEF, excess paracardiac fat amplifies this effect and is directly reducible with GLP-1/GIP agonism (tirzepatide −43 mL, SUMMIT CMR).

---

## Definition and Mechanism

The normal pericardium is relatively stiff and has limited reserve volume. When the total cardiac volume (left heart + right heart) approaches pericardial capacity, further volume expansion causes a steep rise in filling pressures — the pericardial constraint converts right heart volume loading into left heart filling pressure increase via the shared ventricular septum (ventricular interdependence).

**Mechanism steps:**
1. Obesity → excess epicardial adipose tissue (within visceral pericardium) + pericardial fat (outside parietal pericardium, also called paracardiac fat)
2. Paracardiac fat physically fills pericardial space → reduces the residual volume available for cardiac chamber expansion
3. At rest: may not cause frank elevation of filling pressures in mild cases
4. Exercise or supine posture: RV fills more → cardiac volume expands → hits pericardial constraint → PCWP rises sharply (exaggerated exercise-induced filling pressure elevation)
5. Result: exercise intolerance out of proportion to resting haemodynamics — a hallmark of obesity-related HFpEF

**Ventricular interdependence component:** The interventricular septum is shared. When RV pressure rises due to pericardial constraint, the septum bows leftward → reduces LV diastolic volume → further impairs LV filling → compound effect on PCWP.

(source: [[borlaug2023statement]])

## Paracardiac Adipose Tissue Compartments

| Compartment | Location | Clinical relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Epicardial adipose tissue (EAT) | Within visceral pericardium, between myocardium and pericardium | Direct lipotoxicity, inflammatory cytokines |
| Pericardial adipose tissue (PAT) | External to parietal pericardium | Mechanical constraint; SUMMIT CMR primary reduction target |
| Paracardiac adipose tissue | EAT + PAT combined | Total measurement in SUMMIT CMR substudy |

SUMMIT CMR substudy (Kramer 2025; N=106): tirzepatide vs. placebo at 52 weeks:
- Paracardiac AT: −45 mL (95% CI −69 to −22; P<0.001)
- Pericardial AT: −43 g (P<0.001) — primary component of paracardiac reduction
- Epicardial AT: NS (−2 mL, P=0.57) — no significant change
- LV mass: −11 g (95% CI −19 to −4; P=0.004) — accompanies pericardial fat reduction
- LV mass correlated with body weight change (r=0.34, P<0.02)

This is the first RCT to demonstrate that pericardiac adipose tissue is pharmacologically modifiable in HFpEF. Semaglutide (STEP-HFpEF echocardiographic substudy) did NOT reduce LV mass — suggesting tirzepatide's additional GIP agonism may contribute to pericardial fat mobilisation. (source: [[kramer2025summit-cmr]])

## Relevance to Exercise Intolerance in HFpEF

Pericardial restraint provides a mechanistic explanation for the exaggerated exercise-induced filling pressure rise in obese HFpEF:

- At rest: PCWP may be normal or mildly elevated
- During exercise (CPET or upright bicycle): RV preload increase + peripheral vasodilatation → PCWP rises disproportionately
- The slope of PCWP/cardiac output (PCWP/CO slope) is elevated in obese HFpEF vs. non-obese HFpEF and vs. normal controls
- This distinguishes obesity-related HFpEF from "pure" arterial stiffening-mediated HFpEF where the rest-to-exercise filling pressure rise is mediated by reduced arterial compliance rather than volume constraints

(source: [[borlaug2023statement]])

## NT-proBNP Paradox and Pericardial Restraint

Petrie 2024 STEP-HFpEF pooled NT-proBNP analysis (N=1,145) revealed a paradox: patients with the **greatest** weight loss (≥20%) showed **less** NT-proBNP reduction with semaglutide than moderate weight losers. Proposed explanation: large weight loss → reduces pericardial fat → releases pericardial restraint → shifts filling pressure from pericardial-constrained mechanism to intrinsic myocardial stiffness mechanism → different NP secretion response. This paradox, if correct, implies that pericardial restraint is a major NP driver in obese HFpEF — its resolution by weight loss may reduce the haemodynamic stimulus for NP secretion in that subgroup. (source: [[petrie2024stephfpef]])

## Clinical and Therapeutic Implications

**Diagnostic:** Echo or CMR in obese HFpEF may underestimate filling pressures if resting assessment only. Exercise invasive haemodynamics (PCWP during upright bicycle CPET) can unmask pericardial restraint-mediated exercise elevation.

**Therapeutic targets:**
1. **Weight loss** (lifestyle, GLP-1RA, GIP/GLP-1 agonism): directly reduces paracardiac fat; SUMMIT CMR proves concept
2. **Pericardiotomy:** Surgical option under investigation in small pilot studies (Borlaug 2023 mentions animal model + small pilot data); no RCT. Removes pericardial constraint entirely.
3. **Diuresis:** Reduces stressed blood volume and cardiac chamber volumes → reduces pericardial constraint at rest and during mild exercise; does not target the fat compartment

## Contradictions

Tirzepatide reduced pericardial AT by 43 mL (SUMMIT CMR; P<0.001) while semaglutide did NOT reduce LV mass (STEP-HFpEF echocardiography). Whether this reflects the additional GIP agonism of tirzepatide, greater weight loss with tirzepatide, or measurement modality differences (CMR vs. echo) is unresolved. Both SUMMIT and STEP-HFpEF showed symptomatic benefit — so the pericardial fat reduction with tirzepatide is an added structural change, not the sole mechanism of benefit. See [[contradictions]].
