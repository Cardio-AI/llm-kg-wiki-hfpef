# Inspiratory Muscle Training

> IMT trains the respiratory muscles specifically, reducing the fraction of cardiac output diverted to the diaphragm and respiratory musculature during exertion and potentially redirecting perfusion to locomotor muscles; in HFpEF RCTs it produces a VO₂peak benefit comparable to standard moderate continuous training, deliverable at home without supervision.

## Mechanism

IMT trains respiratory muscles specifically, reducing the fraction of cardiac output diverted to the diaphragm and respiratory musculature during exertion; this potentially redirects perfusion to locomotor (skeletal) muscles. Unlike moderate continuous training (MCT), high-intensity interval training (HIIT), and combined resistance/aerobic training — which all work primarily through peripheral skeletal-muscle adaptation (A-VO₂ improvement) — IMT's proposed mechanism operates on respiratory-muscle oxygen demand and efficiency rather than on locomotor muscle training per se. (source: Bohmke2022Nonpharm)

## Clinical Relevance / Evidence

**Palau 2014** (crossover RCT, n=26): IMT at 25–30% of maximum inspiratory pressure (MIP) for 12 weeks produced a VO₂peak increase of +2.9 mL/kg/min (P=0.02). No significant changes were observed in echocardiographic parameters (LAVI, E/e'), consistent with a peripheral (respiratory muscle efficiency) rather than cardiac mechanism of benefit. (source: Bohmke2022Nonpharm)

**Kinugasa 2020** (at-home programme, n=17): 30% MIP training for 24 weeks improved anaerobic threshold and percentage-predicted MIP. The programme was delivered in an accessible, at-home format requiring no supervised facility. (source: Bohmke2022Nonpharm)

**Comparison to other modalities:** IMT's magnitude of VO₂ benefit (~+2.9 mL/kg/min) is comparable to standard MCT (Kitzman 2010: VO₂peak +2.7 mL/kg/min over 16 weeks); its standout feature relative to MCT, HIIT, and combined resistance/aerobic training is home delivery without specialist supervision, an accessibility advantage none of the other modalities offer in the reviewed evidence. (source: Bohmke2022Nonpharm)

## History

IMT is identified as a fourth distinct exercise-training modality for HFpEF (alongside MCT, HIIT, and combined resistance/aerobic training) in the Bohmke 2022 nonpharmacological strategies review, drawing on two RCTs: Palau 2014 (crossover, facility-based) and Kinugasa 2020 (at-home, longer duration). Both predate widespread SGLT2i uptake in HFpEF and represent an entirely nonpharmacological, exercise-based intervention arm. (source: Bohmke2022Nonpharm)

## Open Questions

- Is the VO₂peak benefit from IMT truly independent of cardiac mechanism, given the absence of echocardiographic change in Palau 2014 — or is the trial simply underpowered to detect cardiac remodelling?
- Does IMT provide additive benefit when combined with standard aerobic training (MCT/HIIT), analogous to the additive effect seen between caloric restriction and MCT in SECRET?
- Would a head-to-head IMT vs. MCT trial (neither of which has been directly compared in the reviewed evidence) confirm equivalence, given only indirect magnitude comparison is currently available?
- Does the at-home delivery format (Kinugasa 2020) achieve comparable adherence and long-term outcomes to facility-based programmes?

## Contradictions
None identified — IMT's VO₂peak magnitude (+2.9 mL/kg/min) is consistent with, not contradictory to, the Sachdev 2023 meta-analytic estimate for MCT (+2.8 mL/kg/min); the two modalities appear comparable rather than conflicting. See [[contradictions]].
