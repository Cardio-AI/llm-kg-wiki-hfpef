# Peripheral Mechanisms of Exercise Intolerance in HFpEF

> Impaired peripheral O₂ extraction — not reduced cardiac output alone — is the primary determinant of exercise intolerance in HFpEF; the A-VO₂ difference reserve is the strongest independent predictor of peak VO₂, implicating skeletal muscle dysfunction and impaired microvascular delivery as primary targets.

## Evidence: Haykowsky 2011 (N=48 HFpEF vs. 25 healthy controls)

### Key CPET Findings

| Parameter | HFpEF | Healthy Controls | P |
|----------|-------|-----------------|---|
| Peak VO₂ (ml/kg/min) | 14.3±0.5 | 20.4±0.6 | <0.0001 |
| Peak CO (L/min) | 6.3±0.2 | 7.6±0.2 | <0.0001 |
| Peak A-VO₂ Diff (ml/dl) | 17±0.4 | 19±0.4 | 0.0007 |

**Both** reduced peak CO **and** reduced peak A-VO₂ Diff contribute to lower peak VO₂ in HFpEF (source: [[haykowsky2011exercise]])

### Predictors of Peak VO₂ in HFpEF (Multivariable)

| Variable | Partial correlate | Standardized β | P |
|----------|------------------|----------------|---|
| **A-VO₂ Diff reserve** | **0.58** | **0.66** | **0.0002** |
| ΔHR (rest to peak) | 0.53 | 0.43 | 0.0007 |
| ΔSV (rest to peak) | 0.35 | 0.32 | 0.04 |

**A-VO₂ Diff reserve is the strongest independent predictor** — peripheral O₂ extraction capacity matters more than cardiac output augmentation for exercise capacity in HFpEF (source: [[haykowsky2011exercise]])

## Peripheral Mechanisms

### Skeletal Muscle Dysfunction
- HFpEF patients have reduced oxidative capacity in skeletal muscle (type I to type II fibre shift)
- Mitochondrial dysfunction, reduced capillary density → impaired O₂ utilisation
- Deconditioning amplifies all these changes — physical inactivity perpetuates peripheral dysfunction
- Muscle wasting and sarcopenia (common in elderly HFpEF) reduces absolute exercise capacity

### Impaired Microvascular O₂ Delivery
- Endothelial dysfunction (reduced NO) → impaired exercise-induced microvascular dilation
- Microvascular rarefaction in HFpEF skeletal muscle
- Iron deficiency (prevalent in 59% of HFpEF) → reduced myoglobin + oxidative phosphorylation capacity (source: [[beale2019iron]])
- Results in reduced O₂ extraction per unit blood flow → lower A-VO₂ Diff at peak exercise

### Chronotropic Incompetence
- ΔHR is the second strongest predictor of peak VO₂ in HFpEF (source: [[haykowsky2011exercise]])
- HFpEF patients have blunted HR response to exercise (chronotropic incompetence)
- Contributes to limited CO augmentation AND limits peripheral perfusion

## Frank-Starling Compensation: HFpEF vs. Healthy

HFpEF shows a different SV reserve mechanism during exercise:
- **HFpEF:** Relies on ↑ EDV (Frank-Starling mechanism; EDV reserve ↑ 10.3±2.0% vs. rest)
- **Healthy controls:** Relies on ↓ ESV (contractility reserve; ESV reserve mechanism)
- At submaximal workloads, CO is similar — but this compensation is **finite and inadequate at peak**
(source: [[haykowsky2011exercise]])

## Therapeutic Implications

- **Supervised exercise training:** Targets peripheral mechanisms directly — improves skeletal muscle oxidative capacity, increases capillary density, reduces sympathetic tone → improves A-VO₂ Diff reserve (source: [[sachdev2023exercise]])
- **Iron supplementation:** Addresses iron-deficiency component of peripheral dysfunction (source: [[beale2019iron]])
- **Cardiac rehabilitation:** Combined aerobic + resistance training improves peripheral O₂ extraction in HFpEF
