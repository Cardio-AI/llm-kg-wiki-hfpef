# Atrial Fibrillation-Dominant HFpEF Phenotype

> In AF-dominant HFpEF, AF is not simply a comorbidity — it is a mechanism: loss of atrial kick (which contributes 30–40% of LV filling in stiff ventricle) and uncontrolled rate directly worsen diastolic filling impairment; restoring sinus rhythm is mechanistically rational and supported by meta-analytic and RCT subgroup evidence.

## Definition and Epidemiology

**Clinical definition:** HFpEF (LVEF ≥50%) with documented AF (paroxysmal, persistent, or permanent) as dominant comorbidity contributing to symptoms and hospitalisations
**Prevalence:** 
- 15–30% persistent AF in HFpEF cohorts; up to 50% including paroxysmal AF (source: [[anker2023hfpefphenotype]])
- ~10% of HFpEF as primary "AF-dominant" cluster in Shah 2014 phenomapping (source: [[shah2015phenomapping]])
- AF incidence continues to rise in HFpEF patients due to shared LA remodelling substrate (source: [[anker2023hfpefphenotype]])

**Demographics:** Older patients; more men than obese phenotype; higher comorbidity burden (hypertension, CAD)

## Bidirectional AF-HFpEF Relationship

### HFpEF → AF
1. Elevated LV filling pressure → ↑ LA afterload → LA dilation and fibrosis
2. LA electrical heterogeneity → re-entrant arrhythmias → AF
3. LAVI >34 mL/m² is both a diagnostic criterion for HFpEF **and** the strongest predictor of AF onset (source: [[pieske2019hfapeff]])

### AF → HFpEF Worsening
1. **Loss of atrial kick:** In HFpEF, atrial contraction contributes 30–40% of LV filling (vs. 15–20% in normal ventricle); loss in AF → significantly reduced SV
2. **Uncontrolled ventricular rate:** Rapid rate → shorter diastolic filling time → ↑ PCWP → congestion → HF hospitalisation
3. **Irregular RR interval:** Even at controlled mean rate, beat-to-beat variability impairs diastolic filling in stiff ventricle

### CaMKII and Arrhythmia Pathway in Obese HFpEF
In obese HFpEF + OSA:
- Intermittent hypoxia → ROS → CaMKII oxidation → Ca²⁺ dysregulation → triggered activity → AF (source: [[wester2023sdb]])
- SDB treatment may reduce AF burden as complementary strategy (source: [[wester2023sdb]])

## Prognostic Impact of AF in HFpEF

- HF hospitalisation rate is significantly higher in HFpEF+AF vs. HFpEF alone
- AF episodes are a common precipitant of acute decompensation in HFpEF
- STEP-HFpEF baseline: 52.0% had AF — shows co-prevalence is near-universal in obese HFpEF population (source: [[kosiborod2023stephfpef]])

## Treatment Evidence

### Rhythm Control vs. Rate Control

**Meta-analysis (Al-Sadawi 2022; N=16,825 HFpEF+AF; 5 studies):**

| Comparison | OR | 95% CI | I² | P |
|-----------|-----|---------|-----|---|
| Rhythm vs. rate control (mortality/outcomes) | **0.735** | 0.665–0.813 | **0%** | <0.001 |

- 4 of 5 studies used catheter ablation as primary rhythm control strategy
- I²=0%: exceptionally consistent result across independent cohorts
(source: [[alsadawi2022rhythmcontrol]])

**EAST-AFNET4 HF Subgroup (Rillig 2021; N=798 HF patients, 56.3% HFpEF):**

| Outcome | HR (ERC vs. usual care) | 95% CI | P |
|---------|------------------------|---------|---|
| Primary composite (CV death/stroke/worsening HF/ACS) | **0.74** | 0.56–0.97 | 0.03 |
| Interaction by HF phenotype (HFpEF vs. HFmrEF vs. HFrEF) | — | — | P_interaction=0.63 |

- ERC included both antiarrhythmic drugs and catheter ablation (≈20% ablation rate in ERC arm)
- Benefit consistent regardless of HF type — supports phenotype-agnostic early rhythm control in AF+HF
(source: [[rillig2021eastafnet4]])

### Catheter Ablation (CABA-HFpEF)

**CABA-HFpEF (NCT05508256):** Phase III RCT; catheter ablation (PVI) vs. rate control specifically in HFpEF+AF; DZHK-sponsored; first dedicated HFpEF ablation trial; pending results (source: [[anker2023hfpefphenotype]])

**Mechanistic rationale for ablation in HFpEF:**
- Sinus rhythm restoration → recovery of atrial kick → ↑ LV filling → ↓ PCWP
- Rate regularisation → improved diastolic filling time
- LA reverse remodelling possible after ablation (LA size reduction)

### Rate Control Limitations in HFpEF
- Chronotropic incompetence is common in HFpEF (~30–50%); aggressive rate control may worsen it
- Beta-blockers for rate control: may harm HFpEF patients with chronotropic incompetence — withdrawal improved VO₂ in this subgroup (source: [[anker2023hfpefphenotype]])
- Ivabradine (EDIFY): no benefit in HFpEF; not suitable for rate control in AF
- Rate control provides symptom palliation but does not address atrial kick loss

### Anticoagulation
- All HFpEF+AF patients with CHA₂DS₂-VASc ≥2 (men) or ≥3 (women): anticoagulation indicated per AF guidelines
- DOACs preferred over warfarin; no HFpEF-specific data on anticoagulation outcomes

### Additional Phenotype-Guided Therapy (Anker 2023 Treatment Wheel)
- SGLT2i: universal; may reduce AF burden via volume unloading + atrial pressure reduction
- Dronedarone: antiarrhythmic option in AF+HFpEF; avoid in HFrEF but not contraindicated in HFpEF
- PVI (pulmonary vein isolation): catheter ablation as rhythm control strategy
(source: [[anker2023hfpefphenotype]])

## LA Remodelling and Reverse Remodelling

ARNi (sacubitril/valsartan) reversed LA remodelling in PARAMOUNT (−4.6 vs. +0.37 mL; P=0.003) — structural evidence supporting its use in AF-prone HFpEF phenotype (source: [[solomon2012paramount]])

## Biomarker Profile

| Biomarker | Pattern |
|-----------|---------|
| NT-proBNP | Elevated; LA origin in AF adds atrial contribution |
| LAVI | >34 mL/m² (both diagnostic and AF-risk marker) |
| BNP/NT-proBNP | Higher in AF vs. sinus rhythm HFpEF at same filling pressure |
| E/e' ratio | Elevated; reflects LA pressure transmission |

## Open Questions

- Does catheter ablation (PVI) improve hard outcomes (CV death, HHF) in HFpEF+AF? (CABA-HFpEF will answer)
- Should sinus rhythm be the mandatory goal, or is rate control sufficient in elderly/high-risk HFpEF+AF?
- Does ARNi-mediated LA reverse remodelling translate to reduced AF incidence in HFpEF?
- Is the rhythm control benefit driven by prevention of AF episodes or by improved rate control between episodes?
