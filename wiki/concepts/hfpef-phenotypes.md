# HFpEF Phenotypes

> HFpEF is not one disease but a syndrome of multiple phenotypes with distinct pathophysiology, risk profiles, and potentially different treatment responses. Phenotyping enables identification of patient subgroups with differential prognosis and precision-medicine-based therapeutic stratification.

## Aliases
| Alias | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| HFpEF heterogeneity | alternate-name | umbrella term for multiple distinct subtypes |
| HFpEF subphenotypes | alternate-name | more granular classification within phenotypes |
| Phenotype-directed therapy | mechanism | treatment selection based on phenotype identification |

## Core Concept

HFpEF is fundamentally **heterogeneous**; patients grouped by LVEF ≥50% alone mask critical differences in:
- **Primary driver:** obesity vs. hypertension vs. atrial fibrillation vs. rare etiology
- **Haemodynamic pattern:** isolated diastolic dysfunction vs. combined diastolic+systolic vs. pulmonary hypertension
- **Biomarker signature:** inflammatory vs. fibrotic vs. metabolic
- **Prognosis:** 5-year mortality ranges 10–50% depending on phenotype
- **Treatment responsiveness:** differential benefit from SGLT2i, ARNi, diuretics

## Phenotyping Frameworks

### Shah 2014: Phenomapping (N=413 Prospective Cohort)

[[shah2015phenomapping]] identified **4 clinical phenotypes** via unsupervised clustering:

| Phenotype | % | Key Features | Prognosis |
|-----------|---|--------------|-----------|
| **[[obese-metabolic-hfpef]]** | 30% | BMI >30, diabetes, metabolic syndrome | Intermediate mortality |
| **[[hypertensive-fibrotic-hfpef]]** | 50% | HTN dominant, LV wall thickness, diastolic dysfunction | Best outcomes |
| **[[pulmonary-hypertension-hfpef]]** | 10% | Elevated PVR, RV dysfunction, high NT-proBNP | Worst outcomes |
| **[[atrial-fibrillation-hfpef]]** | 10% | AF-dominant, rapid ventricular rate history | Intermediate/poor |

**Key finding:** Phenotype-based clustering outperformed EF-based classification in predicting 1-year mortality and hospitalization.

### Anker 2023: Biomarker + Clinical Phenotyping (MAYOR-HFpEF, N=300+)

[[anker2023hfpefphenotype]] used **multi-omics phenotyping** (NT-proBNP, hs-troponin, IL-6, CRP, biomarkers of fibrosis):

- **Inflammatory phenotype** (elevated IL-6, CRP, low lymphocytes): ~30% → worse prognosis, higher hospitalization risk
- **Fibrotic phenotype** (elevated PIIINP, PICP): associated with diastolic dysfunction severity, reduced 6MWT
- **Metabolic phenotype** (elevated glucose, markers of mitochondrial dysfunction): obesity-dominant
- **Rare disease phenotype** (ATTR-CM, infiltrative disease): <5% but requires disease-specific treatment

**Therapeutic implication:** Inflammatory phenotype may benefit from targeted anti-IL-6 therapy; fibrotic phenotype from anti-fibrotic agents.

## Key Phenotyping Dimensions

### 1. Primary Comorbidity Driver

| Driver | % HFpEF | Mechanism | Treatment Focus |
|--------|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Obesity** | 40–60% | Systemic inflammation, mitochondrial dysfunction | GLP-1 RA, lifestyle, SGLT2i |
| **Hypertension** | 50–70% | LV wall thickening, stiffness | Antihypertensives, SGLT2i |
| **Diabetes** | 30–50% | Metabolic dysfunction, fibrosis | SGLT2i, GLP-1 RA |
| **Atrial Fibrillation** | 30–40% | Rapid rate, loss of atrial kick | Rate control, anticoagulation |
| **Rare disease** (ATTR-CM, amyloidosis, restrictive CM) | <5% | Protein deposition, infiltration | Disease-specific (e.g., tafamidis for ATTR-CM) |

### 2. Haemodynamic Phenotype

**Pre-capillary vs. Combined PH:**
- **Isolated post-capillary PH** (IpcPH; elevated PCWP, normal PVR): ~70% → better prognosis, responds to diuretics
- **Combined pre/post-capillary PH** (CpcPH; elevated PCWP + PVR): ~20–30% → worse prognosis, requires pulmonary vasodilators
- **Out-of-proportion pulmonary hypertension:** associated with RV dysfunction, lower exercise capacity

### 3. Inflammatory vs. Fibrotic Phenotype

**Inflammatory** (elevated IL-6, TNF-α, CRP, NLR):
- Associated with obesity, metabolic syndrome
- Higher acute decompensation risk
- May respond to SGLT2i, IL-1β inhibition
- Reversible with weight loss, exercise

**Fibrotic** (elevated PIIINP, PICP, elevated myocardial extracellular volume on CMR):
- Associated with hypertension, aging
- Chronic course, less acute hospitalization
- Less responsive to diuretics alone
- May require anti-fibrotic agents (investigational)

### 4. Obesity vs. Non-Obesity Phenotype

**Obese HFpEF** (BMI >30):
- Higher prevalence of OSA (source: [[cowie2017sdb]])
- Greater metabolic dysfunction, systemic inflammation
- Younger age at HFpEF onset
- Weight loss interventions more impactful

**Non-obese HFpEF**:
- Predominant hypertension/aging driver
- Higher burden of atrial fibrillation
- Greater fibrosis on imaging
- Less metabolic improvement from lifestyle intervention

### 5. Rare Disease Phenotypes (Disease-Modifying Opportunities)

**ATTR-CM** ([[attr-cm]]): ~13–19% of contemporary HFpEF cohorts
- Tafamidis is disease-modifying (Class I, AHA 2022)
- Active diagnostic exclusion required

**Restrictive Cardiomyopathy:** <1%
- Infiltrative (amyloidosis, sarcoidosis, hemochromatosis) vs. idiopathic

**Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM):** ~5%
- Genetic, sarcomeric mutations
- Different prognosis, treatment algorithm (beta-blockers, disopyramide)

## Clinical Applications of Phenotyping

### Risk Stratification
- Pulmonary hypertension phenotype: 5-year mortality ~50% vs. hypertensive phenotype ~10%
- Inflammatory biomarkers (NLR, IL-6) identify high-risk patients within EF-defined HFpEF

### Treatment Selection
- **Obese phenotype:** GLP-1 RA, SGLT2i, cardiac rehabilitation
- **AF-dominant phenotype:** rate control, anticoagulation, antiarrhythmic therapy
- **Inflammatory phenotype:** SGLT2i (anti-inflammatory effect), IL-1β inhibition (investigational)
- **Rare disease:** disease-specific therapy (tafamidis for ATTR-CM)

### Prognostication
- Phenotype predicts hospitalization and mortality better than EF alone
- Serial biomarker changes identify worsening phenotype (e.g., escalating inflammatory signature)

## Evolution of Understanding

### Pre-2014: EF-Centric Era
HFpEF = one entity defined by LVEF ≥50%; treatment approached as HFrEF analog (ACE-I, ARB)

### 2014–2020: Phenotype Recognition
Shah and others demonstrated heterogeneity; multiple distinct subtypes with different pathophysiology

### 2020–Present: Precision Medicine Era
- SGLT2i showing benefit across phenotypes (but potentially differential magnitude)
- Subgroup analyses revealing phenotype-specific responders
- Expansion of biomarker phenotyping (multi-omics, imaging-based)
- Movement toward phenotype-directed therapy trials

## Open Questions

- Should HFpEF treatment algorithms be phenotype-based rather than EF/congestion-based?
- Do inflammatory vs. fibrotic phenotypes require fundamentally different therapeutic approaches?
- Can dynamic phenotyping (serial biomarkers, imaging) identify phenotype transitions requiring therapy changes?
- Which phenotypes will respond to emerging therapies (IL-1β inhibition, anti-fibrotic agents)?
- Are there HFpEF phenotypes with near-normal prognosis that require different management intensity?
