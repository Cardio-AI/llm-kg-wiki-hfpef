# Rhythm Control in AF + HFpEF

> Rhythm control, predominantly via catheter ablation, significantly reduces mortality and cardiovascular events in HFpEF patients with atrial fibrillation; the benefit is consistent across independent cohorts and RCT subgroup data, with zero heterogeneity in meta-analytic estimates.

## Aliases
| Alias | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sinus rhythm restoration | alternate-name | describes the physiological goal |
| Early rhythm control (ERC) | research-name | EAST-AFNET4 terminology |
| Rate control | alternate-name | the comparator strategy — targeting HR only |

## Evidence Summary

### Meta-Analysis (Al-Sadawi 2022)

N=16,825 HFpEF+AF across 5 independent studies:
- **Rhythm control vs. rate control (mortality + CV outcomes):** OR 0.735 (95% CI 0.665–0.813; P<0.001)
- **I²=0%** — exceptional consistency across all 5 studies
- 4 of 5 studies used catheter ablation as primary rhythm control strategy
- Net benefit: **~26.5% lower odds of adverse outcome with rhythm control** (source: [[alsadawi2022rhythmcontrol]])

### EAST-AFNET4 HF Substudy (Rillig 2021)

N=798 HF patients from EAST-AFNET4 (56.3% HFpEF):
- **Primary composite (CV death, stroke, worsening HF, ACS):** HR 0.74 (95% CI 0.56–0.97; P=0.03) favouring ERC
- **No interaction by HF subtype:** ERC benefit consistent across HFpEF, HFmrEF, HFrEF (P for interaction = 0.63)
- EAST-AFNET4 ERC arm used both pharmacological rhythm control AND catheter ablation (~20% ablation rate) (source: [[rillig2021eastafnet4]])

### Main EAST-AFNET4 Trial (All Patients)
- ERC reduced primary composite by 21% (HR 0.79; P=0.005) across N=2,789 patients

## Mechanism: Why Rhythm Control Matters in HFpEF

Atrial fibrillation is particularly harmful in HFpEF because:
1. **Loss of atrial kick:** In HFpEF, diastolic filling is dependent on the atrial contribution (up to 30–40% of LV filling); AF eliminates active atrial contraction → filling pressures rise disproportionately
2. **Rapid ventricular rate:** Tachycardia → reduced diastolic filling time → elevated LV filling pressures; rate control alone does not fully address this in HFpEF
3. **Atrial remodelling:** HFpEF-driven inflammation and fibrosis promote AF substrate (source: [[wester2023sdb]])
4. **Rhythm restoration:** Sinus rhythm → restored atrial contribution → reduced filling pressures + improved exercise tolerance

## Catheter Ablation vs. Pharmacological Rhythm Control

- Meta-analysis predominantly catheter ablation (4/5 studies); catheter ablation is the more durable rhythm control strategy
- EAST-AFNET4 combined both approaches; pharmacological rhythm control alone less effective in persistent AF
- **No HFpEF-specific ablation vs. medical rhythm control trial has been published** [knowledge gap — needs future ingest]

## Rate Control as Comparator

Rate control strategies (beta-blockers, digoxin, calcium channel blockers) reduce ventricular rate in AF but:
- Do not restore atrial contribution to LV filling
- Do not eliminate rapid rate-related diastolic impairment at exercise
- Associated with ~26.5% higher odds of adverse outcomes vs. rhythm control in HFpEF (source: [[alsadawi2022rhythmcontrol]])

## Open Questions

- What is the optimal rhythm control strategy in HFpEF+persistent AF? (catheter ablation vs. antiarrhythmic drugs)
- Does rhythm control reduce all-cause mortality in HFpEF specifically? (current data shows composite outcomes; mortality subgroup underpowered)
- Does treating SDB reduce AF recurrence post-ablation in obese HFpEF? (plausible via CaMKII pathway; no RCT data)
