# Acute Heart Failure in HFpEF

> Acute decompensated HFpEF shares clinical features with HFrEF decompensation but differs mechanistically; inflammatory markers (NLR, PLR) at admission and their trajectory during hospitalization provide independent prognostic information that clinical risk scores miss.

## Aliases
| Alias | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ADHF-HFpEF | abbreviation | acute decompensated HF with preserved EF |
| Acute decompensated HFpEF | alternate-name | standard clinical terminology |
| HF hospitalisation | related-term | the primary measured event in trials |

## Clinical Presentation

Acute HFpEF decompensation typically presents with:
- **Elevated filling pressures:** rapid onset dyspnea, orthopnea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea
- **Preserved LVEF** (≥50%) on echocardiography
- **Triggers:** excessive salt/fluid intake, new AF or rapid rate, HTN crisis, infection, medication non-adherence, NSAID use
- **Haemodynamic profile:** elevated PCWP with preserved cardiac output (distinguishing from cardiogenic shock)

## Inflammatory Markers in ADHF-HFpEF

### NLR as Prognostic Marker (Boralkar 2019, N=443 Stanford STRIDE)

| Marker | HR per unit | P | Added value |
|--------|------------|---|-------------|
| **Admission NLR** | 1.18 | 0.04 | Adds to GWTG-HF |
| **NLR trajectory** (admission → discharge) | 1.26 | 0.001 | Strongest predictor |
| NLR trajectory AUC (1-3 year) | Significantly improved | — | Reclassification positive |

- **NLR trajectory > baseline NLR** — rising or persistently elevated NLR during hospitalisation identifies highest-risk patients
- Serial measurement required; single admission value is informative but trajectory adds unique prognostic information
(source: [[boralkar2019nlr]])

### PLR and NLR in Stable HFpEF (Tamaki 2023, N=118)

- Both NLR and PLR (platelet-lymphocyte ratio) independently predict HF hospitalisation in stable outpatient HFpEF
- Immune imbalance pattern: neutrophil dominance + relative lymphopenia marks higher burden
(source: [[tamaki2023nlrplr]])

## Treatment in Acute HFpEF

- **IV diuretics:** First-line; goal is symptom relief and decongestion; careful titration required (HFpEF is sensitive to over-diuresis)
- **BP management:** Aggressive BP control; IV vasodilators (nitroglycerin) for hypertensive acute HFpEF
- **AF with rapid rate:** Rate or rhythm control depending on haemodynamic stability
- **Avoid:** Over-aggressive diuresis → volume depletion → renal failure; NP-targeted titration (potentially harmful, see [[horiuchi2022npguided]])
- **IV FCM:** Consider in iron-deficient ADHF-HFpEF for post-discharge benefit (source: [[vonhaehling2024fair]])

## Post-Discharge Risk

ADHF-HFpEF carries high 30-90 day rehospitalisation rates, similar to HFrEF. NLR trajectory during hospitalisation may identify patients requiring intensified post-discharge follow-up.
