# Noncardiac Dyspnea

> The diagnostic comparator category for exertional dyspnea with normal resting hemodynamics and no exercise-induced elevation in filling pressures; because standard noninvasive markers cannot reliably separate it from early/exercise-unmasked HFpEF, distinguishing the two requires either invasive exercise testing or purpose-built discriminative scores.

## Definition and Diagnostic Framework

Noncardiac dyspnea (NCD) is the label applied to patients presenting with exertional dyspnea who, on invasive hemodynamic testing, do **not** meet the exercise pulmonary capillary wedge pressure (PCWP) threshold for HFpEF (≥25 mmHg), despite sharing the same presenting symptom and normal resting hemodynamics as patients later classified as HFpEF. In the foundational Borlaug 2010 cohort (n=55, Mayo Clinic, exertional dyspnea + LVEF ≥50% + normal BNP + normal resting hemodynamics), 23/55 patients (42%) remained NCD after exercise testing, while the remaining 32/55 (58%) were reclassified as HFpEF by exercise PCWP ≥25 mmHg. (source: borlaug2010exercise)

**Both groups had normal resting hemodynamics** — meaning NCD cannot be distinguished from early, exercise-unmasked HFpEF at rest; only provocative (exercise) testing differentiates them. (source: borlaug2010exercise)

### Hemodynamic Comparison (Borlaug 2010, N=55)

| Parameter | NCD (n=23) | HFpEF (n=32) | P |
|---|---|---|---|
| Age, y | 47±17 | 65±13 | <0.001 |
| BMI, kg/m² | 27.3±5.5 | 32.0±5.9 | 0.004 |
| Resting mean PAP, mmHg | 15±4 | 19±4 | 0.001 |
| Resting PCWP, mmHg | 9±3 | 11±2 | 0.002 |
| Exercise PCWP, mmHg | 13±5 | 32±6 | <0.00001 |
| Exercise mean PAP, mmHg | 23±5 | 43±7 | <0.00001 |
| Exercise CI, L/min/m² | 6.7±1.4 | 4.9±1.0 | 0.0001 |

The NCD group was younger, leaner, and preserved cardiac output augmentation on exercise (higher exercise cardiac index), whereas the HFpEF group showed blunted cardiac output reserve alongside markedly higher exercise filling pressures. Passive leg elevation before exercise produced an immediate PCWP rise of only +2±3 mmHg in NCD versus +7±3 mmHg in HFpEF (P<0.0001), consistent with preserved diastolic (Frank-Starling) reserve in NCD versus its loss in HFpEF. (source: borlaug2010exercise)

## Diagnostic Performance: Why NCD Is Hard to Exclude Noninvasively

All standard noninvasive markers — BNP, E/e' ratio, and the 2016 ESC consensus algorithm — had **AUC <0.70** for distinguishing HFpEF from NCD in this cohort of patients with normal resting hemodynamics, none adequate for reliable discrimination. By contrast, **exercise PASP ≥45 mmHg** identified HFpEF with 96% sensitivity and 95% specificity (AUC 0.99), because exercise PCWP correlated strongly with exercise PASP (R²=0.76, P<0.0001). (source: borlaug2010exercise)

This diagnostic gap motivated the development of scoring systems designed explicitly to separate HFpEF from NCD without requiring invasive testing in most patients:

- **H₂FPEF score** (Reddy 2018): derived in 414 consecutive patients referred for invasive hemodynamic exercise testing (267 HFpEF, 147 NCD; HFpEF prevalence 64% in this referral population) and validated in a further 100 patients (61 HFpEF, 39 controls; AUC 0.886). A score of 0–1 indicates "HFpEF unlikely; consider noncardiac dyspnoea," while 5–9 indicates HFpEF is likely. NT-proBNP did not add incremental diagnostic value beyond the six H₂FPEF variables (BMI, antihypertensive drug count, atrial fibrillation, PASP, age, E/e'). (source: Reddy2018H2FPEF)
- **LA strain vs. E/e':** the Borlaug 2023 JACC Scientific Statement identifies left atrial (LA) strain as superior to E/e' specifically for distinguishing HFpEF from noncardiac dyspnea, consistent with LA function being described elsewhere in that statement as the most robust imaging marker for this discrimination. (source: borlaug2023statement)

## Clinical Relevance

Because a majority of patients referred for invasive testing with exertional dyspnea, normal BNP, and normal resting hemodynamics turn out to have exercise-unmasked HFpEF rather than pure NCD (58% in the Borlaug 2010 cohort), a diagnosis of "noncardiac dyspnea" based on resting evaluation alone is unreliable — it can only be made with confidence after exercise testing (invasive PCWP/PASP, or a validated score such as H₂FPEF) has excluded exercise-induced filling pressure elevation. (source: borlaug2010exercise; source: Reddy2018H2FPEF)

## Open Questions

- What is the true population prevalence of pure noncardiac dyspnea versus exercise-unmasked HFpEF among patients presenting with exertional dyspnea and normal resting BNP?
- Can LA strain alone reliably replace invasive exercise hemodynamics for distinguishing HFpEF from NCD in routine (non-referral-centre) practice?
- Does the H₂FPEF score, derived in a high-HFpEF-prevalence referral cohort (64%), retain its discriminative accuracy in general/community populations with a much lower HFpEF prevalence?

## Contradictions

Standard noninvasive diagnostic markers (BNP, E/e' ratio, ESC consensus algorithm) all had AUC <0.70 for distinguishing HFpEF from noncardiac dyspnea in patients with normal resting hemodynamics — yet these are the markers most commonly relied upon in routine outpatient HFpEF diagnosis. This confirms that resting noninvasive evaluation is inadequate for early or exercise-unmasked disease. See [[contradictions]].
