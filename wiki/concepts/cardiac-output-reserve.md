# Cardiac Output Reserve in HFpEF

> Cardiac output reserve in HFpEF is reduced but not absent at submaximal workloads; CO augmentation is achieved abnormally via EDV (preload) reserve rather than ESV (contractility) reserve; HR augmentation is limited by chronotropic incompetence; the cardiac reserve is insufficient to meet peak demands but coexists with peripheral impairments as a dual mechanism.

## Frank-Starling vs. Contractility: Mechanistic Difference

**HFpEF exercise adaptation (abnormal):**
- EDV ↑ 10.3±2.0% from rest to peak exercise (Frank-Starling mechanism)
- Relies on ↑ filling to augment SV
- Finite and inadequate at high workloads — overfilling elevates PCWP without commensurate SV increase

**Healthy adults:**
- ESV ↓ during exercise (contractility reserve — increased sympathetic activation → ↑ ejection)
- Does not require elevated filling pressures to maintain SV
- More efficient at high workloads

**Consequence:** At submaximal workloads, CO is similar between HFpEF and healthy controls; the divergence appears only at peak — when Frank-Starling compensation fails (source: [[haykowsky2011exercise]])

## Chronotropic Incompetence

- HR augmentation is the second strongest predictor of peak VO₂ in HFpEF (partial correlate 0.53)
- HFpEF patients show blunted HR response to exercise relative to age-predicted maxima
- Mechanism: autonomic dysfunction, beta-blocker use, diastolic dysfunction → limited diastolic filling time at high HR → paradoxically worsened filling pressures at fast rates
- Chronotropic incompetence contributes to limited CO reserve AND limits peripheral perfusion

## Integration with Peripheral Mechanisms

CO reserve impairment alone does not fully explain exercise intolerance in HFpEF:
- A-VO₂ Diff reserve (peripheral O₂ extraction) is a **stronger predictor** of peak VO₂ than CO reserve
- Both are independently impaired
- Peripheral dysfunction amplifies the impact of reduced CO reserve: even the reduced peak CO cannot be fully utilized due to impaired peripheral O₂ extraction
- See [[peripheral-mechanisms-hfpef]] for peripheral contribution

## In Obese HFpEF: Additional Ventilatory Constraint

In obese HFpEF (BMI >30), exercise capacity is also ventilatory-limited (source: [[babb2026ventilatorylimit]]):
- Patients stop at a ventilatory ceiling before cardiac reserve is fully exhausted
- Reducing PCWP (cardiac unloading) does not improve exercise capacity → ventilatory ceiling dominates
- Three mechanisms coexist: cardiac CO reserve, peripheral extraction, and ventilatory ceiling
