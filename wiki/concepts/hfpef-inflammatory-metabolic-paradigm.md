# HFpEF Inflammatory-Metabolic Paradigm

> The inflammatory-metabolic model frames HFpEF as a consequence of systemic comorbidity burden — where inflammation and metabolic dysfunction converge on the myocardium — rather than as a purely cardiac disease; this explains why obesity, diabetes, and hypertension are not merely comorbidities but mechanistic drivers.

## Historical Context

**Paulus and Tschöpe 2013** ([[paulus2013novelparadigm]]) proposed a paradigm shift:
- Prior model: HFpEF = isolated diastolic dysfunction; treat like HFrEF with heart-centred therapy
- **New model:** Comorbidities → systemic inflammation → coronary microvascular endothelial inflammation → NO deficiency → reduced cGMP-PKG → ↑ titin stiffness → diastolic dysfunction

The inflammatory-metabolic paradigm extends this: obesity, diabetes, and metabolic syndrome generate **both** the inflammatory signal **and** metabolic dysfunction (mitochondrial impairment, lipotoxicity) simultaneously.

## Quantitative Evidence: Systemic Inflammation and Outcomes

Meta-analysis (Fu 2024, 8 cohort studies; N=9,744 HFpEF):

| Outcome | HR | I² | P |
|---------|----|----|---|
| **All-cause mortality** | **1.43** (1.19–1.72) | **0%** | <0.05 |
| **CV mortality** | **2.04** (1.33–3.12) | 0% | <0.05 |
| **CV rehospitalisation** | **2.83** (0.92–8.67) | 0% | <0.05 |

- Multiple markers (NLR, PLR, hs-CRP, WBC) converge on same prognostic signal — supports systemic low-grade inflammation as a core driver (source: [[fu2024inflammation]])
- I²=0% across all outcomes: exceptional consistency across independent cohorts

## Inflammatory vs. HFrEF Inflammation

HFpEF inflammation is **fundamentally different** from HFrEF inflammation:
- **HFrEF:** Inflammation is primarily myocardial-injury-driven (post-infarction, viral myocarditis)
- **HFpEF:** Inflammation is systemic, metabolic comorbidity-driven; feeds back to myocardium through microvascular and endocrine pathways
(source: [[fu2024inflammation]])

## Anker 2023: Phenotype-Level Inflammatory Characterisation

Multi-omics phenotyping ([[anker2023hfpefphenotype]]) identified:
- **Inflammatory phenotype** (~30% HFpEF): elevated IL-6, CRP, low lymphocytes
  - Worse prognosis, higher hospitalisation risk
  - May respond to targeted anti-IL-6 or anti-IL-1β therapy
- **Fibrotic phenotype:** elevated PIIINP, PICP; associated with diastolic dysfunction severity
- **Metabolic phenotype:** obesity-dominant, mitochondrial dysfunction biomarkers

## Metabolic Component

Beyond inflammation, metabolic dysfunction drives HFpEF through:
- **Lipotoxicity:** Excess fatty acids → mitochondrial ROS overproduction → cardiomyocyte dysfunction
- **Insulin resistance:** RAAS activation + ↓ NO signalling → vascular stiffness + LV hypertrophy
- **Adipokine imbalance:** EAT → proinflammatory (↑ leptin, ↓ adiponectin)
- **Mitochondrial dysfunction:** ATP supply-demand mismatch → impaired relaxation

## Therapeutic Implications

Inflammation-metabolic targeting in HFpEF:
- **SGLT2 inhibitors:** Reduce systemic inflammation, ROS, mitochondrial dysfunction, EAT
- **GLP-1 RA (semaglutide):** Weight loss → metabolic correction + inflammation reduction
- **IL-1β inhibition (anakinra, canakinumab):** DHART2 pilot: anakinra reduced CRP and improved function; insufficient power; larger trial needed [knowledge gap]
- **Statins:** Non-ischaemic HFpEF benefit may reflect anti-inflammatory + EAT-reducing pleiotropic effects

## Open Questions

- Which specific inflammatory pathway is most therapeutically tractable in HFpEF?
- Should anti-inflammatory therapy be phenotype-selected (inflammatory endotype) rather than applied broadly?
- Does IL-1β inhibition improve hard outcomes in HFpEF? (DHART2 pilot was underpowered)
