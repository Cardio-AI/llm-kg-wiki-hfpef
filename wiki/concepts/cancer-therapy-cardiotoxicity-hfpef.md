# Cancer Therapy-Induced Cardiotoxicity and HFpEF

> Chemotherapeutic and radiotherapy agents used to treat cancer independently cause cardiotoxicity across a spectrum from asymptomatic LV dysfunction to overt heart failure, with thoracic radiotherapy in particular producing a HFpEF-predominant pattern; the population that develops this phenotype has no dedicated HFpEF RCT evidence base because major trials excluded cancer patients.

## Mechanism

Treatment with chemotherapeutic and radiotherapy agents has improved long-term cancer survival but can cause several cardiovascular diseases, HF in particular. Anthracyclines can lead to ventricular dysfunction at any dose, and the likelihood of toxicity is higher in the presence of cardiovascular risk factors or additional cancer-directed therapies. Toxicity due to anthracyclines is acute in ~1% of the treated population and is generally reversible, whereas early-onset and late-onset *chronic* toxicity — observed in up to 2.1% and 5% of cases respectively — is more likely to be irreversible. Long-term cardiovascular mortality in anthracycline-induced cardiomyopathy is similar to other non-ischaemic dilated cardiomyopathies. (source: Savarese2022GlobalBurden)

**Agent-specific incidence:**

| Agent/modality | Effect | Incidence |
|---|---|---|
| Anthracyclines (acute) | Ventricular dysfunction | ~1% |
| Anthracyclines (early-onset chronic) | Ventricular dysfunction, more likely irreversible | ~2.1% |
| Anthracyclines (late-onset chronic) | Ventricular dysfunction, more likely irreversible | ~5% |
| Taxanes (combined with anthracyclines) | HF | up to 1.6% (lower than anthracyclines alone) |
| Trastuzumab (anti-HER2) | HF | up to 4.1%; higher combined with anthracyclines |
| Trastuzumab (anti-HER2) | LV dysfunction (any grade) | up to 18.6%; higher combined with anthracyclines |
| Sunitinib (VEGF inhibitor) | Reversible LV dysfunction | ~15% |
| Bevacizumab (VEGF inhibitor) | HF risk | 5-fold increase |
| Thoracic radiotherapy (general) | Congestive HF | up to 10% |
| Thoracic radiotherapy for breast cancer | **HFpEF specifically** | **64% of affected women** |

(source: Savarese2022GlobalBurden)

The Anker 2023 HFA/ESC phenotype profiling statement adds mechanistic detail specific to anthracycline-associated diastolic dysfunction: doxorubicin produces diastolic dysfunction in 60% of treated patients at 1 year, rising to 80% at 3 years — indicating a progressive, time-dependent diastolic injury pattern rather than a fixed early insult. (source: Anker2023HFpEFPhenotype)

## Clinical Relevance

**A HFpEF-predominant radiotherapy signature:** The disproportionate development of HFpEF specifically (64% of women) following thoracic radiotherapy for breast cancer — compared with the broader ~10% congestive HF rate across radiotherapy generally — suggests radiation-induced cardiotoxicity preferentially produces a preserved-EF phenotype, distinguishing it mechanistically from anthracycline- or trastuzumab-associated cardiotoxicity (which more often produce systolic/mixed dysfunction). (source: Savarese2022GlobalBurden)

**Treatment gap:** All major HFpEF RCTs, including the SGLT2 inhibitor trials, excluded cancer patients. Consequently there is no evidence base for SGLT2i — or by extension any other guideline-directed HFpEF therapy — in this subgroup. General HFpEF management principles are applied with caution, extrapolated rather than evidence-based. (source: Anker2023HFpEFPhenotype)

## History

Cancer therapy-induced cardiotoxicity has been recognised as a distinct HF aetiology within global epidemiological reviews of HF (Savarese 2022), which catalogue agent-specific incidence across anthracyclines, taxanes, HER2-targeted agents, VEGF-pathway inhibitors, and radiotherapy. The HFA/ESC 2023 phenotype profiling statement subsequently flagged this population specifically within the HFpEF phenotyping framework, noting both the progressive nature of doxorubicin-associated diastolic dysfunction (60% at 1 year → 80% at 3 years) and the complete absence of RCT evidence for standard HFpEF therapies in cancer survivors. (source: Savarese2022GlobalBurden; source: Anker2023HFpEFPhenotype)

## Open Questions

- Why does thoracic radiotherapy preferentially produce a HFpEF (vs. HFrEF) phenotype relative to anthracyclines/trastuzumab? — mechanism not detailed in wiki sources.
- Is SGLT2i efficacy and safety preserved in cancer-therapy-induced HFpEF, given the drug class's proven benefit in non-oncologic HFpEF? — no trial data exists.
- Does earlier detection of subclinical diastolic dysfunction (e.g. at the 1-year post-doxorubicin mark, when 60% already show dysfunction) allow for effective preventive intervention before progression to the 80% 3-year rate?
- Should cancer-therapy HFpEF be treated as a distinct phenotype requiring dedicated prospective trials, analogous to the obesity- or amyloid-HFpEF phenotypes?
