# Contradictions

## Active Conflicts

### 1. LVEF ≥50% Cut-off for HFpEF
- **ESC 2021**: Uses LVEF ≥50% to define HFpEF (source: [[mcdonagh2021esc]])
- **EACVI Position**: Defines LV systolic dysfunction as LVEF <52% (male) / <54% (female), creating a zone where patients meet the ESC HFpEF LVEF criterion but also have measurable systolic dysfunction
- **Implication**: Patients with LVEF 50–54% (female) or 50–51% (male) occupy a definitional grey zone; classification depends on which guideline/definition is applied

### 2. TOPCAT: Regional Heterogeneity
- **Overall trial**: Spironolactone neutral vs. placebo in HFpEF (LVEF ≥45%) (source: [[mcdonagh2021esc]])
- **Americas subgroup**: Spironolactone significantly reduced primary composite endpoint (source: [[mcdonagh2021esc]])
- **Explanation**: Suspected enrollment of patients without true HFpEF in non-Americas (Russia/Georgia) cohort, evidenced by very low urinary spironolactone metabolite levels in non-Americas control group
- **Implication**: True treatment effect of spironolactone in HFpEF remains uncertain; Americas data is frequently cited as suggestive of benefit but is not guideline-endorsed

### 3. Sacubitril/Valsartan in HFpEF: FDA vs. ESC
- **PARAGON-HF primary endpoint**: Missed overall (source: [[mcdonagh2021esc]])
- **PARAGON-HF subgroup + meta-analysis**: Significant reduction in CV death and HF hospitalization in patients with LVEF below the median (~57%) (source: [[mcdonagh2021esc]])
- **FDA**: Endorsed sacubitril/valsartan for patients with chronic HF and LVEF "less than normal" (source: [[mcdonagh2021esc]])
- **ESC 2021**: Noted FDA decision but made no equivalent European recommendation for HFpEF (source: [[mcdonagh2021esc]])
- **Implication**: Different regulatory evidentiary thresholds; whether HFpEF patients with LVEF 45–57% have proven benefit depends on jurisdiction

### 4. NP Sensitivity in HFpEF Obesity
- **General NP rule**: BNP <35 pg/mL or NT-proBNP <125 pg/mL (SR) makes HF unlikely (NPV 0.94–0.98) (source: [[mcdonagh2021esc]])
- **HFpEF + obesity**: Up to 20% of invasively proven HFpEF patients have NPs below these thresholds; obesity reduces NP production/increases degradation (source: [[mcdonagh2021esc]])
- **Implication**: Normal NPs do not exclude HFpEF in obese patients — clinical suspicion must remain elevated

### 5. HFmrEF Treatment: Uncertain Evidence Base
- All pharmacological recommendations for [[hfmref]] are Class IIb, Level C — extrapolated from subgroup analyses of HFrEF and HFpEF trials (source: [[mcdonagh2021esc]])
- No dedicated prospective RCT in HFmrEF exists
- **Implication**: HFmrEF treatment is consensus-based extrapolation, not direct evidence

### 6. HFpEF Diagnostic Definition Heterogeneity
- Seven competing guideline definitions (ACC/AHA, ESC, HFSA, TOPCAT, PARAGON, I-PRESERVE, RELAX) enroll 12–90% of the same patient cohort (source: [[ho2019hfpefdefinitions]])
- Reference standard (invasive CPET): 53% of a referred HFpEF-suspect cohort had HFpEF_phys; HFpEF_phys independently predicts CV events regardless of whether the patient meets any guideline definition
- **Implication:** "HFpEF" trial populations are not interchangeable. CV event rates range 75–298 per 1000 person-years in the same cohort depending on definition applied. Discordant trial results may reflect non-comparable populations rather than absent treatment effect.

### 7. Exercise Training: Functional Benefit Without Hard Outcome Proof
- [[supervised-exercise-training|SET]] improves peak VO2 by +2.8 mL/kg/min across 8 RCTs (n=503) — the most consistent functional improvement in HFpEF (source: [[sachdev2023exercise]])
- Hard outcomes (mortality, HF hospitalization) have not been assessed; individual trials underpowered
- Prior HFpEF history (NEAT-HFpEF, nitrate trials) demonstrates that functional improvement in exercise capacity metrics does not predict hard outcome benefit
- **Implication:** Positive VO2 effect does not establish that SET reduces mortality or hospitalisation; the most clinically important question remains unanswered

### 8. Exercise Intolerance: Cardiac vs. Peripheral Primary Mechanism
- Traditional model: [[diastolic-dysfunction]] (elevated filling pressures) as the primary exercise limiter in HFpEF
- Invasive CPET evidence (Sachdev 2023): peripheral skeletal muscle A-VO2 difference accounts for >50% of VO2 reduction; cardiac filling pressure elevation is secondary (source: [[sachdev2023exercise]])
- **Implication:** Therapies targeting filling pressures alone (diuretics, nitrates, RAAS) are insufficient to fully restore exercise capacity; skeletal muscle is the dominant therapeutic target

### 9. SGLT2i for HFpEF: ESC 2021 (No Recommendation) vs. AHA 2022 (Class 2a)
- **ESC 2021**: No recommendation for SGLT2i in HFpEF — EMPEROR-Preserved was published after guideline cutoff (source: [[mcdonagh2021esc]])
- **AHA 2022**: Class 2a, B-R for SGLT2i (empagliflozin or dapagliflozin) in HFpEF to decrease HF hospitalization and CV death (source: [[heidenreich2022aha]])
- **Evidence basis is identical**: Both guidelines are reacting to EMPEROR-Preserved (Aug 2021) and DELIVER (2022); ESC 2021 pre-dated both; AHA 2022 incorporated both
- **Implication**: The discordance is explained entirely by publication timing, not a genuine scientific disagreement. Anticipated to be resolved in the next ESC guideline update. See [[guideline-comparison]].

### 10. Serum Magnesium–SGLT2i Interaction: Opposite Direction in HFrEF vs. HFpEF
- **HFrEF (prior reports)**: Lower baseline serum Mg predicts greater SGLT2i benefit
- **EMPEROR-Preserved (HFpEF/HFmrEF, Ferreira 2026)**: Higher baseline serum Mg associated with greater empagliflozin benefit; lower Mg → less benefit (source: [[ferreira2026emperor]])
- **Same drug (empagliflozin), opposite direction** of Mg-treatment interaction across HF phenotypes
- **Implication**: Either distinct Mg-related biology in HFrEF vs. HFpEF (mechanistically plausible given different underlying pathophysiology) or a statistical artefact from secondary analysis. No causal mechanism established. See [[ferreira2026emperor]].

### 11. HFpEF Operational Definition: ESC 2021 (Table 9) vs. AHA 2022 (Filling Pressures)
- **ESC 2021**: HFpEF requires LVEF ≥50% + signs/symptoms + ≥1 Table 9 structural/functional marker (elevated NPs, LA enlargement, LV hypertrophy, E/e' with low septal e') (source: [[mcdonagh2021esc]])
- **AHA 2022**: HFpEF requires LVEF ≥50% + signs/symptoms + **evidence of elevated LV filling pressures** (directly or by echocardiographic surrogate) (source: [[heidenreich2022aha]])
- **Practical gap**: A patient with elevated E/e' (meeting AHA filling pressure criterion) but normal NPs and no LA enlargement may not meet ESC Table 9; conversely, a patient with borderline NP elevation and LA enlargement might meet ESC Table 9 without clear echocardiographic filling pressure evidence
- **Implication**: The two guidelines may enroll partially different patients in studies using each definition — consistent with the finding that seven HFpEF definitions enroll 12–90% of the same cohort (see [[hfpef-diagnostic-definitions]])
