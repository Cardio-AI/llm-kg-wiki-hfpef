# Semaglutide (HFpEF)

> GLP-1 receptor agonist evaluated in obesity-related HFpEF through the STEP-HFpEF program (non-diabetic and diabetic companion trials); improves symptoms, exercise capacity, weight, and inflammatory markers, with NT-proBNP reduction independent of weight loss suggesting a direct HF disease-modifying mechanism — but not powered for hard outcomes and CRP-independent of benefit.

## Description

Semaglutide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist administered subcutaneously once weekly, dose-escalated to a target of 2.4 mg. It is the intervention studied in the STEP-HFpEF program: **STEP-HFpEF** (NCT04788511, N=529, HFpEF without T2DM) and its companion **STEP-HFpEF DM** (NCT04916470, N=616, HFpEF with T2DM). Both are Phase 3, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in obese HFpEF (BMI ≥30 kg/m², LVEF ≥45%), 52 weeks duration, with dual primary endpoints of KCCQ-CSS change and body weight % change. (source: Kosiborod2023STEPHFPEF; Kosiborod2024STEPHFPEFDM)

Semaglutide is distinguished within the wiki from **tirzepatide** (dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist, Eli Lilly), the intervention in [[summit]] (see redirect page [[tirzepatide-hfpef]]). Semaglutide is GLP-1-selective; tirzepatide is dual-incretin.

## Role in HFpEF

STEP-HFpEF and STEP-HFpEF DM together established GLP-1RA as an option for the obesity-metabolic HFpEF phenotype, independent of T2DM status. Semaglutide improved the dual primary endpoints in both trials:

- **STEP-HFpEF (non-DM):** KCCQ-CSS +7.8 pts (95% CI 4.8–10.9; P<0.001); body weight −10.7 percentage points (95% CI −11.9 to −9.4; P<0.001) (source: Kosiborod2023STEPHFPEF)
- **STEP-HFpEF DM:** KCCQ-CSS +7.3 pts (95% CI 4.1–10.4; P<0.001); body weight −6.4 percentage points (95% CI −7.6 to −5.2; P<0.001) — a smaller weight-loss effect than the non-DM trial, but consistent symptomatic benefit (source: Kosiborod2024STEPHFPEFDM)

Both confirmatory secondary endpoints (6MWD, CRP, hierarchical composite win ratio) were also positive in both trials. (source: Kosiborod2023STEPHFPEF; Kosiborod2024STEPHFPEFDM)

Neither trial was powered for CV mortality or HF hospitalization — a recurrent limitation in HFpEF pharmacotherapy trials (cf. NEAT-HFpEF, CAPACITY-HFpEF). Exploratory HF event signals were favorable in both trials (STEP-HFpEF: 1 vs. 12 events, HR 0.08; STEP-HFpEF DM: HR 0.40, 95% CI 0.15–0.92) but underpowered and not confirmatory. (source: Kosiborod2023STEPHFPEF; Kosiborod2024STEPHFPEFDM)

The lean muscle mass concern applies to semaglutide as to other GLP-1RAs: weight loss includes lean mass reduction, raising a sarcopenic-obesity concern in older HFpEF patients that neither trial's primary analysis directly addressed. (source: Kosiborod2023STEPHFPEF)

Mechanistically, semaglutide's benefit in HFpEF has been probed in two directions — neurohormonal (NT-proBNP) and inflammatory (CRP/hsCRP) — both of which argue for a mechanism beyond simple weight loss:

- **NT-proBNP** ([[petrie2024stephfpef]]): reduced 22% vs. 4.9% with placebo at 52 weeks (ETR 0.82, 95% CI 0.74–0.91; P=0.0002), independent of the magnitude of weight loss (P interaction=0.58). Patients with the greatest weight loss (≥15–20%) showed *less* NT-proBNP reduction — proposed to reflect weight loss reducing epicardial fat/pericardial restraint and releasing myocardial stretch, partially offsetting the direct HF effect. Patients with higher baseline NT-proBNP derived greater symptomatic (KCCQ-CSS, win ratio) benefit. (source: petrie2024stephfpef)
- **Inflammation** ([[verma2024inflammation]]): CRP reduced 36% (treatment ratio 0.64, 95% CI 0.56–0.72; P<0.0001), consistent regardless of baseline CRP category and independent of weight loss magnitude (P interaction=0.91). Critically, clinical benefit (KCCQ-CSS, weight, 6MWD, win ratio) was **not modified by baseline CRP** — patients with and without elevated inflammation benefited equally, arguing against CRP/inflammation suppression as the primary mechanistic pathway and challenging the Paulus-Tschöpe comorbidity-inflammation paradigm as the operative mechanism for this drug. (source: verma2024inflammation)

Sleep-disordered breathing is proposed as a mechanistic link relevant to semaglutide's target population: SDB affects 58–80% of HFpEF patients and is most prevalent in the obese/metabolic HFpEF phenotype (40–60%); GLP-1RA-driven weight loss is expected to reduce OSA severity in this phenotype, though dedicated HFpEF+OSA GLP-1RA trials are still warranted. (source: Wester2023SDB)

A mechanistically distinct comparator, HU6 (a controlled mitochondrial uncoupler; HuMAIN-HFpEF trial), produced fat-specific weight loss (−2.86 kg vs. placebo) with preserved lean mass but no significant improvement in peak VO₂, 6MWD, KCCQ, or NT-proBNP over 19 weeks — raising the question of whether functional benefit in STEP-HFpEF/STEP-HFpEF DM depends on mechanisms beyond weight loss itself (haemodynamic offloading, direct GLP-1 receptor effects) rather than weight loss magnitude or composition. (source: pandey2025humain)

## Evidence

| KCCQ-CSS | +16.6 | +8.7 | +7.8 (4.8–10.9) | <0.001 |
| Body weight | −13.3% | −2.6% | −10.7 pp (−11.9 to −9.4) | <0.001 |
| 6MWD | +21.5m | +1.2m | +20.3m (8.6–32.1) | <0.001 |
| CRP ratio | — | — | 0.61 (0.51–0.72) | <0.001 |
| Win ratio | — | — | 1.72 (1.37–2.15) | <0.001 |
(source: Kosiborod2023STEPHFPEF)

|---|---|---|---|---|
| KCCQ-CSS | +13.7 | +6.4 | +7.3 (4.1–10.4) | <0.001 |
| Body weight | −9.8% | −3.4% | −6.4 pp (−7.6 to −5.2) | <0.001 |
| 6MWD | +12.7m | −1.6m | +14.3m (3.7–24.9) | 0.008 |
| CRP ratio | — | — | 0.67 (0.55–0.80) | <0.001 |
| Win ratio | — | — | 1.58 (1.29–1.94) | <0.001 |
| NT-proBNP ratio | — | — | 0.8 (0.7–0.9) | exploratory |
(source: Kosiborod2024STEPHFPEFDM)

**Safety:** Fewer serious adverse events with semaglutide vs. placebo in both trials (STEP-HFpEF: 13.3% vs. 26.7%; STEP-HFpEF DM: 17.7% vs. 28.8%, P=0.002). GI adverse events were the main cause of semaglutide discontinuation. (source: Kosiborod2023STEPHFPEF; Kosiborod2024STEPHFPEFDM)

**Pooled NT-proBNP/inflammation secondary analyses (N=1,145 pooled):** see Role in HFpEF above. (source: petrie2024stephfpef; verma2024inflammation)

## Status

**Published:** STEP-HFpEF (NEJM September 2023); STEP-HFpEF DM (NEJM April 2024). No guideline recommendation for GLP-1RA in HFpEF as of the most recent systematic guideline review — all 7 major HFpEF guidelines (AHA/ACC/HFSA, ESC, NICE, CCS/CHFS, SHA, NHFA/CSANZ, JCS/JHFS) were reviewed and none contained a GLP-1RA recommendation; explicitly flagged as an evidence gap alongside combined SGLT2/1 inhibition, non-steroidal MRAs, and population screening. (source: mahmood2024guidelines)

Both STEP-HFpEF trials had low background SGLT2i use (3.6% and ~33%, respectively) relative to contemporary practice, leaving the additive-vs-redundant benefit question with SGLT2i background therapy unresolved. Outcome-powered evidence for the GLP-1RA class in HFpEF comes from the related tirzepatide trial [[summit]] (dual GIP/GLP-1RA), not from semaglutide directly. (source: Kosiborod2023STEPHFPEF)

## Contradictions
- NT-proBNP reduction is independent of weight loss magnitude (petrie2024stephfpef), yet CRP reduction is also independent of weight loss magnitude (verma2024inflammation) — two distinct "direct effect" claims from the same drug and population that have not been reconciled into a single unifying mechanism.
- CRP-independence of clinical benefit (verma2024inflammation) sits in tension with the broader Paulus-Tschöpe comorbidity-inflammation paradigm invoked elsewhere in the wiki to explain HFpEF pathophysiology and treatment rationale (see [[coronary-microvascular-dysfunction]]).
- Symptomatic/functional benefit (KCCQ, 6MWD, win ratio) without powered hard-outcome data parallels the general HFpEF pattern of surrogate/functional positivity without confirmed mortality/hospitalization benefit — see [[contradictions]].
