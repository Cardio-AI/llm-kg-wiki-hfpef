# Hypertension in HFpEF

> Hypertension is present in 60–80% of HFpEF patients and is the most prevalent comorbidity in the syndrome — historically the dominant conceptual driver, but mechanistically insufficient alone; aggressive BP control is recommended but RAAS blockade has not improved HFpEF outcomes in any completed RCT.

## Prevalence and Clinical Significance

Arterial hypertension is present in **60–80%** of HFpEF patients — more prevalent than in HFrEF (56%) or HFmrEF (64%) (Swedish Heart Failure registry). This makes it the most common cardiovascular comorbidity in the HFpEF population. (source: [[anker2023hfpefphenotype]])

Hypertension is associated with increased mortality in HFpEF, but the relationship is non-linear: SBP-mortality follows a U-shape — low SBP (<120 mmHg) is associated with worse outcomes (OPTIMIZE-HF registry), likely reflecting reverse causation or advanced disease with "decapitated hypertension" (BP decline over time in end-stage HFpEF). (source: [[anker2023hfpefphenotype]])

## Historical Framing: Hypertensive Heart Disease → HFpEF

The conceptual evolution of HFpEF is partly a story of hypertension's changing role: (source: [[borlaug2023statement]])

1. **Pre-2000:** HFpEF (then "diastolic HF") viewed as hypertensive heart disease — prolonged hypertension → LV hypertrophy → impaired relaxation → diastolic dysfunction → HFpEF
2. **2000–2013:** Epidemiological data showed HFpEF patients are more obese and metabolically burdened than hypertension alone predicts; the LV wall stress is paradoxically LOW in HFpEF despite high SBP (small, thick-walled LV → favorable Laplace geometry)
3. **2013:** Paulus-Tschöpe paradigm shifted the framing: HTN is a predisposing comorbidity but NOT sufficient; the primary mediator is systemic inflammation from the full comorbidity burden (obesity, T2DM, HTN, CKD) driving coronary microvascular endothelial dysfunction
4. **Post-2015 to present:** HTN persists as the most common comorbidity but is one of ≥5 overlapping pathophysiological phenotypes; the "arterial stiffening" phenogroup (Borlaug 2023) is the 5-phenotype model's proxy for the hypertension-dominant HFpEF subtype

Evidence that HTN alone cannot explain HFpEF: ALL-HAT (40,000 patients) found BMI (not SBP/DBP) discriminated HFrEF vs. HFpEF development; MONICA registry showed LA enlargement related to obesity (not HTN). (source: [[paulus2013novelparadigm]])

## Pathophysiology

**Direct cardiac effects of hypertension:**
- Sustained pressure overload → LV concentric hypertrophy → ↑LV mass/volume ratio → impaired active relaxation (↓lusitropy) and ↑passive myocardial stiffness
- LV hypertrophy increases the risk of subendocardial ischemia → CMD
- LA remodelling: pressure-volume overload → LA dilatation and fibrosis → ↑risk of AF and LA myopathy
- Arterial stiffening: aortic stiffness ↑ with HTN → augmented reflected wave → late systolic LV pressure spike → impairs ventricular-vascular coupling and diastolic filling

**Indirect effects (comorbidity cascade):**
- HTN activates RAAS → aldosterone → myocardial fibrosis (collagen deposition → interstitial fibrosis → ↑LV stiffness)
- Endothelial dysfunction from pressure/shear stress overload → ↓NO bioavailability in coronary microvasculature (the Paulus-Tschöpe pathway)
- HTN is also a major risk factor for CKD and T2DM — both independent HFpEF drivers

(source: [[paulus2013novelparadigm]], [[borlaug2023statement]])

## RAAS Trials: Consistent Failure

Despite the hypertension-dominant HFpEF paradigm motivating RAAS trials, no ACE inhibitor, ARB, or ARNI has shown significant outcome benefit in the full HFpEF population:

| Trial | Drug | LVEF | N | Primary result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PEP-CHF (2006) | Perindopril (ACEi) | >40% | 850 | HR 0.92, P=0.55 — neutral |
| CHARM-Preserved (2003) | Candesartan (ARB) | >40% | 3,023 | HR 0.89, P=0.118 — neutral |
| I-PRESERVE (2008) | Irbesartan (ARB) | ≥45% | 4,128 | HR 0.95, P=0.35 — neutral |
| PARAGON-HF (2019) | Sacubitril/valsartan (ARNI) | ≥45% | 4,822 | RR 0.87, P=0.06 — missed |

The consistent failure of RAAS blockade to improve outcomes despite effective BP lowering suggests that the antihypertensive mechanism itself is insufficient in HFpEF — BP-independent mechanisms (fibrosis, microvascular inflammation, metabolic dysregulation) dominate. An important exception: PARAGON-HF showed benefit in women and patients with LVEF below the median, suggesting EF-stratum heterogeneity. (source: [[pfeffer2019hfpef]], [[charm-preserved]], [[i-preserve]], [[paragon-hf]])

## Treatment

### Blood Pressure Targets
ACC/AHA/HFSA guidelines recommend SBP <130 mmHg in HFpEF with hypertension. This target is supported by SPRINT subgroup analyses. SBP <120 mmHg may be harmful — lower threshold is the safety boundary (OPTIMIZE-HF registry). (source: [[anker2023hfpefphenotype]])

### Phenotype-Specific Add-on Therapy (Anker 2023 Treatment Wheel)
For the hypertension phenotype in HFpEF, on top of universal SGLT2i:
- **ACEi/ARB/ARNI:** Antihypertensive and RAAS suppression; not outcome-proven in HFpEF but useful for BP control
- **Indapamide (thiazide-like diuretic):** HYVET study showed 64% reduction in HF occurrence in elderly hypertensives treated with indapamide — strongest diuretic evidence for HF prevention in hypertension
- **Nebivolol (beta-blocker, NO-releasing):** Beta-blocker with direct vascular NO-releasing effect; may be preferable to conventional beta-blockers in HFpEF given chronotropic incompetence concerns
- **Calcium channel blockers:** Effective for BP without negative chronotropy; compatible with HFpEF
- **MRA (spironolactone/finerenone):** Aldosterone suppression reduces myocardial fibrosis; TOPCAT (spironolactone) trend positive in Americas subgroup; FINEARTS-HF positive for finerenone (HR ~0.84, P=0.007)

(source: [[anker2023hfpefphenotype]], [[finearts-hf]])

### Beta-Blockers: Caution in HFpEF
Unlike in HFrEF, beta-blockers have no proven outcome benefit in HFpEF and may worsen exercise tolerance via chronotropic incompetence (30–50% of HFpEF patients). Beta-blocker withdrawal improved VO₂ in HFpEF patients with chronotropic incompetence in one study (Anker 2023). Use only when there is a specific indication (AF rate control, angina, post-MI). (source: [[anker2023hfpefphenotype]])

## Contradictions

**RAAS failure vs. finerenone success:** ACEi, ARB, and ARNI all neutral in HFpEF — yet finerenone (non-steroidal MRA, FINEARTS-HF) was positive. This is not simply "RAAS failure" — the aldosterone/mineralocorticoid pathway appears separable from the angiotensin pathway in HFpEF. Mechanisms may include finerenone's specific anti-fibrotic and anti-inflammatory profile beyond pure RAAS blockade. See [[contradictions]].

**BP paradox:** Lower BP (after treatment) is associated with improved HFpEF outcomes in some cohorts — yet very low SBP (<120 mmHg) is associated with worse outcomes. The J-curve likely reflects a mix of effective treatment (lower BP = better controlled HTN = better HFpEF) and reverse causation (advanced HFpEF causes BP decline). See [[contradictions]].
