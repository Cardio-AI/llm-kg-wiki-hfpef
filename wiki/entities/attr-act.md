# ATTR-ACT

> Tafamidis significantly reduced all-cause mortality (risk ratio 0.70) and CV hospitalizations in ATTR cardiomyopathy — established the first disease-modifying therapy for a major HFpEF subphenotype.

## Description

**Full title:** Transthyretin Amyloid Cardiomyopathy Clinical Trial  
**NCT:** NCT01994889  
**Intervention:** Tafamidis (20 mg or 80 mg oral) vs. placebo  
**Population:** ATTR cardiomyopathy (wild-type or variant), NYHA I–III, LVEF >30%  
**N:** 441 (261 tafamidis, 177 placebo, combined 20+80 mg vs. placebo)  
**Duration:** 30 months  
**Primary endpoint:** Hierarchical composite win ratio (all-cause mortality + CV hospitalizations)  
**Published:** NEJM 2018 (Maurer MS, Schwartz JH, Gundapaneni B, et al.)

Transthyretin (TTR) amyloidosis is caused by misfolding of TTR tetramers; misfold products accumulate in myocardium, causing restrictive cardiomyopathy. Tafamidis is a small molecule that stabilises the TTR tetramer, preventing dissociation into monomers and subsequent amyloid fibril formation. It does not remove existing deposits but halts disease progression.

Two forms: wild-type ATTR (wtATTR, age-related) and variant ATTR (vATTR, autosomal dominant point mutation in TTR gene). Both respond to tafamidis; wtATTR is the more prevalent form in older HFpEF patients.

## Role in HFpEF

ATTR-CM (wild-type) is present in 13–19% of HFpEF patients in contemporary series. (source: [[pfeffer2019hfpef]]) It is a critical HFpEF mimic/subphenotype: patients present with preserved EF, exercise intolerance, and elevated filling pressures — indistinguishable from standard HFpEF without specific testing (bone scintigraphy, serum/urine electrophoresis, genetic testing).

Key clinical implication: active exclusion of ATTR-CM in HFpEF patients is required, because:
1. Tafamidis is disease-modifying (not just symptom-relieving)
2. Standard HFpEF drugs are not contraindicated in ATTR-CM but RAAS inhibitors and beta-blockers may not be tolerated in advanced disease
3. Without diagnosis, the 13–19% of HFpEF with ATTR-CM miss the only proven disease-modifying treatment available for any form of HFpEF

The 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA guideline (Table 1 of Kittleson 2023 ACC ECDP) lists ATTR-CM as an HFpEF mimic requiring specific workup. (source: [[kittleson2023acc]])

## Evidence

Primary composite win ratio significantly favoured tafamidis over placebo. All-cause mortality: risk ratio 0.70 (95% CI 0.51–0.96) — 30% relative reduction. CV hospitalizations also reduced. Tafamidis 80 mg showed greater benefit than 20 mg across subgroups; regulatory approval was for 80 mg (or 61 mg in the equivalent meglumine salt). [Source: Maurer2018ATTRACT — pending full ingest for exact CI and p-values.]

Disease progression (KCCQ, 6MWT) also attenuated in tafamidis vs. placebo.

## Status

**Published:** NEJM 2018. FDA approved tafamidis (Vyndaqel/Vyndamax) for ATTR-CM in 2019.  
**AHA 2022:** Class I, Level B-R for tafamidis in ATTR-CM with HF symptoms. (source: [[heidenreich2022aha]])  
**ESC 2021/2023:** Not included in general HF guidelines (ATTR-CM treated as a separate cardiomyopathy entity by ESC cardiomyopathy guideline 2023).
