## Description

**Full title:** Ferric Carboxymaltose Assessment of Morbidity and Mortality in Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction  
**NCT:** NCT03074591  
**Intervention:** IV ferric carboxymaltose vs. placebo  
**Population:** HFpEF with iron deficiency  
**Primary endpoint:** Change in 6-minute walk test at 52 weeks  
**Status:** Ongoing (referenced in Anker 2023 phenotype statement)

Iron deficiency affects 50–75% of HFpEF patients — predominantly functional iron deficiency (serum ferritin <100 μg/L or ferritin 100–299 + transferrin saturation <20%), rather than absolute iron deficiency alone. Iron is essential for mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation in skeletal muscle and for haemoglobin synthesis. In HFpEF, iron deficiency compounds the exercise limitation from skeletal muscle myopathy and reduced cardiac output reserve. (source: [[sachdev2023exercise]])

Background: In HFrEF, IV ferric carboxymaltose (CONFIRM-HF, AFFIRM-AHF) consistently improved 6MWT, KCCQ, and HF hospitalizations. The question in HFpEF is whether the mechanism — predominantly peripheral skeletal muscle, not systolic dysfunction — makes iron repletion equally or more beneficial.

Companion trial: PREFER-HF (NCT03833336) also tests IV iron in HFpEF.

## Role in HFpEF

Iron deficiency is explicitly listed as one of the 18 comorbidity phenotypes in the Anker 2023 HFA phenotype profiling wheel (Figure 1). The treatment wheel (Figure 2) positions IV ferric carboxymaltose as the phenotype-specific add-on for the iron deficiency branch. (source: [[anker2023hfpefphenotype]])

If FAIR-HFpEF is positive, IV iron would represent one of the most actionable phenotype-guided HFpEF interventions — screened cheaply with serum ferritin/transferrin saturation and treatable with a single infusion. AHA 2022 (Kittleson 2023 ECDP) already notes iron deficiency assessment as part of the HFpEF workup. (source: [[kittleson2023acc]])
