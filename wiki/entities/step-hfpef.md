# STEP-HFpEF

> Semaglutide improved HF symptoms (KCCQ-CSS +7.8 pts, P<0.001) and exercise function (6MWD +21.5m, P<0.001) with significant weight loss in obese HFpEF — the first major GLP-1RA trial in HFpEF, but hard outcomes not powered.

## Description

**Full title:** Semaglutide Treatment Effect in People with Obesity and HFpEF  
**NCT:** NCT04788511  
**Intervention:** Semaglutide 2.4 mg subcutaneous once weekly vs. placebo  
**Population:** HFpEF (LVEF ≥45%), obesity (BMI ≥30 kg/m²), NYHA II–IV, without T2DM (HbA1c <6.5%)  
**N:** 529 (263 sema, 266 placebo) · 96 sites, 13 countries  
**Duration:** 52 weeks + 5-week follow-up  
**Dual primary endpoints:** KCCQ-CSS change + body weight % change at 52 weeks  
**Published:** NEJM 2023;389(12):1069–1084. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa2306963

Semaglutide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist (GLP-1RA) originally approved for T2DM (Ozempic) and obesity (Wegovy at 2.4 mg). Mechanistic rationale in HFpEF: GLP-1RA reduces weight (primarily via appetite suppression and gastric emptying), reduces visceral and epicardial adipose tissue, decreases systemic inflammation (↓CRP, IL-6), and may have direct cardiorenal effects. In HFpEF, obesity drives elevated filling pressures through pericardial restraint, increased plasma volume, and pro-inflammatory adipokine secretion. (source: [[anker2023hfpefphenotype]])

STEP-HFpEF was conducted in patients without T2DM, distinguishing it from STEP-HFpEF DM (NEJM 2024, semaglutide in HFpEF+T2DM) and SUMMIT (NCT04847557, **tirzepatide** [dual GLP-1/GIP, Eli Lilly], published NEJM 2025, co-primary KCCQ-CSS + CV death/worsening HF events).

## Role in HFpEF

STEP-HFpEF establishes symptomatic benefit of GLP-1RA in obese HFpEF. The 2023 ACC ECDP (Kittleson 2023) flagged the GLP-1RA evidence gap and noted STEP-HFpEF as an anticipated result; the published data supports adding GLP-1RA (semaglutide) as an option for obese HFpEF phenotype management. (source: [[kittleson2023acc]])

Critical caveat: the trial was not powered for CV mortality or HF hospitalization. Symptomatic and functional improvement does not automatically translate to outcomes benefit — a recurrent theme in HFpEF (cf. NEAT-HFpEF, CAPACITY-HFpEF). SUMMIT (NCT04847557, **tirzepatide** [dual GLP-1/GIP; Eli Lilly], published NEJM 2025) used co-primary endpoints of KCCQ-CSS + CV death/worsening HF events; HR 0.62 (0.41–0.95; P=0.026) — first event reduction with GLP-1RA class in HFpEF. See [[summit]].

The lean muscle mass concern: semaglutide reduces lean mass as well as fat mass. In HFpEF patients with sarcopenic obesity, GLP-1RA-induced muscle loss may worsen exercise capacity despite weight loss and symptom improvement. This concern applies to semaglutide and was noted by Kittleson 2023 in the GLP-1RA context.

## Evidence

**Dual primary endpoints (both P<0.001):**
- KCCQ-CSS: diff +7.8 pts (95% CI 4.8–10.9) — sema +16.6 vs. placebo +8.7
- Body weight: diff −10.7 pp (95% CI −11.9 to −9.4) — sema −13.3% vs. placebo −2.6%

**Confirmatory secondary (all P<0.001):**
- 6MWD: diff +20.3m (95% CI 8.6–32.1)
- CRP ratio: 0.61 (95% CI 0.51–0.72)
- Win ratio (hierarchical composite): 1.72 (95% CI 1.37–2.15)

**Exploratory:** HF events 1 sema vs. 12 placebo; HR 0.08 (0.00–0.42) — not powered.

Safety: SAEs 13.3% vs. 26.7%; cardiac disorders 7 (2.7%) vs. 30 (11.3%). (source: raw/2023-NEJM-Kosiborod-STEP_HF_study.pdf)

## Status

**Published:** NEJM September 2023. No formal guideline update yet for GLP-1RA in HFpEF (publication post-dates ESC 2023 Focused Update and AHA 2022). The 2023 ACC ECDP (Kittleson 2023) flagged GLP-1RA as an emerging therapy for obese HFpEF phenotype. SUMMIT (NCT04847557) will provide further outcome data. (source: [[kittleson2023acc]])

## Contradictions

Symptomatic benefit (KCCQ +7.8, 6MWD +21.5m) without hard outcome evidence parallels the CAPACITY-HFpEF pattern (negative KCCQ) and inverts it: symptomatic improvement without mortality data leaves the clinical significance ambiguous. The lean mass loss concern creates a biological tension between weight loss as mechanism vs. sarcopenia as risk. See [[contradictions]].
