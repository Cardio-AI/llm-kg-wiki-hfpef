# REDUCE LAP-HF II

> Interatrial shunt device failed to improve outcomes in the overall HFpEF/HFmrEF population — but a pulmonary vascular resistance subgroup analysis suggested benefit in lower PVR patients, illustrating HFpEF phenotypic heterogeneity.

## Description

**Full title:** Reduce Elevated Left Atrial Pressure in Patients With Heart Failure — Phase II  
**NCT:** NCT03088033  
**Intervention:** Implantable interatrial shunt device (Corvia Atrial Shunt, IASD System II — creates a controlled left-to-right atrial shunt to decompress elevated LA pressure) vs. sham  
**Population:** HFpEF/HFmrEF with elevated LA pressure, LVEF >40%; 621 randomised (modified ITT), 626 total randomised  
**Status:** Published — primary results (Shah et al., *Lancet* 2022;399:1130–40 — **not yet ingested into this wiki**); echocardiographic substudy (source: [[patel2024reducelaphf]]); 3-year clinical follow-up in the responder subgroup and 5-year RCT-I follow-up (source: [[litwin2024reducelaphf]]); post hoc HF-duration analysis (source: [[doi2026reducelaphf]])

The mechanistic hypothesis: HFpEF is characterised by chronically elevated left atrial pressure → pulmonary venous hypertension → dyspnoea and exercise intolerance. An interatrial shunt creates a controlled opening between the atria, allowing excess LA pressure to decompress into the right atrium → right ventricle → pulmonary circulation → reduced LA pressure.

The device targets the haemodynamic consequence of diastolic stiffness rather than the underlying myocardial mechanism — a pressure relief valve rather than a disease-modifying approach.

**Note on result (updated 2026-07-15):** The trial's primary endpoint — a hierarchical composite of CV death, nonfatal ischaemic stroke, total (first + recurrent) HF events, and change in KCCQ-OSS — was **neutral in the overall (unselected) randomised population** at 1 year and remained neutral through 3 years (3-year win ratio 1.04, 95% CI 0.83–1.30). This qualitative "neutral overall" result is independently confirmed and re-stated in two later analyses: Litwin et al. 2024 state explicitly "there was no difference in the primary outcome... between shunt and sham in the overall trial" (source: [[litwin2024reducelaphf]]); Doi et al. 2026 state atrial shunt implantation "had no significant effect on the primary composite outcome of cardiovascular death or ischemic stroke, HF events, and health status" (source: [[doi2026reducelaphf]]). Both attribute the original numeric primary-endpoint result to Shah et al., *Lancet* 2022 — **that primary results paper itself has not yet been ingested into this wiki**, so the exact overall-population effect size/CI/P-value for the primary composite endpoint remains [needs source — Shah SJ et al., Lancet 2022;399:1130–40, primary REDUCE LAP-HF II results paper, not yet ingested].

A pre-specified, post hoc-refined "responder" subgroup (peak exercise pulmonary vascular resistance [PVR] ≤1.74 Wood units **and** absence of a cardiac rhythm management [CRM] device at baseline; 50% of randomised patients, n=313) showed a durable, statistically significant benefit through 3 years: win ratio 1.6 (95% CI 1.2–2.2, P=.006); 44% relative reduction in HF events (9 vs. 16 per 100 patient-years; IRR 0.587, 95% CI 0.401–0.859, P=.005; NNT 13 at 3 years); and KCCQ-OSS improvement of +17.9 vs. +7.6 points (between-group difference +10.1, 95% CI 4.3–15.8, P<.001) (source: [[litwin2024reducelaphf]]). Nonresponders derived no clinical benefit and had numerically more HF events with shunt than sham. This suggests the shunt decompresses effectively in patients without established pulmonary vascular disease and without a CRM device, but is neutral/harmful when the right heart is already limited — a clinically important phenotypic subgroup distinction that persists (and strengthens numerically) out to 3 years, though the authors caution that "REDUCE LAP-HF II was an overall neutral study so the outcomes in the responder group... do not carry the same level of strength as they would if the overall trial had shown beneficial results" (source: [[litwin2024reducelaphf]]).

## Role in HFpEF

REDUCE LAP-HF II is the most advanced device-based trial specifically targeting the haemodynamic mechanism of HFpEF. Referenced in Pfeffer 2019 (alongside the minimally invasive pericardial modification trial) as one of two device approaches to elevated filling pressures. (source: [[pfeffer2019hfpef]])

If the PVR subgroup result holds in confirmatory trials, it would represent the first device therapy with a defined responder phenotype in HFpEF — establishing pulmonary vascular resistance (and absence of a CRM device) as a treatment-relevant biomarker for shunt device selection. RESPONDER-HF ([[responder-hf]]) is the direct enrichment-trial response to this finding, though neither of the two newly ingested follow-up papers (litwin2024reducelaphf, doi2026reducelaphf) mentions RESPONDER-HF by name or NCT number — its design/status remain sourced only to [[patel2024reducelaphf]].

A second, independent line of evidence (Doi et al. 2026, source: [[doi2026reducelaphf]]) shows that **longer duration of HFpEF/HFmrEF diagnosis** is itself associated with more advanced biatrial myopathy, worse LV/RV strain, higher resting filling pressures, and greater risk of worsening HF (HR 1.70, 95% CI 1.17–2.48, for diagnosis >3y vs. <1y; P=0.006) — independent of the PVR-based responder/nonresponder classification. Formal interaction testing found **no significant treatment-by-duration heterogeneity**, but numerically, atrial shunt treatment in patients with >3-year diagnosis duration was associated with an *increased* risk of worsening HF events (HR 1.72, 95% CI 1.26–2.35, P=0.001) compared with sham — a second, statistically unconfirmed candidate treatment-effect modifier alongside PVR.

## Evidence

- **Overall trial (n=621, unselected population):** primary composite endpoint neutral at 1 year and through 3 years (3-year win ratio 1.04, 95% CI 0.83–1.30). Qualitatively confirmed by two independent later analyses (litwin2024reducelaphf, doi2026reducelaphf); exact effect size/CI/P for the overall population's primary endpoint is [needs source — Shah SJ et al., Lancet 2022;399:1130–40, primary results paper, not yet ingested].
- **Echocardiographic substudy (Patel 2024):** significant structural reverse remodelling (LV EDV −5.65 mL, P<0.001; LA EF +1.88 pp, P=0.02; RV EDV +9.58 mL, P<0.001) with PVR-based interaction for RV changes (P=0.01) (source: [[patel2024reducelaphf]]).
- **Responder subgroup, 3-year clinical outcomes (Litwin 2024):** win ratio 1.6 (95% CI 1.2–2.2, P=.006); 44% relative HF-event reduction (IRR 0.587 [0.401–0.859], P=.005; NNT 13); KCCQ-OSS between-group difference +10.1 points (P<.001); safety trade-off — higher ischaemic stroke rate (3.2% vs. 0%, P=.032) but lower incidence of worsening kidney dysfunction (10.7% vs. 19.3%, P=.041) with shunt (source: [[litwin2024reducelaphf]]).
- **RCT-I (mechanistic trial), 5-year follow-up (Litwin 2024):** no significant differences between shunt and sham in CV mortality, HF events, embolic stroke, or new-onset AF; reassuring long-term device safety in a small (n=44), underpowered trial (source: [[litwin2024reducelaphf]]).
- **HF-duration substudy (Doi 2026):** longer diagnosis duration → worse cardiac remodelling/hemodynamics and higher HF-event risk (HR 1.70, P=0.006); no significant treatment-by-duration interaction, though a numerically concerning harm signal was observed with shunt therapy in the >3-year-duration subgroup (HR 1.72 for HF events, P=0.001) (source: [[doi2026reducelaphf]]).

## Status

**Published.** Primary trial: Shah et al., *Lancet* 2022 (not yet ingested — [needs source]). Echocardiographic substudy, 3-year responder follow-up, 5-year RCT-I follow-up, and HF-duration post hoc analysis are all ingested (sources: [[patel2024reducelaphf]], [[litwin2024reducelaphf]], [[doi2026reducelaphf]]). The neutral overall result limits guideline incorporation but has motivated a refined responder trial ([[responder-hf]]) targeting the low-PVR, no-CRM-device HFpEF/HFmrEF phenotype; the durability of the responder-subgroup benefit through 3 years (Litwin 2024) strengthens — but, per the authors, does not itself validate — the responder-enrichment rationale, since it remains a post hoc finding within an overall-neutral trial.

## Secondary Analysis: Expanded Worsening-HF Definition (Chaudhary 2025)
A third independent post hoc secondary analysis of this same MITT cohort (n=621) tests an expanded, hierarchical definition of "worsening heart failure" (hospitalisation / urgent outpatient / nonurgent outpatient diuretic escalation) against the standard hospitalisation-only definition, using the same PVR<1.74 WU/no-CRM-device responder split as [[patel2024reducelaphf]] and [[litwin2024reducelaphf]]. Broadening the WHF definition increased the total event rate (17.4%→25.9%) and turned a nonsignificant responder-subgroup treatment effect into a significant one, while non-hospitalisation WHF events added little independent mortality-prediction value. See [[worsening-heart-failure]] and [[chaudhary2025worseninghf]].

## Contradictions

Neutral overall result vs. positive PVR-defined (and CRM-device-absent) subgroup — a pattern of subgroup heterogeneity that characterises HFpEF more broadly. This is now confirmed as a **persisting, durable contradiction through 3 years of follow-up**, not merely an artefact of short follow-up (source: [[litwin2024reducelaphf]]): the responder-group benefit strengthens numerically from 1 to 3 years (NNT falls from 18 to 13), while the overall trial remains neutral. A second, independent heterogeneity signal by HF-duration (numerically worse HF-event outcomes with shunt in patients with >3-year disease duration) was observed but did not reach formal statistical significance for treatment-by-duration interaction (source: [[doi2026reducelaphf]]) — this should be read as hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory. If the PVR/CRM-device responder criterion is confirmed in a dedicated prospective trial (see [[responder-hf]]), it would reframe REDUCE LAP-HF II as an enrichment failure rather than a mechanistic failure; that confirmatory trial has not yet reported results. See [[contradictions]].
