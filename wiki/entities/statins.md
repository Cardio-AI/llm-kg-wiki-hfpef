# Statins

> HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors proposed to act on HFpEF via pleiotropic anti-inflammatory and endothelial mechanisms beyond lipid lowering; observational RICA registry data (N=2,788) show a 1-year all-cause mortality benefit (HR 0.74) restricted to patients without ischaemic heart disease; mechanistic and observational evidence only — no completed HFpEF-specific RCT exists.

## Description

Statins are HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors used primarily for lipid lowering, with proposed pleiotropic effects — anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, endothelial-protective — invoked as mechanistically relevant to HFpEF beyond their conventional cardiovascular risk-reduction indication. In the coronary microvascular dysfunction ("common soil") paradigm of HFpEF, statins are positioned as a candidate therapy acting on the comorbidity-driven inflammatory pathway that impairs coronary endothelial function and promotes myocardial fibrosis and stiffening. (source: damario2019cmd)

## Role in HFpEF

**Mechanistic rationale ([[coronary-microvascular-dysfunction]]):** In statin-treated HFpEF patients, endomyocardial biopsies show reduced myocardial nitrotyrosine, increased myocardial PKG activity, reduced cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, and lower cardiomyocyte resting tension — direct tissue-level evidence consistent with statins acting on the NO/cGMP/PKG diastolic-stiffness axis via reduced oxidative stress, independent of lipid lowering. Statins are also reported to reduce epicardial adipose tissue and proinflammatory lipoproteins, both implicated in the comorbidity-driven inflammatory model of HFpEF. (source: damario2019cmd)

**Observational mortality signal (JASPER study, cited in damario2019cmd):** Statins reduced mortality in HFpEF without coronary artery disease in an observational analysis (Marume 2019) — specifically a lower incidence of non-cardiac death and HF hospitalisation, without a demonstrated effect on cardiac death. (source: damario2019cmd)

**RICA registry ([[ortegahernandez2024statins]]):** The largest single-registry analysis of statin use in HFpEF with subgroup stratification by ischaemic heart disease (IHD) status. In 2,788 Spanish HFpEF patients (LVEF ≥50%), statin therapy at discharge was independently associated with lower 1-year all-cause mortality overall, but the benefit was statistically significant only in patients **without** IHD; in patients with IHD the point estimate was similar in direction but not significant, with a much smaller subgroup (N=438) and wider confidence interval. (source: OrtegaHernandez2024Statins)

This IHD-status-dependent pattern is proposed to reflect a pleiotropic, non-lipid mechanism of benefit (reduced epicardial adipose tissue, reduced systemic inflammation/CRP, reduced myocardial fibrosis and hypertrophy, reduced QT variability) that is most relevant in non-ischaemic, hypertensive/metabolic-phenotype HFpEF — i.e., statins may help the majority of HFpEF patients whose disease is *not* primarily driven by obstructive coronary disease, rather than functioning as a conventional secondary-prevention agent in this population. (source: OrtegaHernandez2024Statins)

## Evidence

**RICA registry — key findings (N=2,788; LVEF ≥50%; mean age 80.1±7.8y; 40.2% on statins):**

| Measure | Statin | Non-statin | P |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-year all-cause mortality | 14.7% | 20.9% | <0.001 |

**Multivariable Cox regression (adjusted):**
- Statin use: HR 0.74 (95% CI 0.61–0.89; P=0.002)

**Subgroup by ischaemic heart disease status:**

| Subgroup | Statin HR (multivariable) | P | N |
|---|---|---|---|
| Without IHD | **0.69 (95% CI 0.56–0.86)** | **<0.001** | larger subgroup |
| With IHD | 0.69 (95% CI 0.43–1.11) | 0.110 (NS) | N=438, wide CI |

A formal interaction P-value between the two subgroups was not reported, so heterogeneity between them is not statistically confirmed — the point estimates are similar (0.69 in both), and the loss of significance in the IHD subgroup is at least partly attributable to its smaller sample size. (source: OrtegaHernandez2024Statins)

Statin-treated patients in RICA were younger, had more ischaemic HF, more hypertension/diabetes/dyslipidaemia, and better baseline functional status (Barthel index, NYHA class) than non-statin patients — confounding by indication is a major limitation of this observational analysis. (source: OrtegaHernandez2024Statins)

**Mechanistic (endomyocardial biopsy) data:** reduced myocardial nitrotyrosine, increased PKG activity, reduced cardiomyocyte hypertrophy and resting tension in statin-treated HFpEF patients. (source: damario2019cmd)

## Status

No RCT evidence specific to HFpEF exists. GISSI-HF, the only completed RCT of a statin (rosuvastatin) including HFpEF patients, showed no mortality benefit — but only ~10% of the GISSI-HF population had HFpEF (defined at LVEF ≥40%, not ≥50%), so it does not constitute a dedicated HFpEF test of the hypothesis. No guideline gives a HFpEF-specific statin recommendation; statin use in HFpEF patients is currently driven by conventional cardiovascular risk indications rather than a HFpEF-specific one. (source: OrtegaHernandez2024Statins)

## Contradictions
- Observational data (RICA, JASPER) suggest a mortality benefit in non-ischaemic HFpEF, but statin-treated patients differ systematically at baseline (younger, better functional status) — confounding by indication cannot be excluded, and no RCT has confirmed causality.
- GISSI-HF (RCT, but not HFpEF-specific and using a lower LVEF ≥40% threshold) showed no mortality benefit with rosuvastatin, in apparent tension with the observational non-ischaemic-HFpEF benefit signal — the populations and endpoints are not directly comparable, but no RCT has resolved the question in a true HFpEF (LVEF ≥50%) population.
