# STRONG-HF

> International RCT showing that high-intensity in-hospital/post-discharge GDMT uptitration guided by NT-proBNP and GDF-15 reduces 180-day all-cause death or HF readmission by ~8% absolute vs. usual care.

## Description

**Full title:** Safety, Tolerability and Efficacy of Rapid Optimization, Helped by NT-proBNP and GDF-15, of Heart Failure Therapies  
**NCT:** NCT03412201  
**Intervention:** High-intensity care — rapid in-hospital + ≤2-week post-discharge uptitration of 3–4 GDMT pillars, guided by NT-proBNP and GDF-15 — vs. usual care  
**Population:** Acute decompensated HF across LVEF spectrum (both HFrEF and HFpEF enrolled; LVEF not restricted); NT-proBNP >1,500 pg/mL at inclusion  
**N:** ~1,078  
**Follow-up:** 180 days  
**Primary endpoint:** Composite of all-cause death + HF readmission at 180 days  
**Published:** Lancet 2022 (Mebazaa A, Davison B, Chioncel O, et al.)

The trial tests whether using biomarker-guided rapid intensification of all available GDMT classes — starting in-hospital and continuing within 2 weeks post-discharge — reduces the very high early post-discharge event rate in acute HF. Prior evidence: post-discharge heart failure carries the highest 90-day event rate of any cardiac diagnosis; intensification was typically deferred to outpatient follow-up. STRONG-HF demonstrates the feasibility and benefit of same-admission/early post-discharge intensification.

## Role in HFpEF

STRONG-HF enrolled across LVEF strata, not restricted to HFpEF. The high-intensity post-discharge care principle is relevant to HFpEF patients given the same high early event rate applies. With ESC 2023 incorporating the result as Class I, the trial operationalises the post-acute HF care pathway for all EF phenotypes, including HFpEF.

The biomarker-guided intensification model is conceptually concordant with the natriuretic peptide-guided management approach discussed in [[natriuretic-peptides]]. SGLT2i initiation in-hospital is separately addressed by [[empulse]] (EMPULSE trial).

## Evidence

Primary endpoint significantly reduced in the high-intensity arm: absolute risk reduction ~8 percentage points for 180-day death or HF readmission. [Source: Mebazaa2022STRONGHF — pending full ingest for exact HR/CI.]

The benefit was observed despite a small but real excess of adverse events (hypotension, renal impairment, hyperkalaemia) in the intensification arm — acceptable safety tradeoff.

## Status

**Published:** Lancet 2022. Incorporated into ESC 2023 Focused Update as Class I, Level B recommendation for in-hospital and early post-discharge intensive uptitration of GDMT guided by NT-proBNP. (source: [[mcdonagh2023escupdate]])
