# SERVE-HF

> ASV significantly increased all-cause and cardiovascular mortality in HFrEF patients with predominantly central sleep apnea, despite effectively suppressing sleep-disordered breathing — the "surprise result" that established a contraindication to ASV in this population and reshaped the treatment landscape for sleep-disordered breathing in heart failure.

## Description

- **Design:** Randomised controlled trial
- **Intervention:** Adaptive servo-ventilation (ASV) vs. control
- **Population:** N=**1,325**; chronic HFrEF (LV ejection fraction ≤45%) with moderate-to-severe, predominantly central sleep apnea (CSA)
- **Publication:** 2015 (source: Cowie2017SDB, Table 1, citing Cowie MR, Woehrle H, Wegscheider K, et al. Adaptive servo-ventilation for central sleep apnea in systolic heart failure. *N Engl J Med.* 2015;373:1095–105)
- **Primary endpoint:** Death from any cause, lifesaving cardiovascular intervention (heart transplantation, LV assist device implantation, or lifesaving shock), or unplanned hospitalisation for HF (source: Cowie2017SDB)

(source: Cowie2017SDB)

## Role in HFpEF

SERVE-HF itself enrolled HFrEF patients, but its result is the central reference point constraining ASV use across the whole heart-failure spectrum, including discussion of sleep-disordered breathing (SDB) in HFpEF. Cowie 2017 notes that CSA predominates in HFrEF while obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) predominates in HFpEF (especially the obese phenotype), and that HFpEF-specific ASV data from adequately powered trials do not exist — CAT-HF (ASV in acute decompensated HF, enrolling both HFrEF and HFpEF) was stopped early specifically because of the SERVE-HF findings. (source: Cowie2017SDB)

## Evidence

- **Primary composite endpoint:** Neutral, P=**0.10** (source: Cowie2017SDB, Table 1)
- **All-cause mortality:** significantly increased in the ASV arm, HR **1.13** (P=0.01) (source: Cowie2017SDB, Table 1)
- **Cardiovascular mortality:** significantly increased in the ASV arm, HR **1.34** (P=0.006) (source: Cowie2017SDB, Table 1)
- ASV was "highly efficacious in reducing SDB," yet there was "no difference in the primary endpoint between the 2 groups, and a significantly higher overall mortality rate was noted in patients treated with ASV," despite use of ASV for ≥4 h/night in only ~50% of patients and a 17% crossover rate to active therapy (source: Cowie2017SDB)
- A subsequent comprehensive time-dependent on-treatment analysis confirmed the increase in cardiovascular death associated with ASV use (source: Cowie2017SDB)
- No differences in plasma natriuretic peptide concentration, 6-minute walk test results, or health-related quality of life were found between groups out to 12 months (source: Cowie2017SDB)
- Excess mortality was driven by an increase in sudden death, particularly in patients with very low ejection fraction and a predominant Cheyne-Stokes respiration pattern of CSA, with no difference in deaths from pump failure or HF decompensation admissions (source: Cowie2017SDB)
- **Proposed explanations for harm (mechanism uncertain):** chance; a direct toxic effect of positive airway pressure in patients with poor LV function and low pulmonary capillary wedge pressure; the possibility that CSA may be at least partially adaptive in severe HF; or rebound sympathetic reactivation during the day following nighttime ASV treatment of CSA (source: Cowie2017SDB)

## Status

**Published (2015); contraindication established.** The European Society of Cardiology guidelines state that ASV "is not recommended" in patients with HFrEF and predominantly CSA, on the basis of the increase in both all-cause and cardiovascular mortality in SERVE-HF (source: Cowie2017SDB). CAT-HF was stopped early after SERVE-HF results were released; with only 126 patients randomised, it showed no difference in a composite global rank score at 6 months (source: Cowie2017SDB). Further data on ASV in HFrEF (predominantly OSA or CSA) awaited from ADVENT-HF (source: Cowie2017SDB).

## Contradictions
SERVE-HF directly contradicted the prior assumption — drawn by analogy with OSA treatment benefit — that correcting CSA with ASV in HFrEF would improve outcomes; instead it caused harm. Note: `wiki/sources/cowie2017sdb.md`'s own narrative text states CV mortality HR 1.28 (P=0.01) and all-cause mortality HR 1.17 (P=0.048), which differs numerically from the values given in that same source's Table 1 (HR 1.13 [P=0.01] all-cause; HR 1.34 [P=0.006] CV mortality) as extracted directly from `raw/2017-JACCHeartFail-Cowi-sleep-disordered_breathing_hf.pdf`. This entity page uses the Table 1 (raw-PDF-verified) values; the discrepancy in the source-summary page should be reviewed. See [[contradictions]].
