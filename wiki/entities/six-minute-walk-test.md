# Six-Minute Walk Test

> Field-based submaximal exercise-capacity test reporting distance walked (6MWD, in metres) over a fixed 6-minute period; used across nearly all major HFpEF exercise, pharmacological, and rehabilitation trials as a primary, co-primary, or secondary functional endpoint, distinct from — and generally simpler than — cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET).

## Description

The 6MWT quantifies functional/exercise capacity as the distance (in metres) a patient walks over a fixed 6-minute period; trial eligibility criteria across this wiki use it as a distance-based threshold for "reduced exercise capacity" — e.g., FAIR-HFpEF enrolled patients with "6MWT <450m," defined operationally as "a 6MWD <450 m, averaged from the last two documented tests within 8 weeks prior to planned randomization" (source: vonHaehling2024FAIR); RELAX enrolled patients with "reduced peak VO₂ or 6MWD" (6MWD ≤450m) (source: [[redfield2013relax]]).

**Test protocol:** standardised by the American Thoracic Society (ATS) in 2002 and subsequently updated jointly by ATS/European Respiratory Society (ERS); "although a standard methodology has been proposed, there are many versions of the protocol used in various centers," so using the same strictly standardised protocol matters most for within-centre/within-patient comparisons. The test is performed preferably indoors, on a flat, straight, hard-surfaced corridor **usually at least 30m long**, marked every 3m, with cones advised at the turnarounds. Before the test the patient is told to be calm, to have taken their medications, and to wear comfortable clothing/shoes; the supervisor records baseline oxygen saturation, heart rate, brachial blood pressure, and Borg dyspnea/fatigue scores. During the test, the patient walks at a self-selected pace and may stop or slow down and resume as soon as able; the supervisor remains present throughout and gives **standardised encouragement phrasing** — e.g., "You are doing well," "Keep up the good work" — which has been shown to affect distance covered, particularly in paediatric populations. At the end, Borg scores are repeated (vitals optionally re-measured) and 6MWD is calculated from laps plus residual distance. A learning effect has been described, and two measurements are recommended at initial assessment to ensure accuracy, though this effect may be less pronounced in older patients with severe respiratory impairment or severe HF. Reproducibility is reported as "very good" when the same trained operator(s) follow an established protocol (source: giannitsi2019sixmwt).

Unlike cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET), which directly measures gas exchange (VO₂, VCO₂, VE) and — in its invasive form — haemodynamics (PCWP, cardiac output) to mechanistically decompose cardiac vs. peripheral contributors to exercise intolerance (source: [[cardiopulmonary-exercise-testing]]), the 6MWT yields only a single distance outcome without decomposing the physiological basis of any limitation. A 2019 narrative review states explicitly that CPET "is an expensive method that demands special equipment and trained personnel, while its availability in many hospital settings is limited," and that patients "often find it difficult to cooperate with the test either due to the complexity of the test or the severe impairment of their functional status" — whereas the 6MWT "requires no specialized equipment or advanced training for physicians" and is "widely available and well-tolerated," able to "complement or substitute the information given by peak VO2 in the case when CPET is unavailable" (source: giannitsi2019sixmwt). This closes most of the prior evidence gap on 6MWT's pragmatic-endpoint rationale, though the source frames this generically for clinical/hospital practice rather than specifically for multi-site RCT operations.

CAPACITY-HFpEF operationalises a "responder" threshold of **≥15m** improvement for defining 6-MWT responders (source: Udelson2020CAPACITY); no other cited trial states a formal minimal clinically important difference (MCID) for 6MWD, and this 15m threshold is not stated to be externally validated within the cited text.

## Role in HFpEF

6MWD/6MWT serves markedly different endpoint roles across the trials in this wiki:

- **Primary endpoint:** FAIR-HFpEF used change in 6MWTD from baseline to week 24 as its sole primary endpoint (source: vonHaehling2024FAIR).
- **Co-primary/confirmatory secondary endpoint:** STEP-HFpEF DM used 6MWD change at 52 weeks as a confirmatory secondary (hierarchically tested) endpoint (source: [[kosiborod2024stephfpefdm]]); STEP-HFpEF (non-DM) and SUMMIT report 6MWD as a confirmatory/co-primary-adjacent secondary endpoint (source: [[step-hfpef]]; source: [[summit]]).
- **Secondary functional outcome in exercise-training/rehabilitation trials:** SECRET (both diet and exercise arms improved 6MWD, +85 ft and +106 ft respectively, both P<0.001), SECRET-II (resistance training add-on trial; 6MWD not separately reported as a headline result), REHAB-HF (+34m overall; HFpEF subgroup +40m vs. HFrEF +27m), and OptimEx-Clin (6MWD not reported as a headline endpoint — peak VO₂ was primary) (source: Kitzman2016SECRET; source: Kitzman2021REHABHF; source: Mueller2021OptimEx).
- **Secondary/neutral endpoint in negative sGC-stimulator and cGMP-pathway trials:** CAPACITY-HFpEF (praliciguat: adjusted difference −16.7m, P=0.28, with fewer responders on active drug: 37% vs. 59%, P=0.007) and VITALITY-HFpEF (vericiguat: −5.5m at 15mg [P=0.45], −1.8m at 10mg [P=0.81]) both showed neutral-to-worse 6MWD signals in trials that also failed their primary endpoints (source: Udelson2020CAPACITY; source: Armstrong2020VITALITY).

## Status

6MWD is the second most ubiquitous functional-capacity outcome measure in this wiki after peak VO₂/CPET, used across both pharmacological and exercise-training HFpEF trials. Its test protocol (ATS/ERS-standardised corridor test, encouragement phrasing, learning effect) and rationale relative to CPET are now sourced (source: giannitsi2019sixmwt). A **formal, externally validated MCID for 6MWD is still not documented** in any wiki page or raw PDF reviewed for this entry, beyond isolated "responder" thresholds (≥15m, CAPACITY-HFpEF) used within single trials, and beyond the general HF-population observation (not HFpEF-specific) that ~80m improvement is needed to show a quality-of-life benefit, versus the 30–50m gains typically seen in trials with favourable morbidity/mortality signals (source: giannitsi2019sixmwt). ## Contradictions

- **CAPACITY-HFpEF 6-MWT responders paradox:** significantly fewer responders on active drug (37% vs. 59%, P=0.007) despite a neutral primary endpoint (peak VO₂, P=0.37) — a large placebo-arm improvement (+27.0m) that the trial's own limitations section attributes to a possible "test-retest learning effect," diminishing sensitivity to detect a true drug effect (source: Udelson2020CAPACITY).
- **6MWD/KCCQ dissociation:** the wiki documents (in `wiki/contradictions.md` #14) that 6MWD and KCCQ frequently move independently within the same RCT (e.g., DETERMINE-Reduced: 6MWD neutral P=0.686 despite significant KCCQ-TSS improvement P=0.022) and correlate only weakly with each other and with accelerometry (r=0.06–0.31), raising the concern that 6MWD may not reliably surrogate for patient-centred benefit. See [[contradictions]].
- **VITALITY-HFpEF and CAPACITY-HFpEF both neutral-to-negative on 6MWD** in trials targeting the NO/cGMP pathway, contrasting with FAIR-HFpEF's positive 6MWD signal (iron repletion) and the SGLT2i/GLP-1RA trials' consistent positive 6MWD signals — suggesting mechanism-dependent rather than universal 6MWD responsiveness in HFpEF.
