# REHAB-HF

> RCT of transitional progressive multidomain rehabilitation initiated during hospitalisation for acute decompensated HF; significantly improved physical performance (SPPB) and 6MWD but did not reduce 60-day rehospitalisation; HFpEF patients showed larger functional gains and benefit on composite endpoint.

## Description

REHAB-HF was a multicentre (4 US sites), randomised, single-blind, controlled trial testing transitional progressive multidomain rehabilitation vs. usual care in 349 older patients (≥60 years) hospitalised for acute decompensated HF (any ejection fraction). The intervention combined strength, balance, mobility, and endurance training, initiated in-hospital and continued for 36 supervised outpatient sessions over 12 weeks. The trial enrolled a highly frail population: mean age 72.7, 97% frail or pre-frail, 97% Black race, 5 comorbidities, and 53% HFpEF.

**Primary endpoint:** Short Physical Performance Battery (SPPB) at 3 months.

(source: raw/2021-NEJM-Kitzman-REHAB-HF_study.pdf)

---

## Role in HFpEF

REHAB-HF demonstrates that structured multidomain rehabilitation is feasible and effective in the frailest, most comorbid post-hospitalisation HF patients — including those with HFpEF. Key contributions:

1. **HFpEF patients responded at least as well as HFrEF patients.** Pre-specified EF subgroup analysis (Mentz 2021 JACC Heart Fail) showed SPPB improvement 1.9 points in HFpEF vs 1.1 points in HFrEF; global rank endpoint significant in HFpEF (P=0.04) but not HFrEF. Interaction P=0.098 (non-significant).

2. **Addresses frailty as a critical comorbidity in HFpEF.** 97% frail or pre-frail at enrolment — a population excluded from most exercise RCTs (e.g., OptimEx-Clin enrolled stable outpatients). REHAB-HF establishes the transitional inpatient-to-outpatient context as an effective rehabilitation window.

3. **Functional but not hard outcomes improved.** The disconnect between significant SPPB/6MWD improvement and non-significant rehospitalisation effect is consistent with the broader HFpEF pattern. Hard outcome data remains absent.

(source: raw/2021-NEJM-Kitzman-REHAB-HF_study.pdf; source: raw/2021-JACC-Mentz-REHAB-HF_study.pdf)

## Evidence

### Main Results (Kitzman 2021, NEJM)

| Endpoint | Rehab | Control | Difference (95% CI) | P |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SPPB at 3 months | 8.3 | 6.9 | +1.5 (0.9–2.0) | <0.001 |
| 6MWD at 3 months | — | — | +34 m | Significant |
| KCCQ at 3 months | — | — | +7.1 | Significant |
| 60-day rehospitalisation | — | — | RR 0.93 (0.66–1.19) | NS |

### HFpEF Subgroup (Mentz 2021, JACC Heart Fail)

| Endpoint | HFpEF | HFrEF | Interaction P |
|---|---|---|---|
| SPPB | +1.9 (1.1–2.6) | +1.1 (0.3–1.9) | 0.25 |
| 6MWD | +40 m | +27 m | NS |
| Global rank (prob. index) | 0.59, P=0.04 | 0.50, P=0.69 | 0.098 |

(source: raw/2021-NEJM-Kitzman-REHAB-HF_study.pdf; source: raw/2021-JACC-Mentz-REHAB-HF_study.pdf)

## Status

**Published:** Main results NEJM 2021;385:203–216; HFpEF subgroup JACC Heart Fail 2021;9:747–757.  
**Current status:** Completed; no mortality/hospitalisation benefit established; remains the only large RCT of multidomain rehabilitation specifically in post-hospitalisation HF across EF strata.  
**Guideline impact:** Consistent with Class I, Level A exercise recommendation in AHA/ACC 2022 guidelines; specifically supports rehabilitation initiation at/following HF hospitalisation.
