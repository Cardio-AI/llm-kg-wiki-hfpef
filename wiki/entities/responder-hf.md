# RESPONDER-HF

> Ongoing successor to REDUCE LAP-HF II that enriches enrollment for the "responder" phenotype (low pulmonary vascular resistance, no cardiac rhythm management device) identified post hoc as predicting favourable structural and haemodynamic response to atrial shunting.

## Description

- **Intervention:** Corvia atrial shunt device (as in [[reduce-lap-hf-ii]])
- **Population:** HFpEF/HFmrEF patients who meet "responder" criteria
- **Status:** Ongoing (as of the 2024 REDUCE LAP-HF II echocardiographic substudy publication)

(source: patel2024reducelaphf)

## Role in HFpEF

RESPONDER-HF is the direct enrichment-trial response to the REDUCE LAP-HF II echocardiographic substudy finding that a "responder" subgroup — defined by peak-exercise pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) <1.74 Woods Units and absence of a cardiac rhythm management (CRM) device — showed more favourable structural remodelling (greater right ventricular accommodation of shunt flow, improved left atrial and left ventricular parameters) than non-responders after atrial shunt implantation. Because REDUCE LAP-HF II's overall trial result was neutral for clinical events, and this neutrality was attributed to dilution by non-responders, RESPONDER-HF tests the atrial shunt device exclusively in the pre-specified responder phenotype. (source: patel2024reducelaphf)

## Evidence

No RESPONDER-HF trial results are available; the trial itself is presumed ongoing. **Neither of the two newly ingested REDUCE LAP-HF II follow-up papers (Litwin 2024, Doi 2026) mentions RESPONDER-HF by name or NCT05233358** — both are secondary analyses of REDUCE LAP-HF II itself, not reports from a distinct successor trial. The rationale for a responder-enriched design remains sourced only to the post hoc REDUCE LAP-HF II echocardiographic analysis (source: [[patel2024reducelaphf]]): see [[reduce-lap-hf-ii]] for the responder-vs-non-responder structural data (e.g., RV end-diastolic volume increase +5.71 mL in responders vs. +15.18 mL in non-responders, P_interaction=0.01; RVEF increased +2.01 percentage points in responders but not in non-responders).

That said, the two new sources substantially **strengthen the durability of the underlying responder-phenotype rationale** with clinical (not just echocardiographic) outcomes:
- **Litwin 2024** (source: [[litwin2024reducelaphf]]) reports 3-year *clinical* outcomes in the same peak-exercise-PVR ≤1.74 WU + no-CRM-device responder subgroup (n=313) used to define RESPONDER-HF's eligibility rationale: win ratio 1.6 (95% CI 1.2–2.2, P=.006); 44% relative reduction in HF events (IRR 0.587 [0.401–0.859], P=.005; NNT 13 at 3 years, improving from NNT 18 at 1 year); KCCQ-OSS between-group difference +10.1 points (P<.001). This is the first ingested source demonstrating that the responder benefit is durable and strengthens over time on hard clinical endpoints, not just structural/echocardiographic ones — supporting (but not proving) the premise that a responder-enriched trial should succeed where the unselected REDUCE LAP-HF II trial did not.
- **Doi 2026** (source: [[doi2026reducelaphf]]) shows shorter HFpEF/HFmrEF diagnosis duration is associated with less advanced cardiac remodelling and a numerically better atrial-shunt response (though the formal treatment-by-duration interaction test was not significant) — a second candidate patient-selection axis (disease duration) that is conceptually complementary to, but distinct from, the PVR/CRM-device responder definition used for RESPONDER-HF. Whether RESPONDER-HF's eligibility criteria incorporate HF duration is [needs source — not stated in either ingested paper].
- Safety caveat: in the PVR/CRM-device responder subgroup, shunt-treated patients had a higher rate of ischaemic stroke at 3 years (3.2% vs. 0%, P=.032) alongside the HF-event/KCCQ benefit (source: [[litwin2024reducelaphf]]) — relevant to weighing net benefit if RESPONDER-HF replicates the responder-subgroup treatment effect.
