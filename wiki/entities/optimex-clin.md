# OptimEx-Clin

> Three-arm European RCT in 180 HFpEF patients comparing high-intensity interval training (HIIT), moderate continuous training (MCT), and guideline control; HIIT and MCT both improved peak VO₂ at 3 months but not at 12 months; HIIT was not superior to MCT — the largest head-to-head comparison of exercise modalities in HFpEF.

## Description

OptimEx-Clin was a three-arm, single-blind, randomised controlled trial at 5 European centres, enrolling 180 patients with HFpEF (LVEF ≥50%) to compare:
- **HIIT:** 3×38 min/week at 80–90% heart rate reserve (HRR) for 3 months supervised, then 9 months telemedically supervised at home
- **MCT:** 5×40 min/week at 35–50% HRR, same supervision structure
- **Guideline control:** single-session exercise counselling per current guidelines

Mean age 70, 67% women, mean peak VO₂ 18.8 mL/kg/min, mean E/e' 15.8, mean NT-proBNP 671 pg/mL.

(source: raw/2021-JAMA-Mueller-HIIT_moderate_guideline_study.pdf)

---

## Role in HFpEF

OptimEx-Clin is the definitive head-to-head comparison of HIIT vs MCT in HFpEF. It resolved a long-standing uncertainty (whether HIIT's advantages in HFrEF translated to HFpEF) and clarified the durability question (gains are not maintained at 12 months with telemedically-supervised home exercise). Key contributions:

1. **HIIT is not superior to MCT in HFpEF.** The main hypothesis was not confirmed. This is the largest, best-powered head-to-head comparison in HFpEF — the finding is definitive for typical outpatient HFpEF populations.

2. **Both exercise modalities improve VO₂ short-term but not durably.** The loss of gains at 12 months highlights the challenge of long-term exercise maintenance. Supervised sessions are required for sustained benefit.

3. **Exercise does not directly improve diastolic function.** No change in E/e' or NT-proBNP in any group — confirms that exercise training improves peripheral/skeletal muscle fitness but does not reverse cardiac diastolic pathology in HFpEF.

(source: raw/2021-JAMA-Mueller-HIIT_moderate_guideline_study.pdf)

## Evidence

### Main Results (Mueller 2021, JAMA)

| Comparison | VO₂peak at 3 months | VO₂peak at 12 months |
|---|---|---|
| HIIT vs control | +1.5 (0.4–2.7), P=0.01 | NS |
| MCT vs control | +2.0 (0.9–3.1), P=0.001 | NS |
| HIIT vs MCT | −0.4 (−1.4 to 0.6), P=NS | NS |

**Secondary outcomes:** E/e' not changed in any group; NT-proBNP not changed; KCCQ improved in MCT vs control at 12 months (exploratory).

(source: raw/2021-JAMA-Mueller-HIIT_moderate_guideline_study.pdf)

## Status

**Published:** JAMA 2021;325(6):542–551.  
**Current status:** Completed.  
**Impact:** Guideline-informing — supports MCT as the evidence-based default modality in HFpEF; does not support preferential recommendation of HIIT over MCT.

## Contradictions

DonelliDaSilveira 2020 (N=19) showed significant HIIT > MCT superiority for VO₂; OptimEx-Clin (N=180) showed no difference. The smaller study's result is likely a false positive due to sample size. See [[contradictions]].
