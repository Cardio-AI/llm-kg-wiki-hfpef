# Minnesota Living with Heart Failure Questionnaire

> Heart-failure-specific quality-of-life questionnaire (MLHFQ): 21 items (six-point Likert 0–5), total score range 0–105 (higher = worse), with physical (8 items, 0–40) and emotional (5 items, 0–25) subscales; reported as a total score in which decreases over follow-up are interpreted as improvement; used as a co-primary endpoint in SECRET and as a secondary quality-of-life measure in I-PRESERVE and RELAX.

## Description

The MLHFQ is a heart-failure-specific instrument reported as a "total score" (source: Kitzman2016SECRET) used to assess "clinical status, quality of life" (source: Redfield2013RELAX). In RELAX, MLHFQ change was one component of a hierarchical composite clinical status rank score, in which patients were ranked from worst outcome (earliest death) to best outcome ("survival with no cardiovascular or cardiorenal hospitalization and the most favorable improvement in heart failure questionnaire") — this ranking logic indicates that a **decrease** in MLHFQ score is treated as the favourable/improving direction (source: Redfield2013RELAX). Baseline median MLHFQ total scores were 43 (IQR 30–62) in RELAX and 42 (IQR 27–58 to 28–58) in I-PRESERVE (source: Redfield2013RELAX; source: Massie2008IPreserve).

**Instrument structure and scoring (source: bilbao2016mlhf):** The MLHFQ is a self-administered, disease-specific questionnaire comprising **21 items**, each rated on a **six-point Likert scale from 0 (none) to 5 (very much)**, reflecting the degree to which HF has impacted different aspects of the patient's life over the preceding month. It yields a **total score (range 0–105)**, plus two subscale scores derived from the original developers' (Rector et al.) structure: a **physical dimension (8 items: items 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 12, 13; range 0–40)** and an **emotional dimension (5 items: items 17, 18, 19, 20, 21; range 0–25)**. The remaining 8 of the 21 items are not part of either subscale and contribute only to the total score. Higher scores indicate worse HRQoL on all three scales. A large Spanish multicentre validation study (N=2565 hospitalized HF patients, CFA + Rasch analysis) confirmed the adequacy of this two-factor physical/emotional structure and additionally supported a third, non-original "social" dimension, recommending a 6-item social subscale (items 8, 9, 10, 14, 15, 16; range 0–30) as the best-performing of several proposed alternatives — this third factor is not part of the standard/original scoring and is not used in any HFpEF trial cited in this wiki (source: bilbao2016mlhf).

## Role in HFpEF

- **Co-primary endpoint:** SECRET used the Minnesota Living with Heart Failure (MLHF) questionnaire total score as one of two co-primary endpoints (alongside peak VO₂) in obese older HFpEF patients randomised to caloric restriction and/or aerobic exercise training; neither intervention met this co-primary QoL endpoint (exercise P=0.70; diet P=0.08), despite both significantly improving peak VO₂ (source: Kitzman2016SECRET).
- **Secondary/tertiary composite tier:** RELAX incorporated MLHFQ change as tier 3 of a hierarchical composite clinical status rank score (evaluated only in patients without a cardiovascular/cardiorenal hospitalisation), in addition to reporting it as a standalone secondary endpoint; both were neutral (source: Redfield2013RELAX).
- **Secondary endpoint:** I-PRESERVE reported MLHFQ score change at 6 months as a secondary endpoint; this was neutral (source: Massie2008IPreserve).

Across all three trials where MLHFQ is reported in this wiki, the instrument was **neutral** — it never distinguished active treatment from placebo/control, regardless of whether the paired primary endpoint (peak VO₂ in RELAX and SECRET; the death/CV-hospitalisation composite in I-PRESERVE) was itself positive, neutral, or mixed.

## Status

The MLHFQ appears in this wiki only in earlier-era HFpEF RAAS-blocker and PDE5-inhibitor trials (I-PRESERVE, 2008; RELAX, 2013) and one diet/exercise trial (SECRET, 2016). More recent HFpEF pharmacological trials cited in this wiki (DAPA-HF, EMPEROR-Preserved, STEP-HFpEF, SUMMIT, FINEARTS-HF, EMPULSE) uniformly use the KCCQ instead — see [[kansas-city-cardiomyopathy-questionnaire]]. No wiki source documents when or why this shift occurred, nor any formal validation, scoring, or comparative-performance literature for the MLHFQ. [needs source]

## Contradictions

- **MLHFQ vs. KCCQ dissociation within the same trial (SECRET):** diet significantly improved KCCQ (+7 pts, P=0.004, clinically meaningful) but did not meet the co-primary MLHFQ endpoint (P=0.08); exercise significantly improved peak VO₂ (P<0.001, co-primary) but not MLHFQ (P=0.70) or KCCQ (P=0.43). Two different QoL instruments administered in the same patients produced different conclusions about whether a quality-of-life benefit occurred — reinforces the known dissociation between exercise-capacity and QoL/symptom endpoints noted elsewhere in this wiki (source: Kitzman2016SECRET). See [[contradictions]] #14.
- **MLHFQ uniformly neutral regardless of primary-endpoint direction:** I-PRESERVE (fully neutral primary), RELAX (fully neutral primary), and SECRET (positive peak VO₂ co-primary) all show a neutral MLHFQ result — the instrument did not track with, or diverge informatively from, the associated primary outcome in any of the three trials, raising an open question (not resolved by any cited source) about its sensitivity in this population.
