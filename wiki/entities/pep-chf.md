# PEP-CHF

> Perindopril failed to reduce the primary composite of mortality/unplanned HF hospitalisation in elderly HF patients with preserved LVEF — the earliest large ACE inhibitor trial in HFpEF; results limited by high early drug discontinuation.

## Description

**Full title:** Perindopril in Elderly People With Chronic Heart Failure  
**NCT:** [verify on ingest — pre-registration era trial]  
**Intervention:** Perindopril 4 mg vs. placebo  
**Population:** Age ≥70 years, LVEF >40%, echocardiographic features of diastolic dysfunction (LVH or dilated LA), clinical HF  
**N:** 850  
**Mean follow-up:** 26.2 months  
**Primary endpoint:** Composite of all-cause mortality + unplanned HF hospitalisation  
**Published:** Eur Heart J. 2006;27:2338–2345 (Cleland JGF, Tendera M, Adamus J, et al.)

PEP-CHF was the first large-scale RAAS-targeted RCT in HFpEF and the oldest trial in the HFpEF pharmacological series. At the time of design, "diastolic heart failure" was still the preferred term and echocardiographic diastolic dysfunction criteria were used for enrollment, not the NP-based threshold enrichment used in later trials (I-PRESERVE, TOPCAT, PARAGON-HF, FINEARTS-HF).

## Role in HFpEF

PEP-CHF anchors the RAAS failure narrative in HFpEF: the same negative result was subsequently replicated with candesartan (CHARM-Preserved, 2003), irbesartan (I-PRESERVE, 2008), and sacubitril/valsartan (PARAGON-HF, 2019, which narrowly missed). These trials collectively establish that RAAS blockade does not reduce hard outcomes in the full HFpEF population. (source: [[pfeffer2019hfpef]])

PEP-CHF timeline:

| Trial | Drug class | LVEF | N | Primary result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PEP-CHF (2006) | ACEi (perindopril) | >40% | 850 | HR 0.92, P=0.55 — neutral |
| CHARM-Preserved (2003) | ARB (candesartan) | >40% | 3,023 | HR 0.89, P=0.118 — neutral |
| I-PRESERVE (2008) | ARB (irbesartan) | ≥45% | 4,128 | HR 0.95, P=0.35 — neutral |
| PARAGON-HF (2019) | ARNI (sacubitril/valsartan) | ≥45% | 4,822 | RR 0.87, P=0.06 — missed |

**Special limitation:** PEP-CHF had substantial drug discontinuation — by year 1, ~36% had discontinued study drug. This early attrition may have diluted the treatment effect and is a major confounder of the neutral result. A 1-year secondary analysis did show numerical (not statistically significant) reduction in HF hospitalisation, which the authors noted as consistent with benefit that was obscured by dropout. However, per protocol analysis data are insufficient to rescue the neutral ITT result.

## Evidence

Primary composite HR 0.92 (95% CI 0.70–1.21; P=0.55). HF hospitalisation: HR 0.86 (P=0.38). Nominally positive at 1 year (post-hoc, not primary). [Source: Cleland2006PEPCHF — pending full ingest for exact numbers.]

## Status

**Published:** EHJ 2006. No guideline recommendation for ACE inhibitors in HFpEF based on this trial. Mentioned in the Pfeffer 2019 invited review as part of the RAAS trial series landscape. (source: [[pfeffer2019hfpef]])

## Contradictions

PEP-CHF's 1-year exploratory data (trending positive) vs. the overall neutral 26-month result — whether early drug discontinuation confounds interpretation is debated. Sits alongside CHARM-Preserved (HR 0.89, HF hosp signal P=0.017) in the "trending but not significant" cluster of RAAS trials that, combined with I-PRESERVE's fully neutral result, have collectively ended ACE-i/ARB pursuit in HFpEF.
