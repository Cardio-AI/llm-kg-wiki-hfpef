# NEAT-HFpEF

> Isosorbide mononitrate did not improve, and instead reduced, real-world daily physical activity in HFpEF, with accelerometer units declining progressively at higher doses (all doses combined: −439 units/day, P=0.02) — the first trial to suggest organic nitrates may worsen functional status in HFpEF rather than help it.

## Description

- **Intervention:** Isosorbide mononitrate, dose-escalated 30→60→120 mg once daily, vs. placebo (double-blind crossover)
- **Population:** HFpEF, LVEF ≥50%, age ≥50, exertional limitation as the primary symptom, objective HF evidence (prior HF hospitalisation, radiographic pulmonary congestion, elevated LVEDP/PCWP, elevated NPs, or Doppler diastolic dysfunction) (source: redfield2015neat)
- **N:** 110; 20 sites, United States only; enrolled Apr 7, 2014–Oct 30, 2014; 6 weeks per crossover period (source: redfield2015neat)
- **Primary endpoint:** Average daily accelerometer units at the 120 mg dose
- **Full paper stub:** [[redfield2015neat]]

## Role in HFpEF

NEAT-HFpEF is part of a cluster of trials testing the NO/cGMP pathway hypothesis in [[hfpef]], alongside [[relax]] (sildenafil/PDE5i), [[indie-hfpef]] (inorganic nitrate), [[vitality-hfpef]] (vericiguat), and [[capacity-hfpef]] (praliciguat). Unlike the other four, which were broadly neutral, NEAT-HFpEF produced an active **harm signal**: accelerometer-measured daily activity declined progressively with dose escalation, and hours of activity per day were significantly reduced at the 120 mg dose (P=0.02). This directly contradicts the common clinical practice of prescribing nitrates for dyspnea relief in HFpEF, and is hypothesised to reflect excessive preload reduction impairing the stroke-volume reserve needed for exertion in a preload-dependent physiology. (source: redfield2015neat)

## Evidence

- **Primary endpoint (120 mg phase):** −381 accelerometer units/day (95% CI −780 to +17), P=0.06 — non-significant trend toward *less* activity (source: redfield2015neat)
- **Hours of activity per day (120 mg phase):** −0.30 hr/day (95% CI −0.55 to −0.05), P=0.02 — significantly fewer hours of activity on nitrate (source: redfield2015neat)
- **All doses combined (accelerometer units):** −439 units/day (95% CI −792 to −86), P=0.02 — significantly less active on isosorbide mononitrate (source: redfield2015neat)
- Activity decreased progressively with each dose escalation (30→60→120 mg); no comparable dose-response occurred with placebo (source: redfield2015neat)
- No significant difference in 6-minute walk distance, quality-of-life scores (KCCQ/MLHFQ), or NT-proBNP (source: redfield2015neat)
- Baseline: mean LVEF 63%; median NT-proBNP ~230 pg/mL; mean age 69y; 57% female; BMI ~35.7 (obese); 92% hypertensive; 35% AF history (source: redfield2015neat)

## Status

- No guideline recommendation for organic nitrates in HFpEF; NEAT-HFpEF is cited as evidence against routine nitrate use for dyspnea in this population (source: redfield2015neat)
- Contributed, alongside [[relax]], [[indie-hfpef]], [[vitality-hfpef]], and [[capacity-hfpef]], to closing the NO/cGMP pathway as a therapeutic target in the general HFpEF population (source: redfield2015neat)

## Contradictions
- Nitrates are widely prescribed in HFpEF to relieve dyspnea, but NEAT-HFpEF found patients were actually *less* active on isosorbide mononitrate — a possible mechanism is excessive preload reduction impairing the stroke-volume reserve needed for exertion. (source: redfield2015neat)
- NEAT-HFpEF (chronic organic nitrate: activity worsened, dose-dependently) directly contrasts with [[indie-hfpef]] (single acute dose of inorganic nitrate: peak VO₂ improved, P=0.005). Same NO-pathway target, opposite direction — possible explanations include acute vs. chronic exposure (nitrate tolerance develops rapidly with continuous organic nitrate), inorganic vs. organic NO donor pathways, and differing exercise postures. (source: redfield2015neat; zamani2015indie)
