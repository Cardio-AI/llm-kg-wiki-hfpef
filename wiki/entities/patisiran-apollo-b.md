# Patisiran (APOLLO-B)

> Double-stranded siRNA targeting transthyretin (TTR) messenger RNA; monthly IV infusion silences hepatic TTR production by >80%; evaluated in ATTR cardiomyopathy in the phase 3 APOLLO-B trial (NCT03997383) — 12-month functional and biomarker endpoints improved. Not FDA-approved for the cardiac ATTR-CM indication in the US as of 2025; approved in Brazil, and for compassionate use only in France (tafamidis 61mg failures) (source: [[berk2025patisiran]]).

## Description

Patisiran is a double-stranded small interfering RNA (siRNA) that targets a sequence within the transthyretin (TTR) messenger RNA, silencing hepatic TTR production. (source: [[anker2023hfpefphenotype]]) It is administered as a monthly intravenous infusion and reduces hepatic TTR production by more than 80%. (source: [[masri2026attrcm]])

Mechanistically it belongs to the RNA-silencer class of ATTR-CM therapeutics (alongside vutrisiran, an siRNA, and the antisense oligonucleotides eplontersen/inotersen), distinct from the TTR-stabiliser class (tafamidis, acoramidis), which prevents tetramer dissociation rather than reducing TTR production. (source: [[masri2026attrcm]])

## Role in HFpEF

ATTR-CM (wild-type or variant) is an important HFpEF subphenotype/mimic, estimated at 13–19% prevalence in unselected HFpEF (autopsy/biopsy series); active exclusion via [[technetium-pyrophosphate-scintigraphy]] is standard in contemporary HFpEF workup. (source: [[masri2026attrcm]]) At the time the Anker 2023 HFA/ESC/ESH phenotyping statement was written, APOLLO-B was still an ongoing trial evaluating patisiran for ATTR amyloidosis-related cardiomyopathy as part of the Table 1 "restrictive cardiomyopathy" HFpEF-mimic workup. (source: [[anker2023hfpefphenotype]])

Patisiran is described in the wiki as the first siRNA agent with cardiac-outcomes evidence in ATTR-CM. (source: [[masri2026attrcm]])

## Evidence

**APOLLO-B (NCT03997383)** — phase 3 RCT, patisiran vs. placebo:
- Population: ATTR-CM (wild-type or variant), aged 18–85 years, with a history of heart failure; NYHA II–III, elevated NT-proBNP (source: [[masri2026attrcm]])
- Enrollment period: October 2019 – May 2021; trial duration 12 months (source: [[masri2026attrcm]])
- Enrollment criteria (shared with ATTRibute-CM/HELIOS-B, contrasting with the stricter ATTR-ACT criteria): NT-proBNP ≥300 pg/mL (up to 8500 pg/mL) and 6-minute walk distance ≥150 m required at screening (source: [[masri2026attrcm]])
- N ≈ 300 (source: [[masri2026attrcm]])
- Baseline background therapy: diuretics 92%, SGLT2 inhibitors 4% — both higher than in the earlier ATTR-ACT trial (68% diuretics; SGLT2i not yet available) (source: [[masri2026attrcm]])
- Tafamidis drop-in during the trial: 2% of the placebo arm initiated tafamidis during follow-up — the lowest drop-in rate among the three post-2019 phase 3 ATTR-CM RCTs (vs. up to 22.8% in ATTRibute-CM) (source: [[masri2026attrcm]])
- Placebo-arm all-cause mortality at 12 months: 5.6% — lower than the 9.0% observed in the placebo arm of ATTR-ACT (2013–2015), consistent with a temporal trend toward less advanced disease at enrollment across the ATTR-CM trial literature (source: [[masri2026attrcm]])
- 12-month outcomes reported in the wiki source summary: 6-minute walk test improved, KCCQ improved, NT-proBNP reduced — "functional outcomes positive" (source: [[masri2026attrcm]])
- Primary 6MWT treatment effect: Hodges–Lehmann estimate of median difference +14.69 m (95% CI: 0.69–28.69) vs. placebo at month 12 (source: [[berk2025patisiran]])

**Post hoc functional-capacity analysis ([[berk2025patisiran]]):**
- Estimated 6MWT minimal clinically important difference (MCID) in this ATTR-CM population: 6.9–7.8 m (KCCQ-OS-anchored; first reported 6MWT MCID specific to ATTR-CM) — the trial's +15 m observed treatment benefit exceeds this MCID (source: [[berk2025patisiran]])
- A +15 m more favorable 6MWT change was associated with lower odds of losing the ability to: dress oneself (OR 0.85), climb stairs (OR 0.84), shower/bathe (OR 0.87), walk 1 block (OR 0.88), hurry/jog (OR 0.88), and do yard/housework or carry groceries (OR 0.89) (source: [[berk2025patisiran]])
- Patisiran-treated patients more often achieved 6MWT improvement ≥15 m (32% vs. 21% placebo, P<0.05) and ≥30 m (24% vs. 14%, P<0.01); fewer patisiran patients worsened by >30 m (34% vs. 45%, P<0.05) (source: [[berk2025patisiran]])
- KCCQ-OS least-squares mean difference vs. placebo: +3.7 points (95% CI: 0.2–7.2) at month 12; benefit consistent across all 4 KCCQ domains; deaths through month 12 (incl. transplants): placebo 2 (1.1%), patisiran 0 (0%) (source: [[berk2025patisiran]])

## Status

Patisiran is **not FDA-approved for the cardiac ATTR-CM indication in the United States** as of 2025. Per [[berk2025patisiran]] (Study Limitations, an analysis co-authored by APOLLO-B trial investigators and Alnylam Pharmaceuticals staff, accepted May 2025): "patisiran is not approved for the treatment of ATTR-CM in the United States but is approved in Brazil and France, where, in the latter case, patisiran is approved for compassionate use in patients failing tafamidis 61 mg." (source: [[berk2025patisiran]])

This resolves the contradiction previously flagged on this page and in [[masri2026attrcm]]: the masri2026attrcm Discussion-section statement — "Currently, tafamidis, acoramidis, and vutrisiran are the only approved therapies available for the treatment of ATTR-CM," omitting patisiran — was correct. The masri2026attrcm Class 2 bullet claiming patisiran's "FDA label expanded for cardiac indication" was in error and has been corrected on that page (source: [[berk2025patisiran]]).

Note: patisiran remains approved in the US for the polyneuropathy indication (hATTR amyloidosis with polyneuropathy, ONPATTRO, approved 2018) — this status concerns the cardiac/ATTR-CM indication specifically, which is what APOLLO-B evaluated. Brazil/France cardiac-indication approval status per [[berk2025patisiran]] is stated without further detail on scope or date in the source paper.
