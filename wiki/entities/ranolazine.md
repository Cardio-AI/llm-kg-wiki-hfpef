# Ranolazine

> Selective inhibitor of the late cardiac sodium current (late I_Na); RALI-DHF (N=20, Phase 2 crossover) reduced invasively measured exercise LVEDP by 4.7 mmHg (P=0.001) in HFpEF, providing proof-of-concept for a Ca²⁺-dysregulation therapeutic pathway distinct from the failed NO/cGMP axis — but no functional exercise-capacity benefit was detected and no Phase 3 confirmatory trial has been conducted.

## Description

Ranolazine selectively inhibits the late cardiac sodium current (late I_Na) in cardiomyocytes. In HFpEF, as in HFrEF, late I_Na is upregulated, leading to excessive diastolic intracellular Na⁺ accumulation. Elevated intracellular [Na⁺] reduces Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger (NCX)-mediated Ca²⁺ extrusion (or drives NCX into reverse mode, promoting Ca²⁺ entry), raising diastolic intracellular Ca²⁺. Elevated diastolic Ca²⁺ maintains myofilament Ca²⁺ sensitisation, impairing active myocardial relaxation (lusitropy) and elevating left ventricular end-diastolic pressure (LVEDP). Ranolazine blocks late I_Na, lowering intracellular Na⁺, normalising NCX activity, reducing diastolic Ca²⁺, and improving relaxation. (source: maier2013ralidhf)

This mechanism is distinct from the NO/cGMP/PKG pathway targeted by nitrates, PDE5 inhibitors, and sGC stimulators (all of which failed in HFpEF — see NEAT-HFpEF, INDIE-HFpEF, RELAX, VITALITY-HFpEF, CAPACITY-HFpEF). Ranolazine instead addresses the calcium-dysregulation arm of diastolic dysfunction. (source: maier2013ralidhf)

## Role in HFpEF

**RALI-DHF (Ranolazine in the Treatment of Diastolic Heart Failure)** is the sole dedicated HFpEF trial of ranolazine: a Phase 2, single-centre, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover proof-of-concept study (N=20; LVEF ≥50%; invasively confirmed elevated LVEDP: >12 mmHg at rest or >15 mmHg with exercise). Ranolazine was given as an IV loading dose followed by oral 500→750 mg twice daily for 2 weeks per arm, with a 2-week washout between crossover periods. (source: maier2013ralidhf)

The primary endpoint — invasively measured LVEDP during supine exercise (20W, LV micromanometer) — was significantly reduced by ranolazine: −4.7 mmHg (95% CI −6.7 to −2.7; P=0.001). Resting LVEDP was also reduced (−3.1 mmHg; P<0.001), NT-proBNP fell, and the mitral E/A ratio improved (a better diastolic filling pattern on echocardiography). However, peak VO₂ did not change significantly — the haemodynamic signal did not translate into a detectable functional exercise-capacity benefit in this small trial. (source: maier2013ralidhf)

D'Amario 2019 situates RALI-DHF within the broader coronary microvascular dysfunction / calcium-overload mechanism: late Na⁺ current upregulation and consequent Ca²⁺ overload are described as facilitated by RAAS activation and strictly linked to fibrotic remodelling, positioning ranolazine as a therapy targeting a specific, mechanistically defined subset of HFpEF pathophysiology (distinct from the NO-deficiency/CMD-driven fibrotic pathway that dominates most other proposed HFpEF therapeutics in the same review). (source: damario2019cmd)

## Evidence

**RALI-DHF (Maier 2013, N=20, crossover, 2-week treatment periods):**

| Endpoint | Result | P |
|---|---|---|
| Exercise LVEDP (primary) | −4.7 mmHg (95% CI −6.7 to −2.7) | 0.001 |
| Resting LVEDP | −3.1 mmHg | <0.001 |
| NT-proBNP | Reduced | — |
| Mitral E/A ratio | Improved | — |
| Peak VO₂ | No significant change | NS |

(source: maier2013ralidhf)

No large Phase 3 RCT has followed RALI-DHF; it remains the only clinical HFpEF evidence for ranolazine to date. (source: maier2013ralidhf)

## Status

No guideline recommendation for ranolazine in HFpEF. The drug's only cardiology indication in current practice is chronic stable angina (outside the HFpEF-specific evidence base summarised here). RALI-DHF's proof-of-concept haemodynamic signal has not been followed by a confirmatory outcomes trial. (source: maier2013ralidhf)

## Contradictions
- RALI-DHF shows a significant haemodynamic effect (exercise LVEDP −4.7 mmHg, P=0.001) but no significant functional benefit (peak VO₂ unchanged) — an invasive-haemodynamic-vs-functional-capacity dissociation seen elsewhere in HFpEF pharmacotherapy trials.
- The positive small-sample haemodynamic signal contrasts with the uniformly negative NO/cGMP pathway trials (RELAX, NEAT-HFpEF, INDIE-HFpEF, VITALITY-HFpEF, CAPACITY-HFpEF) tested in comparably or more rigorously powered designs — suggesting HFpEF pathophysiology is mechanistically heterogeneous, with the Ca²⁺/late I_Na pathway a distinct (and so far unconfirmed) target. This is not a contradiction within the ranolazine evidence itself but a notable asymmetry in how far each mechanistic hypothesis has been tested.
