# HFpEF

> Clinical syndrome of heart failure with LVEF ≥50%, elevated LV filling pressures, and a high comorbidity burden; no proven disease-modifying therapy across the full population.

## Description

HFpEF requires all three of:
1. Symptoms ± signs of heart failure
2. LVEF ≥50%
3. Objective evidence of structural/functional cardiac abnormality consistent with LV [[diastolic-dysfunction]] / raised LV filling pressures (raised [[natriuretic-peptides]] or echocardiographic/haemodynamic markers)

(source: McDonagh2021ESC)

The LVEF ≥50% cut-off is acknowledged as arbitrary. LVEF is a continuous variable; its measurement by [[echocardiography]] carries substantial variability across sex, age, and imaging modality. Patients with very high EF (>65–70%) should prompt investigation for cardiac amyloidosis or HCM. (source: McDonagh2021ESC)

Patients who previously had HFrEF and recovered LVEF to ≥50% should be considered 'HF with improved LVEF' rather than HFpEF; HFrEF treatment should be continued. (source: McDonagh2021ESC)

## Epidemiology and Clinical Profile

HFpEF accounts for 16–47% of HF patients depending on registry, setting, and region (ESC-HF-LT outpatient 16%; GWTG-HF inpatient 47%; Swedish HF Registry 23%; G-CHF global 24%). The wide range reflects diagnostic heterogeneity. HFpEF prevalence is **steadily increasing** and may become the most common HF form; HFrEF prevalence is declining. (source: Savarese2022GlobalBurden)

Patients are older, more often female, and carry more [[atrial-fibrillation|AF]], CKD, and non-CV comorbidities than [[hfref]] or [[hfmref]] patients. Prognosis by 1-year mortality is modestly better than HFrEF (6.3% vs. 8.8%, ESC-HF-LT), but **5-year mortality is comparable** (HFpEF 75.7% vs. HFrEF 75.3%, GWTG-HF). The apparent short-term advantage is offset by higher non-CV mortality in HFpEF (30.7% vs. 20.1% at 1 year). **63% of HFpEF hospitalisations are non-CV** (Olmsted County). (source: Savarese2022GlobalBurden)

Core mechanism: [[diastolic-dysfunction]] — impaired LV relaxation and increased myocardial stiffness → elevated filling pressures → pulmonary congestion and [[exercise-intolerance]]. HFpEF is pathophysiologically heterogeneous; underlying phenotypes include hypertensive heart disease, obesity-related HF, infiltrative cardiomyopathy (amyloid), and others. Treatment of the underlying phenotype may improve outcomes. (source: McDonagh2021ESC)

**Cellular mechanisms (cGMP/PKG paradigm):** Comorbidities (obesity, HTN, metabolic syndrome) → low-grade systemic inflammation → impaired endothelial NO formation → reduced cGMP → reduced PKG (protein kinase G) activity → increased passive myocyte stiffness. Supported by human LV biopsy data demonstrating reduced PKG activity and cGMP concentrations correlating with increased passive myocyte stiffness. Titin is the major cellular determinant of stiffness and can be dynamically modulated by phosphorylation — a potential target. This paradigm explains RAAS trial failures (mechanism mismatch): RAAS blockade addresses neurohormonal activation (central to [[hfref]]) but not the inflammation-NO-cGMP-stiffness pathway. (source: Pfeffer2019HFpEF)

**Cardiac amyloidosis subphenotype:** Wild-type ATTR cardiac amyloidosis is present in **13–19% of persons with prevalent HFpEF**. This subset has pathophysiologically distinct HF and responds to tafamidis/patisiran/inotersen — the first proof-of-concept for mechanistically targeted phenotype therapy within the HFpEF umbrella. ⁹⁹ᵐTc-pyrophosphate SPECT is the noninvasive screen of choice. Patients with LVEF >65% and unexplained LVH should be screened. (source: Pfeffer2019HFpEF)

**Physiologic HFpEF (HFpEF_phys):** Defined by invasive [[cardiopulmonary-exercise-testing|CPET]] as PCWP ≥15 mmHg at rest or ≥25 mmHg during exercise. In a referred cohort (n=461, LVEF ≥50%, NYHA II–IV), 53% met HFpEF_phys criteria. HFpEF_phys independently predicts CV events (HR 1.62, p=0.01) regardless of guideline classification. (source: Ho2019HFpEFDefinitions)

The HFA/ESC 2023 consensus (Anker 2023) provides population-level prevalence estimates for the most clinically important HFpEF comorbidity phenotypes. These frame the phenotype-guided treatment approach described in [[hfpef-phenotype-profiling]].

|---|---|

| Elderly >65 years | 60–70% |

| Chronotropic incompetence | 30–50% |

| Sleep apnoea | 20–50% |

## Evidence

### Diagnostic Markers
Full marker table with sensitivities/specificities: → [[hfpef-diagnosis]] (canonical). Key threshold summary: E/e' >9 (sens 78%, spec 59%), LA volume index >34 mL/m², NT-proBNP >125 pg/mL (SR) or >365 pg/mL (AF). More abnormalities = higher probability. (source: McDonagh2021ESC)

### Trial Landscape
HFpEF represents the core challenge described in [[hfpef-treatment-gap]]. No large RCT has convincingly reduced mortality or morbidity across the full HFpEF population. Key trials: [[charm-preserved]], [[i-preserve]], [[topcat]], [[paragon-hf]].

## Status

| Recommendation | ESC 2021 | AHA 2022 |
|---|---|---|
| Diuretics (congestion) | Class I, C | Class I, C |
| Comorbidity treatment | Class I, C | Class I, C |
| SGLT2i | No recommendation | **Class 2a, B-R** |
| MRA (spironolactone) | No recommendation | Class 2b, B-R |
| Sacubitril/valsartan | No recommendation | Class 2b, B-R |
| Exercise training | Class I (HFrEF) | Class I, A (EF-agnostic) |

(source: McDonagh2021ESC; source: Heidenreich2022AHA)

**SGLT2i are the first pharmacological therapy with a guideline recommendation above Class 2b for HFpEF** (AHA 2022 Class 2a, based on EMPEROR-Preserved + DELIVER). Benefit is driven by HF hospitalization reduction; CV mortality was not significantly reduced. **The mortality gap in HFpEF persists.** See [[hfpef-treatment-gap]] and [[guideline-comparison]] for full analysis.

**Exercise training** ([[supervised-exercise-training]]) remains the most consistently positive intervention for functional outcomes: peak VO2 +2.8 mL/kg/min (8 RCTs, n=503). (source: Sachdev2023Exercise)
