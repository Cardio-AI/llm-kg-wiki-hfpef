# Overview (HFpEF Wiki)

## High-Level Summary

HFpEF (heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, LVEF ≥50%) now accounts for approximately 40–53% of incident HF and is the fastest-growing HF phenotype. The wiki covers the full arc from the 2003 CHARM-Preserved trial through the 2023 phenotype-profiling consensus, plus seven mechanistic/review sources that provide pathophysiological depth.

**The treatment gap is substantially narrowed but not closed.** SGLT2i (dapagliflozin/empagliflozin) now carry **Class I, Level A (ESC 2023)** and Class 2a, B-R (AHA 2022) recommendations for HFpEF; this benefit is hospitalisation-driven, not CV mortality. FINEARTS-HF (2024) added finerenone (non-steroidal MRA) as the second positive pharmacological trial (RR 0.84; P=0.007 for total events). Most importantly, **SUMMIT (tirzepatide, 2025)** demonstrated **38% reduction in CV death and worsening HF** (HR 0.62; 95% CI 0.41–0.95; P=0.026) in obese HFpEF — the first event endpoint reduction with a GLP-1RA class agent in HFpEF. Together with STEP-HFpEF (semaglutide, 2023), this establishes GLP-1RA/dual GLP-1/GIP agonism as evidence-based therapy for the obese-HFpEF phenotype (~50–60% of HFpEF). The two-layer treatment model (Anker 2023; Kittleson 2023) — SGLT2i universal + phenotype-guided add-on — now has its first phenotype-specific event-reduction evidence from SUMMIT.

**Mechanistically**, the field has shifted from a pure "pressure overload → diastolic dysfunction" model to a multiscale paradigm: comorbidity-driven systemic inflammation → coronary microvascular dysfunction (CMD) → endothelial-mesenchymal transition (EndoMT) → myocardial fibrosis → ↓cGMP/PKG → titin hypophosphorylation and isoform shift (N2BA→N2B) → elevated filling pressures. Separately, peripheral skeletal muscle myopathy accounts for >50% of the exercise VO₂ deficit — greater than the cardiac contribution.

**Supervised exercise training (SET)** remains the most consistently effective HFpEF intervention for functional outcomes (peak VO₂ +2.8 mL/kg/min, Class I AHA 2022), though hard outcome data are absent. ~1/3 of patients are non-responders.

See [[guideline-comparison]] for the ESC 2021 / ESC 2023 / AHA 2022 / ACC 2023 contrast. See [[hfpef-treatment-gap]] for the full failed-trial catalogue and emerging therapies.

## Key Concepts

- **[[hf-phenotype-classification]]**: EF-based four-way classification (HFrEF/HFmrEF/HFpEF/HFimpEF); LVEF cut-offs are pragmatic, not mechanistic; AHA 2022 added HFimpEF; 7-registry distribution shows 16–47% HFpEF depending on setting
- **[[hfpef-diagnosis]]**: Three criteria (symptoms + LVEF ≥50% + elevated filling pressures); echocardiographic markers + NPs first-line; HFA-PEFF (ESC) and H₂FPEF (AHA) algorithms; 9 epidemiological definitions used 1997–2015 with thresholds from LVEF >40% to >55%; gold standard is invasive PCWP
- **[[hfpef-diagnostic-definitions]]**: 7 competing definitions enroll 12–90% of same cohort; only 53% have invasively confirmed HFpEF_phys; HFpEF_phys independently predicts CV events (HR 1.62); trial populations are not interchangeable
- **[[diastolic-dysfunction]]**: Core haemodynamic mechanism; cellular drivers now include cGMP/PKG pathway, titin N2BA→N2B isoform shift, EndoMT-driven fibrosis, and calcium overload via late Na⁺ currents; E/e' >9 (sens 78%, spec 59%) + LA volume index + TR velocity >2.8 m/s; confirmed invasively by PCWP
- **[[exercise-intolerance]]**: Dominant HFpEF symptom; primary mechanism is skeletal muscle myopathy (A-VO₂ diff >50% of VO₂ reduction); cardiac (chronotropic incompetence ~50%), pulmonary, and peripheral vascular contributions secondary; SET reverses skeletal muscle myopathy; SECRET trial: exercise + caloric restriction additive. **Obese HFpEF extension:** Dynamic hyperinflation occurs in 62–85% of obese HFpEF at exercise (Leahy 2025); associated with higher PCWP (ΔEELV correlates with ΔPCWP, r²=0.167); Babb 2026 NTG crossover demonstrated that lowering PCWP does not remove the ventilatory ceiling — exercise is ventilatory-limited in obese HFpEF. This adds a ventilatory layer to the established skeletal muscle + cardiac model.
- **[[natriuretic-peptides]]**: BNP/NT-proBNP; key diagnostic biomarkers; limited sensitivity in obesity; AF-adjusted thresholds required; EMPEROR-Preserved used NP ≥300 (SR) or ≥900 (AF) pg/mL for enrichment
- **[[hfpef-treatment-gap]]**: No pharmacological RCT has convincingly reduced HFpEF mortality across full population; SGLT2i Class I ESC 2023 / Class 2a AHA 2022 — first pharmacological Class I HFpEF recommendation; CHAMPION trial (haemodynamic monitoring) reduces HF hosp; SET fills functional gap; mortality gap persists
- **[[hfpef-phenotype-profiling]]**: Two-layer model (Anker 2023; Kittleson 2023): SGLT2i universal + phenotype-guided add-on; 18 comorbidity phenotypes with prevalences; LVEF 50–55% and >65% subgroups; CMD 71–91% (PROMIS-HFpEF); cancer HFpEF; iron deficiency 50–75%
- **[[guideline-comparison]]**: ESC 2021 / ESC 2023 / AHA 2022 / ACC 2023 ECDP on HFpEF: definition, EF taxonomy, staging, diagnostics, pharmacotherapy; SGLT2i Class I (ESC 2023) vs. Class 2a (AHA 2022); sex-stratified ARNI/MRA (Kittleson 2023)
- **[[ml-ai-hfpef]]**: 38 AI/ML studies (Yi 2025): ECG-AI AUC 0.87; NLP identifies 75.4% undiagnosed HFpEF in EHR; ML predicts spironolactone responders (reframes TOPCAT as enrichment failure); 3–4 phenogroups consistent across cohorts
- **[[atrial-fibrillation]]**: Major comorbidity; bidirectional relationship with elevated LA pressure; adjusted diagnostic thresholds; CABA-HFpEF (Phase III AF ablation vs. rate control in HFpEF) ongoing
- **[[supervised-exercise-training]]**: Class I, Level A (AHA/ACC 2022); peak VO₂ +2.8 mL/kg/min; most consistently positive HFpEF intervention; hard outcomes unknown; ~1/3 non-responders (Mirzai 2025); four modalities: MCT (best evidence), HIIT (not superior to MCT per [[optimex-clin]], N=180, gains not sustained at 12 months), combined resistance/aerobic (+5.3 mL/kg/min in 12-month trial), and IMT (Palau 2014: +2.9 mL/kg/min; home delivery possible); post-hospitalisation multidomain rehabilitation ([[rehab-hf]], REHAB-HF, N=349) improves SPPB and 6MWD in frail post-AHF patients with HFpEF — a distinct paradigm from outpatient VO₂-focused trials

## Active Debates

- **LVEF threshold**: ≥50% is arbitrary; EACVI uses sex-specific cut-offs; a continuous EF distribution makes the label imprecise at the margins
- **TOPCAT controversy**: Americas subgroup suggests MRA benefit; non-Americas data suspected contaminated (urinary metabolites near-zero in controls, 4× lower event rate); AHA 2022 Class 2b based on Americas data alone; SPIRIT-HF and SPIRRIT ongoing to resolve
- **Sacubitril/valsartan in HFpEF**: FDA endorsed for LVEF "below normal"; AHA 2022 Class 2b; ESC 2021 no recommendation; ACC 2023 ECDP sex-stratified (PARAGON HR 0.73 women vs. 1.03 men); real or driven by HFmrEF subgroup?
- **SGLT2i: hospitalisation vs. mortality benefit**: AHA 2022 Class 2a based on hospitalisation reduction — CV mortality not significantly reduced; partial gap closure only
- **DETERMINE HFpEF vs. DELIVER KCCQ discordance**: DELIVER showed KCCQ benefit (win ratio 1.11, P=0.009) at 8 months; DETERMINE-Preserved missed KCCQ at 16 weeks (P=0.079); duration vs. method vs. population explanations all plausible. See [[contradictions]] #16
- **SET hard outcomes**: Exercise training improves VO₂ consistently but effect on mortality/hospitalisation unknown; individual trials underpowered; ~1/3 non-responders (Mirzai 2025)
- **HIIT vs. MCT**: HIIT not superior to MCT in HFpEF (OptimEx-Clin, N=180, definitive); DonelliDaSilveira 2020 (N=19) showed HIIT superiority but was contradicted — likely a false positive; no identified HFpEF subgroup definitively benefits from HIIT over MCT
- **Beta-blockers in HFpEF**: Kaddoura 2024 meta-analysis (16 observational studies; N=27,188) reports OR 0.81 (P=0.044) for all-cause mortality — directional signal but the entire evidence base is observational; confounding by indication (sicker patients less likely to receive beta-blockers) is a major limitation; no large dedicated RCT exists; EF-stratified subgroup data from existing trials are inconsistent
- **Ventilatory vs. cardiac limitation in obese HFpEF**: Leahy 2025 + Babb 2026 (NCT04068844): dynamic hyperinflation (DH) occurs in 62–85% of obese HFpEF; DH elevates measured PCWP (ventilatory mechanism); reducing PCWP with NTG does not improve exercise capacity; patients stop at the same mechanical ventilatory ceiling before and after NTG. This challenges the cardiac-centric model of HFpEF exercise limitation. Implications: (a) elevated exercise PCWP in obese HFpEF is diagnostically confounded; (b) haemodynamic targets alone may be insufficient; (c) respiratory/weight-focused interventions (OSA treatment, weight loss, breathing training) may be necessary. Actively debated — whether this is exclusive to obese HFpEF or extends to non-obese phenotypes is unknown.
- **Statins in non-IHD HFpEF**: Ortega-Hernández 2024 RICA registry (N=2,788): statin HR 0.74 (P=0.002); benefit restricted to patients without IHD (HR 0.69; P<0.001). Observational data; consistent with TOPCAT post-hoc (Kjekshus 2009 meta-analysis). Mechanistic rationale: anti-inflammatory, EAT-reducing, PKG-activating pleiotropic effects — all relevant in non-ischaemic HFpEF. No RCT designed specifically for this question. Whether statins should be prescribed in HFpEF for pleiotropic benefit beyond lipid-lowering is clinically unresolved.
- **EAT and pericardial restraint as therapeutic target**: Timóteo 2024 review + Zamani 2023 (NCT04068844): epicardial fat physically compresses the LV (eccentricity index r=0.88–0.91); paracrine dysfunction via TNF-α/IL-1β → fibrosis + AF. SGLT2i, GLP-1RA, and statins all reduce EAT. Whether pericardial restraint can be clinically modified non-surgically (pericardiotomy too invasive) is the open question.
- **NP-guided therapy in HFpEF**: Horiuchi 2022 review confirms NP-guided GDMT titration benefits HFrEF patients <75y but not HFpEF; TIME-CHF HFpEF subgroup trended worse; GUIDE-IT neutral in HFpEF-inclusive populations. NP thresholds in HFpEF have low sensitivity (20–35% of confirmed HFpEF have normal BNP) — particularly in Black patients (Ilonze 2024). NP-guided management paradigm does not translate from HFrEF to HFpEF.
- **Sleep-disordered breathing as HFpEF contributor**: Wester 2023 and Cowie 2017 review SDB prevalence 58–80% in HFpEF; CaMKII pathway links intermittent hypoxia to diastolic dysfunction and AF; Suzuki 2018 shows SDB predicts arterial stiffness in HFpEF specifically (not HFrEF). Whether treating SDB (CPAP/BiPAP) improves HFpEF outcomes is unresolved — SERVE-HF (HFrEF+CSA) showed harm with ASV; no equivalent HFpEF-powered trial completed.
- **CMD as primary vs. secondary**: Microvascular hypothesis (Graziani/Crea, D'Amario 2019) positions CMD as "common soil"; traditional view positions it as consequence of elevated filling pressures — the causal direction is still debated
- **Statins in HFpEF**: Observational and meta-analytic signals for benefit (JASPER study; ↑PKG activity, ↓cardiomyocyte hypertrophy in biopsy data); no large RCT — causal inference not established
- **Diagnostic algorithm choice (HFA-PEFF vs. H₂FPEF)**: Different variables; concordance in borderline patients imperfect; needs prospective head-to-head validation
- **6MWD as HFpEF endpoint**: DETERMINE showed 6MWD neutral even when KCCQ improved in HFrEF; accelerometry and 6MWD measure different dimensions (Docherty 2025)
- **Serum Mg and SGLT2i**: Ferreira 2026 (EMPEROR-Preserved): higher Mg → more empagliflozin benefit in HFpEF; opposite to HFrEF direction — mechanism and clinical implication uncharacterised
- **HFmrEF identity**: Recovering HFrEF vs. declining HFpEF vs. distinct phenotype? No dedicated RCT evidence

## Knowledge Gaps

- **SGLT2i mortality gap**: EMPEROR-Preserved + DELIVER benefit was hospitalisation-driven; CV mortality not significantly reduced — core HFpEF mortality gap persists
- **FINEARTS-HF / SPIRIT-HF outcomes**: Will finerenone (non-steroidal MRA) or spironolactone in properly enrolled HFpEF finally resolve the MRA question left open by TOPCAT? Results pending.
- **SUMMIT published (2025) — first event reduction with GLP-1RA class in HFpEF**: STEP-HFpEF (Kosiborod 2023, N=529; semaglutide) confirmed symptomatic benefit in obese HFpEF (KCCQ-CSS +7.8 pts, body weight −10.7 pp; win ratio 1.72; all P<0.001) but was not powered for hard outcomes. **SUMMIT (Packer 2025, N=731; tirzepatide, Eli Lilly; NCT04847557)** confirmed event reduction: HR 0.62 (95% CI 0.41–0.95; P=0.026) for CV death/worsening HF; KCCQ-CSS +6.9 pts (P<0.001); body weight −13–15%. CMR substudy: LV mass −11 g (P=0.004); paracardiac fat −45 mL (P<0.001). Whether event reduction is tirzepatide-specific (dual GIP/GLP-1 agonism) or class-wide (any GLP-1RA) is unresolved.
- **CABA-HFpEF**: Will catheter ablation vs. rate control in HFpEF+AF reduce outcomes? First dedicated HFpEF-AF trial.
- **Trial-to-real-world generalisability gap**: Landsteiner 2025 (n=872, HC-HFpEF) showed trial NT-proBNP thresholds exclude 67–71% of hemodynamically confirmed HFpEF from PARAGON-HF, EMPEROR-Preserved, and FINEARTS-HF; trial-enrolled patients are sicker and more severely affected than the broader HC-HFpEF population. Whether SGLT2i, finerenone, and other proven therapies benefit the excluded 67–83% of HC-HFpEF is an open question.
- **Exercise hemodynamics standardisation**: Supine (Borlaug 2010 threshold: PCWP ≥25 mmHg) and upright (Landsteiner 2025: PCWP/CO slope >2 mmHg/L/min) exercise protocols yield different absolute PCWP values and different patient classifications — protocol harmonisation required for cross-study comparison.
- **SET hard outcomes**: Effect of exercise training on mortality and HF hospitalisation in HFpEF unknown; individual trials underpowered; ~1/3 non-responders; dedicated powered RCT needed
- **HIIT optimal population**: HIIT not superior to MICT overall (OPTIMEX-CLIN, N=180, HIIT=MCT at 3 and 12 months); which patient subgroup (if any) benefits specifically from HIIT requires further phenotyping
- **IMT in broader HFpEF phenotypes**: Palau 2014 (+2.9 mL/kg/min) and Kinugasa 2020 show IMT comparable to MCT with home-delivery advantage; efficacy across HFpEF phenotypes (non-obese, non-respiratory-limited) unestablished; no large multicentre RCT
- **Dietary intervention evidence gap**: Caloric restriction reduces VO₂ deficit via peripheral fat/skeletal muscle pathway (SECRET trial); DASH + MedDiet have mechanistic rationale; sodium restriction evidence weak in HFpEF (SODIUM-HF predominantly HFrEF); no large HFpEF-specific diet RCT completed; UFA-Preserved 2 (omega-3, NCT03966755) and SECRET-II (NCT02636439) ongoing
- **CMD as therapeutic target**: PROMIS-HFpEF shows 91% CMD prevalence; statins, anti-IL-1 (anakinra), ranolazine, nintedanib, and PDE-9 inhibitors have mechanistic rationale but no large RCT evidence in HFpEF
- **EndoMT targeting**: EndoMT is now recognised as the primary fibrosis driver in HFpEF; no clinical-stage therapy specifically targets EndoMT; nintedanib/pirfenidone (anti-fibrotic/IPF drugs) are the closest candidates
- **Statins RCT**: Observational and meta-analytic signals are consistent but no large RCT has tested statins prospectively in HFpEF — the strongest unresolved pharmacological open question given biopsy evidence of PKG/nitrotyrosine benefit
- **SGLT2i symptom benefit duration**: DETERMINE-Preserved missed all primary KCCQ/6MWD endpoints at 16 weeks; DELIVER showed benefit at 8 months — whether longer follow-up resolves the symptom gap is unclear
- **Iron deficiency correction**: FAIR-HFpEF (von Haehling 2024, Eur Heart J; N=40, stopped early at 20% of target N): IV FCM vs. placebo in HFpEF with iron deficiency; 6MWT difference +49 m at week 24 (P=0.029) and +65 m vs. −8 m at week 32; directionally positive with significant SAE reduction (rate ratio 0.27; P=0.043). Underpowered; requires confirmation in a larger powered trial. ID prevalence in HFpEF is 59% (Beale 2019 meta-analysis, N=1,877).
- **Diagnostic algorithm choice (HFA-PEFF vs. H₂FPEF)**: Concordance in borderline patients imperfect; no prospective head-to-head validation
- **Mg-SGLT2i biology**: Does the Mg-treatment interaction in EMPEROR-Preserved represent causal biology or statistical artefact? Is Mg supplementation a therapeutic target?
- **Cancer HFpEF**: Doxorubicin → diastolic dysfunction 60% at 1y, 80% at 3y; excluded from all major trials; SGLT2i safety in this subgroup unknown
- **ESC 2025 full update**: ESC 2023 Focused Update already upgraded SGLT2i to Class I for HFpEF; the upcoming ESC 2025 full revision may formally incorporate the two-layer phenotype model and incorporate FINEARTS-HF, SUMMIT/STEP-HFpEF, and SPIRIT-HF results
- **Sex differences**: PARAGON-HF sex-stratified signal; ACC 2023 ECDP now sex-stratifies ARNI/MRA recommendations; sex-specific LVEF thresholds remain exploratory
- **6MWD as HFpEF trial endpoint**: DETERMINE showed 6MWD neutral even when KCCQ improved; accelerometry (Docherty 2025) and 6MWD measure different dimensions — optimal functional outcome measure undefined
- **HFmrEF-specific therapy**: No prospective RCT in HFmrEF; all evidence from subgroups of HFrEF and HFpEF trials
- **Tissue endotype heterogeneity as drug target**: Fayyaz 2025 (Nat Rev Cardiol; 56 human myocardial tissue studies) formalised 8 HFpEF pathways — fibrosis, cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, microvascular rarefaction, diastolic dysfunction (titin/SERCA2a/T-tubule), metabolic derangements (↓ATP/NAD⁺), inflammation/oxidative stress, cGMP-PKG impairment, ER stress/DNA damage. These are differentially expressed across patients depending on comorbidities (obesity, T2DM, hypertension). No single drug addresses all 8; phenotype-guided multi-pathway approaches are the rational next step — but no prospective tissue-guided trial has been conducted in HFpEF.
- **Post-hospitalisation HFpEF rehabilitation**: REHAB-HF (N=349, any EF) showed functional benefit (SPPB, 6MWD) but no rehospitalisation reduction; HFpEF subgroup showed larger gains. A dedicated powered HFpEF-only post-hospitalisation rehabilitation RCT (REHAB-HFpEF, NCT05525663) is ongoing — the critical question is whether functional gains translate to hard outcomes in this population.
- **Beta-blockers in HFpEF RCT gap**: Kaddoura 2024 pooled observational data (N=27,188) shows OR 0.81 (P=0.044) for mortality, but all evidence is observational and confounding by indication is probable. No large dedicated RCT. Whether this represents a true treatment effect or healthy prescribing bias is unresolved.
- **Inflammatory marker-guided therapy**: Fu 2024 (8 cohort studies; I²=0%) demonstrates consistent adverse prognostic impact of NLR, PLR, hs-CRP, WBC across HFpEF outcomes (CV mortality HR 2.04; CV rehospitalisation HR 2.83). Whether inflammation-guided patient selection improves trial yield for anti-inflammatory agents (anakinra, colchicine) in HFpEF is untested.
- **Ex-DHF primary endpoint failure mechanism**: Ex-DHF (Nat Med 2025; n=322; 12-month combined exercise training) improved VO₂ (+1.3 mL/kg/min; P=0.003) and NYHA class (OR 5.89; P<0.001) but missed the Packer composite primary (P=0.17). Whether this reflects: (a) insufficient adherence (~53%), (b) Packer composite insensitivity to the VO₂/NYHA gains achieved, or (c) a genuinely null effect on composite outcomes — is unresolved. Adequately powered event-driven exercise trials remain the critical missing evidence.
- **AF-HFpEF diagnostic challenge**: Reddy 2024 shows exercise RHC is the only reliable HFpEF diagnostic method in AF patients — resting biomarkers and echo scores are confounded by AF. As AF prevalence in HFpEF approaches 82% at 1 year, the standard diagnostic workup becomes unreliable in the majority of real-world HFpEF patients. Diagnostic algorithm redesign for AF-HFpEF is an unmet need.
- **Racial/ethnic disparities in diagnosis and treatment**: Ilonze 2024 review: Black patients have lowest NP levels in confirmed HFpEF (20–35% have BNP <100 pg/mL with elevated PCWP); H2FPEF score systematically underdiagnoses Black patients; ATTR V122I variant is present in 3.43% of Black Americans ≥60y and must be excluded. SGLT2i and GLP-1RA are underutilised in Black, Hispanic, and low-income patients. Kittleson 2024 ACC/AHA QM-2 (SDOH screening) directly addresses this gap. Current diagnostic algorithms validated predominantly in white European populations — generalisation may be limited.
- **Ventilatory limitation in obese HFpEF — therapeutic implication gap**: Babb 2026 NTG RCT shows that reducing PCWP alone does not increase exercise capacity in obese HFpEF. The ventilatory ceiling (dynamic hyperinflation + EFL) is the binding constraint. No randomised trial has specifically tested whether treating the ventilatory component (weight loss to ≥10%, PAP therapy for SDB, inspiratory muscle training) improves exercise capacity in obese HFpEF. This is a new, high-priority research gap emerging from the NCT04068844 mechanistic programme.
