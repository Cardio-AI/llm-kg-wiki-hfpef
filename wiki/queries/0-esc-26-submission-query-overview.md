# Question - 1
Which randomized trials demonstrated benefit of SGLT2 inhibitors in HFpEF?

---
## Answer
Two large event-driven RCTs constitute the primary evidence base, supplemented by supportive trial data and a class-effect meta-analysis. Trials are discussed in order of evidential weight.
### Pivotal event-driven trials
**EMPEROR-Preserved** (empagliflozin 10 mg; N=5,988; LVEF >40%; NYHA II–IV; median follow-up 26.2 months) [[Anker2021EMPEROR]]: The first major positive outcomes RCT in HFpEF. Primary composite of CV death or HF hospitalisation: HR **0.79** (95% CI 0.69–0.90; P<0.001). Driven primarily by a 29% reduction in HF hospitalisation (HR 0.71). CV death alone: HR 0.91 (NS). eGFR slope preserved (+1.36 mL/min/1.73m²/yr vs. placebo; P<0.001). KCCQ-CSS improved modestly (+1.32 pts, P<0.05). Benefit consistent regardless of T2DM status. A signal of attenuation at LVEF ≥65% was noted but not replicated in DELIVER.

**DELIVER** (dapagliflozin 10 mg; N=6,263; LVEF >40%; median follow-up 2.3 years) [[Solomon2022DELIVER]]: Primary composite of worsening HF (hospitalisation or urgent visit) or CV death: HR **0.82** (95% CI 0.73–0.92; P<0.001). Worsening HF alone: HR 0.79. CV death alone: HR 0.88 (NS). Total recurrent events rate ratio 0.77 (P<0.001). KCCQ-TSS win ratio 1.11 (P=0.009) — pre-specified hierarchical secondary. Benefit consistent across all LVEF strata including ≥60%, across diabetes status, sex, BMI, and AF status. No heterogeneity detected across 17 prespecified subgroups.

Together, these trials established a class effect across the EF spectrum. A pooled analysis (Vaduganathan et al., referenced in [[Solomon2022DELIVER]]) confirmed no heterogeneity by EF stratum.
### Supportive trial
**EMPULSE** (empagliflozin 10 mg; N=530; LVEF-unrestricted; acute HF; 90-day follow-up) [[Voors2022EMPULSE]]: Empagliflozin initiated in-hospital achieved a hierarchical win ratio of **1.36** (95% CI 1.09–1.68; P=0.0054) for the composite of all-cause death, HF events, and KCCQ-TSS improvement. In the HFpEF subgroup (LVEF >40%; 28–35% of cohort), win ratio was 1.39 (0.95–2.03) — directionally consistent but non-significant due to insufficient power. EMPULSE was not designed as a definitive HFpEF trial; its primary contribution is establishing safety and feasibility of in-hospital SGLT2i initiation across the EF spectrum.

### Trial that did not demonstrate primary endpoint benefit in HFpEF
**DETERMINE-Preserved** (dapagliflozin 10 mg; N=504; LVEF >40%; 16 weeks) [[McMurray2024DETERMINE]]: Three co-primary endpoints (KCCQ-TSS, KCCQ-PLS, 6MWD) all neutral. KCCQ-TSS +3.2 pts (P=0.079) — directional but non-significant. 6MWD +1.6 m (P=0.668). The discordance between DETERMINE-Preserved (neutral at 16 weeks) and DELIVER (positive at 2.3 years) likely reflects shorter follow-up, a symptom-and-function-focused design rather than event-driven endpoint, and different analytical approaches (mean change vs. win ratio). See [[contradictions]] #16.

### Class-effect meta-analysis
**Minisy 2025** [[minisy2025sglt2]]: 9 RCTs, >20,000 patients (CHIEF-HF, DEFINE-HF, DELIVER, DETERMINE-Preserved, EMPEROR-Pooled, EMPEROR-Preserved, EMPULSE, PRESERVED-HF, SOLOIST-WHF; HFpEF defined as LVEF ≥40%):

| Outcome | HR | 95% CI | GRADE |
|---|---|---|---|
| CV death or HHF | **0.83** | 0.76–0.90 | High |
| HHF alone | **0.75** | 0.68–0.84 | High |
| All-cause mortality | 0.92 | 0.85–1.01 (NS) | Low |
| KCCQ improvement | +1.8 pts | — | Moderate |

Moderate heterogeneity (I²=62%); no publication bias (Egger). Mortality neutrality attributed to phenotypic heterogeneity and competing non-CV death, not absence of biological effect. Note: CHIEF-HF, DEFINE-HF, PRESERVED-HF, and SOLOIST-WHF are referenced via this meta-analysis only — no dedicated wiki source pages exist for these four trials.

## Contradictions

- DETERMINE-Preserved (KCCQ-TSS P=0.079, neutral) vs. DELIVER (KCCQ win ratio P=0.009, positive): same drug, same population, different follow-up duration (16 weeks vs. 2.3 years) and endpoint design. See [[contradictions]] #16.
- CV mortality not significantly reduced in any individual HFpEF trial despite directionally favorable HRs (0.88–0.91). Contrasts with mortality benefit in HFrEF (DAPA-HF, EMPEROR-Reduced). See [[contradictions]] #9.
- EMPEROR-Preserved signal of attenuated benefit at LVEF ≥65% not replicated in DELIVER (HR 0.78 in LVEF ≥60% subgroup). See [[contradictions]].

# Question 2
What echocardiographic parameters are most commonly used to assess diastolic dysfunction?

---
## Answer
No single parameter is sufficient — diagnostic confidence scales with the number of abnormal findings [[McDonagh2021ESC]], [[upadhya2025echo]]. Parameters are grouped below by their role in guideline algorithms.
### Core parameters
**E/e' ratio** — the primary non-invasive index of LV filling pressure (LVFP). The E wave (mitral inflow early diastolic velocity, pulsed-wave Doppler) divided by e' (early diastolic mitral annular velocity, tissue Doppler imaging — averaged septal and lateral, or septal alone). Surrogates elevated LVEDP because e' reflects the rate of myocardial relaxation and is relatively load-independent [[McDonagh2021ESC]].

| Threshold | Sensitivity | Specificity | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| E/e' >9 | 78% | 59% | Minor criterion (HFA-PEFF); H₂FPEF component |
| E/e' 9–14 | — | — | Minor HFA-PEFF criterion (1 pt) |
| E/e' ≥15 | 46% | 86% | Major HFA-PEFF criterion (2 pts); high-specificity cut-off |
| E/e' ≥15 at peak stress | — | — | Confirmatory during exercise echo |

Limitation: not applicable in AF; influenced by loading conditions; odds ratio per unit increase 1.22 (95% CI 1.16–1.30) [[upadhya2025echo]]. Should not be used in isolation — specificity at the standard threshold is only 59% [[Ho2019HFpEFDefinitions]].

**Mitral annular e' velocity** (tissue Doppler) — reflects active LV relaxation. Used in E/e' calculation and as a standalone HFA-PEFF criterion: septal e' <7 cm/s OR lateral e' <10 cm/s = major functional criterion [[Pieske2019HFAPEFF]]. e' remains low throughout exercise in HFpEF, distinguishing it from non-cardiac dyspnoea.

**LA volume index (LAVi)** — the "haemoglobin A1c of diastolic function": reflects the cumulative burden of chronically elevated LV filling pressures [[upadhya2025echo]].

| Threshold | Rhythm | Criterion level |
|---|---|---|
| >34 mL/m² | SR | Major HFA-PEFF morphological; ESC 2021 Table 9 |
| 29–34 mL/m² | SR | Minor HFA-PEFF morphological |
| >40 mL/m² | AF | Major HFA-PEFF morphological |

Caveat: LAVi is misleading in obese patients — larger BSA mathematically reduces the indexed value despite absolutely increased LA volume. Minimum LAV (not maximum) better reflects chronic LVFP exposure than the conventionally used maximum value [[upadhya2025echo]].

**TR velocity** — tricuspid regurgitation peak velocity; Doppler estimate of RVSP and indirect marker of elevated LVFP-driven pulmonary hypertension.

| Threshold | Role |
|---|---|
| >2.8 m/s (PASP >35 mmHg) | Major HFA-PEFF functional criterion (2 pts); ESC 2021 Table 9; H₂FPEF component |
| >3.4 m/s at peak stress | Highest-level confirmatory exercise criterion (3 pts) if combined with E/e' ≥15 |

Limitation: TR signal technically inadequate in 30–40% of patients [[upadhya2025echo]].

---
### Structural parameters
**LV mass index (LVMi)** and **relative wall thickness (RWT)** — markers of hypertensive/pressure-overload remodelling.

| Threshold | Criterion level |
|---|---|
| LVMi ≥149 g/m² (M) / ≥122 g/m² (F) AND RWT >0.42 | Major HFA-PEFF morphological (2 pts) |
| LVMi ≥115 g/m² (M) / ≥95 g/m² (F) OR RWT >0.42 OR LV wall ≥12 mm | Minor HFA-PEFF (1 pt); ESC 2021 Table 9 |
Absence of LVH does not exclude HFpEF; 88% specific but only 26% sensitive [[upadhya2025echo]].

---
### Advanced parameters
**GLS (global longitudinal strain)** — speckle-tracking echocardiography measure of longitudinal myocardial deformation; detects subclinical systolic dysfunction concealed by preserved LVEF.

- GLS <−16% (i.e., absolute value <16%) = minor HFA-PEFF functional criterion (1 pt) [[Pieske2019HFAPEFF]]
- Load-dependent and angle-dependent; requires vendor-specific calibration

**LA reservoir strain (LASr)** — the most sensitive and specific non-invasive marker of LVFP, superior to TRV for LVFP estimation [[borlaug2023statement]], [[upadhya2025echo]].

- LASr <18%: added as a third substitute criterion in the 2016 ASE/EACVI algorithm → 99% patient classification rate
- LA stiffness index (E/e' / LASr): best TTE correlate of elevated filling pressures at rest and exercise; included in EACVI 2022 multimodality imaging recommendations
- Requires speckle-tracking software; replicability limited in routine practice

**E/A ratio and deceleration time** — traditional filling pattern classification (Grade I–III), now secondary to the four-parameter approach in the 2016 ASE/EACVI algorithm:

| Pattern | E/A | Grade | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Impaired relaxation | <0.8 | I | Mild DD; usually normal LVFP |
| Pseudonormal | 0.8–2.0 + abnormal E/e'/LAVi/TRV | II | Moderate DD; elevated LVFP |
| Restrictive | >2 | III | Severe DD; markedly elevated LVFP |

---
### Exercise echocardiography

Resting parameters can be normal in exercise-unmasked HFpEF (23–28% of haemodynamically confirmed HFpEF) [[landsteiner2025hemodynamics]]. Exercise-provoked changes:
- E/e' ≥15 at peak stress: highly demonstrative of HFpEF [[upadhya2025echo]]
- Exercise PASP ≥45 mmHg: 96% sensitivity and 95% specificity for LVFP elevation in exertional dyspnoea (AUC 0.99), outperforming all resting markers [[borlaug2010exercise]]
- LASr decreases with exercise in HFpEF (LA stiffness worsens under stress)

---
### Integration: diagnostic algorithms
Both major algorithms combine the parameters above into multi-domain scoring [[Pieske2019HFAPEFF]], [[Reddy2018H2FPEF]]:

**HFA-PEFF (ESC-endorsed):** functional + morphological + biomarker domains, 0–6 points. ≥5 pts = HFpEF likely; 2–4 pts = intermediate → stress testing or invasive evaluation; ≤1 pt = HFpEF unlikely. Sensitivity 70%, specificity 90.5% [[upadhya2025echo]].

**H₂FPEF (AHA 2022-endorsed):** 0–9 points; E/e' >9 (1 pt) and PA systolic >35 mmHg (1 pt) among six items. ≥6 = high probability; ≤1 = low; 2–5 = intermediate. Sensitivity 52.7%, specificity 82.5% [[upadhya2025echo]].

When either score is intermediate, invasive exercise haemodynamic testing (gold standard: PCWP ≥15 mmHg rest, ≥25 mmHg exercise, or PCWP/CO slope >2 mmHg/L/min for upright protocols) is recommended [[borlaug2023statement]].

## Contradictions
- **E/e' >9 specificity (59%):** adequate for screening but insufficient for diagnosis without additional markers [[Ho2019HFpEFDefinitions]]; should never be used in isolation.
- **LASr vs. TRV hierarchy:** [[upadhya2025echo]] argues LASr is superior to TRV for LVFP estimation; the original 2016 ASE/EACVI algorithm weights TRV equally with E/e' and LAVi. EACVI 2022 now includes LASr as a third substitute criterion — evolving, not yet fully resolved consensus. See [[contradictions]].
- **LAVi in obesity:** BSA-indexed LAV underestimates filling pressure burden in obese HFpEF; minimum LAV preferred [[upadhya2025echo]], but not yet standardised in guideline tables.

# Question 3
Do SGLT2 inhibitors benefit obese patients with HFpEF?

---
## Answer
SGLT2 inhibitors provide consistent benefit in obese HFpEF, with subgroup data from DELIVER suggesting a numerically larger effect in patients with BMI ≥30 than in non-obese patients, though no significant heterogeneity was demonstrated [[Solomon2022DELIVER]].

### Subgroup Evidence
**DELIVER (dapagliflozin 10 mg)** — prespecified BMI subgroup analysis [[Solomon2022DELIVER]]:

| BMI subgroup | HR (95% CI) | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| BMI <30 | 0.89 (0.75–1.04) | Non-significant |
| BMI ≥30 | **0.74 (0.63–0.88)** | Significant |
No statistically significant interaction across subgroups. The obese subgroup point estimate (0.74) numerically exceeds the overall trial HR (0.82). All subgroup analyses are underpowered — caution in over-interpreting the within-group estimates.

**EMPEROR-Preserved (empagliflozin 10 mg):** Overall HR 0.79 (0.69–0.90); benefit reported as consistent in the obese subgroup in the Anker 2023 phenotype profiling statement [[Anker2023HFpEFPhenotype]]. Published subgroup tables in the primary paper focus on T2DM status and LVEF strata rather than BMI.

**Class-effect meta-analysis** (9 RCTs, >20,000 patients) [[minisy2025sglt2]]: CV death or HHF HR 0.83 (0.76–0.90; GRADE high certainty); HHF alone HR 0.75 (0.68–0.84). No obesity-specific stratification reported, but the pooled estimate is robust.

### Mechanistic Relevance to Obesity Phenotype
SGLT2i address several obesity-specific HFpEF pathways [[Anker2023HFpEFPhenotype]], [[albulushi2025sglt2fibrosis]]:

- **Natriuresis and preload reduction** — reduces expanded plasma volume, a defining feature of obese HFpEF
- **Visceral/epicardial fat reduction** — directly relevant to pericardial restraint mechanism
- **Anti-inflammatory** — attenuates the adipokine-driven microvascular dysfunction cascade
- **Antifibrotic** — dapagliflozin reduced myocardial ECV by 3.5% vs. 0.8% over 12 months in HFpEF+T2DM (serial CMR RCT, N=100) [[albulushi2025sglt2fibrosis]]; TGF-β pathway inhibition and metalloproteinase remodelling proposed
- **Indirect weight effect** — caloric deficit via glucosuria contributes modest weight reduction

### Guideline Position and Phenotype-Specific Treatment
**ESC 2023 Focused Update:** SGLT2i Class I, Level A for all HFpEF regardless of BMI or T2DM status [[McDonagh2023ESCupdate]].

**Anker 2023 phenotype-based treatment wheel** [[Anker2023HFpEFPhenotype]]: SGLT2i are recommended as universal first-line therapy for all HFpEF phenotypes. For the obese-metabolic phenotype specifically, GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide, tirzepatide) are the recommended phenotype-specific add-on:
- **STEP-HFpEF** (semaglutide 2.4 mg; N=529; non-DM): KCCQ-CSS +7.8 pts, 6MWD +20.3 m, weight −10.7% (both P<0.001) [[Kosiborod2023STEPHFPEF]]. Not powered for hard outcomes; only 3.6% background SGLT2i use.
- **SUMMIT** (tirzepatide; N=731): CV death/worsening HF HR 0.62 (0.41–0.95; P=0.026); pericardial AT −43 mL; LV mass −11 g — first GLP-1RA with hard event reduction in obese HFpEF [[Packer2025SUMMIT]].

No RCT has tested SGLT2i + GLP-1RA combination vs. monotherapy in obese HFpEF.

# Question 4
Is spironolactone beneficial in HFpEF?

---
## Answer
### Clinical bottom line
The evidence for spironolactone in HFpEF is **directional but not conclusive** — the only large outcomes RCT (TOPCAT) missed its primary composite endpoint, a positive subgroup finding is contested by trial integrity concerns, structural and functional regression has been demonstrated in a smaller IPD meta-analysis, the non-steroidal MRA finerenone achieved positive outcomes in a related population, and the definitive pragmatic RRCT (SPIRRIT) has not yet reported. AHA 2022 gives spironolactone Class 2b; ESC 2021 makes no formal recommendation [[pitt2014topcat]], [[ferreira2023spironolactone]], [[Solomon2024FINEARTS]], [[lund2024spirrit]].

---
### TOPCAT (primary evidence)
**TOPCAT** (spironolactone 15–45 mg vs. placebo; N=3,445; LVEF ≥45%; mean follow-up 3.3 years) [[pitt2014topcat]]:

| Endpoint | Spironolactone | Placebo | HR (95% CI) | P |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary composite (CV death + cardiac arrest + HHF) | 18.6% | 20.4% | **0.89 (0.77–1.04)** | **0.14 — neutral** |
| HF hospitalisation alone | 12.0% | 14.2% | **0.83 (0.69–0.99)** | **0.04** |
| CV death | 10.2% | 9.4% | 1.10 (0.90–1.35) | 0.37 |
| All-cause death | 14.7% | 13.4% | 1.09 (0.90–1.31) | — |
The **primary composite was statistically neutral**. The HHF component met significance, but because the primary endpoint did not, this is a nominally positive secondary finding within a neutral trial [[pitt2014topcat]].

#### TOPCAT subgroup findings
**Americas subgroup** (US, Canada, Brazil, Argentina; 1,767 patients): event rate 27.3% vs. 31.8% — directionally favourable but a post-hoc analysis. Exploratory HR ≈0.82 (not powered for this comparison).

**BNP enrollment stratum** (BNP ≥100 pg/mL at entry): HR **0.65** (P=0.003) — the strongest signal within TOPCAT, suggesting benefit in patients with confirmed haemodynamic disease [[pitt2014topcat]].

#### TOPCAT Russia/Georgia contamination
Russia/Georgia placebo event rate: **8.4%** (vs. Americas 31.8% placebo rate). Urinary spironolactone metabolites (canrenone) detectable in **<10%** of Russia/Georgia participants assigned to the spironolactone arm — vs. >80% of Americas participants. This is the central integrity concern: placebo tablets in Russia/Georgia appear to have been poorly distributed or switched, substantially diluting any treatment signal. The Americas-only subgroup is the most interpretable data, but it remains an unplanned post-hoc analysis [[pitt2014topcat]].

---

### Structural and functional effects — Ferreira 2023 IPD meta-analysis
**Ferreira 2023** (ferreira2023spironolactone; individual patient data from HOMAGE [N=452; no prior HF, diastolic dysfunction] + Aldo-DHF [N=398; HFpEF, exercise intolerance] + TOPCAT-Americas [N=134]; total N=984; spironolactone ≈25 mg/day; median ~9 months) [[ferreira2023spironolactone]]:

| Outcome | Effect (vs. placebo) | P |
|---|---|---|
| LA volume index (LAVi) | **−1.1 mL/m²** | 0.03 |
| LV mass index (LVMi) | **−3.6 g/m²** | 0.01 |
| Interventricular septal thickness (IVS) | **−0.2 cm** | 0.01 |
| E/e' ratio | **−1.3** | 0.02 |
| LVEF | **+1.7%** | <0.01 |

No significant subgroup interactions by age, sex, LVEF, BMI, AF, or T2DM. These are modest but mechanistically coherent effects — consistent with aldosterone-mediated cardiac fibrosis and wall stress. The IPD approach provides more statistical power than each constituent trial. **Critical caveat:** TOPCAT-Americas contributed only 134 patients; HOMAGE enrolled pre-HF patients; generalisability to established symptomatic HFpEF is limited [[ferreira2023spironolactone]].

---
### Class-level context: FINEARTS-HF
**FINEARTS-HF** (finerenone, a non-steroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist; N=6,001; LVEF ≥40%; median 32 months) [[Solomon2024FINEARTS]]:

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Primary: total WHF events + CV death (RR) | **RR 0.84 (0.74–0.95); P=0.007** |
| CV mortality | HR 0.93 (NS) |
| KCCQ-TSS | +1.6 pts (P<0.001) |
FINEARTS-HF establishes that MRA-class therapy — aldosterone/mineralocorticoid receptor blockade — achieves outcome benefit in the HFpEF/HFmrEF population at scale. Whether this benefit is a **class effect that includes spironolactone** (supporting TOPCAT Americas) or is **finerenone-specific** (due to its non-steroidal profile, higher selectivity, and absence of hormonal side effects) is the central open question [[Solomon2024FINEARTS]], [[lund2024spirrit]].

Finerenone's non-steroidal selectivity produces meaningfully lower hyperkalaemia incidence than spironolactone at equivalent receptor blockade; whether this translates to outcome differences vs. a steroidally equivalent spironolactone dose is unresolved.

---
### SPIRRIT-HFpEF — pending verdict
**SPIRRIT-HFpEF** (NCT02901184; spironolactone or eplerenone + usual care vs. usual care; HFpEF/HFmrEF, LVEF ≥40%; ~2,400 patients; primary: total CV deaths + HF hospitalisations, recurrent event counting; PROBE design; SwedeHF registry + US TIN) [[lund2024spirrit]]:

Approximately 2,200 patients enrolled as of mid-2024; results expected end 2025. This is the first **registry-based RCT (RRCT)** in chronic HF — pragmatic design, no study echocardiography required, outcomes ascertained via national registries. The primary endpoint was amended from time-to-first-event to total events counting after a 2022 blinded interim review showing fewer events than anticipated.

SPIRRIT will determine whether TOPCAT Americas is reproducible in a broader, generalisable contemporary population, and whether the MRA class effect proven by finerenone extends to spironolactone.

# Question 5
A patient has exertional dyspnea but normal resting echocardiography. HFpEF is suspected. What additional diagnostic strategies are evidence-supported?

---
## Answer
### Framing the clinical problem
"Normal resting echocardiography" in this context means the HFA-PEFF score is low (≤1) or intermediate (2–4), or all Table 9 structural/functional markers are within normal limits. This is the canonical clinical scenario for **exercise-unmasked HFpEF**: patients whose filling pressures are normal at rest but rise pathologically with exertion. Borlaug 2010 (N=55; exertional dyspnea, EF ≥50%, normal BNP, normal resting hemodynamics) found 58% of such patients had invasively confirmed HFpEF — a population that would have been missed by every resting criterion tested [[borlaug2010exercise]]. Landsteiner 2025 (N=872) confirmed in a larger contemporary cohort that exercise hemodynamics unmask HFpEF in **23–28%** of patients not captured by resting measurements [[landsteiner2025hemodynamics]].

The strategies below follow the HFA-PEFF Step F1 → F2 escalation path [[Pieske2019HFAPEFF]] and the Borlaug 2023 gold-standard framework [[borlaug2023statement]].

---
### Strategy 1 — Formal algorithm scoring at rest (re-assessment prerequisite)
Before escalating to functional testing, complete the HFA-PEFF and H₂FPEF scoring with all resting parameters:

| HFA-PEFF score | H₂FPEF score | Action |
|---|---|---|
| ≥5 | ≥6 | HFpEF confirmed — no further testing needed |
| **2–4** | **2–5** | **Intermediate → proceed to functional testing** |
| ≤1 | ≤1 | HFpEF unlikely — search for non-cardiac cause |
NT-proBNP may be disproportionately low in obese patients — normal NP does not exclude HFpEF, particularly at BMI >30 [[Pieske2019HFAPEFF]], [[borlaug2023statement]]. Up to **30% of invasively confirmed HFpEF** have NP below the diagnostic threshold [[borlaug2023statement]].

---
### Strategy 2 — Exercise echocardiography (diastolic stress test)
**Guideline status:** HFA-PEFF Step F1 (non-invasive arm); ESC 2021 Class IIb; adds points to intermediate HFA-PEFF score [[Pieske2019HFAPEFF]], [[McDonagh2021ESC]].

**Protocol:** Standard treadmill or semi-supine/upright cycle ergometer with Doppler acquisition at rest, submaximal, and peak exertion. Key measurements:

| Parameter | Threshold | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| E/e' at peak stress | ≥15 | HFA-PEFF +2 pts; strongly demonstrative of HFpEF |
| E/e' ≥15 AND TR velocity | >3.4 m/s at peak | HFA-PEFF +3 pts; highest confirmatory non-invasive level |
| Exercise PASP | **≥45 mmHg** | AUC **0.99**; sensitivity 96%, specificity 95% for exercise-induced PCWP elevation [[borlaug2010exercise]] |
| LASr (LA reservoir strain) | Decrease with exercise | LA stiffening — supports HFpEF; superior to TRV for LVFP estimation at rest [[borlaug2023statement]] |
Exercise PASP ≥45 mmHg is the single highest-performing non-invasive marker identified in the exercise-unmasked HFpEF literature — all other non-invasive markers tested in the same cohort (BNP, E/e', ESC algorithm) had AUC <0.70 [[borlaug2010exercise]].

**Limitation:** Adequate TR signal is technically obtainable in only 60–70% of patients; image quality degrades during exercise; requires laboratory with exercise-echo capability.

---
### Strategy 3 — Cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET)
Non-invasive CPET provides physiological characterisation without exercise hemodynamics. Key findings suggesting cardiac-origin exercise limitation in HFpEF [[Sachdev2023Exercise]], [[borlaug2023statement]]:

| CPET parameter | HFpEF pattern |
|---|---|
| Peak VO2 | <14 mL/kg/min (or <80% predicted) |
| VE/VCO2 slope | >34 — elevated minute ventilation/CO2 output ratio |
| Oscillatory ventilation | Present in ~30% HFpEF — indicates elevated PCWP |
| Chronotropic incompetence | HR at peak exercise <80% predicted (present in ~50% HFpEF) |
| Heart rate recovery | Slowed (<12 bpm at 1 min post-exercise) |

CPET does not distinguish HFpEF from non-cardiac dyspnea with certainty — skeletal muscle myopathy (the primary mechanism of exercise intolerance in HFpEF) produces similar VO2 patterns as cardiac limitation [[Sachdev2023Exercise]]. However, oscillatory ventilation and severely reduced VO2 shift pre-test probability substantially and guide the decision for invasive testing.

---
### Strategy 4 — Provocative haemodynamic maneuvers (pre-invasive)
When exercise echo is technically inadequate or inconclusive, two loading maneuvers increase preload and can unmask elevated filling pressures [[borlaug2023statement]]:

**Passive leg raise (PLR):** Immediate preload challenge. Non-invasive. PCWP equivalent rise ≥3–4 mmHg supports elevated preload reserve — positive response identifies patients likely to benefit from formal exercise haemodynamics.

**Saline infusion (intravenous):** Rapid volume loading (0.9% NaCl 500–1,000 mL over 10–15 min) with echo monitoring. PCWP ≥18 mmHg (invasive) or E/e' rise with saline = elevated filling pressures. Less physiologically representative than exercise — does not challenge the full cardiac output reserve and does not reproduce the sympathetic activation of exertion [[borlaug2023statement]].

Both are less informative than exercise but useful when exercise testing is not feasible.

---
### Strategy 5 — Invasive exercise haemodynamics (gold standard)
**Indication:** Intermediate HFA-PEFF or H₂FPEF score after non-invasive testing, or clinical suspicion remains high despite negative non-invasive results [[borlaug2023statement]], [[McDonagh2021ESC]].

**Guideline status:** ESC 2021 Class IIb. AHA 2022: filling pressure elevation is the explicit third diagnostic criterion; invasive testing is the reference standard [[McDonagh2021ESC]], [[Heidenreich2022AHA]].

**Diagnostic thresholds by protocol:**

| Protocol | Threshold | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Resting, supine | PCWP ≥15 mmHg OR LVEDP ≥16 mmHg | ESC 2021 / Borlaug 2023 |
| Exercise, supine | PCWP ≥25 mmHg (end-expiration) | [[borlaug2010exercise]] / ESC 2021 |
| Exercise, upright | **PCWP/CO slope >2 mmHg/L/min** | [[borlaug2023statement]] / [[landsteiner2025hemodynamics]] |
The **PCWP/CO slope** is the preferred metric for upright ergometry protocols because absolute PCWP at a given workload depends on the workload achieved; the slope is workload-independent and tracks the cardiac pressure-flow relationship [[borlaug2023statement]], [[landsteiner2025hemodynamics]].

Prognostic value: exercise-unmasked HFpEF (normal resting PCWP but elevated exercise PCWP/CO slope) carries HR **1.42** (95% CI 1.08–1.86) for combined all-cause mortality and CV events vs. completely normal hemodynamics — this group has clinically relevant adverse prognosis [[landsteiner2025hemodynamics]].

The hemodynamic gap between HFpEF and non-cardiac dyspnea emerges within the **first 1.5 minutes of exercise at 20W** — low-level exercise already reveals the filling pressure abnormality, making the test achievable in frail patients [[borlaug2010exercise]].

---
### Strategy 6 — Aetiological workup (HFA-PEFF Step F2)
When HFpEF is confirmed (or when clinical features suggest a specific aetiology), directed testing avoids missed treatable diagnoses [[Pieske2019HFAPEFF]], [[borlaug2023statement]]:

| Clinical clue | Test | Condition to exclude |
|---|---|---|
| Thick walls (IVSd ≥12 mm), low voltage ECG, bicarpal tunnel | DPD/PYP bone scintigraphy; free light chains; serum/urine protein electrophoresis; prealbumin | ATTR-CM; AL amyloidosis |
| Asymmetric hypertrophy, family history | Genetic testing (MYBPC3, MYH7) | HCM |
| Young patient, renal + skin + neuropathy | α-Galactosidase A; GLA gene | Fabry disease |
| Restrictive filling, pericardial calcification | CMR; CT | Constrictive pericarditis |
| Systemic disease, conduction abnormalities | ACE, IL-2R; CMR | Sarcoidosis |
**Cardiac MRI (CMR)** provides tissue characterisation (T1 mapping → diffuse fibrosis; LGE → focal fibrosis/amyloid; T2 mapping → oedema/inflammation) and haemodynamic estimation (LA strain from feature tracking) — particularly useful when HCM or infiltrative disease is suspected and echo is suboptimal [[borlaug2023statement]].

---
### Diagnostic escalation summary

```
Normal resting echo + exertional dyspnea
         ↓
Re-score HFA-PEFF / H₂FPEF
         ↓
  Intermediate (2–4 / 2–5) → Exercise stress echocardiography
                               + CPET
         ↓
  Still inconclusive → Saline / passive leg raise
         ↓
  Still inconclusive → Invasive exercise haemodynamics
                       (PCWP/CO slope >2 upright or PCWP ≥25 supine)
         ↓
  HFpEF confirmed → Step F2 aetiological workup if specific clues
```

## Contradictions

- **Exercise PASP AUC 0.99 vs. resting AUC <0.70 (all markers):** No resting marker achieves diagnostic adequacy for exercise-unmasked HFpEF — yet resting echo remains first-line in practice due to availability. This gap is the central tension [[borlaug2010exercise]], [[Ho2019HFpEFDefinitions]]. See [[contradictions]].
- **Supine vs. upright exercise thresholds:** Borlaug 2010 used supine cycle (PCWP ≥25 mmHg); Landsteiner 2025 used upright ergometry (PCWP/CO slope >2 mmHg/L/min). These thresholds are not interchangeable — position affects baseline PCWP; reclassification across protocols occurs in a non-trivial proportion of patients [[landsteiner2025hemodynamics]]. See [[contradictions]].
- **CPET cardiac vs. peripheral limitation:** Because skeletal muscle myopathy drives >50% of VO2 deficit in HFpEF, a reduced peak VO2 cannot distinguish cardiac from peripheral limitation without simultaneous hemodynamic measurement [[Sachdev2023Exercise]].

### NT-proBNP (N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide)
#### Mechanism
ProBNP is synthesised by ventricular cardiomyocytes in response to elevated wall stress (volume or pressure overload). It is cleaved into two fragments: **BNP** (the active hormone; half-life ~20 min) and **NT-proBNP** (the biologically inactive N-terminal fragment; half-life ~70–120 min). The longer half-life of NT-proBNP makes it more stable and the preferred assay in most clinical settings [[McDonagh2021ESC]]. Both fragments promote natriuresis and vasodilation as compensatory mechanisms against volume overload.
#### Diagnostic role
**ESC 2021: Class I, Level B** for all patients with suspected chronic HF [[McDonagh2021ESC]].

| Biomarker | Sinus rhythm | Atrial fibrillation |
| --------- | ------------ | ------------------- |
| NT-proBNP | ≥125 pg/mL   | ≥365 pg/mL          |
| BNP       | ≥35 pg/mL    | ≥105 pg/mL          |
Values **below** threshold make HF unlikely — negative predictive value 0.94–0.98 in primary care settings [[McDonagh2021ESC]]. NT-proBNP is used as a **rule-out** biomarker; it is a necessary but not sufficient criterion for HFpEF, where it forms one domain within the HFA-PEFF and H₂FPEF algorithms.

**HFpEF-specific limitations:**
- **Reduced sensitivity vs. HFrEF:** Mean NT-proBNP at HFpEF diagnosis is ~3× lower than in HFrEF at equivalent symptom severity. Up to 20% of invasively confirmed HFpEF patients fall below the ≥125 pg/mL threshold [[McDonagh2021ESC]].
- **Obesity suppression:** Adipose tissue degrades BNP via increased clearance; obese patients have lower NPs independent of HF severity. A normal NT-proBNP in an obese patient does **not** exclude HFpEF.
- **AF elevation:** AF elevates NPs via atrial stretch; higher thresholds (≥365 pg/mL) are required in AF to achieve equivalent specificity [[McDonagh2021ESC]].
- **Glycosylation — the tNT-proBNP problem:** Standard Elecsys proBNP II assays (Roche; global standard) target the central NT-proBNP epitope. Glycosylation of NT-proBNP at **Thr-71** prevents antibody binding → the glycosylated fraction is undetected → measured NT-proBNP is falsely lower than the true total concentration. HFpEF has a **lower** NT-proBNP/tNT-proBNP ratio than HFrEF (0.27 vs. 0.32; p=0.019), meaning greater glycosylation-driven underestimation in HFpEF. Drivers: obesity, T2DM, AF, hypertension. Total NT-proBNP (tNT-proBNP) measured by a research assay (Roche; not commercially available) is ~3.6× higher than standard NT-proBNP in HFpEF [[hage2026ntprobnp]].

**Non-cardiac causes of elevated NT-proBNP** include: ACS, PE, myocarditis, LVH, arrhythmias, pulmonary hypertension, valvular disease, renal dysfunction, liver cirrhosis, severe infections, anaemia, and advanced age — all reduce diagnostic specificity [[McDonagh2021ESC]].
#### Prognostic role
Meta-analysis (22 studies; 10,158 HFpEF patients) [[Ammar2025BNP]]:

| Biomarker | Adverse events HR | Mortality HR |
|---|---|---|
| BNP | 1.34 (95% CI 1.20–1.52) | 1.44 (95% CI 1.04–1.84) |
| NT-proBNP | 1.80 (95% CI 1.38–2.35) | 1.65 (95% CI 1.55–1.76) |
Dose-response confirmed: BNP ≥300 pg/mL → HR 7.80 for adverse events vs. HR 2.50 at 30–99 pg/mL. NT-proBNP 300–623 pg/mL → HR 1.45 for HF hospitalisation, rising to HR 3.78 at ≥1752 pg/mL [[Ammar2025BNP]].

**Critical inversion vs. HFrEF:** In HFrEF, low BNP indicates good prognosis. In HFpEF, low BNP may paradoxically indicate poor prognosis — obese HFpEF patients suppress BNP via adipose tissue degradation, resulting in lower apparent NP levels despite equivalent or worse haemodynamic burden and outcomes [[Ammar2025BNP]].

**NP-guided therapy:** NT-proBNP-guided therapy titration is not validated in HFpEF (unlike HFrEF where it carries Class 2a). Serial NT-proBNP change >1,000 ng/L over 6 months predicts CV death/HF hospitalisation (HR ~2) [[morfino2022biomarkers]].

### High-sensitivity Troponin T (hs-TnT)
#### Mechanism
Cardiac troponin T (cTnT) is a structural protein of the cardiac contractile apparatus released into circulation upon cardiomyocyte membrane disruption. In HFpEF, hs-TnT elevation reflects **ongoing low-grade cardiomyocyte injury** from coronary microvascular dysfunction, systemic inflammation, subendocardial ischaemia, and elevated wall stress — rather than the acute macroischaemia of ACS. The "high-sensitivity" assay detects troponin concentrations below the 99th percentile of the normal reference range, enabling detection of subclinical injury [[morfino2022biomarkers]].
#### Diagnostic role
hs-TnT is **not a diagnostic criterion for HFpEF** and does not appear in the ESC 2021 Table 9 markers, the HFA-PEFF functional or morphological criteria, or the H₂FPEF score [[McDonagh2021ESC]], [[morfino2022biomarkers]]. Its diagnostic utility in HF is for:

- **Distinguishing HFpEF phenotypes:** Elevated hs-TnT suggests ischaemic or inflammatory myocardial injury; normal hs-TnT is more consistent with pure haemodynamic/fibrotic phenotype
- **ACS exclusion:** Serial hs-TnT is the standard acute rule-out tool for ACS; persistently elevated chronic hs-TnT in known HFpEF indicates ongoing myocardial injury, not acute event
- **Risk stratification at diagnosis:** Higher baseline hs-TnT identifies higher-risk HFpEF patients for more intensive management

Clinical upper limit of normal (ULN): **14 ng/L** (Roche Elecsys; sex-specific values applicable in some assays).

#### Prognostic role
hs-TnT is a significant independent prognostic biomarker in HFpEF, though its predictive strength is lower than in HFrEF overall [[morfino2022biomarkers]].

**Sex differential (clinically important):** hs-TnT predicts outcomes more strongly in men than in women with HFpEF [[morfino2022biomarkers]]:

| Sex | HR (hs-TnT, per unit increase) |
|---|---|
| Men | **3.33** |
| Women | 1.35 |

Mechanism unclear — hypothesised to reflect higher rates of ischaemic myocardial injury in HFpEF men versus the inflammation/fibrosis-dominant phenotype more common in women; may also reflect differences in cardiomyocyte mass and absolute troponin release per unit of injury.

**PARAGON-HF secondary analysis** ([[gori2021paragon]]): sacubitril/valsartan reduced hs-TnT by 9% vs. valsartan at week 16 and 10% at week 48 (P<0.001). Patients achieving hs-TnT ≤17 ng/L by week 16 had better subsequent outcomes (P=0.046). This establishes hs-TnT as a pharmacodynamic biomarker and potential treatment response marker in HFpEF for ARNi therapy [[morfino2022biomarkers]].

#### Clinical synthesis
hs-TnT adds prognostic information to NT-proBNP in HFpEF — patients with both elevated NT-proBNP and elevated hs-TnT form the highest-risk group. hs-TnT is not cardiac-specific (also elevated in PE, myocarditis, sepsis, renal failure) and must be interpreted in clinical context. In the multi-biomarker framework for HFpEF, hs-TnT represents the **myocardial injury axis**, complementing NT-proBNP (wall stress) and galectin-3/sST2 (fibrosis/inflammation) [[morfino2022biomarkers]].

## Contradictions
- **NT-proBNP obesity paradox:** Lower BNP in obese HFpEF despite equivalent or worse haemodynamic burden — driven by both adipose tissue degradation of BNP and Thr-71 glycosylation preventing Elecsys antibody binding [[hage2026ntprobnp]], [[Ammar2025BNP]]. Relative contribution of each mechanism unquantified.
- **tNT-proBNP vs. standard NT-proBNP:** tNT-proBNP numerically superior prognostic AUROC (0.710 vs. 0.682) but difference NS (p=0.068; N=83) [[hage2026ntprobnp]]. Evidence is mechanistically plausible but underpowered; commercial assay not available.
- **hs-TnT sex differential direction:** Men show HR 3.33 vs. women HR 1.35 — contrast with HFpEF female predominance in epidemiological literature. Whether this reflects true biological sex differences in injury mechanism or sex-differential assay performance is unresolved [[morfino2022biomarkers]]. See [[contradictions]].

## Evidence Quality
**Rating: 4 / 5**
- Sources: 5 (2 major guidelines, 1 systematic review/meta-analysis on NPs, 1 mechanistic NP cohort study, 1 biomarker review)
- Evidence type: guideline (ESC 2021, AHA 2022), meta-analysis (Ammar 2025; 22 studies, 10,158 patients), prospective cohort (Hage 2026 KaRen/MetAnEnd), narrative review (Morfino 2022)
- Publication years: 2021–2026
- Limitations: hs-TnT prognostic data in HFpEF largely from secondary analyses and observational cohorts, not prospective biomarker-specific RCTs; sex differential for hs-TnT not yet prospectively validated; tNT-proBNP (glycosylation-corrected) is research-use-only — its clinical utility in the diagnostic pathway is not established; DnCB excluded from evidence rating as non-existent
