## Question

What parameters and risk factors cause the higher rate of HFpEF development in women?

---

## Answer

### Epidemiological framing

Women constitute approximately **40–50% of HFpEF patients** in clinical trial cohorts, compared with ~25% in HFrEF — making female sex one of the strongest epidemiological discriminators between the two HF subtypes [[borlaug2023statement]]. In invasive HFpEF cohorts women represent ~70% of patients (Beale 2019; N=161) [[beale2019sex]]. This female predominance arises from at least seven overlapping biological pathways, distinct from the comorbidity profile that also concentrates in women.

---

### Mechanism 1 — Sex-specific cardiac structural biology

Women's baseline cardiac structure predisposes to the HFpEF phenotype independently of comorbidities [[beale2018sex]]:

| Structural feature | Women | Men | HFpEF relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| LV chamber size | Smaller | Larger | Reduced volume buffer for filling pressure |
| LV wall stress response | Concentric hypertrophy | Eccentric dilation | Concentric pattern → increased stiffness, not compensatory dilation |
| LV end-systolic elastance (Ees) | Higher | Lower | Greater resistance to filling; higher end-diastolic pressures |
| LVEF trajectory with age | Increases steeply | Stable | LVEF ≥50% threshold may underestimate relative impairment in elderly women |

The concentric-vs-eccentric remodeling difference is fundamental: when pressure-overloaded, men tend toward eccentric dilation (increased preload reserve) while women remodel concentrically (increased stiffness, reduced diastolic reserve). The same hypertensive stimulus thus produces more diastolic dysfunction in women [[beale2018sex]].

**LVEF threshold artefact:** Elderly women have higher normal LVEF than age-matched men. A woman whose age-sex-expected LVEF is 62–65% but presents with LVEF of 52% may have meaningful relative systolic impairment — yet the sex-neutral ≥50% criterion classifies her as HFpEF. This raises the open question of whether female HFpEF prevalence is partly an artefact of sex-neutral LVEF thresholds [[beale2018sex]], [[bozkurt2020sex]].

---

### Mechanism 2 — Vascular aging trajectory

Women's arterial system ages differently from men's, and the vascular phenotype of aging women strongly matches the HFpEF haemodynamic profile [[beale2018sex]], [[beale2019sex]]:

- **Smaller, stiffer aorta:** Women have smaller aortic root dimensions; with age, aortic stiffness increases more steeply in women than men → higher pulse wave velocity → earlier reflected pulse wave → increased systolic LV afterload
- **Higher arterial elastance (Ea):** Ea indexed to BSA higher in women at exercise (1.5 vs 1.2 mmHg/ml, P<0.001)
- **Declining ventricular-arterial coupling:** The Ea/Ees ratio deteriorates with aging in women but is maintained in men — impaired coupling amplifies filling pressure elevation during any increase in cardiac demand
- **Lower systemic vascular compliance:** 1.3 vs 1.1 ml/mmHg (P<0.001; women < men) [[beale2019sex]]
- **Lower pulmonary vascular compliance:** 4.5 vs 3.4 ml/mmHg (P=0.039) — less pulmonary vasodilatory reserve; 82% of HFpEF patients with PH are female [[beale2018sex]]

These are sex-intrinsic differences that manifest independently of hypertension — hypertension substantially accelerates them.

---

### Mechanism 3 — Menopause and estrogen withdrawal

Estrogen has direct cardioprotective effects that are lost at menopause [[beale2018sex]]:

**Cardiac:**
- Promotes NO synthesis in cardiomyocytes and endothelium → ↑cGMP/PKG → titin phosphorylation → reduced passive stiffness
- Estrogen withdrawal reverses this: ↓NO → ↓cGMP → PKG deficiency → titin hypophosphorylation → increased cardiomyocyte stiffness (the cellular substrate of diastolic dysfunction)
- Modulates calcium channel expression and calcium handling kinetics — withdrawal impairs active relaxation

**Systemic/vascular:**
- Estrogen inhibits RAAS (reduces angiotensin receptor sensitivity, reduces aldosterone secretion)
- Menopause-associated RAAS activation → reactive oxygen species → collagen synthesis → interstitial fibrosis → myocardial stiffness
- Sex differences in fatty acid metabolism: women rely more on fatty acid oxidation; menopause shifts substrate utilisation toward less efficient pathways

**Net effect:** HFpEF incidence increases sharply in women after menopause; the timing mirrors the loss of estrogen-mediated cardioprotection rather than the accumulation of hypertensive burden alone [[beale2018sex]].

---

### Mechanism 4 — Amplified comorbidity risk

Several major HFpEF-promoting comorbidities carry disproportionately higher HF risk in women than in men [[beale2018sex]]:

| Comorbidity | HF risk in women | HF risk in men | Relative amplification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Type 2 diabetes | **5.0×** | 2.4× | **2.1× greater in women** |
| Hypertension | **3.0×** | 2.0× | **1.5× greater in women** |
| Obesity | Higher absolute prevalence in HFpEF cohorts | Lower | — |
| Iron deficiency | More prevalent globally and in HFpEF | Less prevalent | Contributes to exercise intolerance |
| Atrial fibrillation | Associated with HFpEF risk; higher HF hospitalisation in women | Less clear | — |

The diabetes amplification is particularly important: T2DM drives the inflammation–NO–cGMP–titin pathway more severely in women, compounds the menopausal estrogen deficiency on NO bioavailability, and accelerates concentric remodelling [[beale2018sex]].

---

### Mechanism 5 — Obstetric and reproductive history

Pregnancy and its complications impose lasting cardiovascular consequences that are HFpEF-relevant [[beale2018sex]]:

**Parity:** Each pregnancy involves sustained volume overload and haemodynamic stress. Parity correlates with measurable diastolic dysfunction approximately **one decade after the last delivery** — with higher parity associated with greater diastolic impairment. The mechanism is likely cumulative volume and pressure overload remodelling of the LA and LV.

**Pre-eclampsia:** Pre-eclampsia confers a **4-fold increased risk of future HF** across all HF subtypes. At 6–12 months postpartum, women with prior pre-eclampsia show persistent LV concentric remodelling, elevated LV filling pressures, and diastolic dysfunction relative to normotensive postpartum controls — an early HFpEF substrate. Pre-eclampsia involves systemic endothelial dysfunction, placental RAAS activation, and immunological vascular injury that outlasts delivery [[beale2018sex]].

---

### Mechanism 6 — Inflammation and immune biology

Women's immune system is inherently more proinflammatory, with direct cardiac consequences [[beale2018sex]]:

- **Myocardial gene expression:** Female cardiac tissue shows sex-differentially expressed genes in NF-κB and KLF4 pathways — both regulating inflammatory cytokine production, fibrogenesis, and macrophage activation in the heart
- **CRP and systemic inflammation:** Women have higher baseline high-sensitivity CRP; inflammatory markers predict HFpEF incidence more strongly in women
- **Autoimmune diseases:** Conditions with predominantly female prevalence (rheumatoid arthritis, lupus, Sjögren's) are independently associated with diastolic dysfunction and accelerate HFpEF pathophysiology via immune-mediated myocardial inflammation and fibrosis

---

### Mechanism 7 — Exercise haemodynamic phenotype

Beale 2019 (N=161; prospective invasive exercise RHC + echo) quantified sex-haemodynamic differences directly [[beale2019sex]]:

| Parameter | Women | Men | P |
|---|---|---|---|
| PCWP per workload (mmHg/W) | **0.8 [0.5–1.2]** | 0.6 [0.4–1.0] | 0.014 |
| ΔSVI (stroke volume reserve) | 8.1 ml | 19.7 ml | 0.007 |
| E/e' at exercise | **14.1** | 9.3 | <0.001 |
| Lactate per workload (mmol/l/W) | **0.09** | 0.04 | 0.003 |
| Pulmonary compliance | **4.5 ml/mmHg** | 3.4 ml/mmHg | 0.039 |

Women mount **greater filling pressure elevation per unit of external work** — the direct haemodynamic expression of reduced diastolic reserve, lower vascular compliance, and smaller stroke volume reserve.

## Contradictions

- **Artifactual vs. biological female predominance:** Female sex raises normal LVEF relative to males; the sex-neutral ≥50% HFpEF threshold may classify some elderly women as HFpEF who have relative systolic impairment below their sex-age-specific normal. This confounds the true biological vs. diagnostic-threshold explanation for female HFpEF preponderance [[beale2018sex]]. See [[contradictions]].
- **Estrogen and sacubitril-valsartan:** Estrogen may upregulate neprilysin and modulate RAAS sensitivity — a speculative mechanism for why women benefit from ARNi therapy at higher LVEF than men — but NT-proBNP and cGMP responses to sacubitril-valsartan are equivalent by sex, making a pharmacokinetic explanation unlikely [[bozkurt2020sex]]. See [[contradictions]].
- **hs-TnT sex inversion:** Despite women's higher HFpEF prevalence and worse haemodynamic phenotype, hs-TnT predicts outcomes more strongly in men (HR 3.33) than women (HR 1.35) — suggesting men's HFpEF is more ischaemic/injury-driven while women's is more fibrotic/stiffness-driven [[morfino2022biomarkers]]. See [[contradictions]].

## Evidence Quality

**Rating: 4 / 5**
- Sources: 6 (1 mechanistic review, 1 prospective invasive haemodynamic cohort, 1 JACC scientific statement, 1 disparities review, 1 trial subanalysis editorial, 1 biomarker review)
- Evidence type: mechanistic narrative review (Beale 2018); prospective invasive cohort (Beale 2019; N=161); scientific statement (Borlaug 2023); narrative review (Ilonze 2024); editorial on RCT subanalysis (Bozkurt 2020)
- Publication years: 2018–2024
- Limitations: No RCT specifically designed to test sex-stratified HFpEF pathophysiology or prevention; obstetric data (parity, pre-eclampsia) from observational cohorts with residual confounding; estrogen withdrawal mechanism is mechanistically plausible but no interventional estrogen trial in HFpEF; LVEF artefact question unresolved; Beale 2019 is a single-centre referral cohort (N=161, 71% women)
