## Question

What parameters and risk factors cause the higher rate of HFpEF development in women?

---

## Answer

**Bottom line:** female HFpEF predominance (women are ~40–50% of trial cohorts, up to ~70% of some invasive-hemodynamic referral cohorts, vs. ~25% in HFrEF) is driven by at least seven overlapping mechanisms: (1) sex-specific cardiac structure — concentric remodelling, higher LV elastance, smaller chambers; (2) vascular/arterial aging — greater arterial elastance and stiffness, lower systemic/pulmonary compliance, worse ischemic predisposition; (3) menopausal estrogen withdrawal — RAAS activation, reduced NO/cGMP, titin hypophosphorylation → fibrosis and stiffness; (4) amplified comorbidity risk — type 2 diabetes confers 5× HF risk in women vs. 2.4× in men, hypertension 3× vs. 2×; (5) obstetric history — parity correlates with diastolic dysfunction ~a decade later, pre-eclampsia confers 4× future HF risk; (6) immune biology — more proinflammatory cardiac gene expression, higher CRP, autoimmune-disease associations; (7) exercise hemodynamics — greater filling-pressure rise per unit of workload, smaller stroke-volume reserve, worse peripheral oxygen kinetics.

A notable outcome paradox: despite this more severe physiological phenotype, women have **better cardiac-specific survival** than men — men more often die *of* HFpEF (right heart failure, sudden cardiac death), women more often die *with* HFpEF (of non-cardiac causes, particularly infection).
