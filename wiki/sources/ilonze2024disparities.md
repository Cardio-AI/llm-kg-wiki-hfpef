# Ilonze 2024 — Racial and Ethnic Disparities in HFpEF

> Racial/ethnic disparities in HFpEF are widespread and operate across the care continuum: Black patients have higher HFpEF burden at younger ages, lower natriuretic peptide levels that mask diagnosis, higher ATTR V122I amyloid prevalence, and are underrepresented in trials; SGLT2i, semaglutide, and spironolactone are effective in Black patients but remain underutilised.

## Core Arguments

### Epidemiological Disparities

| Group | Key Features Vs. White Patients |
|---|---|
| Black patients | Higher HTN burden (earlier onset, more severe); higher obesity; lower NP levels; younger at first HFpEF hospitalisation |
| Hispanic patients | Higher obesity/DM burden; younger at diagnosis |
| Asian patients | Lower absolute BMI but higher cardiometabolic risk at given BMI |

- **First HFpEF hospitalisation rates:** Black women 7.4/1,000 PY (highest) > Black men 6.2 > White women 5.9 > White men 4.9
- Annual percent increase in first HFpEF hospitalisation highest in Black women
- Black young adults (39±6y): 20-fold higher cumulative HF incidence vs. White peers (CARDIA study)

### Diagnostic Challenges in Minority Populations

**Low Natriuretic Peptide Levels in Black Patients:**
- ~20–35% of HFpEF outpatients with confirmed elevated PCWP have low BNP (<100 pg/mL) — predominantly Black patients
- Causes: lower LV diastolic wall stress per filling pressure, NPPB polymorphisms, insulin resistance, increased androgenicity
- Normal NP levels cannot exclude HFpEF in Black patients; may lead to underdiagnosis
- H2FPEF score validated in predominantly White cohorts → systematically underdiagnoses HFpEF in Black patients (ML study confirmed)

**ATTR V122I Amyloid Cardiomyopathy:**
- Isoleucine 122 (V122I) variant in **3.43% of Black Americans ≥60 years**
- Autosomal dominant; male predominance 6:1; poor survival; emerged from West Africa
- May mimic HFpEF clinically; must be excluded in Black patients with unexplained HFpEF phenotype

**HFpEF Phenocopies in Black Patients:**
- Amyloid (ATTR and AL), hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, cardiac sarcoidosis, constrictive pericarditis, high-output HF, Fabry disease, haemochromatosis, hypertensive heart disease

### Disparities in Treatment and Outcomes

| Treatment | Disparity Finding |
|---|---|
| SGLT2i | Underutilised in Black, female, low-income patients (2015–2019 cohort); lower household income associated with less SGLT2i use |
| Spironolactone (TOPCAT) | Equally effective in Black patients; NNT=15.1 vs. 25.9 in non-Black; excess HF hospitalisations in Black patients |
| Semaglutide (STEP-HFpEF) | Significant improvements in KCCQ, 6MWT, CRP, NT-proBNP; mean body weight −13.3% vs. −2.6% placebo |
| GLP-1 RA (general) | Less likely to be received by Black, Asian, Hispanic patients with low income |
| Sacubitril/valsartan | Lower medication adherence (PDC <80%) associated with lower income |

### Social Determinants of Health (SDOH)
- Structural racism, neighbourhood disadvantage, food insecurity, lack of CV specialist access → compound biological disparities
- Implementation science framework proposed: systematic SDOH screening (aligned with QM-2 from [[kittleson2024accaha]]), community-based interventions, clinical trial diversification

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

This review comprehensively synthesises disparities across diagnosis, treatment, and outcomes in HFpEF. Key for understanding why standard algorithms (H2FPEF score, NP thresholds) may fail in minority populations. The ATTR V122I variant in Black patients is an important clinical point — HFpEF in elderly Black patients should prompt ATTR screening.
