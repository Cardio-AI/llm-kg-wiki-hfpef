# TOPCAT — Spironolactone in HFpEF (Pitt 2014)

> Spironolactone was neutral vs. placebo on the primary composite (HR 0.89, P=0.14) in HFpEF overall; the HF hospitalisation component was nominally significant (HR 0.83, P=0.04); suspected enrollment contamination in Russia/Georgia and a high drug discontinuation rate (~33%) make the overall result permanently contested.

**File:** `raw/2014-NEJM-Pitt-TOPCAT_study.pdf` · Design paper: `raw/2011-AHA-Desai-TOPCAT_study.pdf` · **Authors:** Pitt B, Pfeffer MA, Assmann SF, et al. (TOPCAT Investigators)  
**Year:** 2014 · **Journal:** N Engl J Med 2014;370(15):1383–1392 · **DOI:** 10.1056/NEJMoa1313731  
**ClinicalTrials.gov:** NCT00094302  
**Study type:** Multinational double-blind RCT · **N:** 3,445 (spironolactone 1,722; placebo 1,723)  
**Sites:** 233 centers in 6 countries (US, Canada, Brazil, Argentina, Russia, Georgia)  
**Population:** HFpEF; LVEF ≥45% by echo within 6 months; NYHA II–IV; age ≥50 years; dual enrollment pathway  
**Follow-up:** Mean 3.3 years (enrollment Aug 2006–Jan 2012; follow-up through Jun 2013)  
**Intervention:** Spironolactone 15 mg once daily, uptitrated to maximum 45 mg daily over 4 months; vs. matched placebo  
**Primary outcome:** Composite of CV death + aborted cardiac arrest + HF hospitalisation

---

## Key Findings

- **Primary endpoint: NEUTRAL** — HR 0.89 (95% CI 0.77–1.04; P=0.14)
- HF hospitalisation component: HR 0.83 (95% CI 0.69–0.99; P=0.04) — only significant component
- All-cause mortality: HR 0.91 (95% CI 0.77–1.08; P=0.29)
- CV death: HR 0.90 (95% CI 0.73–1.12; P=0.35)
- Americas region event rates: spironolactone 27.3% vs placebo 31.8%; directional but region×treatment interaction P=0.12 (not significant in this paper)
- Russia/Georgia event rates: spironolactone 9.3% vs placebo 8.4% — unusually low placebo events
- Hyperkalemia: 18.7% (spironolactone) vs 9.1% (placebo); serious safety signal
- 34.3% (spironolactone) / 31.4% (placebo) discontinued study drug (limitation)
- Urinary spironolactone metabolite evidence for contamination: NOT reported in this paper (published separately)

## Methods (brief)

Double-blind placebo-controlled RCT. **Dual eligibility pathway:**
- *Hospitalisation-based:* ≥1 HF hospitalisation in prior 12 months
- *NP-based:* BNP ≥100 pg/mL OR NT-proBNP ≥360 pg/mL within 60 days of randomisation (no hospitalisation required)

**Key exclusions:** LVEF <45% in prior 6 months; eGFR <30 mL/min; K+ >5.0 mEq/L; SBP <100 mmHg; planned revascularisation.

**Spironolactone titration:** 15 mg → uptitrated to maximum 45 mg over 4 months. Mean dose at 8 months: 25.0 mg (spironolactone) vs 27.7 mg (placebo equivalent).

## Limitations

- Region×treatment interaction P=0.12: Americas vs. non-Americas divergence does not reach pre-specified significance threshold, making all regional subgroup arguments post-hoc
- 34.3% (spironolactone) and 31.4% (placebo) discontinued study drug (for hyperkalaemia, renal events, or other reasons) — limits power and interpretation
- Urinary spironolactone metabolite contamination evidence (near-zero metabolite levels in Russia/Georgia placebo arm) was not available at publication — published separately post-hoc; likely explains anomalous low event rates in non-Americas placebo
- Two enrollment pathways (hospitalisation-based vs. NP-based) enrolled biologically distinct populations; BNP-based stratum had significantly greater spironolactone benefit (strata interaction P=0.01): BNP stratum HR 0.65 (95% CI 0.49–0.87, P=0.003) vs. hospitalisation stratum HR 1.01 (0.84–1.21, P=0.92)
- NT-proBNP was significantly higher at baseline in the placebo arm vs spironolactone arm (P=0.04), a pre-randomisation imbalance that could bias primary endpoint interpretation
- LVEF ≥45% threshold includes HFmrEF (41–49%) by current definitions
- No central echo reader for LVEF qualification
- Mean spironolactone dose only 25 mg — below the target of 45 mg; below the dose used in RALES (26.8 mg effective in HFrEF)

## Contradictions

Americas region directional benefit (27.3% vs 31.8%) vs. overall neutral primary (HR 0.89, P=0.14) is the central TOPCAT controversy. The region×treatment interaction P=0.12 is non-significant in this paper. Post-hoc urinary metabolite data (published separately) suggests Russia/Georgia placebo patients received open-label spironolactone outside the trial — corrupting the control arm — which explains the anomalous low event rate in non-Americas placebo. Whether eliminating the contaminated cohort reveals a true treatment effect or whether the Americas benefit is itself a post-hoc artifact remains permanently uncertain.
