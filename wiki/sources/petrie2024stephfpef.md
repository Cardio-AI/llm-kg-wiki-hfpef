# Petrie 2024 — Semaglutide and NT-proBNP in STEP-HFpEF Program

> Semaglutide reduced NT-proBNP by 18% vs. placebo (ETR 0.82; P=0.0002) in obesity-related HFpEF; reduction was weight-loss-independent, suggesting direct HF disease-modifying effects; patients with higher baseline NT-proBNP derived greater symptomatic benefit.

## Summary

Prespecified secondary analysis of the STEP-HFpEF program (pooled STEP-HFpEF NCT04788511 and STEP-HFpEF DM NCT04916470; N=1,145 total). Examined: (1) whether semaglutide 2.4 mg reduces NT-proBNP; (2) whether NT-proBNP reduction is independent of weight loss; (3) whether baseline NT-proBNP level modifies treatment effects on the primary endpoints (KCCQ-CSS and body weight). (source: petrie2024stephfpef)

## Design

- **Program:** STEP-HFpEF (N=529, non-DM) + STEP-HFpEF DM (N=616, T2DM); pooled N=1,145
- **Intervention:** Semaglutide 2.4 mg SC once weekly vs. placebo for 52 weeks
- **Endpoints:** NT-proBNP at 20 and 52 weeks; primary trial endpoints stratified by baseline NT-proBNP tertile
- **NT-proBNP tertiles:**
  - T1: <300 pg/mL (N=384)
  - T2: 300–810 pg/mL (N=380)
  - T3: >810 pg/mL (N=381)

## Results

**NT-proBNP effect of semaglutide:**
- Semaglutide reduced NT-proBNP by 22% from baseline vs. 4.9% with placebo at 52 weeks
- Estimated treatment ratio (ETR): **0.82 (95% CI 0.74–0.91; P=0.0002)**
- Effect evident by week 20 (ETR 0.81 at week 20) and persistent at week 52
- Consistent across both STEP-HFpEF and STEP-HFpEF DM subpopulations

**Independence from weight loss:**
- NT-proBNP reduction consistent across all weight loss categories (<5%, 5–<10%, 10–<15%, 15–<20%, ≥20%); P interaction = 0.58
- Among semaglutide-treated patients with the greatest weight loss (≥15–20%), NT-proBNP was actually **less** reduced relative to baseline — paradoxically, greater mechanical unloading may counteract direct HF benefit by reducing myocardial stretch (the primary stimulus for NP release)
- Placebo-treated patients who achieved ≥10% weight loss showed a trend toward **increased** NT-proBNP (P=0.08) — consistent with weight loss releasing epicardial fat → increased cardiac stretch → higher NP release
- These observations strongly support **direct disease-modifying effects** beyond mechanical unloading

**Primary endpoints by NT-proBNP tertile:**
- KCCQ-CSS improvement: T1 +4.5 pts; T2 +6.2 pts; T3 +11.9 pts; P interaction = 0.02
- Body weight % change: T1 –9.4%; T2 –8.3%; T3 –7.5%; P interaction = 0.21 (uniform)
- 6MWD improvement: T1 +17.7m; T2 +9.5m; T3 +23.8m; P interaction = 0.32 (non-significant)
- hsCRP ratio: consistent across tertiles (T1 0.58; T2 0.73; T3 0.61; P interaction = 0.25)
- Hierarchical composite win ratio: T1 1.45; T2 1.42; T3 **2.17** (P interaction = 0.04)

**Safety:** Fewer SAEs and cardiac SAEs across all NT-proBNP tertiles in semaglutide vs. placebo; GI AEs 5–10% in both groups across tertiles.

## Central Conclusion

Semaglutide reduces NT-proBNP in obesity-related HFpEF independently of the degree of weight loss achieved. This strongly suggests a **direct HF disease-modifying mechanism** — not merely haemodynamic improvement secondary to weight reduction. Patients with higher baseline NT-proBNP (more advanced HF, higher filling pressures) appear to derive disproportionately greater symptomatic benefit (KCCQ-CSS), consistent with greater relative improvement in filling pressures.

## Mechanistic Interpretation

The weight-loss paradox (greater weight loss → less NP reduction in sema group) is best explained as opposing mechanisms:
1. Weight loss → reduced epicardial fat → reduced pericardial constraint → NP may rise
2. Direct semaglutide HF effect → reduced filling pressure → NP falls
3. Net result depends on balance; at very high weight loss, effect 1 partially offsets effect 2

## Clinical Implications

- NT-proBNP reduction supports treatment adequacy monitoring with NPs in GLP-1RA-treated HFpEF patients
- Sicker patients (higher NT-proBNP at baseline) derive greater symptomatic benefit — aligns with sema being most beneficial in the metabolic-inflammatory obese HFpEF phenotype with elevated neurohormonal activation
- Weight loss alone does not explain these benefits — direct cardiac and anti-inflammatory mechanisms are operative
