# Extracellular Volume Fraction for Characterization of HFpEF (Rommel 2016 / STIFFMAP)

> Cardiac MRI T1-mapping-derived extracellular volume fraction (ECV) correlates strongly with invasively measured, load-independent LV stiffness constant (β) in HFpEF (r=0.75, P<0.01) and independently predicts intrinsic LV stiffness on multivariate analysis — the first study to comprehensively link noninvasive diffuse-fibrosis imaging to gold-standard pressure-volume haemodynamics in HFpEF, and to show that ECV distinguishes two distinct HFpEF subphenotypes (fibrosis-dominant vs. relaxation-dominant).

## Key Findings

- **Patients with HFpEF were older, more often female, higher BMI, more hypertension, higher NT-proBNP** than controls (Table 1); NT-proBNP elevated in two-thirds of HFpEF
- **ECV significantly higher in HFpEF vs. controls:** 32.9 ± 3.2% vs. 28.9 ± 3.0% (P<0.01)
- **PV-loop-derived findings:** HFpEF had higher baseline LVEDP (17.9 vs. 13.5 mmHg, P<0.01), greater ΔEDPVR with exercise (0.10 vs. 0.021 mmHg/mL, P<0.01), and higher LV stiffness constant β (0.037 vs. 0.021, P<0.01) than controls; contractility (Ees) and arterial elastance (Ea) were comparable between groups
- **ECV correlates strongly with LV stiffness constant β:** r=0.75 (β-standardized=0.75, P<0.01) — the strongest of all noninvasive predictors tested
- **On multivariate regression including ECV, E/e', and LA volume index, ECV was the only independent predictor of intrinsic LV stiffness** (β-standardized=0.75; β-nonstandardized=0.21; P<0.01)
- **Two HFpEF subphenotypes by median ECV split (32.3%):** High-ECV group had significantly higher β (fibrosis-dominant pattern) with no baseline/exercise/echo differences otherwise; low-ECV group had higher arterial elastance (Ea) and a more pronounced hypertensive exercise response, with longer time-constant of active relaxation (τ) — i.e. a relaxation-dominant (not fibrosis-dominant) mechanism
- Late gadolinium enhancement (focal fibrosis) present in only 4/24 HFpEF patients (2 inferolateral midwall, 2 posterior-RV-insertion in hypertrophy) — diffuse (ECV) rather than focal (LGE) fibrosis is the dominant abnormality in this cohort

## Methods (brief)

Single-centre, Leipzig Heart Center, Germany. HFpEF (n=24) recruited by ESC consensus criteria; controls (n=12) were patients without HF symptoms undergoing angiography for suspected CAD, LVEF >50%, E/E' <8, normal NT-proBNP. Exclusion: significant CAD, acute coronary syndrome, prior cerebral ischaemia (1y), MRI contraindications, more-than-mild valvular disease, restrictive/constrictive disease, pregnancy. CMR: 1.5T Philips Intera, modified Look-Locker 3(3)5 pre-/15-min-post-contrast (gadobutrol) T1 mapping over all available short-axis slices; ECV = (ΔR1myocardium/ΔR1blood)·(1−hematocrit) per Arrheden method. Invasive: conductance-catheter PV loops via right femoral artery; end-systolic elastance (Ees) from ESPVR; load-independent stiffness constant (beta) extrapolated from EDPVR (EDP = C·e^(beta·EDV)); handgrip exercise (1 min) for afterload increase; IVC balloon occlusion for preload reduction. Readers blinded to other test results.

## Results

Full baseline, CMR, and PV-loop tables (Tables 1–3 of the source) plus the ECV-vs-beta correlation plot (r=0.75, P<0.01) and the 4-panel ECV-group comparison (ECV, ΔEDPVR, β, and τ across ECV>median, ECV<median, and control) are reported. Reproducibility of PV measurements assessed by Bland-Altman (all parameters within acceptable limits; coefficients of variation 13–34% depending on parameter).

## Limitations

Small sample (n=36 total, n=24 HFpEF) — the paper's own first stated limitation. Single centre. Controls were an angiography-referred population (not a fully healthy reference group) — possible residual confounding from subclinical CAD risk factors despite exclusion of significant stenosis. Cross-sectional design — no outcome/prognostic follow-up. Handgrip (not treadmill/cycle) exercise used for the invasive afterload challenge — may not fully replicate whole-body exertional physiology captured by CPET-based studies.
