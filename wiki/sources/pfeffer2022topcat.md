# Behind the Scenes of TOPCAT — Bending to Inform

> First-person narrative by the TOPCAT principal investigators explaining how post-hoc analyses revealed that data from Russia and Georgia were unreliable, and how Americas-only analysis supports spironolactone benefit in HFpEF.

**File:** `raw/2022-NEJM-Pfeffer-TOPCAT_behind_scenes.pdf` · **Authors:** Pfeffer MA, Claggett B · **Year:** 2022 · **Journal:** NEJM Evidence 1(1)

---

## Core Arguments

**Type:** Clinical Trials Case Study (first-person narrative; no new primary data beyond prior publications).

**Background:**
- TOPCAT (NCT00094302): n=3,445; 6 countries (USA, Canada, Argentina, Brazil, Russia, Georgia); enrollment Aug 2006–June 2013; mean follow-up 3.3 years. Primary outcome: CV death + aborted cardiac arrest + HF hospitalisation.
- Primary result: HR 0.89 (95% CI 0.77–1.04), P=0.14 — **not significant**.

**Discovery of data irregularities:**
- Pre-specified subgroup by eligibility strata revealed paradox: patients enrolled via recent hospitalisation (sicker) had *lower* event rates (6.0 vs 7.0/100 person-years) than those enrolled via natriuretic peptide criteria alone.
- Post-hoc covariate model adding geographic region: Americas patients had **3.96× higher event rates** than Russia/Georgia (HR 3.96, 95% CI 3.22–4.88), regardless of treatment assignment — unprecedented magnitude for international trial.
- Clues of non-compliance: Russia/Georgia enrolled 37.2 participants/site vs 9.4/site in Americas (brisk, suggesting lax eligibility). Spironolactone's pharmacologic effects (K+ increase, BP decrease, creatinine rise) were **absent** in Russia/Georgia patients despite reported adherence.
- **Canrenone analysis (2016):** 12-month blood samples tested for canrenone (active spironolactone metabolite, t½ 16.5 h): **30% of "spiro-treated" Russian patients had undetectable levels** vs only 3% in North America — direct pharmacokinetic evidence of non-adherence or non-enrollment of true HFpEF patients.
- Mortality rates in Russia/Georgia patients were indistinguishable from their age/sex-matched general populations — confirming these patients likely did not have HFpEF.

**Americas-only result:**
- Spironolactone associated with reduced CV death + HF hospitalisations: **HR 0.82 (95% CI 0.69–0.98)**

**Regulatory and guideline implications:**
- 2017 ACC/AHA/HFSA guidelines issued **Class IIb, Level B** recommendation for spironolactone in HFpEF based on Americas data.
- FDA Cardiovascular and Renal Drugs Advisory Committee (December 2020): voted **8 yes / 4 no / 1 abstention** that TOPCAT provides sufficient evidence for "any indication."
- Authors frame the Americas analysis as "best available imperfect data" — not SAP-compliant but informative.

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki
