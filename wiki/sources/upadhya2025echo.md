# The role of echocardiography in the diagnosis of HFpEF

> Comprehensive review of TTE in HFpEF diagnosis; emphasises that no single echocardiographic parameter defines HFpEF, that HFpEF pathophysiology extends beyond diastolic dysfunction to six mechanistic domains, and that the integration of structural, functional, and strain measures — particularly LA reservoir strain (LASr) — provides the most accurate non-invasive estimation of LVFP.

**Full citation:**
Upadhya B, Rose GA, Stacey RB, Palma RA, Ryan T, Pendyal A, Kelsey AM, Bhansali A, Kitzman DW. Role of echocardiography in the diagnosis and management of heart failure with preserved ejection fraction. *Heart Fail Rev.* 2025;30(4):899–922. doi:[10.1007/s10741-025-10516-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10741-025-10516-z)

## Core Arguments

**1. TTE is essential but no single parameter is sufficient:**
Unlike HFrEF (LVEF ≤40% is definitive), no single TTE variable defines HFpEF. Diastolic dysfunction per se is NOT synonymous with HFpEF — DD is common in normal aging and many non-HF conditions.

**2. HFpEF pathophysiology beyond diastolic dysfunction (six domains):**
1. LV/LA/RV dysfunction
2. Pulmonary hypertension and pulmonary vascular disease
3. Pericardial restraint
4. Abnormal systemic vascular impedance
5. Coronary or peripheral microvascular dysfunction
6. Defects in tissue oxygen uptake at the periphery

**3. Diagnostic algorithms — H₂FPEF and HFA-PEFF:**

| Score | Sensitivity | Specificity | Intermediate rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H₂FPEF | 52.7% | 82.5% | ~30–40% | Derived from invasive exercise hemodynamics; practical |
| HFA-PEFF | 70% | 90.5% | ~50% (2–4 zone) | 5–6 pts = diagnostic; 2–4 pts → invasive evaluation |

Both scores have significant intermediate rates. When H₂FPEF or HFA-PEFF is intermediate (2–4 points), invasive exercise hemodynamic evaluation is recommended.

**4. LVEF measurement challenges:**
- LVEF follows a U-shaped mortality curve; lowest mortality at LVEF 60–65%; any deviation associated with increased all-cause mortality
- Sex and race differences: AA men have lowest LVEF, GLS, and circumferential strain → LVEF 50% cutoff may overestimate LV dysfunction in African American men
- LVH (LV hypertrophy): 88% specific but only 26% sensitive for HFpEF diagnosis — no longer considered a fundamental feature
- Concentric AND eccentric LVH present in HFpEF; concentric more common in women
- LAVi misleading in obese HFpEF: larger BSA mathematically decreases LAVi even if absolute LAV is abnormally increased

**5. Echocardiographic parameters — clinically relevant (Table 3):**

| Parameter | Utility | Key limitation |
|---|---|---|
| E/e' ratio | Best single index for LVFP; odds ratio per unit increase 1.22 (CI 1.16–1.30) | Not applicable in AF; influenced by loading; specificity 91–98% at high thresholds but sensitivity only 24–37% |
| LASr (LA reservoir strain) | Superior to TRV for LVFP; third criteria in 2016 ASE/EACVI algorithm when LASr <18%; 99% classification rate | Replicability limited in real-world practice |
| LA stiffness (E/e'/LASr ratio) | Best TTE correlate of elevated filling pressures at rest or exertion | Requires speckle tracking; not universal |
| GLS (global longitudinal strain) | Subclinical systolic dysfunction in HFpEF despite preserved LVEF; minor HFA-PEFF criterion (GLS <-16% absolute) | Load-dependent; angle-dependent |
| TRV > 2.8 m/s | Indicates precapillary cause of elevated LVFP | PV Doppler often technically not feasible |
| LAVI | Chronic diastolic barometer ("hemoglobin A1C of heart diastolic function") | Misleading in obese patients; min LAV (not max) better reflects chronic LVFP exposure |

**6. LA minimum LAV vs. maximum LAV:**
Minimum LAV (not maximum) better reflects continuous exposure of the LA to chronically elevated LV pressures. Patients with permanent AF had LAV 35% larger than those in sinus rhythm even without LVDD.

**7. LA stiffness as the superior LVFP estimator:**
- LASr <18% added to 2016 ASE/EACVI algorithm: 99% of patients classified
- LA stiffness index (E/e'/LASr) best correlates with elevated filling pressures at rest and exercise
- Superior to TRV in LVFP estimation; included in EACVI 2022 multimodality imaging recommendations

**8. Exercise echocardiography:**
- LAVi increases, mitral E increases, E/e' increases during exercise in HFpEF
- E/e' at peak stress "highly demonstrative of HFpEF" — E/e' ratio peak stress
- e' remains low throughout exercise; Peak exercise a' significantly lower in HFpEF
- LA stiffness worsens with exercise (LASr decreases)
- E/e' >14 at rest: high specificity but poor sensitivity for LVFP; must be used as continuous variable

**9. HFpEF mimickers — TTE differential diagnosis (Table 1):**

| Mimicker | Key TTE features |
|---|---|
| Cardiac amyloidosis | ↓LV dimensions, ↑wall thickness, sparkling myocardium, ↓↓lateral e', ↓GLS with apical sparing |
| Cardiac sarcoidosis | Basal IVS thinning, RWMA not in CAD distribution, impaired LV strain in non-CAD pattern |
| Fabry disease | Hyper-refractile subendocardial border (binary sign), concentric LVH, GLS decreased in basal inferolateral wall |
| HCM | >↑LV cavity, asymmetric LVH (septal hypertrophy), dynamic LVOT obstruction, SAM; >15 mm wall for diagnosis |
| Constrictive pericarditis | Pericardial thickening, diastolic septal bounce, medial e' > lateral e' (annulus reversal), respiratory IVC variation |
| Stiff LA syndrome | ↑RVSP, no A wave, large E wave, short DT, no MVD; ↓↓LAsr/contractile strain; extremely small retrograde flow reversal during atrial contraction |

**10. HFpEF phenotyping by TTE (Table 4):**
- **Coronary artery disease phenotype:** LV segmental wall motion; ↑LVMi; adenosine stress echo for coronary flow reserve
- **LA-dysfunction phenotype:** ↓LVEDV, ↑LAVi; ↓a' and ↓A-wave; impaired LA function (LASr at rest or suboptimal increase with volume challenge); ↑LA stiffness
- **RV dysfunction phenotype:** Right-sided chamber dilation, ↓TAPSE, ↓RV FAC; lower TAPSE/RVSP ratio (<0.36 mm/mmHg)
- **PH/PVD phenotype:** ↑PASP, ↑PADP; ↑PVR; notching in mid-RVOT spectral Doppler
- **Obesity phenotype:** ↑LV dimensions/mass; biventricular hypertrophy; ↑epicardial fat thickness; pericardial restraint (septal bounce; eccentricity index)

## Contradictions
LASr vs. TRV hierarchy: Upadhya 2025 argues LASr is superior to TRV for LVFP estimation; the 2016 ASE/EACVI algorithm weights TRV equally with E/e' and LAVi. The EACVI 2022 multimodality recommendation includes LASr as a third substitute criterion when two of the primary three are unavailable. This is an evolving consensus, not a hard contradiction.
