# Litwin 2024 — Long-Term Safety and Outcomes After Atrial Shunting in HFpEF/HFmrEF: 5-Year (RCT-I) and 3-Year (RCT-II) Follow-Up

> Longest-duration follow-up of atrial shunt therapy for HFpEF/HFmrEF to date: at 5 years (RCT-I) and 3 years (RCT-II), device-related adverse events were low; the pre-specified "responder" subgroup (peak exercise PVR <1.74 WU, no cardiac rhythm management device) continued to show significantly fewer HF events and greater KCCQ improvement with shunt vs. sham through 3 years, while the overall (unselected) RCT-II trial population remained neutral on its primary composite endpoint.

**Full citation:**
Litwin SE, Komtebedde J, Borlaug BA, Kaye DM, Hasenfuß G, Kawash R, Hoendermis E, Hummel SL, Cikes M, Gustafsson F, Chung ES, Mohan RC, Sverdlov AL, Swarup V, Winkler S, Hayward CS, Bergmann MW, Bugger H, McKenzie S, Nair A, Rieth A, Burkhoff D, Cutlip DE, Solomon SD, van Veldhuisen DJ, Leon MB, Shah SJ. Long term safety and outcomes after atrial shunting for heart failure with preserved or mildly reduced ejection fraction: 5-year and 3-year follow-up in the REDUCE LAP-HF I and II trials. *Am Heart J.* 2024;278:106–116. doi:[10.1016/j.ahj.2024.08.014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ahj.2024.08.014)

## Summary

Multicentre follow-up analysis (34 co-authors across the REDUCE LAP-HF I and II investigator group) reporting the longest-duration outcome data available for any atrial shunt device trial in HF: 5-year follow-up of RCT-I (mechanistic trial, n=44, NCT02600234) and 3-year follow-up of RCT-II (pivotal trial, n=621, NCT03088033) of the Corvia Atrial Shunt System (IASD System II) in HFpEF/HFmrEF (LVEF >40%). (source: litwin2024reducelaphf)

## REDUCE LAP-HF I: 5-Year Outcomes

- RCT-I: multicentre, prospective, randomised, double-blind, sham-controlled (n=44 randomised, 42 per-protocol); primary efficacy endpoint was change from baseline to 1 month in exercise PCWP
- At 5 years: **no differences between shunt and sham** in CV mortality, HF events, embolic stroke, or new-onset atrial fibrillation
- CV death rate: 3.59 per 100 patient-years (shunt) vs. 5.03 per 100 patient-years (sham); P=.679
- HF-related deaths through 60 months: 0% (0/20) shunt vs. 10.53% (2/19) sham; P=.231
- One ischaemic stroke event in the shunt group (5.00%, 1/20) vs. none in sham (0.00%, 0/19)
- No new-onset AF difference (5.00% shunt vs. 5.26% sham through 60 months); no device embolisation, migration, or need for implant removal/shunt closure in any shunt-treated patient
- Study underpowered for clinical events (main limitation) — reassuring on long-term device safety, not statistically definitive on efficacy

## REDUCE LAP-HF II: 3-Year Outcomes — Overall Trial and Responder Subgroup

**Overall RCT-II trial:** 621 patients (modified intention-to-treat) randomised to Corvia Atrial Shunt (n=309) vs. sham (n=312). At 1 year, there was **no difference in the primary outcome between groups**. At 2 and 3 years, there was **no difference in the primary efficacy endpoint** (3-year win ratio 1.04, 95% CI 0.83–1.30) **between the shunt and sham groups in the overall (unselected) trial population** — i.e., the overall trial result remained neutral through 3 years, consistent with the previously published primary RCT-II results (Shah et al., *Lancet* 2022;399:1130–40 — cited as reference 5; primary numeric results of that paper are not reported in this follow-up paper and have not yet been separately ingested into this wiki).

**Responder subgroup (post hoc, defined after 1-year follow-up in RCT-II):** peak exercise pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) ≤1.74 Wood units **and** absence of a cardiac rhythm management (CRM) device at baseline (pacemaker/ICD) — 50% of randomised patients (n=313: 161 shunt, 152 control).

At 3 years in the responder group (Table 2, win ratio components, treatment vs. sham):
- **Win ratio 1.6 (95% CI 1.2–2.2), P=.006**
- CV death: 4.5% (7) shunt vs. 2.1% (3) sham; P=.298 (not significant, small numbers)
- Nonfatal ischaemic stroke: 1.9% (3) shunt vs. 0.0% (0) sham; P=.099
- Total rate of HF events: 9 per 100 patient-years (shunt) vs. 16 per 100 patient-years (sham); **44% relative reduction**, incidence rate ratio 0.587 (95% CI 0.401–0.859), P=.005
- Absolute risk reduction 7.7%, relative risk reduction 23%, **number needed to treat = 13** at 3 years (NNT 18 at 1 year, 15 at 2 years — benefit strengthens over time)
- Change in KCCQ-OSS from baseline to 36 months: +17.9 (95% CI +13.8, +21.4) shunt vs. +7.6 (95% CI +3.3, +11.8) sham; between-group difference **+10.1 (95% CI +4.3, +15.8), P<.001**
- All KCCQ subdomains (physical limitation, symptom stability/frequency/burden, self-efficacy, QoL, social limitation, overall/clinical summary) favoured shunt at 3 years, all P≤.061 (most P<.02)
- Nonresponders (the other 50%) did **not** derive clinical benefit and had **more** HF events with shunt than with sham

**Safety in the responder group at 3 years (Table 5, n=313):**
- Primary safety endpoint (CV mortality or nonfatal ischaemic stroke): 6.29% shunt vs. 2.22% sham; P=.152 (not significant)
- **Any ischaemic stroke: 3.2% (95% CI 0.2–6.1%) shunt vs. 0.0% (95% CI 0.0–0.0%) sham; P=.032** — a real safety signal in the responder population despite overall device safety being reassuring
- **Worsening kidney dysfunction (eGFR decrease >20 mL/min/1.73m²): 10.7% shunt vs. 19.3% sham; P=.041** — favours shunt (fewer adverse renal events, plausibly related to fewer HF events/less diuretic escalation)
- All-cause death 8.81% shunt vs. 5.19% sham (P=.352); no significant difference in new-onset AF/flutter or MACE
- No device embolisation, migration, or implant removal events reported

Stroke cause: among 5 shunt-arm ischaemic strokes, 3/5 had current or prior AF, all had CHA₂DS₂-VASc 3–7, but only 1/5 was on oral anticoagulation at time of stroke — raises the question of undertreated stroke risk/paradoxical embolism in this population rather than necessarily a shunt-specific mechanism.

## Discussion / Interpretation

- Authors explicitly frame RCT-II as "an overall neutral study," so the responder-subgroup outcomes — while durable to 3 years — **"do not carry the same level of strength as they would if the overall trial had shown beneficial results."**
- The responder phenotype (defined by absence of latent pulmonary vascular disease and absence of a CRM device) is reinforced as a durable, physiologically grounded treatment-selection marker: right heart/pulmonary vasculature's ability to accommodate the shunted volume is proposed as the key determinant of shunt success.
- Frames the RCT-II responder data as motivating "ongoing clinical trials of this and other devices" — no specific successor trial name or NCT number is given in this paper.
