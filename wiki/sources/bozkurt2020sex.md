# Substance and Substrate: LVEF and Sex Subgroup Analyses of PARAGON-HF and PARADIGM-HF Trials

> Editorial commentary on pooled PARADIGM-HF + PARAGON-HF analyses showing sacubitril-valsartan benefit wanes with increasing LVEF and may extend to higher LVEF in women, raising unresolved questions about biological vs. statistical sex differences in ARNi response.

**Full citation:**
Bozkurt B, Ezekowitz J. Substance and Substrate: LVEF and Sex Subgroup Analyses of PARAGON-HF and PARADIGM-HF Trials. *Circulation.* 2020;141(5):362–366. doi:[10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.120.045008](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.120.045008)

---

## Core Arguments

**Type:** Editorial (no original patient data); comments on two companion publications in the same Circulation issue: Solomon et al. (pooled PARADIGM-HF + PARAGON-HF, n=13,195) and McMurray et al. (PARAGON-HF sex analysis, n=4,822).

**Key findings summarised from cited analyses:**
- In the pooled PARADIGM-HF + PARAGON-HF analysis (Solomon et al.), sacubitril-valsartan benefit was greatest at lower LVEF, waned with increasing LVEF, and was statistically significant (for CV death + HFH combined) only in patients with LVEF ≤40% across standard categorical thresholds.
- By continuous LVEF analysis (cubic spline), the upper threshold of benefit extended to approximately LVEF 42.5% by decile analyses; restricted cubic spline suggested potential benefit approaching normal LVEF, **especially in women**.
- In PARAGON-HF women-specific analysis (McMurray et al.): women = 52% of PARAGON-HF; sacubitril-valsartan reduced HF hospitalisations in **women** (HR not stated explicitly; risk significant) but **not men**; CV death not reduced in either sex.
- Women in PARAGON-HF had: higher LVEF, lower NT-proBNP despite worse symptoms, more obesity, less CAD, lower eGFR.
- The upper LVEF threshold of loss of ARNi benefit was **~60% in women vs. ~45–50% in men** across both pooled and PARAGON-HF sex analyses.
- KCCQ improvement even less in women than men with sacubitril-valsartan.
- Women had **higher risk of new-onset AF** with sacubitril-valsartan vs. valsartan; men had lower risk.
- NT-proBNP changes and urinary cGMP/creatinine increases were similar by sex — arguing against pharmacokinetic sex differences in neprilysin inhibition.

**Editorial perspective (Bozkurt & Ezekowitz):**
- Authors urge caution: LVEF was not measured by core lab in PARAGON-HF; post hoc analyses cannot define practice thresholds.
- Potential biological explanation: estrogen may upregulate neprilysin expression and inhibit adrenal angiotensin-receptor binding, thus enhancing sacubitril efficacy and reducing valsartan efficacy in premenopausal/perimenopausal women — but this is speculative.
- Alternatively, sex differences at higher LVEF may reflect presence of contractile dysfunction extending to higher LVEF measurements in women (given women's normally higher LVEF).
- Conclusion: Future randomised trials specifically in women and HFmEF are needed.

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

This editorial contextualises the most discussed sex-LVEF interaction in HFpEF pharmacotherapy. The finding that women with LVEF up to ~60% may benefit from sacubitril-valsartan (if confirmed) would substantially reshape HFpEF treatment thresholds. It also highlights the clinical conundrum of women's paradoxically lower NT-proBNP despite worse symptoms — affecting trial eligibility and natriuretic peptide-based diagnosis.
