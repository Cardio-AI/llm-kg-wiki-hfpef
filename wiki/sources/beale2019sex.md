# Sex Differences in Heart Failure With Preserved Ejection Fraction Pathophysiology: A Detailed Invasive Hemodynamic and Echocardiographic Analysis

> Women with HFpEF have markedly greater exercise-related PCWP elevation, lower systemic and pulmonary vascular compliance, and worse peripheral oxygen kinetics than men, revealing a more severe and distinct haemodynamic phenotype.

**Full citation:**
Beale AL, Nanayakkara S, Segan L, Chan WY, Kiley M, Mariani JA, Lam CSP, Kaye DM. Sex Differences in Heart Failure With Preserved Ejection Fraction Pathophysiology: A Detailed Invasive Hemodynamic and Echocardiographic Analysis. *JACC Heart Fail.* 2019;7(3):239–249. doi:[10.1016/j.jchf.2019.01.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jchf.2019.01.004)

## Core Arguments

**Study:** Prospective cohort; n=161 (114 women [71%], 47 men); Alfred Hospital Melbourne; May 2008–September 2018. Exercise right heart catheterisation (RHC) + echocardiography; weight-corrected cycle ergometry protocol (0.3 W/kg rising to 1 W/kg). HFpEF defined: EF ≥50% + resting PCWP ≥15 or exercise PCWP ≥25 mmHg.

**Key hemodynamic findings (women vs. men):**
- PCWP indexed to workload: **0.8 [0.5–1.2] vs 0.6 [0.4–1.0] mmHg/W** (p=0.014)
- PCWP indexed to stroke volume at exercise: significantly higher in women (p=0.008)
- Peak exercise stroke volume index (SVI): **35 [27–52] vs 47.5 [38–61] ml/m²** (p=0.08); Δ SVI lower in women (8.1 vs 19.7 ml, p=0.007)
- Systemic compliance at rest: **1.3 [0.9–1.5] vs 1.1 [0.9–1.2] ml/mmHg** (p<0.001); lower in women
- Pulmonary compliance at rest: **4.5 vs 3.4 ml/mmHg** (p=0.039); lower in women
- Arterial elastance (Eal) higher in women at exercise: 1.5 vs 1.2 mmHg/ml (p<0.001 when indexed to BSA)
- End-systolic elastance (Eesl) higher in women at rest and exercise; Δ Eesl 2.4 vs 1.5 (p=0.016)

**Key echocardiographic findings (n=60 subset with simultaneous echo):**
- E/e' mean at rest: **13.4 vs 9.2** (p=0.002)
- E/e' mean at exercise: **14.1 vs 9.3** (p<0.001)
- E/e' lateral at exercise: **12.7 vs 9.3** (p<0.001)
- LVEF higher in women at rest (62±5% vs 60±7%, p=0.018)

**Peripheral oxygen kinetics:**
- Exercise lactate indexed to workload: **0.09 [0.07–0.13] vs 0.04 [0.06–0.08] mmol/l/W** (p=0.003) — women produce more lactate per watt, indicating greater anaerobic metabolism and poorer peripheral O₂ utilisation
- No significant sex differences in mixed venous oxygen saturation or oxygen exchange ratios

**Conclusions:** Women with HFpEF demonstrate poorer diastolic reserve (greater PCWP elevation per workload), lower systemic and pulmonary arterial compliance, smaller stroke volume reserve, and poorer peripheral oxygen kinetics vs. men — a more severe and mechanistically distinct haemodynamic phenotype.

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

Provides invasive hemodynamic evidence that sex differences in HFpEF are mechanistically real, not merely epidemiological. Women have a distinct exercise hemodynamic signature: high filling pressures per workload + low compliance + poor peripheral O₂ kinetics. Supports the case for sex-stratified diagnostic thresholds and phenotype-guided therapy. Complements Borlaug 2010 (supine CPET) and Landsteiner 2025 (upright PCWP/CO slope) but adds the sex-comparison dimension.
