# Integrated Left Ventricular Multi-Omics Landscape of Human Cardiometabolic HFpEF (Capone 2026 / HFpEF-PHT)

> Left-ventricular endomyocardial-biopsy multi-omics (proteomics, metabolomics, lipidomics) in overweight/obese HFpEF (n=19) vs. non-failing overweight/obese controls (NFO, n=4) shows HFpEF-specific, obesity-independent myocardial remodelling: blocked proximal glycolysis, succinate accumulation with reduced fumarate hydratase, an energy-deprivation signature (↑AMP/ADP), and upregulated ECM/fibrosis proteins led by EFEMP1 (fibulin-3) — while fatty-acid oxidation and glucose oxidation are largely preserved and obesity-driven rather than HFpEF-specific.

**Study type:** Prospective observational, multi-omics (proteomics + metabolomics + lipidomics) biopsy sub-study · **N:** 23 (19 overweight/obese HFpEF, 4 non-failing overweight/obese controls [NFO]) · **Population:** Recruited 2019–2021 at the Heart Centre Leipzig, HFpEF-PHT cohort (NCT05055180); HFpEF suspected per 2016 ESC guidelines, confirmed by invasive LVEDP >15 mmHg at rest and/or ≥25 mmHg with exercise; NFO excluded for suspected HFpEF · **Follow-up:** None (cross-sectional) · **Intervention/Exposure:** Left-ventricular endomyocardial biopsy (EMB) — global proteomics, targeted metabolomics/nucleotide analysis (73 metabolites/nucleotides), untargeted lipidomics (377 lipid species) — integrated with echo, CMR, invasive haemodynamics, and circulating biomarkers · **Primary outcome:** Not a hypothesis-tested primary endpoint — hypothesis-generating multi-omics characterisation of cardiometabolic HFpEF myocardium vs. NFO

## Key Findings

- **HFpEF group vs. NFO (Table 1):** older (74 vs. 61y, P<0.001), more atrial fibrillation (68.4% vs. 0%, P=0.012), higher NT-proBNP (1002 vs. 55 pg/mL, P=0.009), higher creatinine (111 vs. 75 µmol/L, P=0.016), higher intramyocardial fibrosis on histology (13.2% vs. 7.9%, P<0.001), higher E/e′ (13.8 vs. 8.5, P<0.001), higher invasive LV end-diastolic pressure (17 vs. 11 mmHg, P<0.001) — BMI, sex, LVEF, and diabetes prevalence were not significantly different between groups
- **Proteomics (4,879 proteins quantified, 72 differentially expressed):** GO enrichment showed up-regulation of extracellular matrix organisation/ECM structure proteins and down-regulation of nucleotide-biosynthesis/ATP-metabolic-process proteins in HFpEF vs. NFO; no significant phosphoproteomic differences between groups
- **Glycolysis blocked at the proximal step:** glucose-6-phosphate (G6P) accumulated while pyruvate (the glycolytic end-product) was lower in HFpEF hearts, with reduced protein levels of GPI and PFKM1 — the key rate-limiting glycolytic enzymes — despite similar substrate availability
- **Glucose oxidation preserved, obesity-driven not HFpEF-specific:** pyruvate dehydrogenase (PDH) phosphorylation and PDK4 protein levels were similar between HFpEF and NFO — i.e., reduced glucose oxidation seen in HFpEF vs. lean controls in other cohorts appears attributable to the obesity component of the phenotype, not to HFpEF itself
- **TCA cycle/succinate accumulation:** significant accumulation of succinate, increased succinate/fumarate ratio, decreased fumarate/malate ratio, and reduced fumarate hydratase (FH) protein in HFpEF vs. NFO — interpreted as a marker of reduced complex II (succinate dehydrogenase) activity and impaired oxygen availability ("pseudohypoxia")
- **Energy-deprivation signature:** significantly increased AMP and ADP, and altered ATP/ADP ratio, in HFpEF vs. NFO; no difference in NAD⁺, NADH, or NADH/NAD⁺ ratio between groups
- **Pentose phosphate pathway:** G6P accumulation with reduced NADPH/NADP⁺ ratio despite unchanged PPP enzyme levels, suggesting increased NADPH utilisation or insufficient production
- **Lipidomics (377 species):** medium/long-chain acylcarnitines largely unchanged between groups (preserved mitochondrial long-chain fatty-acid oxidation), with selective remodelling confined to very-long-chain acylcarnitine AcCa(21:4) (reduced in HFpEF) and modest phospholipid/cardiolipin changes
- **ECM/fibrosis proteins up in HFpEF:** LTBP1, IGFBP2, EFEMP1 (fibulin-3), FBLN5, POSTN among the most significantly upregulated; **EFEMP1 showed the strongest overall association with disease severity** — correlated with higher histological fibrosis (R²=0.36, P=0.009), lower LVEF (R²=0.34, P=0.006), higher E/e′ (R²=0.25, P=0.005), and higher NT-proBNP (R²=0.61, P<0.001)
- Findings were robust to sensitivity analyses adjusting for BMI, age, and type 2 diabetes status, and to subgroup analyses restricted to BMI-matched or non-diabetic participants

## Methods (brief)

Single-centre (Heart Centre Leipzig), prospective observational HFpEF-PHT cohort (NCT05055180), 2019–2021. HFpEF suspected per 2016 ESC guidelines in outpatients with NYHA ≥II, LVEF ≥50%, structural/functional evidence of LV diastolic dysfunction, BMI ≥25 kg/m²; confirmed by invasive LVEDP >15 mmHg rest and/or ≥25 mmHg exercise. Exclusions: relevant coronary artery disease, moderate/severe valvular disease, MRI contraindications, confirmed/suspected cardiac amyloidosis. LV EMBs harvested via bioptome, split for histology (formalin-fixed) and snap-frozen multi-omics. Proteomics: DIA mass spectrometry (Spectronaut), Perseus/R downstream analysis, limma-based moderated t-testing, Benjamini-Hochberg correction, padj<0.1 significance threshold. Metabolomics: 73 metabolites/nucleotides via direct-infusion MS, cinnamic-acid internal control, Mann-Whitney testing, P<0.05 significance. Lipidomics: untargeted UHPLC-MS/MS (Orbitrap Exploris 480), LipidBlast/LipidSearch annotation, Mann-Whitney testing. Sensitivity analyses: linear regression models adjusting for BMI, age, T2DM status; BMI-matched and non-diabetic subgroup analyses.

## Limitations

Very small NFO control group (n=4, one further excluded from proteomics for technical failure) reflecting the difficulty of obtaining invasive LV biopsies from non-failing individuals — the paper's own first stated limitation, driving wide inter-individual variability and limiting statistical power for group comparisons. HFpEF and NFO groups differed at baseline in age, BMI trend, and T2DM prevalence despite matching efforts — addressed via sensitivity/subgroup analyses, but residual confounding cannot be fully excluded. Cross-sectional design — no outcome or prognostic follow-up. Single-centre. Correlative, not causal — proteomic/metabolomic associations with clinical severity parameters are hypothesis-generating, not mechanistically proven in this cohort. Ketone body oxidation not thoroughly investigated.
