# Haykowsky 2011 — Determinants of Exercise Intolerance in HFpEF

> In 48 elderly HFpEF patients vs. 25 age-matched healthy controls, peak VO₂ was 30% lower in HFpEF (14.3 vs. 20.4 ml/kg/min); both reduced peak CO and reduced A-VO₂ Diff contributed; critically, the **change in A-VO₂ Diff from rest to peak exercise was the strongest independent predictor of peak VO₂** — implicating peripheral noncardiac factors as primary determinants of exercise intolerance.

**Sites:** Wake Forest University Medical Center (Winston-Salem, NC); University of Alberta (Edmonton); University of Toledo, Ohio  
**Population:** HFpEF — NHANES HF clinical score ≥3; LVEF ≥50%; no CAD, valvular disease, anemia, renal disease, pulmonary disease; NYHA II–III; mean age 69±6y; 85% female; BMI 30.6±6.0

---

## Key Findings

- **Peak VO₂ (primary):** 14.3±0.5 (HFpEF) vs. 20.4±0.6 ml/kg/min (HC); P<0.0001 — 30% reduction
- **Peak CO:** 6.3±0.2 (HFpEF) vs. 7.6±0.2 L/min (HC); P<0.0001
- **Peak A-VO₂ Diff:** 17±0.4 (HFpEF) vs. 19±0.4 ml/dl (HC); P=0.0007 — reduced peripheral O₂ extraction
- **Strongest independent predictor of peak VO₂: change in A-VO₂ Diff from rest to peak exercise (A-VO₂ Diff reserve):**
  - HFPEF: partial correlate 0.58; standardized β 0.66; P=0.0002
  - HCs: partial correlate 0.61; standardized β 0.41; P=0.005
- **Peak HR change more correlated with peak VO₂ than peak SV change in HFpEF** (partial correlate 0.53 vs. 0.35) — chronotropic response is important
- **EDV reserve during exercise**: % change in EDV from rest to peak greater in HFPEF vs HC (10.3±2.0% vs. 2.9±2.1%; P=0.03) — HFpEF relies more on Frank-Starling (EDV reserve), while HCs rely on ESV reserve (contractility); different mechanism, similar net result at submaximal exercise
- CO similar at submaximal workloads; diverges only at peak — cardiac reserve insufficient at peak

## Methods (brief)

**Exercise protocol:** Upright cycle ergometry; initial 12.5 W, increased to 25 W for 3 min, then 25-W increments every 3 min to exhaustion. Expired gas analysis (Medgraphics CPX). Simultaneous 2D echocardiography (Sonos 5500) — resting and during exercise (at 12W and 25W stages).

**Echo methodology:** LV volumes (EDV, ESV) by single-plane ellipsoid; SV = EDV − ESV; CO = SV × CO; A-VO₂ Diff = VO₂/CO (Fick equation); only patients with adequate acoustic windows included (48/59 HFpEF, 25/28 HC).

## Limitations

- Predominantly white female cohort — does not represent the full HFpEF phenotypic spectrum (e.g., obese Hispanic/Black males)
- A-VO₂ Diff calculated indirectly via Fick equation (VO₂/CO) rather than by direct invasive oximetry — overestimates true A-VO₂ Diff vs. invasive methods; relative comparisons remain valid
- Peak SV and CO may be underestimated due to technical difficulties acquiring adequate echo windows during peak exercise (only patients with adequate images included)
- No skeletal muscle biopsy or peripheral flow measurements — peripheral mechanism inferred from A-VO₂ Diff reserve, not directly measured
- Cross-sectional design; cannot determine whether reduced A-VO₂ Diff reserve is primary or secondary to deconditioning

## Contradictions

At rest, HFpEF patients had significantly higher LVEF than HCs (59% vs. 53%; P<0.0001) despite having more hypertrophic wall thickness and smaller EDV — suggesting preserved resting EF is maintained by hyperdynamic state, not normal LV mechanics. The divergent mechanisms of SV reserve during exercise (EDV reserve in HFpEF vs. ESV reserve in HCs) produce similar submaximal CO but different peak exercise capacity — suggesting the Frank-Starling compensation is finite and inadequate at high workloads.
