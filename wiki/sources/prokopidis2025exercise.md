# Exercise Training in HFpEF vs HFrEF (Prokopidis 2025)

> Systematic review and meta-analysis (46 studies; inception to October 2024) comparing exercise training effects in HFpEF vs HFrEF: HFpEF had higher VO₂peak (+0.78 mL/kg/min; P=0.02) and cardiac output (+1.15 L/min), but lower 6MWD (−18.09 m; P=0.03) than HFrEF; after comorbidity adjustment, VO₂peak and 6MWD differences became non-significant; age was a significant moderator.

## Core Arguments

1. **HFpEF had marginally higher VO₂peak than HFrEF.** WMD +0.78 mL/kg/min (k=20 comparisons, I²=89%, P=0.02). However, this became non-significant after adjusting for comorbidities — suggesting the apparent VO₂ difference reflects comorbidity burden rather than EF-specific physiology. (source: raw/2025-ESC-Prokopidis-exercise_HFrEF_vs_HFpEF_systematic_review.pdf)

2. **HFrEF had higher 6MWD than HFpEF.** WMD −18.09 m (k=20, I²=86%, P=0.03) in favour of HFrEF. Became non-significant after comorbidity adjustment. (source: raw/2025-ESC-Prokopidis-exercise_HFrEF_vs_HFpEF_systematic_review.pdf)

3. **HFpEF had higher cardiac output after exercise.** Pooled cardiac output: HFpEF higher by 1.15 L/min (k=12, P=0.02). Stroke volume also higher in HFpEF (SMD −1.00, k=14, P<0.01). Despite worse diastolic function and higher filling pressures, HFpEF maintains higher cardiac output and SV response than HFrEF. (source: raw/2025-ESC-Prokopidis-exercise_HFrEF_vs_HFpEF_systematic_review.pdf)

4. **Age is a significant moderator of VO₂peak response.** Meta-regression: age significantly moderated VO₂peak difference (z=2.19, P=0.03) — older patients with HFpEF have worse VO₂peak response, consistent with the accelerated sarcopenia and chronotropic incompetence with age. (source: raw/2025-ESC-Prokopidis-exercise_HFrEF_vs_HFpEF_systematic_review.pdf)

5. **Publication bias detected for cardiac output; not for VO₂peak, SV, or 6MWD.** Suggests CO effect size may be inflated; VO₂peak and 6MWD estimates are more reliable. (source: raw/2025-ESC-Prokopidis-exercise_HFrEF_vs_HFpEF_systematic_review.pdf)

6. **Comorbidity burden explains most EF-type differences.** After adjustment, the between-phenotype differences in VO₂peak and 6MWD disappear — HFpEF and HFrEF patients with comparable comorbidity profiles respond similarly to exercise training. This supports the concept that comorbidities, not EF itself, drive exercise capacity differences. (source: raw/2025-ESC-Prokopidis-exercise_HFrEF_vs_HFpEF_systematic_review.pdf)

## Methods (brief)

- Systematic search: inception to October 2024 (46 studies included)
- Population: HFpEF (LVEF ≥50%) vs HFrEF (LVEF ≤40%)
- Outcomes: VO₂peak, 6MWD, cardiac output, stroke volume
- Statistics: random-effects model; meta-regression for moderators; I² for heterogeneity
- High heterogeneity (I²=86–89%) across VO₂peak and 6MWD outcomes

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

- Provides comparative perspective: exercise training effects in HFpEF are not substantially different from HFrEF when comorbidities are controlled for
- The higher CO and SV in HFpEF post-exercise reflects preserved systolic reserve in HFpEF vs HFrEF
- Age as a moderator has implications for trial design (older populations may show attenuated VO₂ response)
- Supports the "comorbidity-driven" model of HFpEF rather than the "EF-type-driven" model
