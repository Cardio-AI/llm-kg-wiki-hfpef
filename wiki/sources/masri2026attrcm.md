# ATTR-CM Trials Systematic Review (Masri 2026)

> Masri 2026 (87-page systematic review, Progress Cardiovasc Dis): comprehensive synthesis of ATTR-CM clinical trials across three therapeutic classes — TTR stabilizers (tafamidis, acoramidis), RNA silencers (siRNA: patisiran/vutrisiran; ASO: eplontersen), and degraders/clearance agents — confirming multiple approved treatment options and that early diagnosis before structural deterioration maximises benefit.

## Core Arguments

**Scope:** Systematic review of all completed major ATTR-CM clinical trials as of 2026. Covers wild-type ATTR-CM (ATTRwt-CM) and variant/hereditary ATTR-CM (ATTRv-CM); cardiac outcomes focused. Includes 87 pages of trial data synthesis across three therapeutic mechanistic classes.

---

### Class 1: TTR Stabilizers

**Tafamidis (ATTR-ACT, NCT01994889; Maurer 2018, NEJM; [[maurer2018attract]]):**
- N=441; tafamidis 80 mg vs. 20 mg vs. placebo; 30 months
- All-cause mortality: RR **0.70** (P<0.001); HF hospitalisation: RR 0.68
- First approved disease-modifying therapy for ATTR-CM; AHA 2022 Class I
- Benefit largest in NYHA I–II; greatest absolute benefit in wild-type; preserved benefit in ATTRv (including V122I African American variant)

**Acoramidis (ATTRibute-CM; NCT03860935):**
- More potent kinetic stabiliser than tafamidis (near-complete TTR stabilisation vs. ~50% with tafamidis)
- Phase 3 trial completed; survival benefit confirmed; CV hospitalisation reduction
- FDA approved 2024; superior plasma TTR stabilisation compared with tafamidis

---

### Class 2: RNA Silencers — siRNA (RNAi-mediated TTR knockdown)

**Patisiran (APOLLO-B, NCT03997383):**
- Monthly IV infusion; reduces hepatic TTR production by >80%
- Population: ATTR-CM (NYHA II–III, elevated NT-proBNP); N~300
- 12-month results: 6MWT improved, KCCQ improved, NT-proBNP reduced; functional outcomes positive
- First siRNA with cardiac outcomes evidence in ATTR-CM; FDA label expanded for cardiac indication

**Vutrisiran (HELIOS-B, NCT04153149):**
- Quarterly SC injection (more convenient than patisiran monthly IV)
- HELIOS-B (2024–2025): cardiovascular death + HF hospitalisation composite: HR **0.72** (P<0.001)
- Established vutrisiran as first RNA silencer with proven *event-based* cardiac outcome reduction in ATTR-CM
- Approved FDA 2024 for cardiac ATTR indication

---

### Class 3: RNA Silencers — ASO (Antisense oligonucleotide)

**Eplontersen (CARDION trials, NCT04882735 and others):**
- Enhanced generation ASO with GalNAc conjugate for hepatocyte targeting; monthly SC injection
- Reduces TTR production; Phase 3 cardiac outcomes trial ongoing/published 2026
- Better tolerability profile than earlier inotersen (renal/platelet issues resolved)

**Inotersen (NEURO-TTR):**
- Cardiomyopathy data suggestive but renal and platelet safety concerns limited cardiac adoption; eplontersen supersedes in practice

---

### Class 4: Emerging degraders and gene editing

- **Small-molecule degraders:** investigational; degrade TTR amyloid fibrils rather than preventing formation
- **CRISPR/base-editing approaches (NTLA-2001, BEACON study):** single-dose gene editing of *TTR* gene in hepatocytes; profound TTR reduction (>90%); Phase 1/2 results promising; potential for permanent single-treatment cure

---

### Cross-trial themes

1. **Early treatment advantage:** Structural progression (increasing LV wall thickness, declining GLS) is arrested with treatment but may not fully reverse; patients treated at NYHA I–II show substantially more benefit than NYHA III
2. **ATTR-HFpEF overlap:** 13–19% of unselected HFpEF patients may have ATTR-CM (autopsy/biopsy series); Tc-99m-PYP scintigraphy is non-invasive diagnostic gold standard (Grade 3+ = ATTR without biopsy); active exclusion is now standard in HFpEF workup ([[kittleson2024accaha]] QM-6)
3. **Multiple simultaneous options:** For the first time, clinicians must choose among stabilizers, silencers, and emerging agents — treatment selection guided by cardiac vs. neurological phenotype, convenience, cost, and tolerability
4. **Wild-type vs. variant:** Treatment benefit is broadly consistent across ATTR subtypes; V122I (African American variant; ~3.43% of Black Americans ≥60y) is underdiagnosed and undertreated
5. **Treatment effect size:** Larger event-rate reductions (HR 0.70–0.72) than most HFpEF pharmacological interventions; confirms ATTR-CM as a distinctly tractable HFpEF subphenotype

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

ATTR-CM is a critical HFpEF subphenotype (13–19% prevalence). This systematic review substantially updates the previous wiki coverage of ATTR-CM therapy — which was limited to tafamidis (ATTR-ACT, [[maurer2018attract]]). The post-2018 evidence base (APOLLO-B patisiran, HELIOS-B vutrisiran, ATTRibute-CM acoramidis, eplontersen) positions ATTR-CM as now fully tractable with multiple mechanistic classes. This directly informs [[kittleson2024accaha]] QM-6 (amyloid screening in HFpEF), [[kittleson2023acc]] (Table 1 HFpEF mimics), and the ATTR-CM entity [[attr-cm]].

The large treatment effect sizes (HR 0.70–0.72) contrast with the modest benefits seen with SGLT2i in the broader HFpEF population (HR 0.79–0.80), reinforcing that ATTR-CM is a mechanistically distinct and pharmacologically targetable subphenotype.
