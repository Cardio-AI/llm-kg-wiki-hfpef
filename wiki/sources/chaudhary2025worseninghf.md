# Chaudhary 2025 — Expanded Definition of Worsening Heart Failure

> Post hoc analysis of REDUCE LAP-HF II (n=621) expanding the definition of worsening heart failure (WHF) beyond hospitalization+IV diuretics (level 1) to include urgent outpatient encounters with IV or intensified oral diuretics (level 2) and nonurgent encounters with any oral diuretic augmentation (level 3): expanding the definition raised the WHF event rate from 17.4% to 25.9% and turned a nonsignificant atrial-shunt treatment effect into a significant one in the pre-specified "responder" subgroup, but level 2/3 events added little independent mortality-prediction value beyond level 1 alone.

## Core Arguments

**Trial and design:** Post hoc secondary analysis of [[reduce-lap-hf-ii]] (NCT03088033) — the randomised, double-blind, sham-controlled trial of the Corvia interatrial shunt device (IASD) vs. sham in HF with LVEF ≥40% and exercise PCWP ≥25 mmHg (with PCWP–RA gradient ≥5 mmHg). Modified intention-to-treat (MITT) population, N=**621** (309 shunt, 312 sham), followed to 24 months.

**Expanded WHF taxonomy (hierarchical, adjudicated by blinded CEC plus post hoc re-review by 3 authors):**
- **Level 1** — hospitalisation requiring IV diuretics (the conventional, "standard" WHF definition used in most prior trials)
- **Level 2** — urgent outpatient encounter (e.g., emergency department) treated with IV diuretic agents or intensification of oral diuretic agents (defined as doubling a prior loop diuretic dose, adding a new loop diuretic, or adding a thiazide-type diuretic to a loop diuretic)
- **Level 3** — any other clinical encounter with ≥1 documented HF symptom (dyspnoea, orthopnoea, PND, oedema, weight gain) plus ≥2 objective findings (tachycardia, elevated BNP, chest X-ray evidence of HF, elevated JVP, rales, wheezing, hepatomegaly, abdominal distension) and any escalation of HF therapy — i.e., nonurgent, routine-visit diuretic augmentation not meeting level 1/2 criteria

Three composite definitions were tested: **Level 1 alone** (standard); **Level 1 or 2**; and **Any WHF** (level 1, 2, or 3).

**Responder definition (as previously derived from REDUCE LAP-HF II):** peak exercise pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) <1.74 Wood units AND absence of a cardiac rhythm management device. N=313 responders, N=265 nonresponders, N=43 unclassified (missing PVR data).

**Key results:**
- By 24 months, 161/621 (25.9%) had ≥1 WHF event of any level: 108 (17.4%) with any level 1 event, 22 (3.5%) with level 2 but no level 1, 31 (5.0%) with level 3 only. Expanding from level 1 alone (17.4%) to any WHF (25.9%) significantly increased incidence (P=0.0003).
- **No treatment effect in the overall MITT population** for any of the three definitions (per-patient P=0.683–0.855; shunt vs. sham).
- **Responder subgroup, recurrent (total first-plus-recurrent) events analysis:** a numerical shunt benefit present for level 1 alone became statistically significant when level 2 and 3 events were added — rate ratio 0.56 (95% CI 0.33–0.96), P=0.009 for the treatment-by-responder interaction (any WHF definition). Per-patient responder analysis: level 1 alone P=0.103; level 1-or-2 P=0.046; any WHF P=0.034 — significance progressively strengthened as the definition expanded.
- **Nonresponder subgroup:** shunt patients had numerically *more* WHF events than sham (34.4% vs. 23.0%, P=0.029), driven mainly by level 1 events — consistent with harm/neutrality in high-PVR patients (aligning with the previously established PVR-responder interaction in this trial, see [[contradictions]] #23).
- **24-month mortality:** any WHF definition was strongly associated with all-cause mortality (HR 2.60, 95% CI 1.35–5.01, P=0.0042 for "any WHF" vs. no WHF). Level 1 alone carried the strongest per-level hazard (HR 3.54, 95% CI 1.83–6.87, P=0.0002); adding level 2/3 events did **not** further sharpen mortality prediction (level 1-or-2 HR 3.27; any WHF HR 2.60) — the hazard ratio point estimate actually attenuated as lower-acuity events were folded in, because most deaths (15/17 among any-WHF patients) occurred in patients who had experienced ≥1 level 1 event.
- **Quality of life (KCCQ-OSS, baseline to 24 months):** occurrence of WHF by any definition was associated with less improvement in KCCQ-OSS; inclusion of level 2/3 events modestly strengthened this association relative to level 1 alone, and fewer patients with any WHF achieved a 24-month KCCQ-OSS >75 (14.5% vs. 29.4% with no WHF, P<0.001).
- **Cross-trial context (Discussion):** cites MADIT-CRT (outpatient IV-diuretic WHF events carrying similar mortality risk to hospitalisation, HR 10.7 vs. 12.4), Ambrosy et al. (27.6% of WHF events are nonurgent outpatient), the VICTORIA CEC probable-event reanalysis (Felker), DAPA-HF (adding outpatient oral-diuretic intensification increased the primary composite event rate by 36% while preserving HR 0.73), and [[deliver]] (adding outpatient oral-diuretic augmentation increased the number of patients with an event by 54%, preserved HR 0.76 [0.69–0.84]) as parallel precedents for expanded WHF definitions increasing trial power without reversing treatment direction.

**Study limitations (explicit):** WHF classification and responder status were **not pre-specified** — this is an exploratory post hoc analysis. Level 3 is not a standardised definition across the field (contrast with level 1's basis in the Standardized Data Collection for Cardiovascular Trials Initiative). Because WHF levels were counted hierarchically (a patient with an earlier level 2/3 event but a later level 1 event was classified as level 1), no time-to-first-level-2-or-3-event inference is possible.

**Conclusion:** An expanded WHF definition that includes all outpatient HF-treatment encounters increases the total event count and statistical power to detect a device treatment effect among responders, and better captures the negative quality-of-life impact of WHF at 24 months — but only events involving hospitalisation (level 1) were independently associated with subsequent mortality in this analysis.

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

This paper is the first source in this wiki to directly interrogate **how "worsening heart failure" is operationally defined** as a clinical-trial construct — a question that is otherwise left implicit across many trials already in the wiki that use "worsening HF," "WHF event," or "recent HF hospitalisation" as an inclusion criterion or endpoint component:

- [[soloist-whf]] enrolled patients with "recently worsening heart failure" (T2DM population, enrolled at/shortly after hospitalisation) — corresponding to something close to this paper's level 1 definition, though SOLOIST-WHF's own eligibility wording is not broken into levels.
- [[paraglide-hf]] explicitly enrolled a "recent worsening HF (WHF) event within 30 days (HF hospitalisation, emergency department visit, or ambulatory urgent HF visit requiring IV diuretics)" — i.e., level 1 and level 2 combined, but excluding this paper's level 3 (nonurgent, oral-diuretic-only escalation).
- [[finearts-hf]]'s primary endpoint is explicitly "total WHF events + CV death" — the same total (first-plus-recurrent) events framework this paper uses methodologically, though FINEARTS-HF's own WHF definition detail is not expanded in the FINEARTS-HF source page.
- [[deliver]] and DAPA-HF ([[mcmurray2019dapahf]]) are cited directly in this paper's Discussion as trials that similarly broadened their WHF/primary-composite definitions to include outpatient oral-diuretic intensification, with preserved treatment effect.
- [[empulse]] and [[strong-hf]] both intervene explicitly in and around the acute/post-WHF transition period, making the definitional question here (what counts as a WHF "event" for risk stratification and endpoint construction) directly relevant to how those trials' populations and endpoints should be interpreted.

The finding that hospitalisation-requiring (level 1) WHF carries essentially all of the excess mortality signal, while nonurgent oral-diuretic-only escalation (level 3) contributes to event-rate/power gains but not to mortality prediction, is a methodologically important caveat for any future reading of "WHF-reduction" trial results in this wiki: a positive effect on a broadened WHF composite does not necessarily imply a proportionate effect on the hospitalisation/mortality-relevant subset of that composite.

## Contradictions
Within this single paper: level 1 (hospitalisation) WHF alone predicts 24-month mortality about as strongly as the expanded "any WHF" definition (HR 3.54 vs. 2.60) — i.e., adding level 2/3 events to the composite *attenuates* the mortality hazard ratio's point estimate even as it *increases* statistical power to detect the shunt's treatment effect in the responder subgroup. A trial result showing benefit on an "any WHF" composite therefore does not guarantee a proportionate effect on the hospitalisation/mortality-relevant component of that composite.
