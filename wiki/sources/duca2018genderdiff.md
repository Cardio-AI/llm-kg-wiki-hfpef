# Duca 2018 — Gender-Related Differences in HFpEF

> Prospective single-centre cohort (N=260, invasive hemodynamics + CMR + exercise testing): men with HFpEF are more hemodynamically and structurally compromised and die more often of cardiac causes (16.5% vs. 6.1%), while women die more often of non-cardiac causes (10.5% vs. 2.5%) — "men rather develop a right heart phenotype and die from HFpEF, whereas women are more likely to die with HFpEF."

**Full citation:**
Duca F, Zotter-Tufaro C, Kammerlander AA, Aschauer S, Binder C, Mascherbauer J, Bonderman D. Gender-related differences in heart failure with preserved ejection fraction. *Sci Rep.* 2018;8:1080. doi:[10.1038/s41598-018-19507-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-19507-7)

**Study type:** Prospective observational cohort · **N:** 260 (181 women, 79 men) · **Population:** HFpEF diagnosed per ESC/AHA consensus criteria (signs/symptoms of HF, LVEF ≥50%, NT-proBNP >220 pg/mL, echocardiographic diastolic dysfunction E/e' >15, confirmed by right heart catheterisation — PAWP >12 mmHg — where diastolic dysfunction was equivocal, E/e' 8–15); significant CAD, cardiac amyloidosis, and severe COPD excluded · **Follow-up:** median 30.0 months (IQR 13.0–48.0)
**Intervention/Exposure:** sex/gender (observational, no intervention) · **Primary outcome:** cardiac death, combined endpoint (cardiac death or HF hospitalisation), all-cause death

## Key Findings

- **Baseline sex differences:** men had lower exercise capacity (% predicted 6MWD: 66.7% men vs. 78.1% women, P=0.036), more comorbidity burden (active/former smoking 40.5% vs. 23.8%, P=0.009; sleep apnoea 20.3% vs. 5.0%, P<0.001; COPD 46.8% vs. 27.1%, P=0.002), higher diastolic pressure gradient (3.0 vs. 1.0 mmHg, P=0.010), higher LV mass index (61.7 vs. 53.6 g/m², P=0.002), higher RV end-diastolic volume index (85.0 vs. 73.7 mL/m², P=0.011), lower LVEF (58.0% vs. 66.0%, P=0.005), and lower RVEF (50.0% vs. 55.0%, P=0.006).
- **Mode of death differed by sex:** among 45 deaths, men died almost exclusively from right heart failure (RHF 73.3% men vs. 36.7% women, P=0.020) and sudden cardiac death (SCD 13.3% men vs. 0.0% women, P=0.041); women died more often from infection (23.3% women vs. 0.0% men, P=0.042).
- **Cardiac death:** 16.5% men vs. 6.1% women (P=0.008). Male sex was an independent predictor in both univariable (HR 2.727, 95% CI 1.221–6.087, P=0.014) and multivariable Cox regression (HR 2.639, 95% CI 1.023–6.805, P=0.045, adjusted for NT-proBNP and exercise capacity).
- **Non-cardiac death:** 2.5% men vs. 10.5% women (P=0.030) — inverse pattern.
- **Combined endpoint (cardiac death or HF hospitalisation):** male sex independently associated with shorter time-to-event (HR 1.588, 95% CI 1.029–2.450, P=0.037).
- **All-cause death:** no significant sex difference (HR 1.164, 95% CI 0.626–2.164, P=0.631) — male sex did not predict all-cause mortality in this cohort, contrasting with the I-PRESERVE trial (~3,000 patients), where male sex *was* an independent predictor of all-cause death. Authors attribute the discrepancy to this cohort's systematic exclusion of significant CAD and cardiac amyloidosis, both of which are male-predominant, poor-prognosis conditions that can mimic HFpEF.

## Methods (brief)

Single-centre (Medical University of Vienna) prospective registry, December 2010 – November 2016. All patients underwent right heart catheterisation (Swan-Ganz, invasive hemodynamics), cardiac MRI (1.5T, functional + late gadolinium enhancement), and transthoracic echocardiography. Cardiac amyloidosis actively excluded via CMR, ⁹⁹mTc-DPD scintigraphy, and serum/urine immunofixation (biopsy if necessary) — a stricter exclusion than many HFpEF trial cohorts, which the authors argue explains why male-sex-predicts-all-cause-mortality (seen in I-PRESERVE) did not replicate here.

## Limitations

Single-centre design (constant work-up protocol, but limits generalisability). Relatively small cohort (45 deaths total) limits event-based statistical power compared to multicentre trial registries. Duration of HF prior to enrolment not systematically assessed. Left- and right-heart catheterisation were not performed systematically in all patients, though CMR was used to characterise the full cohort.

## Contradictions
Duca's finding that male sex predicts cardiac death but *not* all-cause death directly contrasts with I-PRESERVE, where male sex predicted all-cause death. Authors attribute this to stricter CAD/amyloidosis exclusion in this cohort (both male-predominant HFpEF-mimicking conditions) — see [[hfpef-mimics-differential-diagnosis]]. Also sits alongside the broader "worse physiology, better cardiac-specific survival" pattern in women documented across the sex-differences literature (women have worse hemodynamic/vascular phenotype per Beale 2019/Lau 2022, yet lower cardiac-specific mortality per this study) — logged as a new entry in [[contradictions]].
