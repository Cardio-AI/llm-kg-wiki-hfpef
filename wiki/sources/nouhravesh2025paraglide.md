# In-Hospital vs Out-of-Hospital Sac/Val Initiation in PARAGLIDE-HF

> In PARAGLIDE-HF (n=466), 69.5% initiated Sac/Val in-hospital and 30.5% out-of-hospital; treatment effect on NT-proBNP was consistent regardless of initiation location (P_interaction=0.99) — supporting flexible initiation timing in WHF-HFpEF without loss of efficacy or safety.

## Core Arguments

**Study:** Pre-specified subgroup analysis of PARAGLIDE-HF (NCT03988634). N=**466**.

**Initiation setting distribution:**
- In-hospital: **69.5%** of patients (during index WHF hospitalisation)
- Out-of-hospital (ambulatory post-discharge): **30.5%**
- Both settings required identical post-stabilisation criteria: SBP ≥100 mmHg for ≥6h, no IV diuretics/vasodilators for 6h, no inotropes for 24h

**Treatment effect by initiation location:**
- Primary endpoint (NT-proBNP time-averaged proportional change, weeks 4&8): **P_interaction = 0.99**
- No significant interaction — in-hospital and out-of-hospital initiation are equivalent for efficacy
- Safety outcomes also consistent between settings

**Clinical implication:** Sac/Val can be started safely in either the inpatient or outpatient setting post-WHF event. Clinicians do not need to defer initiation to post-discharge follow-up if stabilisation criteria are met during hospitalisation. This enables inpatient initiation as part of discharge planning — improving compliance with the transition-of-care period.

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

Implementation evidence for Sac/Val initiation in the post-WHF HFpEF population. Real-world transition-of-care programmes can initiate Sac/Val during index hospitalisation without losing efficacy relative to outpatient initiation. Given that the post-discharge "vulnerable period" is associated with high rehospitalisation rates, earlier in-hospital initiation may improve long-term adherence and RAAS suppression.
