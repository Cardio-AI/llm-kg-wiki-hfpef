# Symptomatic Hypotension with Sac/Val in PARAGLIDE-HF

> In PARAGLIDE-HF, symptomatic hypotension (SH) occurred in 24.0% with Sac/Val vs 15.5% valsartan (overall 19.7%; P=0.020); SH risk was highest in patients with lower baseline SBP, white race, and LVEF >60% — identifying actionable pre-initiation risk factors for clinical practice.

**Full citation:**
Fudim M, Cyr DD, Ward JH, et al. Association of Sacubitril/Valsartan vs Valsartan with Blood Pressure Changes and Symptomatic Hypotension: The PARAGLIDE-HF Trial. *J Card Fail.* 2024;30(12):1568–1577. doi:10.1016/j.cardfail.2024.04.030

## Core Arguments

**Study:** Pre-specified safety analysis of PARAGLIDE-HF (NCT03988634). N=466.

**Symptomatic hypotension (SH) incidence:**
- Overall: **19.7%** across both arms
- Sac/Val arm: **24.0%**
- Valsartan arm: **15.5%**
- Absolute difference: 8.5 pp; **P=0.020** — statistically significant excess SH with Sac/Val

**Independent predictors of SH (multivariable):**
- **Lower baseline SBP** — higher risk with lower pre-treatment BP; volume-depleted post-WHF patients at particular risk
- **White race** — higher SH risk vs non-white patients; may reflect differences in baseline RAAS activity or aldosterone physiology
- **LVEF >60%** — small, stiff LV cannot tolerate volume depletion; highest SH risk subgroup

**Clinical significance:**
- SH with ARNI in HFpEF is not a minor side effect — in PARAGON-HF, patients with SH had higher subsequent mortality and HF hospitalisations
- LVEF >60% association is actionable: more cautious dose titration warranted in this subgroup
- Women in PARAGLIDE-HF had higher LVEF (more LVEF >60%) and worse renal function — consistent with higher SH risk ([[rambarat2025paraglide]])

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

Directly addresses the practical challenge of initiating Sac/Val in the post-WHF HFpEF population. The LVEF >60% risk signal is particularly important because women with HFpEF — who represent 52–60% of the HFpEF population — tend to have higher LVEF (more concentric remodelling, smaller LV volume) and are thus concentrated in this high-risk SH subgroup. Pre-initiation assessment should include SBP, LVEF, and volume status.
