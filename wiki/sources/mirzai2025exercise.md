# Exercise Training in HFpEF — 2025 Review

> This state-of-the-art review synthesises evidence from ~30 RCTs across multiple exercise modalities in HFpEF (2004–2024); moderate-intensity continuous training (MICT) has the largest evidence base, HIIT is not superior to MICT for ΔpVO₂, and ~1/3 of patients do not respond — hard outcome data remain absent.

## Core Arguments

### Exercise Intolerance in HFpEF — Mechanistic Basis
Peak VO₂ is ~30% lower than healthy controls. Four contributing mechanisms (Figure 1):
1. **Skeletal muscle dysfunction** — sarcopenia, myosteatosis, mitochondrial dysfunction, impaired O₂ extraction, reduced capillary density
2. **Reduced cardiac reserve** — impaired cardiac output during exercise, chronotropic incompetence, ventriculoarterial uncoupling
3. **Reduced pulmonary reserve** — impaired pulmonary vasodilation, V/Q mismatch, pulmonary hypertension
4. **Systemic/metabolic factors** — endothelial dysfunction, systemic inflammation, excess visceral adiposity

Training achieves greater ΔpVO₂ in HFpEF than HFrEF primarily through peripheral (skeletal muscle) adaptations rather than central cardiac changes — consistent with Sachdev 2023.

### Evidence by Exercise Modality

**Moderate-Intensity Continuous Training (MICT)**
- Largest evidence base; most RCTs; 3×/week for 12–24 weeks
- Consistently improves peak VO₂, 6MWD, QoL, and symptom burden
- AHA/ACC 2022 Class I recommendation (source: [[sachdev2023exercise]])

**High-Intensity Interval Training (HIIT)**
- Potential benefit in highly selected populations
- Improves aerobic capacity, cardiac haemodynamics, ventilatory efficiency
- No consistent superiority over MICT for ΔpVO₂ (OptmEx-Clin, Mueller 2021: HIIT vs. MICT, 48 weeks, n≈120, no difference)
- 4×4-minute intervals at 85–95% peak HR; requires closer supervision

**Multi-Modality / Combined Aerobic + Resistance**
- Combined endurance + resistance (e.g., Ex-DHF Pilot, Edelmann 2011): improved pVO₂ and diastolic function
- Protein supplementation + exercise (Azhar 2020): improved 6MWD and blood pressure; protein alone ineffective
- Appropriate for patients with significant muscle weakness or frailty

**Low-Intensity Training**
- Tai chi (Yeh 2013), walking programs: suitable for vulnerable/frail patients with limited exercise tolerance
- Inferior to MICT for pVO₂ but feasible in patients who cannot tolerate structured exercise

**Emerging Modalities**
- Inspiratory muscle training (IMT): improves respiratory muscle strength and aerobic threshold; neutral pVO₂ (TRAINING-HF, Palau 2019)
- Functional electrical stimulation (FES): improves pVO₂ and exercise capacity in sedentary patients

**Exercise + Dietary Interventions**
- SECRET 1 (Kitzman 2016): exercise + caloric restriction — additive effects on pVO₂; diet alone no significant benefit
- HEART camp (Alonso 2022): multicomponent behavioural program + MICT + resistance over 72 weeks — improved adherence, functional capacity, and symptoms

### Key Challenges
- **Non-responders:** ~1/3 of participants do not show meaningful ΔpVO₂ benefit
- **Long-term durability:** benefits largely observed during supervised training; decay after program ends
- **Hard outcomes:** no RCT has powered for all-cause mortality or hospitalisation as primary endpoint
- **Implementation:** barriers include access, reimbursement, patient motivation, deconditioning severity
