# Microvascular Dysfunction in HFpEF

> Narrative review arguing that coronary microvascular dysfunction (CMD) is the "common soil" of HFpEF — comorbidity-driven systemic inflammation impairs endothelial physiology, triggering EndoMT, fibrosis, and myocardial stiffening — and surveying pharmacological targets arising from this paradigm.

**Full citation:**
D'Amario D, Migliaro S, Borovac JA, Restivo A, Vergallo R, Galli M, Leone AM, Montone RA, Niccoli G, Aspromonte N, Crea F. Microvascular Dysfunction in Heart Failure With Preserved Ejection Fraction. *Front Physiol.* 2019;10:1347. doi:[10.3389/fphys.2019.01347](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2019.01347)

---

## Core Arguments

### 1. The Microvascular Hypothesis (Central Thesis)

Paradigm shift from the traditional "overload model" (hypertension → afterload → diastolic dysfunction) to the "microvascular hypothesis": comorbidities trigger systemic inflammation → endothelial activation → CMD → myocardial fibrosis and stiffening → HFpEF. CMD is not a secondary finding but the primary pathogenetic mechanism. This unifies the diverse comorbidity landscape of HFpEF under a single mechanistic framework. (source: damario2019cmd)

Graziani and Crea (2018) proposed CMD as the "common soil" for both microvascular angina and HFpEF — the same microvascular disease producing different clinical phenotypes depending on context.

### 2. Pathophysiological Cascade (Figure 1)

```
Comorbidities:
  Obesity, metabolic syndrome, arterial HTN, OSA, DM,
  physical inactivity, COPD, iron deficiency
     ↓
Systemic inflammation:
  ↑hs-CRP, ↑IL-1R, ↑TNF-α, ↑GDF-15, ↑sST-2
     ↓
Endothelial cell (primary injury at microvessels):
  Microvascular inflammation + endothelial activation
     ↓
EndoMT (endothelial-mesenchymal transition):
  Endothelial cells → mesenchymal/myofibroblast phenotype
  ↑α-SMA, ↑collagen type I, ↑crosslinking
  TGF-β/SMAD pathway activation
     ↓
Cardiomyocyte (secondary injury):
  ↓NO → ↓vasodilatation peptides
  ↑vasoconstriction → microvascular ischaemia
  ↑ROS → ↓cGMP → ↓PKG activity
  ↓titin phosphorylation → ↑myocyte stiffness
  ↑collagenation → ↑LV stiffening
  ↑concentric LV remodelling
     ↓
HFpEF phenotype
```

(source: damario2019cmd)

### 3. Cellular and Molecular Mechanisms

**cGMP/PKG pathway (detailed):** NO breakdown → ↓sGC function → ↓cGMP-induced PKG activation → ↑cardiomyocyte resting tension from titin hypophosphorylation and abolition of antihypertrophic/antifibrotic effects of PKG. PKG acts as a physiological brake on TGF-β-mediated negative remodelling by phosphorylating SMAD complexes. Disease stages are time-dependent: early phase dominated by NO impairment; advanced phase by endothelin-1-mediated vasoconstriction. (source: damario2019cmd)

**Titin isoform shift:** Diastolic LV stiffness occurs primarily due to changes in sarcomeric titin — specifically an isoform shift from the compliant N2BA to the stiffer N2B isoform. Together with titin hypophosphorylation attributable to PKG inactivity, this leads to reduced elastance (increased stiffness) of myocytes. Titin connects Z-disk of sarcomere to M-band, preventing overstretching. (source: damario2019cmd)

**EndoMT (endothelial-mesenchymal transition):** Endothelial cells acquire mesenchymal or myofibroblast phenotype — loss of endothelial markers, upregulation of α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) and collagen type I. TGF-β pathway is the primary driver. Results in: quantitative increase in total collagen, overexpression of collagen type I relative to type III, ↑crosslinking between collagen molecules → interstitial fibrosis. EndoMT in hypoxic microvascular endothelial cells → cardiomyocyte apoptosis via paracrine TGF-β₁/SMAD signalling. Repeated micro-ischaemia episodes → focal fibrosis explaining low coronary flow reserve as predictor of diastolic dysfunction. (source: damario2019cmd)

**Calcium overload:** Diastolic dysfunction in HFpEF characterised not only by increased LV passive stiffness but also by impaired myocardial active relaxation from intracellular calcium overload. In HF, ↑late sodium currents → ↑cytosolic Na⁺ → ↑Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchange activity during diastole → excessive cytosolic Ca²⁺ load. This mechanism is facilitated by RAAS activation and appears strictly related to fibrotic remodelling. RALI-DHF trial (ranolazine): proof-of-concept that inhibiting the late Na⁺ current reduces LVEDP and PCWP significantly after 30-min infusion in HFpEF. (source: damario2019cmd)

**Cellular senescence:** Cell-cycle arrest → triggers inflammation → reduced NO-dependent endothelial signalling → ↑fibrosis. Animal model with senescent cellular phenotype: reduced basal eNOS function, ↑fibrosis from local inflammation. (source: damario2019cmd)

**Epicardial adipose tissue:** Chronic inflammatory conditions (including HFpEF) → deranged adipogenesis → ↑proinflammatory adipokine production → atrial and ventricular fibrosis. Epicardium is the transducing component mediating adverse systemic and metabolic effects onto the heart. Plasma volume expansion especially in high-BMI patients → persistent cardiac volume overload. (source: damario2019cmd)

**miRNA:** miRNA 146 and 155 — inflammatory cytokine production via NF-κB; miRNA 126 — endothelial homeostasis and vascular integrity, VCAM expression, monocyte recruitment. Biomarker profiles in HFpEF (related to inflammation and ECM reorganisation) are distinct from HFrEF profiles (related to cellular proliferation and metabolism). (source: damario2019cmd)

### 4. CMD Measurement and Evidence

CMD comprises structural and functional abnormalities affecting heart microcirculation. Camici/Crea classification (4 types): (1) CMD in absence of obstructive CAD; (2) CMD in presence of myocardial disease; (3) CMD in presence of obstructive CAD; (4) iatrogenic CMD.

| Tool | Finding in HFpEF | Clinical relevance |
|---|---|---|
| FMD (brachial artery) | Reduced; even lower than hypertensive controls | Endothelial dysfunction present; not simply afterload |
| Reactive hyperemia index (RHI) | Impaired; prognostic value documented | Peripheral microvascular surrogate |
| Coronary flow reserve (CFR) | Reduction closely related to HFpEF diagnosis; predicts MACE + HF hosp (Taqueti 2018) | Structural CMD |
| IMR (index of microvascular resistance) | Linearly correlated with LVEDP (Dryer 2018) | Links CMD directly to haemodynamic burden |
| TIMI frame count + myocardial blush grade | Reduced in HFpEF without obstructive CAD | Angiographic CMD evidence |
| PROMIS-HFpEF (Shah 2018) | 91% CMD prevalence (66% endothelium-independent + 24% endothelium-dependent) | Near-universal CMD burden in HFpEF |

(source: damario2019cmd)

### 5. OSA in HFpEF

Prevalence: **16.8%** of HFpEF patients. Independently associated with HF admission risk (**RR 2.2**, 95% CI 2.12–2.21). Treatment of sleep-disordered breathing → significant reduction in LA volume → improvement in diastolic function. However, OSA even if properly treated may worsen long-term cardiac function with higher levels of BNP documented among HFpEF+OSA vs. HFpEF alone. Sympathetic activation, intermittent hypoxia, and haemodynamic changes from OSA contribute to HFpEF development and sustaining. (source: damario2019cmd)

### 6. Iron Deficiency in HFpEF

Iron deficiency (ID) is common in HFpEF and is associated with:
- Reduced exercise capacity (6MWT, VO₂max)
- Worse diastolic dysfunction — **severity of diastolic dysfunction is proportional to severity of ID**
- Worse HRQOL (EQ5D questionnaire)

**Absolute ID** (↓serum iron + ↓ferritin) vs. **functional ID** (serum ferritin 100–299 µg/L + transferrin saturation <20%) — both associated with ↑1-year mortality + HF admissions in HF. IRON-HF study: IV iron improved functional capacity; anemia correction similar between oral and IV iron. FAIR-HFpEF (NCT03074591) ongoing — will provide definitive evidence for IV iron (ferric carboxymaltose) in HFpEF. (source: damario2019cmd)

### 7. Therapeutic Targets Arising from CMD Hypothesis

**PDE-9 inhibition:** PDE-9 is upregulated in HFpEF LV biopsies. Blockade modulates cGMP *independently* of NO synthesis (unlike PDE-5, which still requires active NO production). Animal HFpEF model: PDE-9 inhibition regressed hypertrophy, fibrosis, and vascular dysfunction. Potentially more tractable than PDE-5 inhibition in HFpEF where NO bioavailability is reduced. (source: damario2019cmd)

**Statins:** Beyond cholesterol lowering — anti-inflammatory and pleiotropic endothelial effects. In statin-treated HFpEF patients, endomyocardial biopsies show: ↓myocardial nitrotyrosine, ↑myocardial PKG activity, ↓cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, lower cardiomyocyte resting tension. Observational data: JASPER study (Marume 2019) — statins reduced mortality in HFpEF without CAD; lower incidence of non-cardiac death + HF hospitalisation even without effect on cardiac death. Meta-analysis confirms benefit; statins reduce epicardial adipose tissue and proinflammatory lipoproteins. No large RCT in HFpEF yet. (source: damario2019cmd)

**Ranolazine (RALI-DHF):** Inhibits late Na⁺ current → ↓Ca²⁺ overload → improved active relaxation. Proof-of-concept RALI-DHF trial: 30-min infusion → significant ↓LVEDP and ↓PCWP; improved diastolic function parameters; non-invasive E/e' ratio unaltered. (source: damario2019cmd)

**Anti-fibrotic approaches:** HFpEF and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) share microvascular dysfunction as a fundamental mechanism. Nintedanib (tyrosine kinase inhibitor targeting PDGFR/FGFR/VEGFR): approved for IPF; improved cardiac fibrosis + function in animal models; one ongoing study in HFpEF (NCT02932566). Pirfenidone: anti-fibrotic, anti-inflammatory, antioxidant; animal data; no human HFpEF RCT yet. (source: damario2019cmd)

**Anti-IL-1 targeting (Anakinra):** IL-1 cytokine causal in impairing both systolic and diastolic function in animal models. D-HART pilot study: anakinra in HFpEF with high CRP → significant ↓systemic inflammation + measurable ↑exercise capacity; CRP reduction correlated with peak VO₂ improvement. (source: damario2019cmd)

**SGLT2i mechanism in CMD context:** EMPA-REG, CANVAS, DECLARE-TIMI 58 data in T2DM show HF hospitalisation reduction; CVD-REAL study: ↓HF hosp/mortality across multiple SGLT2i. Potential microvascular mechanism: ↑myocardial arterioles/capillaries, ↑VEGF, ↓TGF-β and caspases → improved myocardial perfusion and coronary reserve — independent of BP normalisation or cardiac hypertrophy reversal. (source: damario2019cmd)

**A1-Agonists (Neladenoson/PANACHE Phase 2b):** A1 adenosine receptor activation → improved mitochondrial function + contraction/relaxation coupling. PANACHE trial evaluating neladenoson bialanate. Proposed mechanisms: ↑SERCA2a activity, ↓Ca²⁺ overload, ↓lipotoxicity, ↓interstitial fibrosis, ↑myocardial capillary density. (source: damario2019cmd)

**Relaxin:** RXFP1 receptor; 48h infusion → bradykinin-evoked NO relaxation; animal model: ↓vascular fibrosis, ↑myocardial capillary density; diabetic model: reversed endothelial dysfunction via NO-mediated relaxation. Human HFpEF studies needed.

## Contradictions
- PDE-5 inhibitors (sildenafil) show contradictory data in HFpEF. D'Amario 2019 suggests sildenafil remains viable in specific subpopulations (pulmonary HTN, RV dysfunction), despite RELAX trial neutrality. This is consistent with the heterogeneity argument but conflicts with the broad null finding. See [[contradictions]].
- NEAT-HFpEF ISMN was neutral — D'Amario 2019 attributes this partly to high hypotension incidence limiting effort tolerance, which is mechanistically plausible. The organic vs. inorganic nitrate distinction may also matter (different vascular vs. cellular targets). See [[contradictions]].
