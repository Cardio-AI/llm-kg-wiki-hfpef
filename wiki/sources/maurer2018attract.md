# ATTR-ACT — Tafamidis in ATTR Cardiomyopathy (Maurer 2018)

> Tafamidis (transthyretin tetramer stabiliser) reduced all-cause mortality by 30% (HR 0.70) and CV hospitalisations by 32% (RR 0.68) over 30 months in ATTR-CM — establishing the first disease-modifying therapy for the most common underdiagnosed HFpEF mimic (ATTRwt prevalence ~13% among HFpEF patients).

**File:** `raw/2018-NEJM-Maurer-ATTR-ACT_study.pdf` · **Authors:** Maurer MS, Schwartz JH, Gundapaneni B, Elliott PM, Merlini G, et al.  
**Year:** 2018 · **Journal:** N Engl J Med 2018;379:1007–1016 · **DOI:** 10.1056/NEJMoa1805689  
**ClinicalTrials.gov:** NCT01994889  
**Study type:** Multicenter double-blind RCT · **N:** 441 · **Sites:** 48 sites, 13 countries · **Funder:** Pfizer  
**Enrollment:** December 2013 – August 2015  
**Population:** ATTR cardiomyopathy (wild-type or hereditary variant); NYHA class I–III  
**Follow-up:** 30 months  
**Intervention:** Tafamidis 80 mg (n=176) or 20 mg (n=88) vs. placebo (n=177); pooled tafamidis N=264  
**Primary outcome:** Hierarchical composite (Finkelstein-Schoenfeld): all-cause mortality + CV hospitalisations

---

## Key Findings

- **Primary (Finkelstein-Schoenfeld): P<0.001; win ratio 1.695 (95% CI 1.255–2.289)** — tafamidis superior to placebo
- All-cause mortality: 29.5% (tafamidis) vs 42.9% (placebo); HR 0.70 (95% CI 0.51–0.96)
- CV hospitalisations: 0.48 vs 0.70/patient-year; RR 0.68 (95% CI 0.56–0.81)
- 6MWD: tafamidis reduced decline by 75.68 m vs placebo (P<0.001)
- KCCQ-OS: tafamidis reduced decline by 13.65 pts vs placebo (P<0.001)
- **ATTRwt subgroup: P<0.001** (N=336; 76% of trial); **ATTRm subgroup: P=0.30** (N=105)
- NYHA I–II: significant benefit; NYHA III: P=0.78 (possible paradoxical hospitalisation increase explained by longer survival in sicker patients)
- ATTRwt prevalence among HFpEF patients: ~13% (background epidemiology cited in paper)

## Methods (brief)

Double-blind placebo-controlled RCT. ATTR-CM diagnosed by bone scintigraphy (DPD or PYP grade ≥2) or endomyocardial biopsy with exclusion of AL amyloidosis by serum/urine immunofixation and free light chains. Stratified by ATTR type (wild-type vs. hereditary variant) and NYHA class. Primary composite tested hierarchically: first all-cause death (time-to-event), then CV hospitalisations (frequency). Tafamidis mechanism: stabilises TTR tetramer → prevents misfolding and amyloid fibril deposition.

## Limitations

- ATTRm subgroup underpowered (N=105); absence of effect not equivalent to evidence of no effect
- 90% male — limited generalisability to women (ATTRwt affects men predominantly; but TTR variants may affect women equally)
- 30-month follow-up — long-term durability and safety unknown
- Not a general HFpEF trial; applicable only to ATTR-CM subphenotype
- Tafamidis is expensive; access highly variable globally
- AL amyloidosis exclusion required serum/urine immunofixation — not always available in routine practice
- No direct comparison of 80 mg vs 20 mg as pre-specified primary analysis

## Contradictions

Tafamidis significantly reduces mortality in ATTR-CM (HR 0.70, P<0.001 for ATTRwt subgroup) while all other HFpEF trials targeting non-specific pathways have been negative. This underscores the heterogeneity of HFpEF and the need for aetiology-specific therapy. The overall HFpEF literature (TOPCAT, PARAGON-HF, RELAX, NEAT-HFpEF, etc.) is a catalogue of neutral results; ATTR-ACT is the exception — but only because it enrolled a biologically homogeneous subphenotype.
