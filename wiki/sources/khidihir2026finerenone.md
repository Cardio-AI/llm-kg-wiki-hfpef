# Finerenone in HFpEF (Khidihir & Kalra 2026)

> Narrative review synthesising the pharmacological basis, clinical trial evidence, subgroup analyses, safety profile, and future directions for finerenone in HFpEF/HFmrEF — situating it within the evolving cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic (CKM) combination-therapy landscape and against steroidal MRAs (spironolactone, eplerenone).

## Summary

Review by the University of Louisville (Kalra lab) synthesising the pharmacology, clinical evidence, and future trial landscape for finerenone in HFpEF/HFmrEF, published shortly after FINEARTS-HF and the concurrent emergence of several ongoing mechanistic finerenone trials. (source: Khidihir2026Finerenone)

## Key Points

**Pharmacology vs. steroidal MRAs:** Finerenone is a non-steroidal, dihydropyridine-derived, third-generation, highly selective MR antagonist (>500-fold selectivity for MR over other hormonal receptors, no calcium-channel activity) — distinct from spironolactone (broad off-target androgen/progesterone/glucocorticoid receptor binding → gynecomastia, sexual dysfunction) and eplerenone (more selective but still steroidal). Finerenone's unique receptor conformation favours nuclear corepressor recruitment over coactivators, selectively suppressing profibrotic (TGF-β, connective tissue growth factor) and proinflammatory (IL-1β, TNF-α, MCP-1) gene programs while minimally affecting mineralocorticoid transport genes — the proposed mechanistic basis for its more favourable electrolyte/renal-accumulation profile (steroidal MRAs preferentially accumulate in renal tissue; finerenone distributes more evenly between heart and kidney). Pharmacokinetics: ~44% oral bioavailability, >92% protein-bound, hepatic CYP3A4 metabolism (drug-interaction caution with potent CYP3A4 inhibitors), terminal half-life 2–3h.

**"Aldosterone escape" rationale:** ~40% of patients on chronic ACEi/ARB therapy show return of aldosterone to baseline levels over weeks — a mechanistic argument for MR-level (not just upstream RAAS) blockade regardless of ACEi/ARB use.

**Preclinical antifibrotic evidence:** In rodent/large-animal models, finerenone reduced myocardial collagen deposition, macrophage infiltration, profibrotic cytokine expression, and vascular stiffening independent of blood-pressure changes; in a DOCA-salt uninephrectomy/isoproterenol model, finerenone reduced fibrosis and myocardial stiffness more effectively than equivalent-dose spironolactone.

**Supportive (non-HFpEF-specific) trial evidence:** FIDELIO-DKD (diabetic CKD) — 1.8% absolute risk reduction for the composite CV/renal secondary outcome; HHF relative risk reduction 0.67 (95% CI 0.52–0.87), though only 7.7% of enrollees had HF. FIGARO-DKD — composite CV outcome HR 0.87 (0.76–0.98, P=0.03); HHF HR 0.71 (0.56–0.90, P=0.004). FIDELITY pooled analysis: HHF HR 0.78 (0.66–0.92).

**FINEARTS-HF baseline/AF/hyperkalemia detail not previously in this wiki's [[finearts-hf]] page:** Baseline characteristics (N=6,001): mean age 72y, 45.5% female, 79.1% White, mean LVEF 53±8%, 36% LVEF <50%, 60% prior HFH, 84% HF event within 3 months, 40.6% T2DM, 88.7% hypertension, mean eGFR 62 mL/min/1.73m², 14% baseline SGLT2i use. **Finerenone's AF benefit:** FINE-HEART pooled analysis across major finerenone trials — 17% reduction in new-onset AF/flutter (HR 0.83, 95% CI 0.71–0.97; NNT 126 over 4 years), consistent across CKD/T2DM/HFmrEF-HFpEF subgroups, without heterogeneity by comorbidity burden; supported by a 29% reduction in new-onset AF in FIDELIO-DKD and a similar directional trend in FINEARTS-HF; benefit preserved in patients with pre-existing AF. **SGLT2i co-administration:** concurrent SGLT2i use (~16% of FINEARTS-HF participants) did not increase hyperkalemia risk — consistent with SGLT2i's known renal-potassium-excretion-enhancing effect, supporting a pharmacological rationale for finerenone+SGLT2i combination.

**Retrospective head-to-head vs. spironolactone (no completed RCT exists):** A propensity-matched global federated-network retrospective cohort (251 patients/group, mean age 73y, 47% female) found numerically similar acute HF events (finerenone 6.0% vs. spironolactone 4.4%; HR 1.26, 95% CI 0.58–2.74) and lower all-cause mortality with finerenone (4.4% vs. 8.0%; HR 0.48, 95% CI 0.23–1.00), with comparable MACE and hyperkalemia/AKI rates — hypothesis-generating only, given the retrospective design and modest sample. The only prospective randomised finerenone-vs-spironolactone comparison remains the small Phase 2a ARTS trial (HFrEF + moderate CKD, ~30-day tolerability focus, not HFpEF), which showed similar efficacy with a better finerenone hyperkalemia profile (~5% vs. ~10%).

**Safety/monitoring:** Hyperkalemia is finerenone's most clinically important adverse event (FINEARTS-HF: 3.0% vs. 1.5% placebo hyperkalemia-related discontinuation); recommended monitoring — check potassium/renal function 1 week after initiation, then at 4 weeks, then every 6 months; withhold if K >5.5 mEq/L; hypotension more frequent in the HFimpEF (improved EF) prespecified subanalysis. Gynecomastia/sexual dysfunction — hallmark steroidal-MRA side effects — were not increased with finerenone.

**Ongoing/planned mechanistic imaging trials** (not previously tracked in this wiki): **FINE-FOCUS** (NCT07583173) — multicentre RCT of finerenone's effect on myocardial fibrosis/structure in symptomatic HF with LVEF ≥40%, with an ¹⁸F-FAPI-PET/CT fibrosis substudy. **FINE-REMODEL** (NCT07442448) — diabetic kidney disease + HF LVEF ≥40%, cardiac-imaging remodelling endpoints. **FINE-MECH** (NCT07270367) — complementary mechanistic imaging trial (cardiac-imaging focus per the review; design details not otherwise specified in the source).
