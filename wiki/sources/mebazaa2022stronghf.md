# STRONG-HF — Rapid GDMT Uptitration in Acute HF (Mebazaa 2022)

> High-intensity post-discharge GDMT uptitration guided by NT-proBNP reduced 180-day death or HF readmission by ~8% absolute risk (RR 0.66) vs usual care in acute HF across the LVEF spectrum — establishing the evidence base for ESC 2023 Class I post-discharge care recommendation.

**File:** `raw/2022-Lancet-Mebazaa-STRONG-HF_study.pdf` · **Authors:** Mebazaa A, Davison B, Chioncel O, Cohen-Solal A, Diaz R, Filippatos G, Metra M, Ponikowski P, Sliwa K, Voors AA, Edwards C, Novosadova M, Takagi K, Damasceno A, Saidu H, Gayat E, Pang PS, Celutkiene J, Cotter G, et al.  
**Year:** 2022 · **Journal:** Lancet 2022;400(10367):1938–1952 · **DOI:** 10.1016/S0140-6736(22)02076-1  
**ClinicalTrials.gov:** NCT03412201 · **Funded by:** Roche Diagnostics  
**Study type:** Multinational open-label randomised parallel-group RCT · **N:** 1,078 ITT (high-intensity 542; usual care 536); 1,085 randomised (7 in error)  
**Screened:** 1,641; enrolled from 87 hospitals in 14 countries (Argentina, Austria, Bulgaria, Colombia, France, Hungary, Israel, Mozambique, Nigeria, Russia, Serbia, Slovakia, South Africa, Tunisia)  
**Population:** Acute HF, LVEF-unrestricted (≤40% vs >40% stratification); NT-proBNP >2,500 pg/mL at screening AND >1,500 pg/mL at randomisation with >10% decrease; age 18–85; hospitalised ≤72 hours; haemodynamically stable  
**Follow-up:** 180 days (primary); 90 days (safety)  
**Intervention:** High-intensity care: rapid in-hospital + ≤2-week post-discharge uptitration of ≥3 GDMT pillars (beta-blocker, ACEi/ARB/ARNI, MRA) to 100% optimal doses; NT-proBNP-guided visits at 1, 2, 3, and 6 weeks post-randomisation (mean **4.8** clinic visits, SD 1.0, over 90 days vs. **1.0** in usual care); vs. usual care per local practice  
**Primary outcome:** 180-day composite of all-cause death + HF readmission (first event per patient; down-weighted analysis for protocol amendment)

---

## Key Findings

- **Primary endpoint (180-day all-cause death or HF readmission): significant** — ARD 8.1% (95% CI 2.9–13.2; P=0.0021); adjusted RR 0.66 (95% CI 0.50–0.86); event rate 15.2% (74/506) vs. 23.3% (109/502) (down-weighted Kaplan-Meier)
- EQ-5D VAS improvement: +3.49 points (95% CI **1.74–5.24**, not 0.74–5.24) at day 90 in favour of high-intensity care (P<0.0001)
- Heart failure readmission at 180 days: ARD 7.6% (3.0–12.1); adjusted RR 0.56 (0.38–0.81); P=0.0011
- All-cause death at 180 days: ARD 1.6% (−2.3 to 5.4); RR 0.84 (0.56–1.26); P=0.42 (non-significant)
- Finkelstein-Schoenfeld hierarchical composite at 90 days: Mann-Whitney odds 1.28 (1.13–1.46); P=0.0002
- NT-proBNP at day 90: adjusted ratio of geometric means 0.436 (high-intensity) vs. 0.564 (usual care); P=0.0003
- Safety signal: any adverse event 41% (high-intensity) vs 29% (usual care); serious adverse events similar (16% vs 17%); hypotension 5% vs <1%; renal impairment 3% vs <1%
- LVEF subgroup (prespecified): HFrEF (LVEF ≤40%) ARD **6.3%** (per Figure 4 data); HFpEF (LVEF >40%) ARD **12.5%** — NOTE: the subgroup labels are reversed vs. what was stated in the prior source page summary; HFpEF (>40%) ARD is actually 12.5% and HFrEF (≤40%) is 6.3% per Figure 4
- Trial stopped early: DSMB recommended stopping at ~1,069 randomised patients (second interim futility analysis at ~1,000 with 90-day data) because primary endpoint reduction was larger than expected; trial terminated for efficacy (not futility)

## Methods (brief)

Open-label RCT across multiple countries. Entry required: NT-proBNP >2,500 pg/mL at admission AND >10% decrease from admission to randomisation (confirming partial congestion relief). Excluded: intolerance to ≥1 GDMT class, eGFR <20, SBP <95 mmHg, potassium >5.5 mEq/L, HR <55 bpm.

**High-intensity care:** Uptitration of ≥3 GDMT pillars to target doses within 2 weeks of discharge; visits at 1 and 2 weeks post-discharge with NT-proBNP measurement to guide further titration. Mean 8 clinic visits over 90 days.

**Usual care:** Local usual practice; discharge letter disclosed lab values and NT-proBNP. Follow-up per local standards.

## Limitations

- Open-label design — patient-reported outcomes (EQ-5D VAS) subject to performance bias; high-intensity patients knew they were receiving intensive care
- Primary endpoint amended twice: (1) added 180-day patient contact; (2) changed primary endpoint from 90-day to 180-day and increased target enrolment from 900 to 1,800; DSMB stopped trial early for larger-than-expected benefit (not futility) at second interim analysis at ~1,069 patients
- 10% of patients (112/1,078) had final follow-up visit early (within 3 weeks of study termination, data cut-off Oct 13, 2022); down-weighting applied to cohort 1 results
- LVEF-unrestricted — HFpEF subgroup (32%) was not powered; numerically the >40% LVEF subgroup had a larger ARD (12.5%) but no significant interaction
- Causes of readmission not centrally adjudicated; based on investigator determination
- SGLT2 inhibitors not widely used during execution (trial designed 2018; approved/recommended late in trial); modern acute HF bundle would include SGLT2i
- Generalisability to healthcare systems with limited post-discharge follow-up capacity is uncertain; required average 4.8 specialist visits over 90 days

## Contradictions

The LVEF subgroup data (Figure 4, corrected from prior source page entry): LVEF >40% (HFmrEF/HFpEF) ARD = 12.5%; LVEF ≤40% (HFrEF) ARD = 6.3%. This is counter-intuitive given that traditional GDMT (beta-blocker, RAAS, MRA) has its strongest evidence base in HFrEF. The numerically larger benefit in the HFpEF subgroup may reflect: (a) greater baseline under-treatment of HFpEF patients at enrolment, (b) lower event rate denominator in smaller HFpEF subgroup (N=316 vs 692) inflating the relative numbers, or (c) random variation in an underpowered subgroup. No statistically significant interaction (P=0.27). The HFpEF subgroup finding is hypothesis-generating, not definitive.
