# FINEARTS Restoration: Revisiting the Role of Steroidal MRAs in HFmrEF/HFpEF

> JCF Ignite! editorial using Bayesian re-analysis to argue that FINEARTS-HF proves a class effect of all MRAs in HFmrEF/HFpEF, and that spironolactone — despite TOPCAT's frequentist null result — is a viable option when finerenone is inaccessible (~24× cost difference).

## Core Arguments

**1. FINEARTS-HF as a Bayesian prior for TOPCAT:**

Bayesian re-analysis of TOPCAT using FINEARTS-HF results (logHR = −0.163, SE = 0.0542) as a strong optimistic prior:
- **TOPCAT overall** (likelihood: HR 0.90, 95% CrI 0.78–1.05):
  - Posterior HR 0.87 (95% CrI 0.79–0.94)
  - Posterior probability HR < 1 (any benefit): **100%**
  - Posterior probability HR < 0.95 (clinically relevant benefit): **98%**
- **TOPCAT-Americas** (likelihood: HR 0.84, 95% CrI 0.70–0.99):
  - Posterior HR 0.85 (95% CrI 0.77–0.93)
  - Posterior probability HR < 1: **100%**
  - Posterior probability HR < 0.95: **99%**

**2. Pooled FINEARTS-HF + TOPCAT meta-analysis:**
- HR 0.87 (95% CI 0.79–0.95) for composite of CV death and HF hospitalisation
- Absolute risk reduction: ~1.5% over approximately 3 years
- Consistent across EF strata (EF <60% and ≥60%)

**3. MRA class effect argument:**
- No evidence of between-agent differences in hemodynamic or biochemical safety profiles — FINEARTS-HF did not show finerenone superior to theoretical sMRA for adverse effects
- Gynecomastia and sexual side effects isolated to spironolactone; not seen with eplerenone or finerenone
- No deaths from hyperkalemia in either TOPCAT or FINEARTS-HF
- Therefore, the theoretical safety advantage of finerenone over spironolactone is not confirmed by trial data

**4. Access/cost argument:**
- Finerenone: ~$3.61/day in Canada (coverage restricted to diabetic kidney disease)
- Spironolactone: ~$0.15/day — approximately 24-fold less expensive
- For patients with limited income or insurance, spironolactone will remain the only accessible MRA option
- Decision to use spironolactone (vs. finerenone, vs. no MRA at all) requires shared decision-making on efficacy, access, and adverse effects

**5. Future trials:**
- SPIRRIT-HFpEF (spironolactone; NCT02901184; target N=2000; registry-randomized): ongoing
- SPIRIT-HF (spironolactone; NCT04727073; target N=1300): ongoing
- A positive SPIRRIT or SPIRIT-HF result would confirm the class effect prospectively

## Contradictions
The Bayesian analysis uses FINEARTS-HF as a strong prior, which effectively assumes the two drugs share the same true effect size. If finerenone and spironolactone have materially different class effects — which remains possible — this analysis would overstate the posterior probability of benefit for spironolactone. Authors acknowledge this but argue there is no mechanistic basis for believing class effects differ.
