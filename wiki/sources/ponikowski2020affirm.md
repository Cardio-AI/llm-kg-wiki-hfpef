# Ponikowski 2020 — AFFIRM-AHF: IV Iron After Acute HF

> AFFIRM-AHF demonstrated that ferric carboxymaltose (FCM) in patients with acute HF, iron deficiency, and **LVEF <50%** reduced total HF hospitalisations (RR 0.74; P=0.013) and the composite of first HFH+CV death (HR 0.80; P=0.030), with the **primary composite not reaching significance** (RR 0.79; P=0.059); the trial population was predominantly HFrEF.

**Study type:** Randomised double-blind placebo-controlled trial · **NCT:** NCT02937454  
**N:** 1,132 patients (558 FCM, 574 placebo)  
**Sites:** 121 sites, 15 countries  
**Population:** **HFrEF/HFmrEF (LVEF <50%)** with iron deficiency (serum ferritin <100 ng/mL or ferritin 100–299 ng/mL + TSAT <20%) stabilised after acute HF episode requiring IV diuretics; NYHA II–IV  
**Note:** This is primarily a **HFrEF trial** — LVEF <50% required. Small HFpEF subgroup not powered for analysis.

### Key Details
- COVID-19 disrupted enrolment and follow-up (starting March 2020); pre-COVID sensitivity analysis shown above
- FCM reduced hospitalisation burden but did NOT reduce CV mortality
- Significant haemoglobin and ferritin increases with FCM
- Safety: no significant difference in adverse events; no excess of adverse iron-related events

## Methods

Stabilised acute HF patients (≥24h of IV diuretics, haemodynamic stability) randomised 1:1 to FCM or saline placebo at hospital discharge. FCM dosing: 500–2000 mg IV based on weight/Hb. Follow-up at 2, 6, and 12 weeks; primary endpoint was composite of total HFH + CV death at 52 weeks. Pre-specified COVID sensitivity analysis excluded events/follow-up from March 2020 onwards.

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

AFFIRM-AHF establishes IV FCM benefit for hospitalisation reduction in HFrEF+ID after acute decompensation. Its restriction to LVEF <50% creates the critical evidence gap for HFpEF, which was subsequently addressed by [[vonhaehling2024fair]] (small HFpEF-specific trial). The primary endpoint narrowly missed significance due to COVID disruption; total HFH and composite secondary endpoints were significant. CV mortality was not reduced — a key distinction from HFrEF pharmacotherapy.
