# Doi 2026 — Heart Failure Duration, Cardiac Remodeling, Dysfunction, and Hemodynamic Severity in HFpEF/HFmrEF: Insights From REDUCE LAP-HF II

> Post hoc analysis of REDUCE LAP-HF II (N=626): longer duration of HFpEF/HFmrEF diagnosis is independently associated with more advanced biatrial myopathy, worse LV/RV strain, higher resting filling pressures, and a higher risk of worsening HF (HR 1.70 for diagnosis >3y vs. <1y); duration of HF diagnosis did **not** significantly modify the response to atrial shunt therapy. Confirms in a secondary source that the trial's overall primary composite outcome (CV death or ischaemic stroke, HF events, and health status) was neutral.

## Summary

Post hoc secondary analysis of the REDUCE LAP-HF II trial (Corvia Atrial Shunt vs. sham; N=626 randomised, 522 with known duration-of-diagnosis data included in primary analysis) plus an independent validation cohort from Mayo Clinic (n=150, all LVEF ≥50%). Tests whether duration of HFpEF/HFmrEF diagnosis (categorised <1 year, 1–3 years, >3 years) is associated with cardiac structure/function, hemodynamics, and risk for worsening HF, and whether HF duration modifies response to atrial shunt therapy. (source: doi2026reducelaphf)

## Population

- 522 of 626 randomised REDUCE LAP-HF II patients had known duration-of-diagnosis data: <1 year n=211 (40.4%), 1–3 years n=152 (29.1%), >3 years n=159 (30.5%)
- Overall: median age 72y (Q1–Q3 66–77), 63.2% female, 82.5% HFpEF (EF ≥50%)/17.5% HFmrEF (EF 41–49%)
- Longer diagnosis duration associated with: more men, higher prevalence of ischaemic heart disease/MI, more HFmrEF, greater loop diuretic and beta-blocker use, less RAAS-blocker use, higher NT-proBNP (Table 1)

## Cardiac Structure, Function, and Hemodynamics by Duration (Table 2)

Longer duration of diagnosis associated with progressively worse remodeling and dysfunction (all trend P≤0.044 unless noted):
- **LV:** lower LVEF (55.2 → 54.4 → 52.2%, P=0.0008); higher LV mass index (77.1 → 79.7 → 86.5 g/m², P=0.0002); higher LV end-diastolic volume index (52.1 → 51.9 → 56.8 mL/m², P=0.044); worse LV global longitudinal strain (GLS): **16.4% [Q1-Q3 14.3–18.6] vs. 17.7% [15.4–20.0] vs. 18.4% [16.3–20.5]; P<0.0001**
- **RV:** larger RV end-diastolic volume (40.0 → 45.0 → 47.0 mL, P=0.002); lower TAPSE, RV s' velocity; worse RV free wall strain: **20.8% [19.2–22.7] vs. 22.2% [17.6–26.1] vs. 24.4% [19.2–27.2]; P<0.0001**
- **Biatrial myopathy** (composite LA/RA remodeling+dysfunction definition): 48.8% vs. 40.8% vs. 28.2% (from longest to shortest duration, i.e., more myopathy with longer duration); P<0.0001
- **Resting hemodynamics:** PCWP elevation (≥15 mmHg) more common with longer duration (64.9% vs. 70.7% vs. 75.8%); P=0.024; total pulmonary resistance and RV effective arterial elastance also worse with longer duration
- **Elevation in PCWP restricted to exercise only (normal at rest)** was **more common in shorter-duration patients**: 35.1% (<1y) vs. 29.3% (1-3y) vs. 24.2% (>3y); P=0.025 — i.e., shorter-duration disease is more likely to be an exercise-only hemodynamic abnormality, longer duration more likely resting congestion
- Findings independently **confirmed in the Mayo Clinic validation cohort** (n=50 per duration group; Table 4) across LV mass index, LA volume index, LV GLS, RV free wall strain, TAPSE

## Duration and Risk for Worsening HF

- Over median follow-up 1,541 days (Q1–Q3 1,179–1,828): HF event rate in the **sham arm** increased with duration — 15.5 per 100 patient-years (<1y) → 17.7 (1–3y) → 19.0 (>3y)
- Kaplan-Meier: **HR 1.70 (95% CI 1.17–2.48), P=0.006** for HF events, diagnosis >3 years vs. <1 year (reference)
- Similar findings within previously identified responder/nonresponder subgroups; **no significant treatment-by-duration interactions** (Supplemental Table 3)

## Effect of HF Duration on Response to Atrial Shunt Therapy (Table 3)

No statistically significant heterogeneity in atrial shunt treatment effect by duration of diagnosis overall, but numerically divergent direction across strata:
- **Diagnosis <1 year (n=211):** composite primary endpoint win ratio 1.18 (95% CI 0.85–1.64), P=.32; HF event IRR 0.74 (0.34–1.63), P=.46 (nonsignificant trend toward benefit); ΔKCCQ-OSS at 12 mo +3.9 (95% CI −1.3 to +9.2) favouring shunt, P=.14
- **Diagnosis 1–3 years (n=152):** composite win ratio 1.04 (0.71–1.53), P=.83; HF event IRR ~0.77–0.9 range, P=.44; ΔKCCQ-OSS +1.3 (−5.1 to +7.8), P=.69
- **Diagnosis >3 years (n=159):** composite win ratio 0.83 (0.57–1.20), P=.31; **atrial shunt treatment associated with an *increased* risk for worsening HF events: HR 1.72 (95% CI 1.26–2.35), P=0.001** (shunt 22.2 vs. sham 19.0 events per 100 patient-years); nonsignificant *worsening* in KCCQ-OSS with shunt (−2.1 [95% CI −8.7 to +4.5], P=.62)
- Authors' formal test for interaction across the three duration categories (win ratio, HF events, KCCQ) was **not significant** — so this apparent harm signal in the >3-year subgroup should not be treated as a confirmed effect modifier, but is noted as a numerically concerning finding warranting further study
- **Confirms overall trial primary result:** "In REDUCE LAP-HF II, atrial shunt treatment had no effect on the hierarchical composite outcome in patients with HFpEF/HFmrEF" (citing Shah et al., *Lancet* 2022 as reference 12 — primary results paper itself not yet ingested into this wiki)

## Interpretation / Perspectives (as stated by authors)

- **Competency in medical knowledge:** Longer duration of HFpEF/HFmrEF diagnosis is associated with more advanced structural/functional remodeling (including atrial myopathy and right heart dysfunction) and a higher HF-event risk profile. Earlier identification and staging may allow initiation of therapies before progression to more advanced, less reversible remodeling.
- **Translational outlook:** treatment response to atrial shunt therapy was not meaningfully modified by HF duration in this analysis; prospective studies are needed to validate HF duration as a staging biomarker and to test whether earlier-stage phenotypes derive greater benefit from targeted pharmacologic/device therapy.
- Explicitly analogises this progressive-worsening pattern to prior work (Sorimachi, Obokata, Omote) on progressive atrial/ventricular myopathy over time in HFpEF, reinforcing a "disease progression" (rather than purely static-phenotype) model of HFpEF/HFmrEF.
