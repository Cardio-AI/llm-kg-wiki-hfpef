# Tirzepatide for Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction and Obesity — SUMMIT

> Tirzepatide (dual GLP-1/GIP agonist) reduced the composite of CV death and worsening HF by 38% (HR 0.62; 95% CI 0.41–0.95; P=0.026) and improved KCCQ-CSS by 6.9 points at 52 weeks (P<0.001) in obese HFpEF — the first RCT to demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in hard HF events with a GLP-1RA class agent.

**Full citation:**
Packer M, Zile MR, Kramer CM, et al.; SUMMIT Trial Study Group. Tirzepatide for Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction and Obesity. *N Engl J Med.* 2025;392(5):427–437. doi:[10.1056/NEJMoa2410027](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2410027)
**Journal:** N Engl J Med. 2025;392(5):427–437 · **DOI:** 10.1056/NEJMoa2410027 · Published online November 16, 2024  
**Study type:** Phase 3 RCT · **N:** 731 (364 tirzepatide, 367 placebo) · **Follow-up:** Median 104 weeks  
**Enrollment:** April 20, 2021 – June 30, 2023 · **Sites:** 129 centres, 9 countries  
**Intervention:** Tirzepatide 2.5 mg SC weekly up-titrated every 4 weeks to max 15 mg/wk vs. matching placebo  
**Sponsor:** Eli Lilly and Company · **NCT:** NCT04847557

## Key Findings

Tirzepatide reduced the composite of CV death and worsening HF events by 38% (HR 0.62; P=0.026) over a median 2-year follow-up. At 52 weeks, KCCQ-CSS improved by a between-group median difference of 6.9 points (P<0.001), 6MWD by 18.3 m (P<0.001), body weight by −11.6 percentage points (P<0.001), and hs-CRP by −34.9 percentage points (P<0.001). NT-proBNP was not significantly reduced (ratio 0.90; 95% CI 0.79–1.01), consistent with NPs being unreliable markers of haemodynamic burden in obesity-related HFpEF. CV death and all-cause death were numerically higher with tirzepatide but not statistically significant.

## Methods

**Design:** International, double-blind, 1:1 randomised placebo-controlled Phase 3 trial. Randomisation stratified by: (1) HF decompensation within 12 months (yes/no); (2) history of T2DM (yes/no); (3) BMI <35 vs. ≥35 kg/m².

**Inclusion criteria:**
- Age ≥40; NYHA II–IV; LVEF ≥50%; BMI ≥30 kg/m²
- 6MWD 100–425 m; KCCQ-CSS ≤80
- Plus ≥1 of: NT-proBNP >200 pg/mL (sinus rhythm) or >600 pg/mL (AF), left atrial enlargement on echo, elevated filling pressures at rest or exercise
- Plus ≥1 of: HF decompensation within 12 months OR eGFR <70 mL/min/1.73m² (enrichment for event risk and CKD)
- T2DM permitted (HbA1c ≤9.5%) — distinguishes SUMMIT from [[step-hfpef]] (T2DM excluded)
- Elevated NPs not mandatory — accommodates obesity-related HFpEF where NPs are frequently in the normal range

**Prespecified co-primary endpoints:**
1. Time-to-first adjudicated CV death or worsening HF event (hospitalisation, urgent IV therapy, or outpatient oral diuretic intensification); alpha = 0.04; full trial duration
2. Change from baseline in KCCQ-CSS at 52 weeks; alpha = 0.01

**Key secondary endpoints (alpha-protected):** Change in 6MWD at 52 weeks; percent change in body weight at 52 weeks; percent change in hs-CRP at 52 weeks.

**Assessment schedule:** KCCQ-CSS at baseline, 24, and 52 weeks. eGFR (creatinine and cystatin C) at baseline, 12, 24, and 52 weeks. UACR at 24 and 52 weeks.

**Statistical analysis:** Cox proportional hazards model with 3 covariates (T2DM history, HFpEF-ABA score ≥0.8 vs <0.8, NT-proBNP <200 vs ≥200 pg/mL) for the event endpoint. Hodges–Lehmann method with multiple imputation for KCCQ-CSS. Intention-to-treat principle throughout.

**Endpoint revision:** Original primary was a hierarchical composite (all-cause death/WHF + KCCQ + 6MWD) plus 6MWD change. Approximately 1 year before trial end — before any unblinding — endpoints were revised to two standalone co-primaries following STEP-HFpEF results (August 2023: semaglutide WHF HR 0.08 on only 13 events), a discussion with the FDA, and the projection that sufficient events would accrue to test CV death/WHF prospectively as a standalone endpoint. No blinded data were accessed prior to the amendment.

**Note on deaths:** CV death and all-cause death were numerically higher with tirzepatide (not statistically significant; event counts 8 vs 5 and 19 vs 15 respectively). Authors note that most CV deaths in HFpEF are not preceded by worsening HF and may not reflect a drug-related signal at these event counts. Sensitivity analyses excluding deaths of undetermined cause from the composite were consistent with primary results.

**NT-proBNP:** Non-significant net reduction. This supports the authors' argument that NP elevation should not be a mandatory eligibility criterion for tirzepatide in obesity-related HFpEF. Effect on primary endpoints did not differ by baseline NT-proBNP subgroup (<200 / ≥200 pg/mL).

**Heart rate:** Mild chronotropic effect (+2.8 bpm). Semaglutide (STEP-HFpEF) did not report a significant HR increase; the clinical significance in HFpEF, where chronotropic incompetence is common, is uncertain.

### Subgroup Consistency

Effect consistent across all prespecified subgroups: age (<65 / ≥65 yr), sex, BMI (<35 / ≥35 kg/m²), T2DM (yes/no), HFpEF-ABA score (<0.8 / ≥0.8), EF (<60% / ≥60%), NT-proBNP (<200 / ≥200 pg/mL), KCCQ-CSS (<median / ≥median), eGFR, systolic BP, MRA use. No significant interaction terms for either primary endpoint.

### Safety

- Non-fatal AEs leading to discontinuation: 23 (6.3%) tirzepatide vs. 5 (1.4%) placebo
- GI-related discontinuation: 15 (4.1%) tirzepatide; 0 placebo
- Serious AEs: similar frequency between groups (Table S6)
- GI symptoms were largely transient; only ~4% of patients discontinued for GI reasons overall

## Substudy Results

### CKD Subanalysis (Packer et al., JACC 2025;85:1721–1735) — see [[packer2025summit-ckd]]

Of 731 randomised, **n=719 had baseline cystatin C measurements** (357 tirzepatide, 362 placebo). CKD was defined as eGFR-cystatin C <60 mL/min/1.73m²:
- CKD: 441/719 (61%), Tirzepatide n=212, Placebo n=229
- No CKD: 278/719 (39%), Tirzepatide n=145, Placebo n=133

eGFR-cystatin C was consistently ~9 mL/min/1.73m² lower than eGFR-creatinine (partly attributable to adipocyte synthesis of cystatin C). CKD patients had markedly worse clinical profile: AF 33.1% vs 12.9%, NYHA III 34.7% vs 16.2%, HHF within 12 months 70.7% vs 24.5%, NT-proBNP median ~281 vs ~68 pg/mL, 6MWD 278 vs 340 m, KCCQ-CSS 51.8 vs 56.0.

Tirzepatide benefit was consistent across CKD and no-CKD strata (interaction P=0.77). eGFR-creatinine showed an early dip at 12 weeks (likely artefactual — reduced muscle mass lowers creatinine generation) followed by improvement at 52 weeks. eGFR-cystatin C showed no early dip and net improvement at 52 weeks (+3.3 mL/min/1.73m²; P=0.004 across all patients), preferred as the less-biased estimate of true GFR trajectory.

### CMR Substudy (Kramer et al., JACC 2025;85:699–706) — see [[kramer2025summit-cmr]]

175 patients underwent baseline CMR; 106 completed paired CMR (50 tirzepatide, 56 placebo). 33% had inadequate image quality (breath-holding/ECG-gating challenges in obese patients). Predominantly Latin American (Brazil, Argentina); only 1 US participant per arm.

| CMR endpoint | Placebo-corrected change | 95% CI | P |
|---|---|---|---|
| LV mass | −11 g | −19 to −4 | 0.004 |
| Paracardiac adipose tissue | −45 mL | −69 to −22 | <0.001 |
| — Pericardial fat (driver) | −43 mL | −65 to −21 | <0.001 |
| — Epicardial adipose tissue | −2 mL | −9 to 5 | 0.57 (NS) |
| LVEDV | −7 mL | −16 to 2 | 0.10 (NS) |
| LVEF | −1% | −4 to 1 | 0.27 (NS) |

LV mass reduction correlated with weight loss (r=0.34; P<0.02). Pericardial fat, not EAT, drove the paracardiac fat decrease. No T1 mapping or late gadolinium enhancement — mechanism of LV mass reduction (cardiomyocyte hypertrophy vs. fibrosis) not determinable.

## Limitations

- BMI ≥30 required — excludes non-obese HFpEF (~40–50% of the HFpEF population)
- Endpoint revision (pre-specified, pre-unblinding, FDA-approved) may raise methodological concerns despite procedural correctness
- Numerically higher CV death (8 vs 5) and all-cause death (19 vs 15) with tirzepatide — not statistically significant, but mortality benefit is not established
- Median NT-proBNP ~183 pg/mL — below the 200 pg/mL threshold; generalisability to higher-NP HFpEF cohorts uncertain
- CMR substudy: 33% inadequate image quality; geographic representation limited (essentially no US/European patients); T1 mapping absent — cannot determine whether LV mass reduction reflects reduced cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, reduced fibrosis, or both
- Heart rate increase (+2.8 bpm): long-term implications in HFpEF (where chronotropic incompetence is common) are unknown
- SGLT2i use 15–19% at baseline — potential interaction with SGLT2i not evaluated

## Contradictions

SUMMIT CMR substudy: tirzepatide reduced LV mass by −11 g (P=0.004) by CMR. STEP-HFpEF echocardiographic substudy: semaglutide did not reduce LV mass by echo. Whether this reflects GIP co-agonism (tirzepatide = GLP-1+GIP; semaglutide = GLP-1 only) or CMR vs. echo measurement sensitivity is unresolved. See [[contradictions]].
