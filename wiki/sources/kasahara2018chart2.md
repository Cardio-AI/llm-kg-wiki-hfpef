# Kasahara 2018 — BNP Prognostic Impact Across HF Subtypes: CHART-2

> In 4,301 HF patients from the CHART-2 registry (including 2,893 HFpEF), BNP prognosticated mortality and hospitalisation equally across HFpEF, borderline EF, and HFrEF (interaction P=0.300); optimal BNP cut-offs derived by CART analysis were **30, 100, and 300 pg/mL** — applicable regardless of EF subtype.

## Key Findings

### BNP Levels by EF Subtype
| Subtype | Median BNP (pg/mL) |
|---|---|
| HFpEF (LVEF ≥50%) | 85.3 |
| Borderline (LVEF 40–49%) | 126 |
| HFrEF (LVEF <40%) | 208 |

HFpEF has substantially lower BNP than HFrEF even when controlling for outcomes — consistent with the "low-BNP HFpEF" phenotype.

### Prognostic Value

- **BNP HR per log₂ increase:** ~1.46 across all three HF subtypes (all P<0.001)
- **EF × BNP interaction test:** P=0.300 — BNP's prognostic impact is NOT significantly different across HFpEF, borderline, or HFrEF subgroups
- **Primary outcomes:** All-cause death and all-cause hospitalisation

### Optimal BNP Cut-offs (CART Analysis)
- Derived by classification and regression tree (CART): **30, 100, and 300 pg/mL**
- Risk strata: BNP <30 → lowest risk; 30–99 → low-intermediate; 100–299 → high-intermediate; ≥300 → highest risk
- These cut-offs apply consistently across EF subtypes

## Methods

CHART-2 (Chronic Heart Failure Analysis and Registry in the Tohoku District 2): prospective registry of HF patients in Japan. BNP measured at enrolment. Multivariable Cox proportional hazards models with BNP as log-transformed continuous variable. CART analysis to identify optimal cut-points. Landmark analysis at 1 year to evaluate time-varying BNP.

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

Establishes that BNP's prognostic value in HFpEF is equivalent to its value in HFrEF on a per-log unit basis — despite absolute levels being lower in HFpEF. The cut-offs (30/100/300 pg/mL) are clinically actionable for risk stratification across all HF subtypes. Supports and extends the [[ammar2025bnp]] meta-analysis finding that BNP dose-response is similar across EF groups. Particularly relevant for the "low-BNP HFpEF" discussion given that even BNP <100 pg/mL has prognostic meaning.
