# Ex-DHF Trial: Exercise Training in HFpEF

> Ex-DHF (ISRCTN86879094; n=322, 12 months, combined endurance + resistance training): **primary endpoint NOT met** (Packer composite score, Kendall's tau-b −0.073, P=0.17), but VO₂ improved by +1.3 mL/kg/min (P=0.003) and NYHA class improved substantially (OR 5.89, P<0.001); adherence ~53% likely attenuated composite endpoint benefit.

**Full citation:**
Edelmann F, et al. Combined endurance and resistance exercise training in heart failure with preserved ejection fraction: a randomized controlled trial. *Nat Med.* 2025;31:306–314. doi:[10.1038/s41591-024-03342-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-024-03342-7)

---

## Core Arguments

**Trial:** Randomised controlled trial. ISRCTN86879094 (Ex-DHF). N=**322**. Duration: **12 months**. Population: HFpEF patients. Intervention: supervised combined endurance (aerobic) + resistance training vs usual care.

**Primary outcome — Packer composite score:**
- Kendall's tau-b = **−0.073**, P=**0.17** — **NOT STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT**
- Packer hierarchical composite: all-cause mortality > HF hospitalisation > KCCQ > 6MWD
- Primary endpoint failure; driven by modest between-group differences in the composite hierarchy

**Secondary outcomes:**
- VO₂peak: **+1.3 mL/kg/min** at 12 months (P=0.003) — clinically meaningful aerobic benefit
- NYHA functional class improvement: **OR 5.89** (P<0.001) — substantial symptomatic benefit
- Adherence: **~53%** — substantially below anticipated; real-world exercise adherence barrier

**Interpretation:** The adherence gap (~53%) is the critical confounder. Per-protocol analyses would likely show larger effects. The 12-month sustained VO₂ improvement despite imperfect adherence suggests meaningful physiological training adaptation. Composite endpoint insensitivity may reflect short follow-up (12 months) for detecting hospitalisation-driven signals.

**Context:** Ex-DHF is the largest and longest exercise RCT completed in HFpEF to date. It extends the time horizon beyond earlier positive trials (HF-ACTION, SECRET) to examine whether exercise benefit translates to hard composite endpoints.

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

Ex-DHF's primary endpoint failure with significant secondary benefits mirrors the challenge of pharmacological HFpEF trials: composite endpoints powered for clinical events require either larger samples or longer follow-up than achievable in exercise trial infrastructure. The NYHA OR 5.89 is the largest symptomatic treatment effect observed in any HFpEF intervention trial — yet the composite was not met. This positions composite endpoint design, not exercise efficacy, as the methodological question.

Adherence (~53%) is a structural barrier for exercise as therapy — more demanding on patient behaviour than pill-taking. The gap between intention-to-treat and per-protocol effects may be large.

Current ESC 2023 guidelines maintain exercise training as recommended (Class I, Level A) based on VO₂ and quality-of-life evidence. Ex-DHF supports physiological rationale but raises the bar for what the HFpEF field should demand from exercise trials.
