# SOGALDI-PEF Trial (Ferreira 2025)

> Investigator-initiated, two-centre, randomised, open-label blinded-endpoint crossover trial (N=108) testing dapagliflozin + spironolactone combination vs. dapagliflozin alone in HFmrEF/HFpEF: the combination produced a significantly greater reduction in NT-proBNP (11% relative reduction) and blood pressure, at the cost of a larger eGFR decline and more hyperkalemia — the first dedicated RCT directly comparing an SGLT2i/MRA combination against SGLT2i alone in this population.

**Study type:** Investigator-initiated, 2-centre, randomised, open-label, blinded-endpoint crossover trial · **N:** 108 randomised (105 completed ≥1 sequence, 104 completed both) · **Population:** HFmrEF/HFpEF (LVEF >40%), age ≥50y, NYHA II–IV, structural heart disease, elevated NT-proBNP/BNP (AF-adjusted thresholds), eGFR ≥30, K ≤5.5 mmol/L; not on SGLT2i/MRA at baseline · **Follow-up:** 3 sequential 12-week treatment periods (originally 3-arm crossover; protocol-amended to 2-sequence design after enrolment start) · **Intervention/Exposure:** Dapagliflozin 10mg alone vs. dapagliflozin 10mg + spironolactone 25mg (every-other-day to daily, titrated by eGFR/K) · **Primary outcome:** Between-sequence difference in log-transformed NT-proBNP (combination vs. dapagliflozin alone)

## Key Findings

- **Primary outcome met:** dapagliflozin/spironolactone combination reduced log NT-proBNP more than dapagliflozin alone — difference −0.11 (95% CI −0.22 to −0.01 log units, P=0.035), corresponding to an **11% relative reduction** (geometric mean ratio 0.89, 95% CI 0.80–0.99)
- **Responder analysis:** odds of achieving a ≥20% NT-proBNP reduction were higher with the combination — OR 2.27 (95% CI 1.16–4.44, P=0.016)
- **Secondary/exploratory outcomes (combination vs. dapagliflozin alone):** SBP −5.2 mmHg (95% CI −8.4 to −2.0); eGFR −6.4 mL/min/1.73m² (95% CI −8.3 to −4.4), with corresponding creatinine +0.11 mg/dL; log-UACR −0.32 (95% CI −0.54 to −0.11); weight −1.23 kg (95% CI −1.83 to −0.64); serum potassium +0.32 mmol/L (95% CI 0.23–0.41)
- **No significant difference between the combination and dapagliflozin alone for LAV, LVM, LVEF, PASP, DBP, hemoglobin, or KCCQ-OSS** in the primary sequence comparison
- **Safety:** hyperkalemia (K >5.0 mmol/L) 25.7% (combination) vs. 7.6% (dapagliflozin alone); K >5.5 mmol/L 4.8% vs. 0.9%; worsening kidney function (eGFR decline ≥30% from baseline) 20.0% vs. 6.7%; hypotension (SBP <100 mmHg) more frequent with combination (8.6%) and spironolactone-alone (9.8%) sequences than dapagliflozin alone (3.8%)
- **Atrial fibrillation modified the treatment effect:** NT-proBNP reduction with the combination was seen in patients in sinus/paced rhythm (difference −0.18, 95% CI −0.31 to −0.04) but not in those with AF/flutter (difference +0.02, 95% CI −0.15 to 0.19; P-interaction=0.084)
- Treatment-sequence carry-over effects were statistically nonsignificant (interaction P=0.51 for the 3-sequence design, P=0.30 for the 2-sequence sensitivity analysis)
- Trial enrolled 108 of 2,032 screened (5.3% enrolment yield) — most common exclusions: not meeting inclusion criteria (1,301, chiefly natriuretic peptide out of range n=201 and LVEF <40% n=47), already on MRA/SGLT2i at screening (721), cancer (53)

## Methods (brief)

Two-centre (Porto: São João, Gaia/Espinho), investigator-initiated and investigator-led, sponsored by the Faculty of Medicine, University of Porto, with independent data/safety monitoring (BlueClinical CRO). Originally designed as a 3-treatment-sequence crossover (dapagliflozin, spironolactone, combination — 6 possible orderings); protocol amended in October 2023 to drop the spironolactone-alone arm and shorten washout from 4 weeks to 1 week (after EMPEROR-Preserved/DELIVER confirmed SGLT2i benefit, making an SGLT2i-free arm less ethically justifiable); remaining participants randomised into 2 sequences (dapagliflozin→combination or combination→dapagliflozin). NT-proBNP measured centrally in triplicate (Alinity i NT-proBNP immunoassay, Abbott), treating-physician-blinded. Multilevel mixed models for repeated measures, nested at patient level, unstructured covariance, robust variance estimator; no imputation for missing data; no multiplicity correction for secondary/subgroup analyses (explicitly noted as exploratory).

## Results

Baseline characteristics (N=108): median age 76y (Q1–Q3 71–81), 57% women, 58% hypertensive aetiology, 41% AF, 45% diabetes, median NT-proBNP 746 pg/mL (401–1493), median LVEF 59% (57–63), 45.6% eGFR <60. ## Limitations

Crossover design (not parallel-group) — cannot fully separate order/period effects from treatment effects despite formal nonsignificant interaction tests; mid-trial protocol amendment (dropping the spironolactone-alone arm) complicates the originally-planned 3-sequence analysis and reduces power for some comparisons (spironolactone-alone analyses limited to n=41). Open-label design with blinded-endpoint assessment only (not double-blind) — NT-proBNP assessors were blinded to sequence, but patients/treating physicians were not. Modest sample size (n=108) for subgroup/interaction analyses (e.g. the AF-modification finding, P=0.084, did not reach conventional significance). Single-country (Portugal), predominantly elderly (median 76y) population — generalisability to younger/other-ethnicity HFpEF populations untested. No hard clinical outcome (mortality, HF hospitalisation) endpoints — biomarker/surrogate trial only.
