# Global Burden of Heart Failure — Savarese 2022

> HF affects >64 million worldwide; incidence stable or declining in developed countries while HFpEF prevalence steadily increases and may become the predominant HF form; 5-year mortality ~50–75% despite decades of progress; costs rising.

**Full citation:**
Savarese G, Becher PM, Lund LH, Seferovic P, Rosano GMC, Coats AJS. Global burden of heart failure: a comprehensive and updated review of epidemiology. *Cardiovasc Res.* 2022;118(17):3272–3287. doi:[10.1093/cvr/cvac013](https://doi.org/10.1093/cvr/cvac013)

Invited review commissioned by ESC. Comprehensive synthesis of global epidemiological data on HF prevalence, incidence, aetiology, outcomes, and costs by EF phenotype and geographic region.

---

## Core Arguments

### 1. Global Prevalence

HF affects an estimated **>64 million people worldwide**. Overall prevalence 1–3% in the general adult population of industrialised countries. Prevalence is increasing due to population ageing, improved survival post-MI, and prolonged HF survival with evidence-based therapy. (source: Savarese2022GlobalBurden)

**Selected national estimates (Figure 1):**

|---|---|

| UK | 1.5–1.6 |
| Sweden | 1.8–2.2 |
| Norway | 1.9–2.4 |
| USA | 2.4–3.0 |
| Canada | 3–4 |
| Taiwan | 6 |
| Indonesia | 5 |
| South Korea | 0.6 |
| China | 1.3 |
| Japan | <1 |
| South America | ~1 |
| Africa | Data missing |

USA: ~6 million Americans ≥20 years with HF (2015–2018 NHANES); projected to rise to 3.0% by 2030. (source: Savarese2022GlobalBurden)

### 2. HFpEF Prevalence and Phenotype Distribution

HFpEF prevalence is **steadily increasing** and is projected to become the most common HF form. HFrEF prevalence is stable or declining. The proportion of HFpEF varies substantially by registry, setting, and EF definition:

| Registry | Setting | HFrEF (EF≤40%) | HFmrEF (EF 41–49%) | HFpEF (EF≥50%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ESC-HF-LT (Europe) | Outpatient | 60% | 24% | 16% |
| Swedish HF Registry | National | 56% | 21% | 23% |
| Italian National Network | Hospitalised (acute) | ~52% | n/a | 23% |
| OPTIMIZE-HF (USA) | Hospitalised | 49% | 17% | 24% |
| GWTG-HF (USA) | Hospitalised | 39% | 14% | 47% |
| G-CHF (global, 40 countries) | Mixed | 54% | 21% | 24% |
| ASIAN-HF | Asian, mixed | 81% | 6% | 13% |
| China-HF Registry | Hospitalised, China | 40% | 23% | 36% |
| Japan | Hospitalised | — | — | 43% |

(source: Savarese2022GlobalBurden)

**Key interpretation:** The wide range (16–47%) reflects diagnostic criteria, clinical setting (inpatient vs. outpatient), geographic region, and inclusion criteria. Asian registries show markedly lower HFpEF proportions, potentially reflecting different aetiology profiles and patient demographics. GWTG-HF (inpatient USA) shows highest HFpEF proportion — partly driven by inclusive EF ≥50% definition and obesity-heavy US population.

### 3. Incidence

Global incidence: 1–20 cases per 1000 person-years; most data from Europe/North America show 2–3 cases per 1000 population.

**Selected incidence estimates (Figure 2):**

| Country/Region | Incidence (/1000/year) |
|---|---|
| Germany | 6.5 |
| Canada | 5.3–8.1 |
| Norway | 3.4–3.5 |
| Europe (HFA Atlas) | 3.2 |
| Sweden | 3.1 |
| USA | 2.2–3.2 |
| Australia | 2.2–3.4 |
| UK | 3.3–3.5 |
| Belgium | 2.6–2.7 |
| India | 0.7–1.5 |

**EF-specific incidence trend (Olmsted County, USA, 2000–2010):**
- HFrEF incidence: declined by 45% (overall decrease 37%)
- HFpEF incidence: declined by 28% — less than HFrEF, and increasing in absolute terms in both sexes
- Net effect: HFpEF share of incident HF is growing

Incidence is declining in developed countries overall. HFpEF incidence declining more slowly and prevalence rising due to longer survival. (source: Savarese2022GlobalBurden)

### 4. Aetiology

**IHD:** Most common HF aetiology globally. Population-attributable risk: 65% for men, 48% for women. In HFpEF: 32% IHD vs. 56–60% in HFrEF (Swedish HF Registry). IHD likely plays a mechanistically different (microvascular/metabolic) role in HFpEF vs. HFrEF.

**Hypertension:** Primary aetiology in 15% of HF overall. Comorbid hypertension: 56–72% of HFpEF patients vs. lower in HFrEF. Role as HFpEF driver (LVH, myocardial stiffness) is mechanistic, not just incidental.

**Valvular disease:** 20% of HFpEF patients (ESC-HF-LT) vs. 4% HFrEF, 10% HFmrEF — substantially enriched in HFpEF. Particularly paradoxical low-gradient aortic stenosis and functional MR.

**Idiopathic DCM:** ~30% of HF in ESC-HF-LT; primarily HFrEF; largely absent from HFpEF.

**RHD:** Still major HF cause in Sub-Saharan Africa (up to 40%). Represents 25.5% of 2015 GBD heart failure deaths globally.

**Chagas cardiomyopathy:** Most common non-ischaemic CMP in South America; 300,000 carriers in USA (immigrants); ~22% of Chagas patients reach HF stage C/D.

**Cancer therapy-induced:** Anthracyclines → HF incidence 1–2%; trastuzumab + anthracyclines → up to 18.6%; breast cancer radiotherapy → up to 10% develop HFpEF. (source: Savarese2022GlobalBurden)

### 5. Outcomes

**All-cause mortality remains high:**

| Time point | Approximate mortality |
|---|---|
| 30-day | ~2–3% |
| 1-year | ~15–30% |
| 3-year | ~30–50% |
| 5-year | ~50–75% |

**EF-specific comparison (ESC-HF-LT, 1-year mortality):**
- HFrEF: 8.8%; HFmrEF: 7.6%; **HFpEF: 6.3%**

**EF-specific comparison (GWTG-HF, 5-year mortality):**
- HFrEF: 75.3%; **HFpEF: 75.7%** — comparable at 5 years

The 5-year convergence is explained by **cause-of-death divergence** (Figure 3):

| Registry | Phenotype | CV death | Non-CV death | HF-specific death |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ESC-HF-LT (1y) | HFpEF | 37% | 30% | 21% |
| ESC-HF-LT (1y) | HFrEF | 33% | 20% | 26% |
| GWTG-HF (5y) | HFpEF | 52% | 37% | — |
| GWTG-HF (5y) | HFrEF | 65.9% | — | — |

**Key interpretation:** HFpEF has lower CV mortality at 1 year vs. HFrEF, but higher non-CV mortality (30.7% vs. 20.1%). Non-CV deaths (cancer, respiratory, infections) offset the CV mortality advantage by 5 years. CV mortality is declining over time; non-CV mortality is rising — this is more pronounced in HFpEF and reflects the increasing comorbidity burden driving non-CV deaths. (source: Savarese2022GlobalBurden)

### 6. Hospitalizations

- HF = leading cause of hospitalisation in patients >65 years
- Accounts for 1–2% of all hospital admissions in the Western world
- 30–40% of HF patients hospitalised in first year; 50% re-admitted within 1 year
- **HFpEF: 63% of hospitalisations are non-CV** (Olmsted County) — distinct from HFrEF
- Re-hospitalisation costs substantially higher for HFpEF than HFrEF (Irish registry data)
- HFpEF uses greater total healthcare resources than HFrEF or unknown EF (Swedish data)

**HFpEF-specific:** Expected to drive cost increases disproportionately as prevalence rises in elderly; data on EF-specific costs still limited. (source: Savarese2022GlobalBurden)

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

Primary contribution: comprehensive global epidemiological data quantifying the HFpEF burden across prevalence, incidence, aetiology, mortality, hospitalisation, and costs. Fills the epidemiology gap in the wiki (existing pages were thin on numbers). Key HFpEF-specific findings:

1. HFpEF fraction varies 16–47% by registry — not a fixed proportion
2. HFpEF incidence declining slower than HFrEF; prevalence rising
3. HFpEF 5-year mortality comparable to HFrEF (75.7% vs 75.3%) despite lower CV death
4. Non-CV mortality dominates in HFpEF — cancer, respiratory, infections
5. HFpEF hospitalisations 63% non-CV (Olmsted County)
6. IHD less prevalent in HFpEF (32%) vs HFrEF (56–60%); valvular disease enriched (20% vs 4%)
7. Costs rising; HFpEF projected as dominant future cost driver

## Contradictions
- HFpEF mortality at 1 year appears lower than HFrEF (6.3% vs. 8.8%), but at 5 years outcomes are comparable (75.7% vs. 75.3%). This apparent paradox is explained by non-CV mortality rising in HFpEF — not improvement in HFpEF outcomes, but offset by competing non-CV causes. This distinction matters for trial endpoint design in HFpEF (CV-specific endpoints miss non-CV deaths). See [[contradictions]].
- The wide variation in HFpEF prevalence across registries (16–47%) reflects diagnostic heterogeneity and setting differences, not a true biological variation. This has direct implications for [[hfpef-diagnostic-definitions]] and trial generalisability.

See [[contradictions]].
