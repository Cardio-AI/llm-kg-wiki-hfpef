# CHARM-Preserved

> Candesartan missed its unadjusted primary endpoint (HR 0.89, P=0.118) but reached borderline significance on covariate adjustment (P=0.051); adjudicated HF hospitalisations significant on adjusted analysis (P=0.047); new-onset diabetes reduced 40% (P=0.005); first large RCT to show a hospitalisation signal with RAAS blockade in HFpEF.

**File:** `raw/2003-LANCET-Yusuf-CHARM-preserved_study.pdf` · **Authors:** Yusuf S, Pfeffer MA et al. · **Year:** 2003 · **Journal:** Lancet 362:777–781  
**Study type:** RCT · **N:** 3,023 · **Population:** Symptomatic HF, LVEF >40%, NYHA II–IV, history of cardiac hospitalisation  
**Follow-up:** Median 36.6 months · **Intervention:** Candesartan 32 mg/day vs. placebo  
**Primary outcome:** CV death or HF hospitalisation (adjudicated)  
**NCT:** NCT00634712 (retrospectively registered) · **Enrollment:** Mar 1999–Jul 2000 · **Sites:** 618 centres, 26 countries  
**Sponsor:** AstraZeneca

---

## Key Findings

- Primary composite (unadjusted): HR **0.89** (0.77–1.03), **P=0.118** — missed
- Primary composite (covariate-adjusted): HR **0.86** (0.74–1.00), **P=0.051** — borderline
- Annual event rates: 8.1% candesartan vs. 9.1% placebo
- CV death: 170 vs. 170; HR 0.99 (P=0.918) — no effect
- **HF hospitalisation (adjudicated, adjusted):** HR 0.84 (0.70–1.00), **P=0.047** — significant
- **HF hosp (investigator-reported):** 230 vs. 279 patients; total admissions 402 vs. 566; **P=0.017 / P=0.014**
- **New-onset diabetes:** 47 vs. 77; HR **0.60** (0.41–0.86), **P=0.005** — 40% reduction, significant
- Broader composite (CV death+CHF+MI+stroke, adjusted): HR 0.86 (0.75–0.99), **P=0.037** — significant
- Investigator-reported composite: HR 0.85 (0.73–0.98), P=0.028 — significant

## Methods

**Design:** Double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled trial. Part of the CHARM programme (three parallel component trials). ITT analysis. Both unadjusted and pre-specified covariate-adjusted analyses.

**Dose:** Started 4 or 8 mg; forced titration doubling every 2 weeks; target 32 mg once daily from 6 weeks. Patients seen at 2, 4, 6 weeks, then 6 months, then every 4 months.

**Eligibility:**
- Age ≥18, NYHA II–IV ≥4 weeks
- History of hospital admission for cardiac reason
- LVEF >40%
- ACEi initially excluded; permitted after HOPE trial publication (mid-trial protocol change)
- Physicians free to prescribe all other treatments except ARBs

**Statistical power:** 80% power to detect 18% relative risk reduction (annual event rate 11%; N=2,900 target).

**Adjudication:** Independent committee blinded to treatment and CHARM component; adjudicated CV death, MI, and HF hospitalisations. Investigator-reported outcomes pre-specified as supportive analysis.

## Limitations

- LVEF >40% includes HFmrEF (41–49%, 35% of cohort) by modern taxonomy — not pure HFpEF ≥50%
- Primary unadjusted endpoint missed; covariate-adjusted P=0.051 borderline; hospitalisation significance dependent on adjustment
- No NP entry criterion — diagnosis based on clinical judgment + LVEF; pre-modern diagnostic era
- Only 20% on ACEi at baseline (protocol changed mid-trial after HOPE); very different background therapy from modern trials
- ACEi protocol change mid-trial introduces heterogeneity in background RAAS exposure
- Mean age 67.2y — younger than contemporary HFpEF trials (mean ~72y in EMPEROR-Preserved/DELIVER)
- Pre-registration era (NCT retrospective); no formal trial registration at time of conduct
- New diabetes reduction may be overestimated (40% in this trial vs. 22% in CHARM-Overall); chance exaggeration acknowledged by authors
- Modest baseline imbalance favouring placebo arm in several prognostic factors

## Contradictions
- Unadjusted primary missed (P=0.118); adjusted borderline (P=0.051); adjudicated HF hosp adjusted P=0.047; investigator-reported composite P=0.028. Multiple analytical frameworks give different conclusions from the same data — classic selective-reporting concern in borderline trials.
- Investigator-reported vs. adjudicated hospitalisation results diverge: investigator-reported composite P=0.028 (significant); adjudicated primary P=0.118 (not significant). The discrepancy is acknowledged but unexplained. See [[contradictions]].
- New diabetes 40% reduction (P=0.005) is a stronger result than the primary efficacy outcome — an unexpected secondary finding in a trial that "failed."
