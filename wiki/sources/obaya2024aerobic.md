# Upper vs Lower Limb Aerobic Training in HFpEF

> RCT (n=40, 12 weeks): lower-limb aerobic training (cycling) produced significantly greater VO₂ improvement than upper-limb training (arm ergometer) in HFpEF (21.51 vs 19.26 mL/kg/min post-training, P<0.001 between groups); LVEF unchanged in both groups — supporting lower-limb modality preference and confirming that exercise benefit in HFpEF is peripheral, not cardiac systolic.

## Core Arguments

**Trial:** Randomised controlled trial. N=**40**. Duration: **12 weeks**. Population: HFpEF patients. Intervention: lower-limb aerobic training (cycle ergometer) vs upper-limb aerobic training (arm ergometer) — same exercise duration and intensity protocol.

**Primary outcome — VO₂peak post-training:**
- Lower-limb group (cycling): **21.51 mL/kg/min**
- Upper-limb group (arm ergometer): **19.26 mL/kg/min**
- **Between-group P<0.001** — cycling significantly superior for aerobic capacity
- Both groups improved from baseline; the between-group advantage indicates modality-specific peripheral adaptation

**LVEF:**
- **Unchanged in both groups** — expected in HFpEF; confirms exercise benefit is not mediated by improvement in systolic function

**Mechanism:** Lower-limb training engages larger muscle mass — greater cardiac output demand, higher peripheral O₂ extraction capacity, more substantial skeletal muscle adaptations (mitochondrial density, capillary recruitment) than upper-limb training alone.

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

Provides practical exercise prescription guidance for HFpEF: lower-limb modalities (cycling, walking, step training) are preferable to upper-limb modalities for improving peak aerobic capacity. The unchanged LVEF finding confirms that the therapeutic target of aerobic exercise in HFpEF is peripheral skeletal muscle physiology, not cardiac systolic performance — consistent with the pathophysiological role of muscle deconditioning in HFpEF exercise intolerance.
