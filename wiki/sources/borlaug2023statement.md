# Heart Failure With Preserved Ejection Fraction — JACC Scientific Statement

> Comprehensive 2023 state-of-the-field statement covering HFpEF epidemiology, pathophysiology, diagnosis, and treatment, synthesising evidence through the SGLT2i era and providing a 24-item knowledge gap agenda.

**File:** `raw/2023-JACC-Borlaug-HFpEF_scientific_statement.pdf` · **Authors:** Borlaug BA, Sharma K, Shah SJ, Ho JE · **Year:** 2023 · **Journal:** J Am Coll Cardiol 81(18):1810–1834

## Core Arguments

### Epidemiology
- HFpEF incidence: ~27 cases/10,000 person-years; rising steadily across community cohorts
- Lifetime risk at age 45: 1 in 10 (both sexes); women > men for prevalence (but incident similar); 5-year mortality 75.3% (GWTG registry); 30-day readmission 21%
- Projected to become the dominant HF subtype; HFpEF hospitalizations doubled (189,260 → 495,095) from 2008 to 2018
- Race disparities: Black women have highest HFpEF hospitalization rates; underdiagnosis likely due to lower NP levels in Black individuals
- Phenotypic shift over 2 decades: isolated hypertensive LV hypertrophy → multimorbid (obesity, T2DM, metabolic syndrome, CKD)

### Pathophysiology
Four overlapping mechanistic pathways (Figure 4, Table 3):

**1. Intrinsic myocardial dysfunction (ventricular stiffening)**
- Increased passive myocardial stiffness: titin phosphorylation changes, collagen cross-linking, interstitial fibrosis
- LV relaxation impairment (active) and compliance reduction (passive) → elevated filling pressures
- Moderate fibrosis in 27% on endomyocardial biopsy

**2. Obesity-cardiometabolic stress**
- Excess body fat → increased total and stressed blood volume → elevated filling pressures even without frank hypervolemia
- Pericardial fat → pericardial restraint and ventricular interdependence (↑ ventricular interaction)
- Impaired RV sarcomere function; reduced venous capacitance
- Myocardial energetics: reduced efficiency with increasing BMI; gene expression shifts (mitochondrial pathways up in HFpEF; ER stress, autophagy, angiogenesis down)

**3. Microvascular inflammation (Paulus/Tschope 2013 paradigm)**
- Comorbidities (obesity, T2DM, HTN) → systemic proinflammatory state → CMD → ↓NO/cGMP → cardiomyocyte kinase G dysfunction → hypertrophy, stiffness, fibrosis
- ~75% of HFpEF patients have CMD evidence (PROMIS-HFpEF data)
- Expanded to include metabo-inflammation: nutrient overload → macrophage/adipocyte activation → chronic microvascular inflammation
- Note: trials targeting cGMP pathway (isosorbide, vericiguat) were neutral; SGLT2i improve cGMP indirectly via nutrient deprivation/autophagy — revised mechanistic hypothesis

**4. Noncardiac mechanisms**
- Skeletal muscle: type I → II fibre shift, sarcopenia, ↓ mitochondrial density → peripheral O2 extraction impairment
- Lung: diffusion abnormalities (mild-moderate) common in HFpEF; contribute to dyspnea
- Kidney: CKD prevalent; aldosterone activation; volume retention
- Adipose tissue: visceral fat excess; adipokine dysregulation
- Autonomic: chronotropic incompetence (~50%); baroreflex insensitivity; abnormal venous capacitance

### Cardiac Pathophysiology and Disease Progression (Central Illustration)
- 4-chamber involvement: LV → LA → pulmonary vasculature → RV (sequential but also parallel)
- LA function is the most robust imaging marker (LA strain > E/e' for distinguishing HFpEF from non-cardiac dyspnea)
- AF is a "biomarker" of LA myopathy; present in ~80% of patients with resting LA hypertension
- Roughly 80% of HFpEF patients have PH; PH progression → RV dysfunction → "Stage D" features
- Spectrum: exercise-induced LA hypertension → resting LA hypertension → right heart failure

### Multiple Phenotype Hypothesis (Figure 5)
Five overlapping phenogroups within HFpEF:
1. Obese/cardiometabolic
2. Arterial stiffening
3. Ischemic (CMD/epicardial CAD)
4. Pulmonary vascular disease
5. LA myopathy

Non-HFpEF "masqueraders" (Table 2): cardiac amyloidosis, HCM, sarcoidosis, hemochromatosis, Fabry, restrictive/infiltrative CMP, high-output HF, myocarditis, congenital HD, valvular, pericardial, CAD — each has specific clues, evaluation, and treatment differing from garden-variety HFpEF.

### Diagnosis
- Relies on symptoms + LVEF ≥50% + objective evidence of congestion (elevated filling pressures)
- ~1/3 of HFpEF patients have NPs below typical thresholds (obesity, African ancestry, NPPB polymorphisms, insulin resistance)
- Scoring systems: H₂FPEF (6 items, 0–9) and HFA-PEFF (4-step). H₂FPEF shown to be more sensitive with fewer variables (Figure 7); both validated against invasive hemodynamics
- Gold standard: invasive hemodynamic testing — PCWP ≥15 mmHg (rest, supine) or PCWP ≥25 mmHg (exercise end-expiration), or PCWP/CO slope >2 mmHg/L/min (upright); resting PCWP >15 mmHg also diagnostic
- Alternative provocative maneuvers: saline infusion, passive leg raise (PCWP ≥18 mmHg with saline/leg raise) — less physiologic than exercise, don't challenge CV system to same degree
- Table 1: Five HFpEF definition types compared (universal, hemodynamic, epidemiologic, HF hospitalization, ICD codes) — each has distinct use case and limitation
- Table 4: Comprehensive laboratory evaluation panel including NTproBNP/BNP, troponin, prealbumin (TTR), free light chains, iron studies, TSH, uric acid, lipids, HbA1c, UACR

### Treatment (Figure 8 algorithm)
- **SGLT2i first-line** for all HFpEF regardless of diabetes: empagliflozin (EMPEROR-Preserved, 18–21% ↓ primary endpoint) and dapagliflozin (DELIVER) showed equivalent benefit; benefit independent of EF subgroup (applies across EF spectrum ≥40%)
- **Diuretics**: for volume overload relief; MRA preferred upfront if MRA-eligible
- **MRA (spironolactone/eplerenone)**: may reduce HF hospitalization, particularly lower-range LVEF; TOPCAT data (Americas subgroup); ongoing SPIRRIT/SPIRIT-HF trials
- **ARNI (sacubitril/valsartan)**: PARAGON-HF missed primary overall (P=0.06); benefit in women and EF <median (~57%); FDA approved "below normal EF"; PARALLAX showed no functional improvement; authors recommend if EF 55–60% or after diuretic, with uptitration to max tolerated
- **Exercise training**: improves aerobic capacity and QoL; moderate intensity = high intensity
- **CHAMPION trial (CardioMEMS)**: PA pressure monitoring → 50% reduction in HF hospitalization vs. standard care (NNT 2 over 18 months) — Class 2a in AHA 2022
- **REDUCE LAP-HF II** (atrial shunt): no significant effect on primary endpoint overall; subgroup: patients without latent PVD (exercise PVR ≤1.74 WU) appeared to improve
- **Pericardiotomy**: small pilot studies showing diastolic reserve improvement; animal model support; awaiting randomized data
- **Splanchnic nerve ablation (REBALANCE-HF)**: open-label roll-in data positive; blinded phase not yet reported
- **24 knowledge gaps identified** (Table 6): ranging from noninvasive diagnostic accuracy to therapies targeting specific phenogroups

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

This is the single most comprehensive synthesis of HFpEF knowledge through 2023. Updates epidemiology, pathophysiology hierarchy (obesity/cardiometabolic now as prominent as hypertensive stiffening), the phenotype model, treatment algorithm (SGLT2i first-line now established), and provides a research agenda. Also introduces the 5-phenotype Venn diagram as the current standard framing.

## Contradictions

- cGMP/NO pathway therapies (isosorbide mononitrate [NEAT-HFpEF], vericiguat [VITALITY-HFpEF]) were neutral despite mechanistic rationale — the Paulus/Tschope microvascular inflammation → cGMP pathway may not be the primary driver of functional impairment. SGLT2i may work via a different route through this same pathway (nutrient deprivation/autophagy). See [[contradictions]].
- Sacubitril/valsartan: FDA approved vs. ESC no recommendation — divergent regulatory interpretation of the same PARAGON data. See [[contradictions]].
