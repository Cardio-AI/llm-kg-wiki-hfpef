# Babb 2026 — Ventilatory Limitation to Exercise in Obese HFpEF: No Room to Breathe

> In 42 obese HFpEF patients, sublingual nitroglycerin (NTG) lowered PCWP and cardiac output but did **not** change exercise capacity, breathing mechanics, or lung volumes — patients stopped exercise at the **same mechanical ventilatory limitation point** before and after NTG, demonstrating that exercise is ventilatory-limited, not primarily cardiac-limited, in obese HFpEF.

## Key Findings

### Effect of NTG on Hemodynamics
- NTG reduced PCWP at rest (P<0.01) and at 20W (P<0.01) — confirmed cardiac unloading
- NTG reduced cardiac output (CO) at rest (P<0.01) and at 20W (P<0.01) — venodilation, reduced preload
- Increased a-v̄O₂ difference at rest and 20W (compensatory peripheral O₂ extraction)

### Effect of NTG on Exercise Capacity
- **No significant change in exercise capacity** (work rate at peak exercise; P=NS)
- Peak work rate was identical before and after NTG treatment

### Effect of NTG on Breathing Mechanics
- **No differences in breathing mechanics at rest, 20W, or peak exercise after NTG** — EELV, EILV, FVC, FEV₁, EFL essentially unchanged
- EELV before vs. after NTG: R²=0.96 (near-perfect correlation) — lung volumes completely unchanged by PCWP reduction

### Ventilatory Response Blunting
- V̇E/V̇CO₂ qualitatively **declined** from 20W to peak both before and after NTG
- Normally V̇E/V̇CO₂ rises at heavy exercise; the observed decline indicates blunted ventilatory response at maximal exercise (approaching mechanical ventilatory ceiling)
- This blunted response was present BEFORE and persisted AFTER NTG

### Interpretation: Ventilatory Limitation Model
Patients appeared to stop exercise due to three convergent ventilatory constraints:
1. **Operational lung volumes:** EELV increases with exercise (dynamic hyperinflation) → EILV approaches TLC → tidal volume expansion limited → respiratory rate must increase instead
2. **Expiratory flow limitation (EFL):** ~74% of patients had EFL at rest; increases with exercise; constrains expiratory flow even at high intrathoracic pressures
3. **Obesity-related factors:** ↑ work of breathing (chest wall mass), ↓ respiratory muscle efficiency, ↓ FVC at baseline → smaller reserve throughout exercise

## Methods

Single-blind crossover; NTG (400 μg sublingual, fixed dose) vs. placebo (PLC). Two constant-load tests at 20W (6 min each), followed by maximal incremental test. Invasive PA and radial artery catheters; cardiorespiratory gas exchange; inspiratory capacity manoeuvres (breath-by-breath breathing mechanics). Order randomised.

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

This is the most direct challenge to the cardiac-centric model of exercise intolerance in obese HFpEF. Lowering PCWP — the gold standard treatment target — did not increase exercise capacity because patients were limited by their respiratory system, not their cardiovascular system. This does NOT mean PCWP management is unimportant (it reduces dyspnoea and hospitalisation), but it means exercise capacity in obese HFpEF may require respiratory-focused interventions (weight loss, breathing training, OSA treatment) rather than purely haemodynamic ones. Directly paired with [[leahy2025heartlung]] (DH-PCWP correlation). Together they define a new paradigm for exercise intolerance in obese HFpEF.
