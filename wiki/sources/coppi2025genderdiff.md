# Coppi 2025 — Gender-Specific Differences in Diastolic Dysfunction and HFpEF (Review)

> Narrative review synthesising sex/gender differences in HFpEF across pathophysiology (SERCA2a/calcium handling, postmenopausal estrogen withdrawal, arterial elastance), diagnosis (sex-specific echocardiographic/CMR reference ranges), and treatment (SGLT2i/GLP-1RA trial subgroup data by sex) — concludes women exhibit greater ventricular stiffness and diastolic impairment with menopause as a key inflection point, but current diagnostic criteria and guidelines are not sex-adapted.

## Core Arguments

**This is a narrative review, not a primary study.** Per this wiki's sourcing discipline, claims below are what Coppi et al. state as synthesis/interpretation of their own — not independent verification of every paper they cite. Where a specific finding traces to a primary source already ingested in this wiki (e.g. Beale, Shah/PROMIS), the wiki links to that primary source directly rather than routing through this review.

- **Epidemiology:** lifetime risk of HFpEF is nearly double that of HFrEF in women (10.7% vs. 5.8%), while similar in men. ARIC data: first-HFpEF-hospitalisation rates highest in Black women (7.4/1,000 person-years), then Black men (6.2), White women (5.9), White men (4.9) — annual rate increase most pronounced in Black women. [needs source — ARIC citation not independently ingested]
- **Pathophysiology — SERCA2a/calcium handling:** reduced SERCA2a expression impairs ventricular relaxation; estrogen deficiency impairs intracellular calcium metabolism via ERα/ERβ/GPR30 receptors, which normally act as inhibitors of myocardial hypertrophy. Estrogens also modulate natriuretic peptides and inhibit fibroblast proliferation (limiting fibrosis/stiffness); their postmenopausal decline is proposed to remove this protective effect.
- **Structural/hemodynamic sex differences (review's own synthesis, echoing but not identical to Beale 2018):** women exhibit greater LV stiffness, smaller LV cavity, higher arterial elastance; men with HFpEF tend to have greater LV mass/volume, lower arterial elastance, higher CAD incidence — "hemodynamically distinct pathophysiological substrate."
- **Adiposity pattern differs by sex:** women trend toward a more "obese phenotype" (higher metabolic syndrome/diabetes prevalence, worse diastolic dysfunction); in men, visceral adiposity and myocardial lipid infiltration are more prominent — proposed as a differential mechanism for how obesity drives diastolic dysfunction by sex.
- **Diagnostic imaging — sex-specific reference ranges proposed** (review's Table 1, citing others): LA reservoir strain (women ≥35% normal/<23% abnormal vs. men ≥33%/<23%); LAVI (<34 mL/m² normal in both, no sex split given); GLS (women ≤−20% normal/>−16% abnormal vs. men ≤−19%/>−15%); ECV (women <28% normal/≥28% abnormal vs. men <27%/≥27%) — review states current guidelines do **not** specify sex-specific cut-offs for LAVI or LAEF despite proposed differences, and that ECV/T1-mapping sex-specific thresholds are "increasingly advocated," not yet established.
- **Treatment — sex-stratified RCT subgroup data (Table 2 of the review, citing EMPEROR-Preserved/DELIVER/SUMMIT primary papers directly):** in EMPEROR-Preserved, relative risk reduction for the primary outcome was numerically greater in women (HR 0.75) than men (HR 0.81); in DELIVER, HR 0.81 (women) vs. 0.82 (men); in SUMMIT (tirzepatide), 38% reduction in HF events reported in both sexes. Review is explicit that none of these sex-subgroup differences reached statistical significance and that sex-stratified analyses remain underpowered across all three trials — this wiki should not present these as confirmed sex-differential efficacy, only as a hypothesis-generating trend the review itself flags as non-significant.
- **Clinical paradox noted:** women with HFpEF report lower quality of life but have superior overall survival compared with men — consistent with the mortality pattern in [[duca2018genderdiff]] (men die more of cardiac causes; women more often die with, not of, HFpEF).
- **Knowledge gaps flagged by the review:** underrepresentation of women in HFpEF trials; lack of sex-specific diagnostic thresholds in current guidelines; pregnancy-related risk factors (pre-eclampsia, gestational diabetes, recurrent pregnancy loss, early menopause, PCOS) underrecognised as HFpEF risk factors; mechanistic studies needed to explain the SGLT2i/GLP-1RA sex-subgroup trends.

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

Provides a review-level synthesis and several diagnostic reference-range tables (LA strain, GLS, ECV by sex) not previously in the wiki, plus explicit sex-subgroup RCT data for EMPEROR-Preserved/DELIVER/SUMMIT that corroborates (and should be cross-checked against) this wiki's existing entity pages for those trials. Its SERCA2a/estrogen-receptor mechanistic framing adds molecular detail beyond what `beale2018sex.md` covers (which focuses on structural/hemodynamic/RAAS mechanisms).
