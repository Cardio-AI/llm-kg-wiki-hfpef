# Multiorgan Physiological Deficits and Exercise Intolerance in HFpEF (Landsteiner 2026)

> The largest invasive cardiopulmonary exercise testing (iCPET) HFpEF cohort assembled to date (N=820) shows exercise intolerance arises from 7 organ-specific physiological deficits (cardiac, vascular, pulmonary, peripheral) that combine heterogeneously across patients; a higher deficit count strongly predicts adverse outcomes (≥5 deficits: HR 3.90 for CV event/mortality), and circulating metabolite signatures of these deficits predict incident heart failure up to two decades before onset in an independent community cohort (MESA, N=6,345).

## Key Findings

- **7 exercise physiological deficits defined** (organ system in parentheses): elevated exercise LV filling pressure — PCWP/CO slope >2 mmHg·L⁻¹·min⁻¹ (cardiac); decreased stroke volume <80% predicted (cardiac); decreased heart rate augmentation <80% predicted (cardiac/autonomic); increased pulmonary vascular resistance — peak exercise PVR >1.5 Wood units (pulmonary vascular); decreased breathing reserve — peak VE/MVV >80% (pulmonary mechanical); increased metabolic cost of exercise initiation — internal work >30W (metabolic); decreased peripheral O₂ extraction — Cavo₂ <80% predicted (peripheral)
- **HFpEF exercise limitation is broadly multiorgan, not purely cardiac:** prevalence of each deficit in the 820-patient cohort — elevated filling pressure 43%, decreased SV 16%, blunted HR augmentation 48%, increased PVR 38%, decreased breathing reserve 25%, increased metabolic cost 45%, decreased peripheral O₂ extraction 44%; most patients had concurrent abnormalities across ≥2 organ systems, and deficits beyond the central PCWP/CO slope were not simply proportional to it
- **Deficit count strongly predicts outcomes:** ≥5 deficits vs. 0 deficits → HR 3.90 (95% CI 1.74–8.75, P<0.0001) for the composite CV hospitalisation/mortality outcome; per-additional-deficit HR 1.28 (95% CI 1.17–1.39, P<0.0001); monotonic decline in peak VO₂ across increasing deficit-count strata (P<0.0001 across groups)
- **Metabolomic signatures constructed via LASSO regression for each of the 7 deficits (+ peak VO₂)** using 425 metabolites; validated in MESA using metabolites measured in both cohorts
- **Deficit-specific metabolite scores predicted incident HF over ~18.6 years in MESA (N=6,345):** e.g., the exercise PCWP/CO-slope metabolite signature carried HR 1.43 (per 1-SD) for incident HF, independent of traditional risk factors, BMI, and adjustment for NT-proBNP; metabolite scores combined yielded up to ~20% continuous net reclassification improvement over traditional HFpEF risk factors
- **Metabolic pathway findings:** peak VO₂ (as a global integrator) associated with metabolites linked to liver steatosis, lipid/oxidative metabolism (carnitines, Krebs-cycle intermediates), nitric oxide signalling (symmetric dimethylarginine), and anti-inflammatory microbial metabolites; central deficits (e.g. PCWP/CO slope) linked to mitochondrial β-oxidation, vascular inflammation/hypertension (glutamate), and urea-cycle catabolites; peripheral O₂ extraction deficit linked to inflammation, insulin resistance, and glycolysis markers
- **TWAS (transcriptome-wide association study) using the largest HF GWAS to date (~2 million individuals, ~153,000 HF cases) identified genes with cross-tissue effects on HF risk** functionally implicated by the deficit metabolome — including genes tied to inflammation (*ILRUN*), fibrosis/hypertrophy (*TNKS*, *LOXL4*, *NFATC2IP*, *SH2B1*), mitochondrial function/mitophagy (*HSD17B12*, *KAT8*, *CAC12*, *TUFM*, *LPL*, *MRPS16*, *PCCB*), and vascular reactivity (*NOS3*, *PNMT*) — plus several less-studied genes/lncRNAs (*MAB21L4*, *INCA1*, *DARS-AS1*, *SLC5A11*)

## Methods (brief)

Massachusetts General Hospital Exercise Study (MGH-ExS) iCPET cohort, 2006–2023, consecutive clinically-indicated referrals; HFpEF defined per the universal HF definition. Invasive CPET: right heart catheterisation with continuous PA/right atrial pressure, upright cycle ergometry ramp protocol, breath-by-breath gas exchange, Fick cardiac output from simultaneous arterial/mixed-venous O₂ content. Fasting resting plasma metabolomics via 2 LC-MS platforms (amino acids/acylcarnitines/dipeptides; sugars/organic acids/purines/pyrimidines), Broad Institute Metabolite Profiling Platform, no interlaboratory variability. Deficit thresholds and definitions adapted with minimal alteration from the authors' prior published methodology. Statistical analysis: unpaired t-tests/ANOVA for group comparisons; Cox models (age/sex/BMI-adjusted) for deficit-outcome and metabolite-signature-outcome associations; LASSO regression (cross-validated, 70/30 train/test split) for multimetabolite deficit signatures; MESA validation used Cox models with 3 adjustment tiers (demographic; + traditional CV risk factors; + log NT-proBNP in a subset); TWAS via MR-JTI approach (Genotype-Tissue Expression-derived), Bonferroni-corrected across 6 target tissues (skeletal muscle, visceral adipose, kidney cortex, liver, lung, + shared/cross-tissue).

## Results

Baseline iCPET HFpEF cohort (Table 1): age 61.7±14.1y, 54% female, BMI 30.6±6.7, LVEF 65.4±7.4%, peak VO₂ 14.8±5.2 mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ (73.2±20.3% predicted), PCWP/CO slope 2.3±1.7 mmHg·L⁻¹·min⁻¹. Full deficit-prevalence table, outcome hazard ratios per deficit and per deficit-count stratum (Kaplan-Meier P<0.0001 across the 6 deficit-count groups), and the full TWAS gene-prioritisation results (quantile-quantile enrichment plot, top-20-gene tissue heatmap) are reported in the source (Figures 2–5, Tables 1–3, Supplemental Data File 1).

## Limitations

iCPET discovery cohort is a single-centre, tertiary-referral clinical population (selection for diagnostic uncertainty) — may not generalise to community HFpEF. MESA validation used peripheral-blood metabolomics without invasive haemodynamic confirmation of incident HFpEF in all cases (HF subtype classification relied on adjudicated records, not universally CMR/echo-confirmed at the event). Metabolite-signature associations are, by the authors' own statement, not proof of causal mechanistic involvement — TWAS/GeneMAP prioritisation is hypothesis-generating. Some nonproportionality of hazards by age was observed in fully adjusted models (addressed via time-by-age interaction terms without attenuating significance, per the authors).
