# Timóteo 2024 — Pericardium, Epicardial Adipose Tissue, and HFpEF

> EAT contributes to HFpEF via two mechanisms: (1) pericardial restraint — physical compression of LV increasing filling pressures; and (2) paracrine dysfunction — proinflammatory adipokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6) secreted directly onto adjacent myocardium, promoting fibrosis, inflammation, microvascular dysfunction, and AF.

## Core Arguments

### EAT Anatomy and Assessment
- EAT: fat between visceral pericardium and myocardium; ~75% lies over RV; ~20% of total heart weight in non-obese individuals
- Strongly associated with VAT, obesity, metabolic syndrome — but independent effect of EAT beyond systemic adiposity
- CT: preferred modality for EAT volume (3D reconstruction, Hounsfield units); echo: measures thickness (parasternal long/short axis); CMR: volume + functional characterisation
- EAT density (HU) inversely correlated with EAT volume; lower EAT density (≤−76.2 HU) = more severe metabolic syndrome + higher mortality/HF readmissions in HFpEF

### Mechanism 1: Pericardial Restraint
- EAT accumulation → pericardium becomes taut → ventricular filling restraint → ↑ LVEDP → ↑ PCWP
- 20–40% of LVEDP related to extrinsic pericardial restraint at rest; increases with volume loading
- RV cannot dilate freely → septal shift → biventricular interdependence; modest increases in venous return → disproportionate ↑ filling pressures
- Direct evidence: animal models — pericardiectomy improves LV compliance; pilot human study (Borlaug group, 4 obese HFpEF women) — pericardiectomy improved LVEDV at similar filling pressures + improved peak VO₂ at 3 months

### Mechanism 2: Paracrine/Endocrine Dysfunction
- EAT in obesity → ↓ adiponectin (anti-inflammatory), ↑ proinflammatory: leptin, resistin, TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, IL-10, MCP-1, PAI-1
- Direct diffusion to myocardium via shared microcirculation (no fascial barrier between EAT and myocardium)
- Activates pro-fibrotic pathways → myocardial stiffness, endothelial dysfunction, microvascular rarefaction
- Atrial EAT → ↑ AF recurrence after catheter ablation (3-fold ↑ subclinical AF in monitoring studies); epicardial fatty infiltration → conduction abnormalities
- Leptinaldosterone-neprilysin axis: leptin → ↑ aldosterone → ↑ sodium retention + neprilysin activity → HFpEF development

### Treatment Targets for EAT
- **Statins:** Reduce EAT + anti-inflammatory properties; atorvastatin > simvastatin/ezetimibe for EAT reduction; observational mortality benefit in HFpEF (see [[ortegahernandez2024statins]])
- **SGLT2 inhibitors:** ↓ EAT volume and density; inhibit CaMKII activity; reduce myocardial fibrosis; improve diastolic filling dynamics
- **GLP-1 agonists:** Largest EAT reduction (2x greater than SGLT2i); dose-dependent, rapid; semaglutide specifically studied in STEP-HFpEF
- **Pericardiotomy:** Pilot surgical study (4 obese HFpEF women, thoracoscopic); improved peak VO₂ and QoL at 3 months; percutaneous pericardiotomy device tested in porcine HFpEF model
- **MRA/ARNi:** Block mineralocorticoid receptor → reduce EAT pro-inflammatory remodelling; neprilysin inhibition → anti-fibrotic effects; insufficient clinical EAT data in HFpEF

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

This is the most comprehensive review of pericardium/EAT in HFpEF. Provides the mechanistic framework linking obesity-HFpEF to pericardial restraint and paracrine inflammation. Key for understanding why GLP-1 agonists/SGLT2i work and why pericardial interventions are being investigated.
