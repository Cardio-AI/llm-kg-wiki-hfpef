# SECRET (Kitzman 2016)

> In obese older HFpEF patients, both caloric restriction and aerobic exercise training significantly improved peak VO₂ (each ~+1.2–1.3 mL/kg/min; P<0.001), and the effects were additive (+2.5 mL/kg/min combined); diet but not exercise improved QoL by clinically meaningful margins; neither reached significance for the MLHF co-primary QoL endpoint.

**Full citation:**
Kitzman DW, Brubaker P, Morgan T, Haykowsky M, Hundley G, Kraus WE, Eggebeen J, Nicklas BJ. Effect of Caloric Restriction or Aerobic Exercise Training on Peak Oxygen Consumption and Quality of Life in Obese Older Patients With Heart Failure With Preserved Ejection Fraction: A Randomized Clinical Trial. *JAMA.* 2016;315(1):36–46. doi:[10.1001/jama.2015.17346](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2015.17346)
**Study type:** 2×2 factorial RCT; attention-controlled; single-blind outcomes assessment · **N:** 100 enrolled; 92 completed · **Population:** Obese (BMI ≥30) older (age ≥60y) HFpEF (LVEF ≥50%, NYHA ≥II) · **Follow-up:** 20 weeks  
**Intervention:** Caloric restriction (~400 kcal/day deficit) and/or aerobic exercise training (1 hr 3× weekly, walking) vs. attention control (telephone calls every 2 weeks) · **Co-primary outcomes:** Peak VO₂ (mL/kg/min) and Minnesota Living with Heart Failure (MLHF) questionnaire total score · **NCT:** NCT00959660 · **Site:** Wake Forest School of Medicine (Winston-Salem, NC); February 2009 – November 2014 · **Funding:** NIH/NIA R37AG018915; IRB#00005668

## Key Findings

- Both diet and exercise independently and significantly improve exercise capacity (peak VO₂) in obese HFpEF
- Effects are additive: diet+exercise combined produces +2.5 mL/kg/min — the largest exercise capacity gain seen in any HFpEF intervention up to this point
- QoL primary endpoint (MLHF): Not met by either intervention (exercise P=0.70; diet P=0.08)
- Diet improved KCCQ by 7 units (P=0.004) — clinically meaningful (threshold ≥5 units); and SF-36 PCS by 4 units (P=0.02)
- VO₂ improvement correlated with lean mass preservation/change (not fat loss per se)
- Diet also reduced LV mass (−4 g by MRI; P=0.03) and improved mitral E/A ratio (+0.10; P=0.01)
- No study-related serious adverse events

## Methods (brief)

**Design:** 2×2 factorial RCT; 4 arms: (1) exercise only (n=26), (2) diet only (n=24), (3) exercise+diet (n=25), (4) attention control (n=25; biweekly telephone calls). Prespecified secondary outcomes per ClinicalTrials.gov: KCCQ (quality of life; range 0–100, higher=better); body composition (DXA); thigh muscle composition (MRI). Randomisation stratified by β-blocker use and sex.

**Enrolment:** 1586 medical records reviewed → 577 telephone screened → 167 screening visits → 100 randomised (exercise 26; diet 24; exercise+diet 25; control 25)

**Population:** Age ≥60y; BMI ≥30 kg/m²; LVEF ≥50% (mean 61±6%); NYHA class ≥II; clinically stable; no recent cardiac medication change; not undertaking regular exercise or diet

**Key exclusions:** Anemia; COPD; stroke; psychiatric conditions; cancer; recent medication change; significant ischemic/valvular disease; LV segmental wall motion abnormalities

**Exercise intervention:** 1-hour supervised sessions 3× weekly × 20 weeks; primarily walking; individualised exercise prescription based on CPET; intensity progressed by HR reserve; exercise attendance 84% (SD 14%)

**Diet intervention:** Hypocaloric diet (~400 kcal/day deficit); metabolic kitchen meals provided; 1.2 g/kg ideal body weight protein/day; 25–30% fat; registered dietitian supervised; calcium supplement 600 mg/day; diet adherence 99% (SD 1%)

**Baseline characteristics:**
- Mean age 66–67 y (SD 5); 80% women (n=81); ~55% white; ~45% Black (Table 1: White 28–31/~51 per factorial group, approximately 55% across groups)
- BMI 39.3 (5.6); Body fat ~45%
- NYHA II: 53%; NYHA III: 47%
- LVEF mean 61±6%; LV mass ~210 g; E/e' ~12.9 (SD 3.5)
- BNP median ~22–25 pg/mL (IQR range ~18–40 across groups; Table 1) — notably low; 94–96% hypertension; 29–41% DM; 1–2% AF
- Baseline peak VO₂: 14.3–14.7 mL/kg/min (SD 2.3–2.9) across groups; 6MWD ~1337–1368 ft (~408–417 m)

## Results

| Outcome | Exercise effect (diff, 95% CI) | P | Diet effect (diff, 95% CI) | P | Interaction P |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Peak VO₂ mL/kg/min | +1.2 (0.7 to 1.7) | <0.001 | +1.3 (0.8 to 1.8) | <0.001 | 0.67 |
| MLHF total score | −1 (−8 to 5) | 0.70 | −6 (−12 to 1) | 0.08 | 0.54 |

### VO₂ Correlates
- ΔVO₂ correlated with Δlean mass (r=0.32, P=0.03) and ΔSM:IMF ratio (r=0.27, P=0.02) — improvement in skeletal muscle quality/quantity, not just fat loss, drives VO₂ gain

## Limitations

- N=100: underpowered for clinical events (mortality, HF hospitalisation); no outcome data
- Single centre (Wake Forest); predominantly female and Black population — limited generalisability
- 20-week duration: may be insufficient to detect QoL benefit; shorter than most exercise RCTs
- MLHF not a validated primary endpoint for HFpEF (lacks content validity); KCCQ (which showed diet benefit) is now preferred
- Attention-control imperfect blinding — open-label intervention knowledge affects QoL reporting
- BNP median 22 pg/mL — very low; borderline HFpEF severity by contemporary standards; would not meet EMPEROR-Preserved or FINEARTS-HF enrolment criteria

## Contradictions

Peak VO₂ significantly improved by both diet and exercise (co-primary MET), but MLHF QoL (co-primary) not met by either. KCCQ (exploratory) showed clinically meaningful diet benefit. Reinforces the known dissociation between exercise capacity endpoints and QoL/symptom endpoints in HFpEF trials — see [[contradictions]] #14 (6MWD vs. KCCQ dissociation).
