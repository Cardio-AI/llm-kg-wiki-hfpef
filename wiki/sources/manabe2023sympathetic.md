# Sympathetic and Hemodynamic Responses to Exercise in HFpEF

> During dynamic exercise, HFpEF patients exhibit excessive sympathetically-mediated vasoconstriction — greater than both HFrEF and non-HF controls — which accounts for the exaggerated BP response and reduced skeletal muscle blood flow; during static exercise, sympathetic responses are similar to non-HF controls, implicating other mechanisms.

**Full citation:**

## Core Arguments

### Sympathetic Neural Responses

**Dynamic exercise (MSNA by microneurography):**
- Single case report (Badrov 2022, n=1 HFpEF, n=1 control): MSNA burst frequency ↑ in HFpEF (+13 at mild, +16 at moderate intensity) vs. ↓ in controls (−6, −7) — paradoxical sympathoexcitation
- Patients with HFrEF also show paradoxical MSNA increase during dynamic cycling (+5±4 mild, +7±7 moderate) but HFpEF response appears higher
- Plasma norepinephrine at peak upright cycling: HFpEF = controls (Borlaug 2006) — but NE is a crude indirect measure; MSNA is more sensitive
- Interpretation: HFpEF may show greater sympathetic reactivity to dynamic exercise than non-HF controls and possibly HFrEF, but data are from case reports only

**Static (isometric) exercise (handgrip):**
- Single case report (Bunsawat 2021): ΔMSNA burst frequency at 30% and 40% MVC similar between HFpEF and healthy control
- HFrEF shows markedly higher ΔMSNA during static handgrip vs. controls
- Conclusion: HFpEF resembles non-HF controls during static exercise — different mechanism from dynamic exercise intolerance

### Blood Pressure Responses

**Dynamic exercise:**
- HFpEF > hypertension controls and > HFrEF at peak exercise (Kato 2008, Namasivayam 2022)
- Greater reduction in peripheral vascular resistance required for normal BP response is absent in HFpEF
- SVRI reduction with exercise negatively correlated with VO₂peak in HFpEF (Borlaug 2010)
- Pulsatile BP (pulse pressure/SBP) correlated with arterial stiffness in HFpEF but with SV only in HFrEF — different hemodynamic drivers of BP excess

**Static exercise:**
- HFpEF shows smaller ΔBP than non-HF controls and HFrEF during 40% MVC handgrip
- Consistent with normal sympathetic reactivity during static exercise

### Skeletal Muscle Blood Flow

- Leg blood flow (LBF) and leg vascular conductance (LVC) lower in HFpEF than controls during single-leg knee extension (Lee 2016, Doppler ultrasound)
- HFpEF requires higher arterial perfusion pressure to maintain a given LBF → lower leg vascular conductance vs. HFrEF
- Both HFpEF and HFrEF have reduced LBF vs. controls during submaximal cycling (Puntawangkoon 2009, MRI)
- Forearm blood flow also reduced in HFpEF vs. hypertension controls during dynamic handgrip (Ratchford 2020)

### Functional Sympatholysis

- In healthy muscle, locally released metabolites override sympathetic vasoconstriction to ensure O2 delivery (functional sympatholysis)
- One study in HF with mildly reduced EF (≥45%): sympatholysis responsiveness to tyramine intact but peak vasodilatory capacity to ATP reduced — attenuated peripheral vasodilatory reserve
- Whether functional sympatholysis is impaired specifically in HFpEF remains unknown — major knowledge gap

### Pharmacological Evidence

- ARB (candesartan, 4 weeks): HFpEF patients had reduced peak SBP and longer exercise duration; hypertensive controls did not improve — suggests HFpEF-specific benefit via sympathetic suppression
- ACE inhibitor (perindopril, 1 year): improved 6MWT in HFpEF

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

This mini review fills a gap in the wiki's treatment of exercise intolerance mechanisms. The existing [[exercise-intolerance]] page identifies peripheral vascular limitation as a component but does not specify the sympathetic mechanism. This paper provides the physiological substrate: excess MSNA and sympathetically-mediated vasoconstriction during dynamic exercise → higher SVR → ↓ muscle blood flow and ↓ VO₂peak. The static vs. dynamic distinction reveals that static exercise intolerance in HFpEF is not sympathetically mediated, implying other mechanisms (possibly cardiac or ventilatory) dominate there.

## Contradictions

Evidence base is almost entirely from case reports (n=1 per study for MSNA data). Findings in individual patients should not be generalised to the HFpEF population until replicated in larger cohorts. The sympathetic mechanism is inferred rather than directly demonstrated at the population level. See [[contradictions]].
