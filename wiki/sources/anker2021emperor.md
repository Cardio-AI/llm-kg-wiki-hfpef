# EMPEROR-Preserved

> Empagliflozin significantly reduced CV death + HF hospitalization in HFpEF (LVEF >40%), becoming the first positive major outcomes trial in this population — published after the 2021 ESC Guidelines cutoff.

**File:** `raw/[outcomes paper not yet ingested]` · **Authors:** Anker SD et al. · **Year:** 2021 · **Journal:** NEJM  
**Study type:** RCT · **N:** 5,988 · **Sites:** 622 sites in 23 countries  
**Population:** HFpEF/HFmrEF (LVEF >40%), NYHA II–IV; NT-proBNP >900 pg/mL (no AF) or >300 pg/mL (with AF); echocardiographic structural/functional criteria required  
**Follow-up:** ~26 months · **Intervention:** Empagliflozin 10 mg/day vs. placebo  
**Primary outcome:** CV death + HF hospitalization  
**Registry:** NCT03057951

## Key Findings

[Expand on paper ingest — published August 2021, after ESC Guidelines cutoff; not included in ESC 2021 recommendations]

- Empagliflozin significantly reduced the primary composite of CV death + HF hospitalization
- Benefit driven primarily by reduction in HF hospitalizations
- Consistent results across pre-specified subgroups including LVEF subgroups
- Became the first major positive RCT in HFpEF

## Methods (brief)

**Disease definition for enrollment:**
- LVEF >40% (includes HFmrEF 41–49% and HFpEF ≥50%)
- NYHA Class II–IV
- NT-proBNP: >900 pg/mL (patients without AF) OR >300 pg/mL (patients with AF) — lower AF threshold reflects blunting of NP by tachycardia
- **Echocardiographic criteria** (≥1 required): LA volume index >34 mL/m² OR LA diameter >3.8 cm OR LV septal/posterior wall thickness ≥1.1 cm OR E/e' ≥10
- eGFR ≥20 mL/min/1.73m²

**Key exclusions:** ACS/stroke/cardiac surgery in prior 90 days; systolic BP >180 mmHg; T1DM; prior SGLT2i or GLP-1 RA use within 3 months.

**Sites:** 622 sites across 23 countries — the largest HFpEF outcomes trial conducted. (source: 2026-JACCHF-Ferreira-Findings-EMPEROR-Preserved.pdf)

**Intervention:** Empagliflozin 10 mg once daily vs. placebo (on top of background standard of care).

**Parameters collected:**
- *Imaging (echo)*: LVEF, LA volume index, LV wall thickness, E/e' (obligatory for eligibility)
- *Biomarkers*: NT-proBNP (qualifying and serial), BNP, serum Mg, creatinine, eGFR, HbA1c, potassium
- *Functional*: NYHA class, KCCQ, 6MWD
- *Safety labs*: Serial renal function, electrolytes

Alongside [[solomon2022deliver]] (DELIVER, dapagliflozin), the primary evidence base for SGLT2i in HFpEF. Both trials were incorporated into [[heidenreich2022aha]] (AHA 2022 Class 2a recommendation).

## Results

Results from Anker 2021 (primary outcomes paper not yet ingested — summary from ESC 2021 and AHA 2022):

- **Primary endpoint:** Empagliflozin significantly reduced CV death + HF hospitalization (HR ~0.79; 95% CI: ~0.69–0.90; p<0.001) — first major positive RCT outcome in the HFpEF population
- Benefit was driven primarily by **reduction in HF hospitalizations** (HF hospitalization alone also significant)
- CV death: numerically lower on empagliflozin, but CI crossed 1.0 in primary analysis
- Results **consistent across pre-specified subgroups** including LVEF strata (HFmrEF 41–49% and HFpEF ≥50%), diabetes status, and NT-proBNP
- Renal decline (eGFR slope): significantly attenuated with empagliflozin — consistent with HFrEF SGLT2i data

(source: 2021-ESC-Guidelines-Heart-Failure.pdf; source: 2022-CirculationsAHA-Heidenreich-AHA-ACC-HFSA-Guideline-HF.pdf; outcomes paper figures pending ingest)

## Limitations

- LVEF >40% inclusion: includes HFmrEF (41–49%) patients; degree to which benefit extends specifically to HFpEF (≥50%) is uncertain from primary analysis alone — though subgroup consistency was reported
- Published August 2021: after ESC 2021 publication cutoff; incorporated into AHA 2022 at Class 2a (see [[heidenreich2022aha]])
- Primary composite was driven by hospitalizations; CV mortality alone was not significantly reduced — the mortality gap in HFpEF remains open
- AF-adjusted NT-proBNP threshold enriches for patients with structural disease but may introduce selection bias
- External validity: 622 international sites — degree to which results translate across health systems and comorbidity patterns varies
