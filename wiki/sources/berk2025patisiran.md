# Berk 2025 — Patisiran Functional-Capacity Post Hoc Analysis of APOLLO-B

> Post hoc analysis of the phase 3 APOLLO-B trial (N=360, patisiran vs. placebo in ATTR-CM): estimates the minimal clinically important difference (MCID) for the 6-minute walk test (6MWT) at ~7–8 m using a KCCQ-OS-anchored method, and shows the trial's +15 m patisiran treatment benefit translates into materially lower odds of losing the ability to perform activities of daily living (ADLs). Also states directly that patisiran is **not FDA-approved** for ATTR-CM in the United States (approved in Brazil; compassionate-use only in France for tafamidis 61mg failures) as of the paper's 2025 acceptance.

**Full citation:**
Berk JL, Lairez O, Schwartzmann P, Bender S, White MT, Jay PY, Danese D, Witteles R. Benefits of Patisiran on Functional Capacity in ATTR Cardiac Amyloidosis: Post Hoc Analysis of APOLLO-B. *JACC Adv.* 2025;4(8):101876. doi:[10.1016/j.jacadv.2025.101876](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacadv.2025.101876)

## Summary

Post hoc analysis of APOLLO-B (NCT03997383), authored by APOLLO-B trial investigators plus Alnylam Pharmaceuticals employees (funded by Alnylam). APOLLO-B's primary outcome, the 6MWT, showed a patisiran treatment benefit of +15 m (Hodges–Lehmann estimate of median difference: 14.69 m [95% CI: 0.69–28.69]) vs. placebo at 12 months in the original trial. This paper asks what magnitude of 6MWT change is clinically meaningful to patients, using KCCQ-OS-anchored MCID estimation and a proportional-odds model linking 6MWT change to ADL performance. (source: berk2025patisiran)

## Methods

- Population: APOLLO-B trial, 360 patients randomized 1:1 to placebo (n=179) or patisiran (n=181); ATTR-CM (ATTRv or ATTRwt), 12-month double-blind period; median age 76y; ~80% ATTRwt; well-balanced baseline characteristics (source: berk2025patisiran)
- MCID estimation: change from baseline in 6MWT at month 12 anchored to established KCCQ-OS change categories (large/moderate/small deterioration; stable; small/moderate/large improvement, per Spertus 2005 thresholds); MCID = difference between median 6MWT change in a given category and the "stable" category; IQR via bootstrap (10,000 samples); sensitivity analysis using KCCQ Physical Limitation score as the anchor (source: berk2025patisiran)
- ADL association: proportional-odds cumulative logit model fit per KCCQ Physical Limitation domain item, using a +15 m 6MWT preservation margin (the trial's observed treatment effect) as the basis for calculating relative odds of ADL worsening; covariates: baseline 6MWT, baseline KCCQ ADL score, study visit, and 6MWT-change-by-visit interaction (source: berk2025patisiran)

## Key Findings

**6MWT MCID:**
- Small/moderate/large KCCQ-OS deterioration associated with 6MWT declines of −12.78 m / −24.40 m / −48.52 m (vs. stable-category −5.85 m); small/moderate/large improvement associated with +1.96 m / +13.54 m / +5.50 m (source: berk2025patisiran)
- Estimated MCID for a clinically significant small deterioration or improvement in 6MWT: **6.9 m** and **7.8 m**, respectively (source: berk2025patisiran)
- Sensitivity analysis (KCCQ Physical Limitation-anchored): MCID 6.7 m (improvement) and 10.4 m (worsening) (source: berk2025patisiran)
- First reported 6MWT MCID specific to an ATTR-CM population; prior 6MWT MCIDs from other diseases do not necessarily transfer, since MCID depends on baseline disease and 6MWT performance (source: berk2025patisiran)

**6MWT change vs. ADL performance (per +15 m more favorable 6MWT change at month 12, odds ratio for worsening ability):**
- Dress oneself: OR 0.85 (95% CI: 0.81–0.90)
- Shower/bathe: OR 0.87 (95% CI: 0.82–0.93)
- Walk 1 block on level ground: OR 0.88 (95% CI: 0.83–0.93)
- Perform yard/housework or carry groceries: OR 0.89 (95% CI: 0.84–0.93)
- Climb stairs without stopping: OR 0.84 (95% CI: 0.80–0.89)
- Hurry or jog: OR 0.88 (95% CI: 0.83–0.93)
(source: berk2025patisiran)

**Treatment response (patisiran vs. placebo at month 12):**
- 6MWT improvement ≥15 m: 32% (patisiran) vs. 21% (placebo), P<0.05; ≥30 m: 24% vs. 14%, P<0.01
- 6MWT worsening >30 m: 34% (patisiran) vs. 45% (placebo), P<0.05
- KCCQ-OS improvement ≥5 points: 34% (patisiran) vs. 24% (placebo), P<0.05; greater proportion of placebo patients deteriorated by ≥5/≥10/≥20 points and died by month 12 (deaths incl. transplants: placebo n=2 [1.1%], patisiran n=0)
- KCCQ-OS least-squares mean difference (patisiran vs. placebo): +3.7 points (95% CI: 0.2–7.2) overall; benefit across all 4 KCCQ domains and the Clinical Summary score; largest item-level effects for shortness of breath, hurrying/jogging, fatigue, and "enjoyment of life"; dressing oneself was the one item that did not favor patisiran
(source: berk2025patisiran)

**Clinical framing:** the +15 m 6MWT treatment effect is framed as consistent in magnitude with preventing the additional 10.7 m/year 6MWT decline observed after an outpatient worsening-HF event (loop-diuretic intensification) in ATTR-CM, per Fontana 2025 HELIOS-B analysis (source: berk2025patisiran)

## Regulatory Status of Patisiran (resolves wiki contradiction)

Study Limitations section states directly: **"patisiran is not approved for the treatment of ATTR-CM in the United States but is approved in Brazil and France, where, in the latter case, patisiran is approved for compassionate use in patients failing tafamidis 61 mg."** (source: berk2025patisiran)

This is an explicit, unambiguous statement from a paper co-authored by the APOLLO-B trial's own investigators and Alnylam Pharmaceuticals staff, current as of manuscript acceptance (May 14, 2025; published Aug 2025). It resolves the previously flagged contradiction between `wiki/entities/patisiran-apollo-b.md` and `wiki/sources/masri2026attrcm.md`: patisiran does **not** carry a US FDA label for the cardiac ATTR-CM indication — the masri2026attrcm Discussion-section list (tafamidis, acoramidis, vutrisiran only) was correct, and the "FDA label expanded for cardiac indication" bullet elsewhere in that same source summary was in error. See updates to both pages.

## Limitations (per paper)

- Post hoc, hypothesis-generating; not pre-specified in APOLLO-B
- ADL performance was not directly assessed in APOLLO-B; inferred from KCCQ Physical Limitation domain patient-reported items
- 6MWT MCID anchored to KCCQ-OS; other anchoring approaches could yield different estimates; both measures are continuous, so any categorical cutoffs are somewhat arbitrary
- APOLLO-B's ≥150 m 6MWT screening inclusion criterion means the estimated MCID may not generalize to more severely limited patients excluded from the trial
- Funded by Alnylam Pharmaceuticals; 3 authors are Alnylam employees/shareholders; medical writing support funded by Alnylam
