# PARAGON-HF

> Sacubitril/valsartan failed to significantly reduce CV death + total HF hospitalizations vs. valsartan in HFpEF overall, but a significant EF-by-treatment interaction showed benefit concentrated in LVEF below the median (~57%), informing FDA endorsement for LVEF "less than normal."

**File:** `raw/[not yet ingested]` · **Authors:** Solomon SD et al. · **Year:** 2019 · **Journal:** NEJM  
**Study type:** RCT · **N:** ~4,800 · **Population:** HF with LVEF ≥45%, elevated NPs, NYHA II–IV  
**Follow-up:** ~35 months · **Intervention:** Sacubitril/valsartan vs. valsartan  
**Primary outcome:** Total HF hospitalizations + CV death (rate ratio)

---

## Key Findings

- Primary endpoint **missed** — sacubitril/valsartan did not significantly reduce the primary composite vs. valsartan
- Significant EF-by-treatment interaction: benefit concentrated in LVEF **below the trial median (~57%)**
- Meta-analysis with PARADIGM-HF: reduction in CV death and HF hospitalization in patients with LVEF below normal
- FDA endorsed [[sacubitril-valsartan]] for patients with chronic HF and LVEF "less than normal" based on this data

## Methods (brief)

[Expand on paper ingest]

Inclusion: HFpEF (LVEF ≥45%), elevated NPs, NYHA II–IV, stabilised on optimised background therapy. Active comparator design (valsartan arm, not placebo) to allow ethical equipoise. Primary endpoint was rate-based (total hospitalization count), not time-to-first-event.

## Limitations

[Expand on paper ingest]

- Active comparator (valsartan) rather than placebo limits interpretation of absolute benefit magnitude
- Post-hoc subgroup analysis of EF-by-treatment interaction; trial was not powered for this
- HFmrEF patients (LVEF 45–57%) likely drove the subgroup signal
