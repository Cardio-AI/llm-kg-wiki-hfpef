# Rillig 2021 — Early Rhythm Control in AF+HF: EAST-AFNET4 HF Substudy

> In 798 HF patients from EAST-AFNET4 (56.3% HFpEF), early rhythm control (ERC) significantly reduced the primary cardiovascular outcome vs. usual care (HR 0.74; 95% CI 0.56–0.97; P=0.03), with no significant interaction by HF type (HFpEF vs. HFmrEF vs. HFrEF; P=0.63).

## Key Findings

### Primary Outcome (HF Subgroup)
- **Primary composite (CV death, stroke, worsening HF, acute coronary syndrome; time to first):** HR 0.74 (95% CI 0.56–0.97; P=0.03) favouring early rhythm control
- **No interaction by HF phenotype:** ERC benefit consistent across HFpEF, HFmrEF, and HFrEF subgroups (P for interaction = 0.63)

### Secondary Outcomes (Exploratory)
- Worsening HF: directionally reduced with ERC in HFpEF
- Stroke: numerically lower with ERC
- CV death: numerically lower with ERC; individual endpoints underpowered
- Hospitalisation for HF: reduced with ERC but not significant in HF subgroup alone

### Context: EAST-AFNET4 Main Results
- Main trial (all patients, N=2,789): ERC reduced primary composite by 21% (HR 0.79; 95% CI 0.66–0.94; P=0.005)
- ERC included both antiarrhythmic drugs AND catheter ablation at physician discretion; ~20% ablation rate in ERC arm

## Methods

Pre-specified analysis of HF subgroup in EAST-AFNET4 (randomised 1:1 to ERC vs. usual care in early AF). HF defined as any of: prior HF hospitalisation, clinical HF diagnosis, reduced LVEF, elevated NPs, or echo diastolic dysfunction. EF subtype classified at enrolment. Primary endpoint identical to main trial. No adjustment for multiple testing; underpowered for individual subgroup endpoints.

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

This is the most important trial evidence for rhythm control in AF+HFpEF patients. The HFpEF-predominant subgroup had significant benefit from ERC — comparable to HFmrEF and HFrEF — supporting a class-independent effect. Together with [[alsadawi2022rhythmcontrol]] (meta-analysis showing rhythm control over rate control), this establishes early rhythm control as a promising treatment strategy in AF+HFpEF. The benefit likely derives from AF→sinus rhythm conversion restoring atrial contribution to diastolic filling, which is disproportionately important in HFpEF.
