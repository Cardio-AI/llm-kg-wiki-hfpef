# HEART Camp HFpEF Subgroup

> Secondary analysis of the HEART Camp RCT (N=59 HFpEF subset; 25 HEART Camp, 34 Enhanced Usual Care; NCT01658670; J Card Fail 2022;28:431–442) showing that a 18-month behavioral exercise coaching intervention dramatically improved exercise adherence (42% vs. 14% at 12 months; 56% vs. 0% at 18 months) and produced superior 6MWT (+63 m vs. +13 m at 18 months; P=0.048) and KCCQ improvement in HFpEF patients — while the HFrEF subgroup showed no benefit, suggesting HFpEF-specific responsiveness to adherence-focused interventions.

**Full citation:**
Alonso WW, Kupzyk K, Norman JF, Lundgren SW, Fisher A, Lindsey ML, Keteyian SJ, Pozehl BJ. The HEART Camp Exercise Intervention Improves Exercise Adherence, Physical Function, and Patient-Reported Outcomes in Adults With Preserved Ejection Fraction Heart Failure. *J Card Fail.* 2022;28(3):431–442. doi:[10.1016/j.cardfail.2021.09.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cardfail.2021.09.003)

## Core Arguments

1. **Exercise adherence is the primary barrier to exercise benefit in HFpEF.** In controlled RCT settings, SET consistently improves peak VO₂ and KCCQ. Real-world exercise programs achieve far lower adherence. Ex-DHF (2025) reported ~53% adherence even under close supervision. HEART Camp directly targets the adherence problem rather than the exercise dose.

2. **HEART Camp dramatically improved adherence in HFpEF.** At 12 months: 42% HEART Camp vs. 14% EUC meeting exercise guidelines (P=0.025, medium effect size). At 18 months: 56% HEART Camp vs. 0% EUC (P<0.001, large effect size). The 18-month gap (56% vs. 0%) is a remarkable divergence — EUC patients had completely abandoned exercise by 18 months.

3. **Adherence improvement translated into functional and KCCQ benefit.** 6MWT at 18 months: +63.25 m (HEART Camp) vs. +13.16 m (EUC), P=0.048. KCCQ-OSS, CSS, and TSS all showed significant Time×Group interactions (P=0.009, 0.001, 0.003 respectively). These are clinically meaningful differences in a sample of N=59.

4. **HFrEF subgroup showed no benefit from HEART Camp.** This is the most striking finding: HFrEF patients in the HEART Camp group did not achieve greater adherence or physical function improvement than EUC. The mechanism is unclear but may reflect: (a) HFrEF patients having more medical complexity limiting exercise even when motivated; (b) HFrEF patients receiving more intensive cardiac monitoring outside the trial; or (c) HFpEF patients having a larger exercise capacity reserve that can be unlocked by motivation.

5. **HEART Camp is a multicomponent behavioral intervention, not additional pharmacotherapy.** Components: exercise coach contact (phone/in-person), self-monitoring tools, goal setting, social support from family/peers, problem-solving support. No additional supervised exercise sessions beyond what EUC received — the intervention is purely motivational and behavioral scaffolding.

(source: Alonso2022HEARTcamp)

## Study Design

**Trial:** HEART Camp (Heart failure Exercise And Resistance Training CAMP)  
**NCT:** NCT01658670  
**PI/Sponsor:** University of Nebraska Medical Center  
**Design:** Multi-site RCT (parent study N=204; HFpEF secondary analysis N=59)  
**Intervention:** 18-month behavioral coaching program vs. Enhanced Usual Care (EUC)
- HEART Camp: exercise coach contacts + self-monitoring + goal setting + social support scaffolding
- EUC: written exercise materials + monthly nurse calls (attention control)  
**Duration:** 18 months  
**Population (HFpEF subset):** LVEF ≥40% (HFpEF criterion in this era); N=59 (25 HEART Camp, 34 EUC)  
**Primary outcome (HFpEF subgroup):** Exercise adherence (% meeting AHA/ACC exercise guidelines)  
**Secondary outcomes:** 6MWT, KCCQ (OSS, CSS, TSS), NYHA class  
**Publication:** J Card Fail 2022;28(3):431–442

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

HEART Camp addresses the most clinically underappreciated barrier to exercise training benefit in HFpEF: long-term adherence. Even when exercise is unambiguously effective in RCTs, real-world implementation fails because patients stop exercising. Key implications:

- **Implementation science for SET:** The standard SET evidence base (Sachdev 2023 meta-analysis: +2.8 mL/kg/min) assumes adherence equivalent to supervised trial conditions. HEART Camp suggests that behavioral scaffolding can sustain exercise adherence at levels approaching supervised RCT conditions over 18 months.

- **Ex-DHF lesson:** Ex-DHF (2025, n=322, Edelmann) missed its primary composite endpoint despite VO₂ improvement, partly attributed to ~53% adherence. HEART Camp's model of sustained coaching might have shifted the adherence curve enough to affect primary endpoint outcomes.

- **HFpEF-specific phenomenon:** The HFrEF non-response is biologically plausible — HFrEF cardiac limitation may override the peripheral benefit of adherence improvement. In HFpEF, where peripheral skeletal muscle is the primary exercise intolerance mechanism, behavioral activation of exercise directly targets the bottleneck.

- **Scalability:** Behavioral coaching (phone contacts, written materials, goal setting) is low-cost and deliverable remotely — important for the rural, older HFpEF population. INABLE-Training (Borlaug 2024) showed exercise benefit even in 63% rural, predominantly NYHA III patients; HEART Camp suggests this benefit is sustainable with structured adherence support.

## Contradictions

None internal. The HFpEF vs. HFrEF differential response is a novel finding but based on a post-hoc secondary analysis of a subset (N=59 HFpEF). Replication in a powered, prospective HFpEF-specific adherence intervention trial is needed. See [[contradictions]] if HF subtype-specific behavioral responsiveness becomes a recurring theme.
