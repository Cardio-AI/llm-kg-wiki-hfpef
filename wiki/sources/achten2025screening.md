# Achten 2025 — HFpEF Screening in Obesity: Rationale and Roadmap

> Individuals with obesity develop HFpEF a decade earlier than non-obese; systematic screening is not recommended in current guidelines but is clinically warranted — proposes HFpEF-ABA score → NP → echo stepwise algorithm; NP thresholds require downward adjustment at high BMI.

**Full citation:**

## Summary

Narrative review (Maastricht UMC + Zuyderland Medical Center, Netherlands). Argues the case for systematic HFpEF screening in obese individuals — distinct from general HF screening — given earlier onset, specific pathophysiology, and availability of effective (SGLT2i, GLP-1RA) and potentially preventive treatments. Reviews diagnostic challenges specific to obesity. (source: achten2025screening)

## Epidemiology and Rationale for Screening

- Obesity (~48% of HFpEF cases) is associated with a distinct obesity-HFpEF phenotype
- Patients with obesity develop HFpEF **approximately one decade earlier** than individuals without obesity
- Risk of obesity-related cardiac structural changes: **20% after 15 years of obesity, 95% after 25 years**
- Current guidelines recommend HF screening for comorbidities in established HF patients (not for screening in asymptomatic obese individuals)
- The AHA cardio-kidney-metabolic (CKM) framework supports proactive screening in high-risk cardiometabolic profiles but does not specify HFpEF screening thresholds
- Early identification is critical given available effective therapies (SGLT2i, GLP-1RA, weight management)

## Pathophysiology of Obesity-HFpEF

- **Visceral adipose tissue (VAT):** Secretes adipokines (NLRP3 inflammasome activation) → systemic inflammation → microvascular dysfunction → cardiac fibrosis
- **Epicardial adipose tissue (EAT):** Mechanically compresses the myocardium (pericardial restraint); locally releases pro-inflammatory cytokines; ~48% higher in HFpEF patients with obesity vs. without
- **Haemodynamic:** Increased plasma volume → elevated filling pressures; RV dysfunction from pulmonary venous hypertension
- **RV dysfunction:** More pronounced in obesity-HFpEF (pericardial restraint + elevated PASP)
- **Timing:** Metabolic → structural → haemodynamic cascade begins years before symptoms; screening window is the pre-symptomatic phase

## Diagnostic Challenges in Obesity

**Natriuretic peptides:**
- NPs are systematically **lower in obesity** due to decreased myocardial stretch (EAT pericardial restraint reduces wall stress) and enhanced NP clearance by adipose tissue
- ESC NT-proBNP 125 pg/mL cutoff: sensitivity decreases from **77% to 67%** in patients with BMI >35 kg/m²
- In a multicenter study, 48–63% of confirmed obese HFpEF patients were below the diagnostic NT-proBNP threshold (on diuretics, confounding further)
- **Clinical implication:** NT-proBNP thresholds need BMI-adjusted downward calibration in obese screening populations

**Echocardiography:**
- Excess adipose tissue attenuates ultrasound → image quality degradation; technical adjustments required
- BSA (body surface area) indexing overestimates cardiac volumes in obesity because BSA is largely determined by fat mass → LAVi and LVMI may be underestimated when BSA-indexed
- Recommendation: **index to height²** for left atrial volume and LV mass in obese patients
- HFA-PEFF vs. H2FPEF discordance: AF and BMI are components of H2FPEF → inflates H2FPEF score in obese AF patients; HFA-PEFF score increases AF-adjusted NP thresholds; authors recommend using **both scores side-by-side**

## Screening Tools

**HFpEF-ABA score:**
- Three clinical variables: Body mass index (BMI), Age, history of Atrial fibrillation
- Pre-echo screening tool to assess likelihood of HFpEF and identify who needs echocardiographic referral
- Outperformed NT-proBNP alone for pre-echocardiographic risk stratification
- Not validated in prospective screening populations yet; control group of the derivation study had mean BMI ~27 (lower than obese screening target)

**Machine learning-derived score (Bermea 2024):**
- Identifies: BMI, eGFR, LVMI, left atrial to left ventricular volume ratio as strongest predictors
- Outperformed H2FPEF score but was not compared to HFA-PEFF
- Not validated broadly; proof-of-concept only

## Proposed Stepwise Screening Algorithm

1. **Population-level risk stratification:** HFpEF-ABA score + natriuretic peptide assessment
2. **High-risk identification:** BMI ≥30 kg/m² + elevated NP (with adjusted thresholds) + signs/symptoms
3. **Targeted diagnostic workup:** Echocardiography tailored to obesity (height²-indexed parameters; optimal imaging technique)
4. **Intermediate probability:** Stress echocardiography or invasive haemodynamic assessment (RHC)
5. **Confirmed HFpEF:** Initiate treatment (SGLT2i as first-line per DELIVER BMI-consistent benefit; GLP-1RA if obesity-driven phenotype)

## Treatment in Obesity-HFpEF After Screening

- **SGLT2i:** Consistent benefit across full BMI spectrum in DELIVER — no attenuation at higher BMI; some analyses suggest **greater absolute benefit** at higher BMI (larger absolute risk reduction)
- **GLP-1RA (semaglutide):** STEP-HFpEF 2023 — KCCQ +7.8 pts (P<0.001); 6MWD +21.5m; −13% body weight; tirzepatide (SUMMIT 2025) — event reduction HR 0.62 (0.41–0.95) — first event reduction with GLP-1RA class
- **Metabolic bariatric surgery (MBS):** Sustained weight loss >35% vs. ~15% with GLP-1RA; improved QoL and HF hospitalisations; age-limited (typically <65y) — limiting applicability to typical HFpEF patient (mean age ~75y)

## Open Questions

- What is the prevalence of (early) HFpEF in asymptomatic obese individuals? — no prospective data
- What BMI-specific NP cutoffs achieve adequate sensitivity without unacceptable false-positive rate?
- Do screening-detected cases respond better to treatment than symptom-detected cases? (early intervention hypothesis)
- Can GLP-1RA or SGLT2i halt pre-HFpEF structural progression in obese individuals?
- Should waist circumference replace BMI as the screening criterion (better correlates with visceral fat)?
