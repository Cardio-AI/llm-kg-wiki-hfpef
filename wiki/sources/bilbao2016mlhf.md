# Bilbao 2016 — MLHFQ: Comparison of Different Factor Structures

> Large Spanish multicentre validation study (N=2565 hospitalized HF patients) of the Minnesota Living with Heart Failure Questionnaire (MLHFQ): confirms the original two-factor (physical/emotional) structure via CFA and Rasch analysis, and additionally supports a third "social" factor — recommending Munyombwe's 6-item social subscale as the best-performing of several proposed alternatives.

**Full citation:**
Bilbao A, Escobar A, García-Pérez L, Navarro G, Quirós R. The Minnesota Living with Heart Failure Questionnaire: comparison of different factor structures. *Health Qual Life Outcomes.* 2016;14:23. doi:[10.1186/s12955-016-0425-7](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12955-016-0425-7)

---

## Core Arguments

- The MLHFQ is a self-administered, heart-failure-specific health-related quality of life (HRQoL) questionnaire, translated and culturally adapted into at least 34 languages (source: bilbao2016mlhf).
- **Instrument structure:** 21 items, each rated on a six-point Likert scale from 0 (none) to 5 (very much), representing degrees of impact of HF on HRQoL (source: bilbao2016mlhf).
  - **Total score:** range 0–105 (sum of all 21 items), higher score = worse HRQoL (source: bilbao2016mlhf).
  - **Physical dimension:** 8 items (items 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 12, 13), range 0–40 (source: bilbao2016mlhf).
  - **Emotional dimension:** 5 items (items 17, 18, 19, 20, 21), range 0–25 (source: bilbao2016mlhf).
  - The remaining 8 items (of the 21 total) are not part of either subscale and are only used in calculating the total score (source: bilbao2016mlhf).
  - This original two-dimension structure derives from the questionnaire's original developers (Rector et al., 1987/1992) (source: bilbao2016mlhf).
- Using data from 2565 patients hospitalized for HF across 13 Spanish hospitals (Dec 2008–May 2013), confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) and Rasch (item response theory) analysis both supported the adequacy of the original physical and emotional two-factor structure (RMSEA ≈0.08, CFI/TLI >0.90, all factor loadings >0.40, P<0.001) (source: bilbao2016mlhf).
- The study is explicitly framed as **the first to compare multiple proposed MLHFQ factor structures head-to-head** (two-factor: Rector, Heo; three-factor with an added "social" dimension: Ho, Moon, Lambrinou, Garin, Munyombwe), using both classical test theory (CFA) and item response theory (Rasch) methods (source: bilbao2016mlhf).
- **Total-score unidimensionality:** Rasch analysis of the total score showed good fit overall (infit/outfit 0.7–1.3) except for two markedly misfitting items — item 8 ("working to earn a living difficult") and item 10 ("sexual activities difficult") — but principal component analysis of residuals found no additional factor with eigenvalue >3, supporting the unidimensionality assumption underlying use of the total score (source: bilbao2016mlhf).
- **Third "social" factor:** the authors recommend adding a third, social dimension to the standard physical/emotional scoring, and specifically endorse **Munyombwe et al.'s 6-item social subscale** (items 8, 9, 10, 14, 15, 16 — earning a living, recreational activities, sexual activities, hospitalization, medical costs, medication side effects; score range 0–30) as psychometrically best among the proposals tested — highest known-groups validity (effect size up to 0.72 for recent readmission), highest correlation with SF-12 domains, and the only social structure meeting all Rasch model requirements (source: bilbao2016mlhf).
- Sample characteristics: mean age 77.3 years, 50.3% men; LVEF ≤45% in 39.15% and LVEF >45% in 60.85% of the cohort — i.e., a majority of this hospitalized-HF validation sample had preserved or near-preserved ejection fraction, though the study does not stratify its psychometric results by LVEF (source: bilbao2016mlhf).
- Baseline MLHFQ (mean ± SD): total score 57.85 ± 22.69; physical subscale 27.40 ± 9.05; emotional subscale 12.40 ± 7.28 (source: bilbao2016mlhf).
- Limitation acknowledged by the authors: sample is Spanish-language/Spain-only, so generalizability to other language versions/populations of the MLHFQ is uncertain; no responsiveness (change-over-time sensitivity) analysis was performed (source: bilbao2016mlhf).
- The paper does **not** discuss the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ) or any historical shift in outcome-instrument choice in HFpEF trials — it is confined to MLHFQ internal structure and psychometrics.

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

- Directly closes a previously flagged evidence gap on [[minnesota-living-with-heart-failure-questionnaire]]: the numeric scoring range, item count, and domain structure of the MLHFQ, which no other cited wiki source had described.
- Provides the item-level/domain-level structure needed to interpret MLHFQ score changes reported in HFpEF trials already in this wiki (I-PRESERVE, RELAX, SECRET) — e.g., a baseline total score of ~42–43 (as seen in RELAX/I-PRESERVE) can now be read against a defined 0–105 scale.
- Note on population: although not an HFpEF-specific study (it is a general hospitalized-HF cohort, mixed LVEF), the majority-preserved/near-preserved EF split (60.85% with LVEF >45%) means this validation sample overlaps substantially with an HFpEF-relevant population.
- Does not address why later HFpEF trials shifted from MLHFQ to KCCQ — that evidence gap on [[minnesota-living-with-heart-failure-questionnaire]] remains open.
