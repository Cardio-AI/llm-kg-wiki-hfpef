# Suzuki 2018 — Severe SDB Is an Independent Predictor of Arterial Stiffness in HFpEF but Not HFrEF

> In 221 HF patients (70 HFpEF + 151 HFrEF), severe SDB (AHI >30/h) independently predicted higher pulse wave velocity (PWV) in HFpEF (β=0.234; P=0.005) but not in HFrEF (P=0.068), suggesting a phenotype-specific interaction between SDB and vascular stiffening in HFpEF.

**Year:** 2018 · **Journal:** ESC Heart Fail 2018;5:284–291  
**Study type:** Prospective cross-sectional; single centre (Fukushima Medical University Hospital)  
**N:** 221 HF total — 70 HFpEF (LVEF ≥50%), 151 HFrEF (LVEF <50%)  
**Population:** Stable HF; SDB assessed by cardiorespiratory monitoring (Alice NightOne); mean age ~68y  

---

## Key Findings

- **Overall SDB prevalence:** ~67% in HFpEF; similar in HFrEF
- **Severe SDB (AHI >30/h):** ~30–35% of both groups
- **Arterial stiffness measure:** Pulse wave velocity (PWV) — brachial-ankle
- **HFpEF group:** Severe SDB independently predicted higher PWV (β=0.234; P=0.005) in multivariable regression (adjusted for age, sex, BMI, hypertension, diabetes, medications)
- **HFrEF group:** Severe SDB did NOT independently predict PWV (β=NS; P=0.068) after adjustment
- **Mechanistic implication:** SDB-induced intermittent hypoxia → sympathetic activation + ROS production → endothelial dysfunction → vascular stiffening; this pathway is particularly relevant in HFpEF where arterial stiffness is a primary pathophysiological driver

## Methods

Cardiorespiratory sleep monitoring in all consecutive HF patients at admission. PWV measured by brachial-ankle oscillometry (omron). HFpEF defined as LVEF ≥50% with evidence of diastolic dysfunction or elevated filling pressures. Multivariable linear regression with PWV as dependent variable; SDB severity (AHI >30 vs ≤30) as independent variable; adjusted for standard cardiovascular confounders.

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

This is one of the few studies specifically examining the SDB–arterial stiffness interaction stratified by HF subtype. The HFpEF-specific finding supports the hypothesis that SDB contributes to HFpEF pathophysiology via vascular stiffening — a mechanism separate from the cardiac/diastolic dysfunction pathway.
