# INDIE-HFpEF

> Single acute dose of inorganic nitrate (beetroot juice NO₃⁻ 12.9 mmol) improved peak VO₂ (+1.0 mL/min/kg, P=0.005) and total work (+6.5 kJ, P=0.04) in HFpEF via peripheral haemodynamic augmentation (↑CO +32.5%, P=0.006; ↓SVR −10.6%, P=0.03) not O₂ efficiency; primary endpoint (efficiency) was neutral (P=0.64); aortic augmentation index reduced (−9.1%, P=0.03); N=17 crossover pilot; 88% male, 100% HTN; single dose ≠ chronic treatment; contrasts with NEAT-HFpEF (chronic organic nitrate: activity worsened). **Stub errors corrected on ingest:** prior entry said journal=JACC HF, intervention=KNO₃, N≈65, result=negative peak VO₂ — all incorrect.

**Full citation:**
Zamani P, Rawat D, Shiva-Kumar P, et al. Effect of Inorganic Nitrate on Exercise Capacity in Heart Failure With Preserved Ejection Fraction. *Circulation.* 2015;131(4):371–380. doi:[10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.114.012957](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.114.012957)
**Study type:** Randomised, double-blind, crossover (single dose) · **N:** 17 (modified ITT; 20 entered; 1 AF on baseline echo, 2 no-show) · **Population:** HFpEF, LVEF >50%, symptomatic HF + elevated filling pressures (E/e' >8 + ≥1: enlarged LA, elevated NT-proBNP, PCWP >12 mmHg on prior cath)  
**Follow-up:** Crossover; ≥5-day washout (mean 11.8 days) · **Intervention:** Concentrated NO₃⁻-rich beetroot juice (12.9 mmol NO₃⁻, 140 mL; BEET IT Sport) vs. identical nitrate-depleted placebo; given 3 h before supine-cycle maximal-effort CPET  
**Primary outcome:** Exercise efficiency (total work performed / total O₂ consumed) during maximal-effort CPET  
**NCT:** NCT01919177 · **Enrollment:** University of Pennsylvania; IRB approved · **Sponsor:** NIH; equipment loans from Atcor Medical, Cardiodynamics, APC Cardiovascular

## Key Findings

- **Primary (efficiency): NEUTRAL** — 4.5±0.8 vs 4.6±1.1 kJ/L O₂; diff −0.1±1.0; P=0.64
- **Peak VO₂: SIGNIFICANT** — 12.6±3.7 vs 11.6±3.1 mL O₂/min/kg; diff **+1.0±1.2; P=0.005**
- Total work performed: 55.6±35.3 vs 49.2±28.9 kJ; diff **+6.5±11.9 kJ; P=0.04**
- Exercise duration: 15.3±4.9 vs 14.5±4.4 min; diff +0.8 min; **P=0.02**
- Ventilatory threshold: 7.6±1.8 vs 7.0±1.4 mL/min/kg; diff +0.5; **P=0.03**
- Cardiac output change at peak exercise: 121.2±59.9% vs 88.7±53.3%; diff +32.5±41.0%; **P=0.006**
- SVR change at peak exercise: −42.4±16.6% vs −31.8±20.3%; diff −10.6±16.9%; **P=0.03**
- Heart rate change: 78.0±24.1% vs 65.6±21.0%; diff +12.4%; **P=0.001**
- Aortic augmentation index: 132.2±16.7% vs 141.4±21.9%; diff **−9.1±15.4%; P=0.03** — wave reflections reduced
- Steady-state VO₂ (25W constant-intensity): 6.7±1.0 vs 6.7±0.8; P=0.70 — NS
- Plasma NOₓ confirmed: 326 vs 10 µmol/L; **P=0.0003** — confirms nitrate bioavailability

## Methods

**Design:** Double-blind crossover. Each subject received both NO₃⁻ and placebo in random order separated by ≥5-day washout. Subjects asked to avoid mouthwash (oral bacteria needed for nitrate→nitrite conversion) and PDE5 inhibitors for ≥2 days before study days. Blood pressure monitored 2 h post-administration; BP unchanged between arms.

**Exercise protocol:** Supine-cycle ergometer. Maximal-effort CPET: graded protocol (12.5W for 3 min → 25W for 3 min → +25W every 3 min). Then ~15 min later: constant-intensity 25W protocol (6 min, steady-state VO₂). Custom MATLAB software for CPET analysis; all breath-by-breath data quantified blinded to treatment assignment.

**NIRS (skeletal muscle oxygenation):** Continuous measurement via near-infrared spectroscopy on left gastrocnemius during exercise. Post-ischaemic mVO₂ recovery assessed by rapid cuff inflations; monoexponential time constant correlates with MRI phosphocreatine recovery kinetics (validated surrogate of mitochondrial oxidative capacity).

**Arterial tonometry:** Radial artery tonometry (SphygmoCor) for aortic augmentation index and pulse wave velocity.

**Statistical analysis:** Modified ITT; paired t-test or Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Power: 80% to detect ≥0.72 SD standardised difference; N=17; α=0.05. No adjustments for multiple comparisons — increases Type I error risk.

**Eligibility:**
- Symptomatic HF (orthopnea, PND, lower-extremity oedema, dyspnea on exertion)
- LVEF >50%
- E/e' >8 + ≥1: LAVI >34 mL/m², elevated NT-proBNP within past year, elevated filling pressures (PCWP >12 mmHg on prior cath), or chronic loop diuretic use
- Stable medical therapy; able to exercise

**Exclusion:** Non-cardiac exercise limitation (orthopaedic, peripheral arterial with claudication, neuromuscular disorders); gait instability; nonsinus rhythm; infiltrative/hypertrophic cardiomyopathy; pericardial disease; primary pulmonary arteriopathy; ACS or revascularisation within 60 days; clinically significant valvular disease; significant lung disease contributing to exercise intolerance; ischaemia on stress within past year not revascularised; eGFR <30; current nitrate/PDE5i use

## Limitations

- **N=17** — severely underpowered for clinical conclusions; crossover reduces variability but small numbers inflate risk of false positives especially with multiple secondary tests
- **Single acute dose** — whether benefit persists with chronic inorganic nitrate administration unknown; NEAT-HFpEF showed chronic organic nitrate harm; INDIE tests acute inorganic
- **Supine exercise** — peak VO₂ values markedly lower than upright (all values ~12 mL/min/kg); hemodynamic responses may differ from upright ambulatory exercise
- **88% male** — opposite of typical HFpEF trial demographics (~50% female); severely limits generalisability to women
- **100% hypertensive, 71% DM, 94% obese, 82% Black** — highly selected cardiometabolic phenotype; not representative of heterogeneous HFpEF
- **No adjustment for multiple comparisons** — many secondary endpoints tested; several positive findings should be interpreted as exploratory
- **53% met ESC HFpEF criteria** — 47% of enrolled patients did not meet ESC criteria; diagnostic heterogeneity
- **Median NT-proBNP 144 pg/mL** — below most modern HFpEF trial entry thresholds (typically ≥300–600 pg/mL); some subjects may have exercise intolerance from non-HF causes

## HFpEF Wiki Relevance

INDIE-HFpEF is a mechanistic pilot distinct from the large therapeutic trials:

1. **Not simply a negative study:** The stub incorrectly characterised this as negative for peak VO₂. The primary endpoint (efficiency) was neutral, but peak VO₂ improved significantly via peripheral haemodynamic augmentation. This is mechanistically important — inorganic NO₃⁻ works through peripheral vasodilation and cardiac output increase, not by improving the intrinsic oxygen cost of work.

2. **Inorganic vs. organic nitrate distinction:** NEAT-HFpEF ([[redfield2015neat]]) tested chronic isosorbide mononitrate (organic nitrate) and found activity WORSENED dose-dependently. INDIE tests a single acute dose of dietary inorganic nitrate (NO₃⁻ → NO₂⁻ → NO via oral bacteria/gut). These are different NO donors with different kinetics, tolerance profiles, and cellular targets. The contrast is important for the NO/cGMP pathway story.

3. **Mechanism: peripheral, not central:** VO₂ improved via ↑CO and ↓SVR (confirmed hemodynamic data) — not via improved O₂ efficiency. This places the limiting factor in HFpEF exercise intolerance at the peripheral/vascular level, complementing the central diastolic filling limitation hypothesis.

4. **Aortic wave reflections:** Reduction in augmentation index (−9.1%, P=0.03) is mechanistically intriguing — if sustained, could reduce LV pulsatile load and potentially favourably affect diastolic function long-term.

## Contradictions
- INDIE-HFpEF (single-dose inorganic NO₃⁻): peak VO₂ improved P=0.005 vs. NEAT-HFpEF (chronic organic nitrate): 6-min walk activity WORSENED (dose-response P=0.01). Same mechanistic target (NO pathway), opposite direction. Possible explanations: (1) acute vs. chronic — nitrate tolerance develops rapidly with continuous organic nitrate exposure; (2) inorganic (dietary, oral bacteria) vs. organic pathway — different NO donors with distinct cellular targets; (3) supine (INDIE) vs. ambulatory/upright exercise (NEAT). Neither study is definitive; the combination suggests NO pathway manipulation is not a simple "more NO = better" equation. See [[contradictions]].
- Primary endpoint neutral (P=0.64) while multiple secondary endpoints positive — without adjustment for multiple comparisons, the positive secondaries may represent Type I error. However, the consistency of direction across haemodynamic measures (CO, SVR, HR, AI) strengthens the mechanistic narrative. See [[contradictions]].
