# Total NT-proBNP in heart failure with preserved vs reduced ejection fraction

> NT-proBNP is underestimated by conventional Elecsys assays in HFpEF because glycosylation of the peptide at Thr-71 prevents antibody binding; total NT-proBNP (tNT-proBNP), measured by a research assay detecting both glycosylated and unglycosylated forms, has similar associations with clinical characteristics and potentially superior prognostic ability compared to standard NT-proBNP.

**Full citation:**
Hage C, Mang A, Daubert JC, Donal E, Myhre PL, Linde C, Lund LH. Total NT-proBNP improves prognostication compared to conventional NT-proBNP in patients with heart failure. *Int J Cardiol.* 2026;458:134554. doi:[10.1016/j.ijcard.2026.134554](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijcard.2026.134554)

## Core Arguments

**Background — the glycosylation problem:**
Standard NT-proBNP assays (Elecsys proBNP II STAT; Roche) use antibodies targeting the central NT-proBNP epitope. When this epitope is glycosylated (at Thr-71), it remains undetected — resulting in falsely low measured concentrations. NT-proBNP glycosylation is increased in:
- Obesity (high BMI)
- Diabetes / glucose perturbations
- Some ethnic groups
- Acute heart failure (reduced processing of proBNP → more glycosylated NT-proBNP relative to BNP)

Since HFpEF has higher obesity and DM prevalence, conventional NT-proBNP may systematically underestimate true cardiac stress in this population.

**Methods:**
- tNT-proBNP (research-use-only assay, Roche Diagnostics): sandwich immunoassay using two monoclonal antibodies to glycosylation-free epitopes at N- and C-terminal portions of NT-proBNP — detects both glycosylated and unglycosylated forms
- Studies: KaRen (prospective HFpEF; LVEF ≥45%, NT-proBNP >300 ng/L; n=83 with tNT-proBNP subsamples) and MetAnEnd (HFrEF; LVEF <40%; n=79)
- Primary composite outcome: all-cause death + HF hospitalisation

**Key Results:**

| Measure | HFpEF | HFrEF | P |
|---|---|---|---|
| tNT-proBNP (median; pg/mL) | 3830 (IQR 1990–6482) | 10,468 (IQR 4667–21,007) | <0.001 |
| NT-proBNP (median; pg/mL) | 1055 (IQR 466–2344) | 3196 (IQR 1423–6967) | <0.001 |
| NT-proBNP/tNT-proBNP ratio | 0.27 | 0.32 | 0.019 |

Lower ratio in HFpEF means **more glycosylation** in HFpEF than HFrEF → current assays underestimate true NT-proBNP more in HFpEF than HFrEF.

**Associations with glycosylation (low ratio = high glycosylation) in HFpEF:**
- Higher systolic BP (OR 0.29)
- Lower eGFR (OR 0.34)
- Higher heart rate (OR 11.5; HFpEF)
- Atrial fibrillation (OR 4.11; HFpEF)

**Prognostic performance (AUROC for composite outcome):**
- All patients: tNT-proBNP 0.710 vs. NT-proBNP 0.682 (p=0.068, NS)
- HFpEF: tNT-proBNP 0.7194 vs. NT-proBNP 0.6821 (p=0.4758, NS)
- HFrEF: tNT-proBNP 0.7058 vs. NT-proBNP 0.6701 (p=0.1138, NS)
- Direction consistent across all comparisons: tNT-proBNP numerically superior, but small N limits power

**Cox proportional hazards — tNT-proBNP in HFpEF:**
- Univariable: HR 1.69 (95% CI 1.07–2.66; p=0.024)
- Model 1 (age, sex, eGFR <60): HR 1.51 (0.92–2.49; p=0.101) — NS
- Model 2 (age, sex, diabetes): HR 1.77 (1.08–2.88; p=0.022) — significant

**Conclusion:** HFpEF has higher NT-proBNP glycosylation than HFrEF; tNT-proBNP is not commercially available yet (research-use-only) but may be a superior biomarker for HF populations with high obesity/DM/glucose perturbation prevalence. The commonly used Elecsys proBNP II assay underestimates NT-proBNP in these populations.

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

**Critical implication for HFpEF diagnosis:**
- Current ESC diagnostic thresholds (NT-proBNP ≥125 pg/mL at rest; ≥360 pg/mL during or after exercise) are based on standard assays that may be falsely low in obese/diabetic HFpEF patients
- FINEARTS-HF enrollment required NT-proBNP ≥300 pg/mL — this threshold would exclude a subset of true HFpEF with high NT-proBNP glycosylation
- H₂FPEF and HFA-PEFF scores both rely on NT-proBNP thresholds — their diagnostic performance may be attenuated in obese/DM populations
- Explains part of the "obesity paradox" in natriuretic peptides (lower NT-proBNP despite worse outcomes in obese HFpEF)

## Contradictions
Small N (n=83 HFpEF, n=79 HFrEF) limits statistical power — all comparisons with tNT-proBNP are exploratory. The tNT-proBNP assay is research-use-only and not yet available for clinical use. Whether tNT-proBNP will improve clinical decision-making at scale requires larger prospective validation.
