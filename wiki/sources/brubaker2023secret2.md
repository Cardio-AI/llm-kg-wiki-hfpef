# SECRET-II

> Parallel-arm RCT (N=88, Wake Forest; NCT02636439) comparing 20 weeks of caloric restriction + aerobic training + resistance training (RT+CR+AT) vs. caloric restriction + aerobic training alone (CR+AT) in obese older HFpEF patients; both groups improved similarly in peak VO₂ (+5–7%), KCCQ (+15–20 points), and body weight; resistance training added leg strength (+4.9 Nm vs. CR+AT; P=0.05) and muscle quality (+0.07 Nm/cm²; P=0.043) but did not improve peak VO₂ more than aerobic training alone and failed to prevent skeletal muscle mass loss — a key hypothesis-refuting finding.

## Core Arguments

1. **Both CR+AT and RT+CR+AT improve aerobic capacity and symptoms.** Peak VO₂ increased ~5% with CR+AT and ~7% with RT+CR+AT; KCCQ improved 15–20 points in both groups. These within-group improvements are clinically meaningful but do not differ statistically between groups (P=0.21 for VO₂peak between-group).

2. **Resistance training adds musculoskeletal benefit but not aerobic benefit.** RT+CR+AT added leg strength (+4.9 Nm more than CR+AT; P=0.05) and muscle quality (+0.07 Nm/cm²; P=0.043), confirming that resistance training targets the appropriate musculoskeletal pathway. However, these gains did not translate to greater VO₂peak improvement, implying that skeletal muscle strength gains and aerobic capacity gains are mechanistically separable.

3. **Resistance training did NOT prevent skeletal muscle mass loss.** The original hypothesis was that the caloric restriction–induced weight loss (which inevitably causes some lean mass loss alongside fat mass loss) would be offset by resistance training. Both groups lost similar amounts of skeletal muscle mass. This refutes the hypothesis that RT is protective against muscle wasting during intentional weight loss in HFpEF.

4. **Cardiac remodeling (LV mass reduction, reduced arterial stiffness) occurred equally in both groups.** No between-group difference in any cardiac or vascular structural endpoint. This confirms that caloric restriction + aerobic training is sufficient for cardiac remodeling — resistance training adds no additional structural cardiac benefit.

5. **Highly female-predominant, racially diverse, obese population.** ~85% women; ~52% non-White; BMI 40±6 kg/m². This is one of the most demographically representative HFpEF exercise trial populations, given that HFpEF with obesity disproportionately affects women and racial minorities.

## Trial Design

**Full title:** Caloric Restriction and Exercise Training in Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction (SECRET-II)  
**NCT:** NCT02636439  
**PI/Sponsor:** Dalane Kitzman / Peter Brubaker, Wake Forest University  
**Design:** Parallel-arm RCT  
**Intervention arms:**
- **CR+AT:** Caloric restriction (−500 kcal/day deficit) + aerobic training (3×/week supervised MCT)
- **RT+CR+AT:** Same CR+AT + resistance training (2×/week, lower extremity focus)
**Duration:** 20 weeks  
**Population:** HFpEF (LVEF ≥50%), obesity (BMI ≥30 kg/m²), age ≥60 years, NYHA II–III  
**N:** 88 randomized (44 RT+CR+AT, 44 CR+AT); 77 completed  
**Primary endpoint:** Change in peak VO₂ from baseline to 20 weeks  
**Key secondary endpoints:** 6MWD, KCCQ, body composition (skeletal muscle mass, fat mass), leg strength, muscle quality (Nm/cm²), LV mass, arterial stiffness  
**Predecessor:** SECRET (NCT00959660; Kitzman 2016, NEJM/JAMA) — the original 2×2 factorial caloric restriction vs. aerobic exercise trial; SECRET-II adds resistance training arm  
**Publication:** Circ Heart Fail 2023;16:e010161

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

SECRET-II directly extends SECRET (NCT00959660), which showed that caloric restriction and aerobic exercise have additive effects on peak VO₂ (+3.1 mL/kg/min combined vs. control). SECRET-II asks whether resistance training can add further benefit:

- **Exercise training hierarchy:** Aerobic training remains the dominant exercise modality for VO₂ benefit. Resistance training's musculoskeletal (strength, muscle quality) gains are not additive to aerobic VO₂ improvement.
- **Skeletal muscle mass:** The failure to preserve lean mass during weight loss has implications for combined CR+exercise strategies in obesity HFpEF. Despite 20 weeks of supervised resistance training, skeletal muscle atrophy was not prevented.
- **GLP-1RA context (SUMMIT, STEP-HFpEF):** Both semaglutide and tirzepatide achieve substantial weight loss in HFpEF with obesity. SECRET-II findings raise the question of whether the rapid, pharmacologically induced weight loss from GLP-1RA trials is accompanied by greater skeletal muscle loss than exercise-supervised weight reduction programs — a concern not yet addressed in SUMMIT or STEP-HFpEF.
- **Muscle quality vs. mass:** That RT improved muscle quality (force per unit area) without preventing absolute mass loss is consistent with resistance training improving muscle fibre type composition and metabolic efficiency rather than hypertrophy.

## Contradictions

- **Resistance training did not prevent skeletal muscle loss despite 20 weeks of supervised RT** — refutes the primary hypothesis that RT+CR would spare lean mass. Mechanistic explanation unclear: may reflect CR-induced catabolism dominating over RT-induced anabolism at the intervention intensities used, or inadequate protein intake during caloric restriction. See [[contradictions]] if lean mass preservation is a recurring theme across GLP-1RA trials.
