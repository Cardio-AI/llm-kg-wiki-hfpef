# PARADIGM-HF

> Sacubitril/valsartan (LCZ696) superior to enalapril in HFrEF: primary composite HR 0.80 (0.73–0.87) P<0.001; CV death HR 0.80 (0.71–0.89); all-cause death HR 0.84 (0.76–0.93); KCCQ improved +1.64 pts (P=0.001); paradoxically lower creatinine and hyperkalemia vs. enalapril; trial stopped early for overwhelming benefit; established ARNI as HFrEF pillar and directly motivated PARAGON-HF in HFpEF.

**File:** `raw/2014-NEJM-McMurray-PARADIGM-HF_study.pdf` · **Authors:** McMurray JJV, Packer M, Desai AS et al. · **Year:** 2014 · **Journal:** NEJM 371(11):993–1004  
**Study type:** RCT (active-comparator) · **N:** 8,442 randomised (LCZ696 4,187; enalapril 4,212) · **Population:** HFrEF, LVEF ≤40% (amended ≤35%), NYHA II–IV, elevated NPs  
**Follow-up:** Median 27 months · **Intervention:** LCZ696 (sacubitril/valsartan) 200 mg BID vs. enalapril 10 mg BID  
**Primary outcome:** CV death or first HF hospitalisation  
**NCT:** NCT01035255 · **Enrollment:** Dec 8, 2009–Nov 23, 2012 · **Sites:** 1,043 centres, 47 countries  
**Sponsor:** Novartis (collected/managed data; independent academic statistician replicated analyses)

---

## Key Findings

- Primary composite (CV death or HF hosp): HR **0.80 (0.73–0.87)**, **P<0.001** (exact P=4.0×10⁻⁷)
- CV death: HR **0.80 (0.71–0.89)**, **P<0.001**; NNT=32
- HF hospitalisation: HR **0.79 (0.71–0.89)**, **P<0.001**; NNT=21
- All-cause death: HR **0.84 (0.76–0.93)**, **P<0.001**
- KCCQ clinical summary score at 8 months: **+1.64 pts [0.63–2.65]**, **P=0.001** — significant QoL benefit
- New-onset AF: HR 0.97 (0.72–1.31), P=0.83 — neutral
- Renal decline: HR 0.86 (0.65–1.13), P=0.28 — non-significant trend favouring LCZ696
- Safety: **paradoxically lower creatinine elevation** (3.3% vs. 4.5%, P=0.007) and **lower hyperkalemia** (K >6.0: 4.3% vs. 5.6%, P=0.007); more hypotension (14.0% vs. 9.2%) and less cough (11.3% vs. 14.3%)
- Benefit consistent across all prespecified subgroups; nominally significant NYHA interaction (P=0.03) not replicated for CV death (P=0.76)
- Trial stopped early at third interim analysis (March 28, 2014) — overwhelming benefit boundary crossed

## Methods

**Design:** Double-blind, randomised, active-comparator RCT. Three phases: (1) single-blind enalapril run-in → (2) single-blind LCZ696 run-in → (3) double-blind randomised treatment. Sequential run-in excludes patients intolerant to either drug. ITT. 43 patients excluded post-randomisation (6 invalid, 37 from 4 premature-closure GCP sites).

**Run-in:**
- Enalapril run-in: 10,513 entered; median 15 days (IQR 14–21); 1,102 (10.5%) discontinued (most commonly: cough, hyperkalaemia, renal dysfunction, hypotension)
- LCZ696 run-in: 9,419 entered; median 29 days (IQR 26–35); 977 (10.4%) discontinued
- Net ~18% of screened patients excluded before randomisation — enriched tolerant, stable population
- 36-hour washout between enalapril and LCZ696 run-in phases to minimise angioedema risk from ACEi + neprilysin inhibition overlap

**Dose:** LCZ696 target 200 mg BID (mean achieved 375±71 mg/day). Enalapril target 10 mg BID (mean 18.9±3.4 mg/day — effective HFrEF dose per prior mortality trials). LVEF amended from ≤40% to ≤35% (December 15, 2010 protocol change).

**Eligibility:**
- Age ≥18, NYHA II–IV, LVEF ≤40% (→≤35%)
- BNP ≥150 or NT-proBNP ≥600 pg/mL; OR BNP ≥100 or NT-proBNP ≥400 if HF hospitalisation within 12 months
- Stable beta-blocker + ACEi (or ARB ≥10 mg enalapril equivalent) ≥4 weeks
- eGFR ≥30 mL/min/1.73m²; K ≤5.2 mmol/L at screening, ≤5.4 at randomisation; SBP ≥100 mmHg

**Statistical power:** 80% power to detect 15% RRR in CV death; 8,000 patients followed 34 months; 1,229 expected CV deaths; annual CV death rate 7.0% in enalapril group. Three prespecified interim analyses; stopping boundary P<0.0001 at first, P<0.001 at second and third.

## Limitations

- Active comparator (enalapril 10 mg BID), not placebo — reflects replacement of ACEi with ARNI; magnitude vs. no treatment unknown. Enalapril dose equivalent to mortality-proven doses used in CONSENSUS/SOLVD
- Sequential run-in excluded ~18% of screened patients (intolerant to enalapril or LCZ696); enriched tolerant, stable population — may overestimate real-world benefit
- Stopped early at third interim analysis — well-documented inflation of treatment effect estimates with early stopping
- LVEF amendment (≤40% → ≤35%) mid-enrollment introduces heterogeneity in enrolled population; ~11% enrolled with LVEF 36–40%
- 21% female — much lower than HFpEF trials (~50%); male-dominated HFrEF cohort limits sex-specific inferences
- Predominantly NYHA II (70%) — may underrepresent severe HF
- Active-comparator design: comparison is sacubitril/valsartan vs. enalapril, NOT vs. valsartan alone; isolating neprilysin inhibition contribution vs. ARB component not possible

## HFpEF Wiki Relevance

PARADIGM-HF is an HFrEF trial but is foundational to this wiki for four reasons:

1. **ARNI mechanism establishment:** Defines the sacubitril/valsartan pharmacology that PARAGON-HF then tested in HFpEF ([[solomon2019paragon]])
2. **EF-spectrum analysis:** PARADIGM (LVEF ≤35%) + PARAGON (LVEF ≥45%) pooled analysis demonstrates benefit declining continuously with increasing EF, supporting FDA label for "LVEF below normal"
3. **Renal safety paradox:** Paradoxically lower creatinine and hyperkalemia vs. enalapril — same unexpected safety profile seen in PARAGON-HF vs. valsartan; neprilysin inhibition may be renoprotective
4. **KCCQ comparison:** PARADIGM KCCQ benefit +1.64 pts P=0.001 vs. PARAGON +1.0 pt NS — EF-dependent symptom response to ARNI

## Contradictions
- Active-comparator design means sacubitril/valsartan vs. enalapril; benefit size vs. no RAAS therapy not determinable. In HFrEF, enalapril reduces mortality ~16% vs. placebo (CONSENSUS/SOLVD). LCZ696 reduces CV death by additional 20% vs. enalapril — combined vs. placebo ~30%. These layers are not separable from the PARADIGM-HF data alone.
- KCCQ benefit: PARADIGM +1.64 pts (P=0.001) vs. PARAGON +1.0 pts (NS) vs. DELIVER win ratio positive — graded EF response to ARNI on QoL, or different comparator (enalapril vs. valsartan vs. placebo)?
- Renal paradox: neprilysin inhibition increases ANP/BNP → natriuresis → expected renal stress; yet creatinine rise and hyperkalemia are LOWER with LCZ696 vs. enalapril. Same pattern in PARAGON-HF. The mechanism likely involves reduced efferent arteriolar vasoconstriction from neprilysin substrate accumulation, offsetting the expected RAAS-driven renal effects.
