# Joseph 2016 — QRS Duration as Predictor of Outcomes in HFpEF (TOPCAT)

> In a post-hoc analysis of the TOPCAT trial (N=3,445 HFpEF), QRS duration ≥120 ms was present in 17.9% of patients and was independently associated with a 27% higher risk of the primary composite outcome and 38% higher risk of HF hospitalisation; **QRS duration shows a continuous risk relationship with a threshold near 100 ms**.

## Key Findings

- **QRS ≥120 ms prevalence:** 17.9% of TOPCAT participants
- **Primary composite (CV death, aborted cardiac arrest, HF hospitalisation):** HR 1.27 (P=0.009) for QRS ≥120 ms
- **HF hospitalisation:** HR 1.38 (P=0.003) for QRS ≥120 ms
- **Continuous relationship:** Risk increases progressively across QRS duration; inflection point near ~100 ms (below standard LBBB threshold)
- **No interaction with spironolactone:** QRS duration did not modify treatment effect of spironolactone vs. placebo (P for interaction NS)
- QRS ≥120 ms associated with more LV hypertrophy, larger LV volumes, lower LVEF (still within preserved range), higher NT-proBNP, and more comorbidities vs. narrower QRS

## Methods

Post-hoc analysis of TOPCAT — a multinational RCT of spironolactone vs. placebo in HFpEF (LVEF ≥45%). Baseline 12-lead ECG QRS duration analyzed as continuous variable and dichotomized at ≥120 ms. Cox proportional hazards models adjusted for age, sex, NYHA class, LVEF, NT-proBNP, eGFR, AF history, and other confounders.

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

QRS duration ≥120 ms (wide QRS/LBBB) in HFpEF identifies a higher-risk phenotype with more advanced LV remodelling. The continuous relationship starting near 100 ms is clinically important — risk elevation precedes the conventional CRT threshold of 120 ms. This suggests intraventricular conduction delay is a marker of myocardial disease severity in HFpEF, distinct from its role as a treatment target (CRT) in HFrEF.
