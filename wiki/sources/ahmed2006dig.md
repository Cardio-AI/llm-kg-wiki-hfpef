# DIG-Preserved (Ancillary DIG Trial)

> Digoxin reduced worsening HF hospitalisations (2y HR 0.66 P=0.012; overall HR 0.79 P=0.094 trend) but simultaneously increased unstable angina hospitalisations (2y HR 1.76 P=0.004), producing net null effect on primary composite (HF hosp + HF death: overall HR 0.82 [0.63–1.07] P=0.136) and no effect on mortality (all-cause HR 0.99, CV HR 1.00); 2-year pre-specified outcomes were significant (P=0.034/0.044) but attenuated over median 37-month follow-up; small N=988 (vs. CHARM-Preserved N=3,023 with same endpoints); digoxin toxicity suspicion higher (10% vs 4%); LVEF ≥45% in 1991–1993 cohort pre-dates modern HFpEF phenotyping.

**Full citation:**
Ahmed A, Rich MW, Fleg JL, et al. Effects of Digoxin on Morbidity and Mortality in Diastolic Heart Failure: The Ancillary Digitalis Investigation Group Trial. *Circulation.* 2006;114(5):397–403. doi:[10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.105.601252](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.105.601252)
**Study type:** RCT (ancillary trial of main DIG trial, run in parallel) · **N:** 988 (digoxin 492; placebo 496) · **Population:** Chronic ambulatory HF with LVEF >45% and normal sinus rhythm  
**Follow-up:** Mean 37 months (vital status complete through Dec 31, 1995) · **Intervention:** Digoxin 0.125–0.50 mg/day (dose adjusted by age, sex, weight, creatinine) vs. placebo  
**Primary outcome:** Combined HF hospitalisation or HF mortality (time to first event)  
**NCT:** None (enrolled Jan 1991–Aug 1993, pre-ClinicalTrials.gov era) · **Sites:** US (186 centres) + Canada (116 centres)  
**Sponsor:** NHLBI; Department of Veterans Affairs Cooperative Studies Program; study drugs (Lanoxin and placebo) provided by Glaxo Wellcome

## Key Findings

- **Primary (HF hosp + HF death):** HR **0.82 (0.63–1.07)**, P=0.136 at study end — **NS**
- At 2 years (pre-specified, FDA approval basis): HR **0.71 (0.52–0.98)**, **P=0.034** — significant early benefit
- CHARM-Preserved endpoint (HF hosp + CV death): overall HR **0.88 (0.70–1.11)** P=0.269 NS; at 2y HR 0.75 (0.57–0.99) P=0.044
- **All-cause mortality: HR 0.99 (0.76–1.28)** P=0.925 — completely flat
- **CV mortality: HR 1.00 (0.73–1.36)** P=0.978 — exactly null
- **HF mortality: HR 0.88 (0.54–1.43)** P=0.598 — NS
- **Worsening HF hosp: HR 0.79 (0.59–1.04) P=0.094 (trend); at 2y: HR 0.66 (0.47–0.91) P=0.012** — digoxin reduced HF hosp
- **Unstable angina hosp: HR 1.37 (0.99–1.91) P=0.061 (trend); at 2y: HR 1.76 (1.20–2.59) P=0.004** — digoxin increased angina hosp
- All-cause hosp: HR 1.03 (0.89–1.20) P=0.683 — flat null; offset between HF reduction and angina increase

## Methods

**Design:** Double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled ancillary trial. Conducted in parallel with the main DIG trial (N=6,800, LVEF ≤45%). The main DIG trial included 988 patients with LVEF >45% (normal or near-normal LVEF); their results were reported in the main publication but not analysed in detail until this 2006 paper. NHLBI public-use dataset analysis.

**Dose:** 4 daily doses available: 0.125, 0.25, 0.375, 0.500 mg; assigned by age, sex, weight, creatinine. Median daily dose 0.25 mg throughout. At 12 months: 78% of digoxin and 77% of placebo patients still on study drug. Study drug discontinued in 33% overall (32% digoxin, 33% placebo).

**Eligibility:**
- Symptomatic chronic HF (ambulatory)
- LVEF >45% with normal sinus rhythm at baseline
- US and Canada; enrolled Jan 1991–Aug 1993

**Pre-specified 2-year analysis:** Effect of digoxin on outcomes at 2 years was pre-specified in the original DIG protocol (FDA approval basis); these are not post-hoc analyses. The 2-year significant findings should be interpreted within this context.

**Statistical analysis:** Cox proportional-hazards (HR, CI); Wilcoxon rank-sum for hospitalisation count comparisons; all 2-sided P<0.05 significant. Vital status collected to Dec 31, 1995 (98.9% complete).

**Offsetting mechanism:** Digoxin reduced worsening HF hospitalisations (−3.7 ARD, 2y HR 0.66 P=0.012) but increased unstable angina hospitalisations (+4.2 ARD, 2y HR 1.76 P=0.004). These competing effects produced the net null result in overall hospitalisations. The authors note the increased angina may relate to digoxin's effects on platelet and endothelial cell activation.

**Digoxin toxicity:** 66 patients total (7%) identified as suspected/confirmed digoxin toxicity; digoxin group 48 (10%) vs placebo 18 (4%) P<0.001. At 2 years: 15 digoxin (3%) vs 6 placebo (1%) P=0.049. Only 1 toxicity patient hospitalised for that reason.

**Open-label digoxin in placebo group:** 32 patients (7%) in placebo group required open-label digoxin for worsening HF or AF during the study, vs 53 (11%) in digoxin group requiring it earlier — introduces contamination that would bias toward null.

## Limitations

- **N=988 — ~7× smaller than main DIG** (N=6,800) and ~3× smaller than CHARM-Preserved (N=3,023); severely underpowered for all outcomes beyond exploratory
- **Post-hoc detailed analysis of a pre-specified ancillary trial** — the ancillary trial was pre-specified, but this 2006 paper is the first detailed publication of its results; results reported briefly in the original 1997 DIG publication
- **LVEF ≥45% in 1991–1993** — pre-dates modern HFpEF phenotyping (no echocardiographic structural criteria, no NP thresholds, no diastolic assessment); ~26% had LVEF 45–49%; significant diagnostic heterogeneity
- **No beta-blockers** — virtually no patients on beta-blockers at enrollment (pre-MERIT-HF/CIBIS-II era); current HFpEF background therapy includes beta-blockers in ~80%+
- **Normal sinus rhythm required** — excludes AF patients who constitute ~35–40% of modern HFpEF cohorts
- **2-year benefit does not persist** — primary significant at 2y (P=0.034) but NS at 37 months (P=0.136); pattern consistent with either early treatment effect attenuation or digoxin toxicity accumulation offsetting benefit
- **Digoxin toxicity signal** (48 vs 18 patients, P<0.001) introduces an active harm that may mask benefit in longer follow-up
- **Competing HF reduction/angina increase:** net-null overall hospitalisation obscures mechanistically different competing effects; not a simple "neutral" drug

## HFpEF Wiki Relevance

DIG-Preserved is historically significant as the **first completed RCT** in diastolic/preserved-EF HF (pre-dating CHARM-Preserved 2003 by 12 years in enrollment, though the detailed results were published in 2006):

1. **Not simply "neutral":** The stub characterisation "did not significantly reduce HF hospitalisations" misses the competing signal. Digoxin reduced worsening HF hospitalisations (2y P=0.012) while increasing unstable angina (2y P=0.004). This offset is mechanistically important — digoxin's negative chronotropic and mild inotropic effects may benefit HF symptoms while its vascular/platelet effects increase ischaemic events in a population with 56% ischaemic aetiology.

2. **Historical baseline:** DIG-Preserved shows that in 1991–1993 HFpEF patients, 86% were already on ACEi (standard of care), yet 25% still had the primary outcome over 37 months. This high event rate informed subsequent trial sizing.

3. **Normal sinus rhythm restriction:** Modern HFpEF trials increasingly include AF patients (DELIVER: ~37%; EMPEROR-Preserved: ~37%). DIG-Preserved's NSR requirement selected a more homogeneous but less representative population.

## Contradictions
- Primary composite neutral (HR 0.82 P=0.136) despite significant reduction in worsening HF hospitalisations (HR 0.79 P=0.094 trend; 2y P=0.012). Net null caused by offsetting increase in unstable angina (HR 1.37 P=0.061 trend; 2y P=0.004). Not a "clean negative" — two competing effects cancel. Whether the angina signal is a drug effect or chance is unresolved.
- 2-year significant primary (P=0.034) vs study-end NS (P=0.136): early benefit lost over time. Possible explanations: digoxin toxicity accumulation, progressive background therapy changes, or regression to the mean in the small sample. See [[contradictions]].
