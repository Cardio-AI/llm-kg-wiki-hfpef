# Resistance Training and Locomotor Muscle Function in HFpEF

> Pilot RCT (n=22, 12.5 weeks): resistance training reduced leg fat percentage (32.6→31.6%), increased lower limb lean mass index, improved VO₂peak (17.1→19.4 mL/kg/min), and nearly doubled leg press strength (92→129 kg) without adverse events — proof-of-concept that peripheral muscle adaptation drives exercise benefit in HFpEF.

**Full citation:**
Sharif SM, Smith JR, Bruhn EJ, Shultz AM, Olson TP. Impact of Structured Resistance Training on Locomotor Muscle Composition in Patients with HFpEF. *J Card Fail.* 2024;30(2):410–414. doi:[10.1016/j.cardfail.2023.10.475](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cardfail.2023.10.475)

---

## Core Arguments

**Trial:** Pilot randomised controlled trial. N=**22**. Duration: **12.5 weeks**. Population: HFpEF patients. Study focused on locomotor muscle adaptations to resistance training.

**Intervention:** Structured resistance training programme targeting lower limb muscle groups.

**Body composition outcomes:**
- Leg fat percentage (LF%): **32.6% → 31.6%** in RT group (decreased)
- Lower limb lean mass (LLM) index: **increased** in RT group
- Fat-specific changes in locomotor muscles — mechanistically distinct from systemic weight loss strategies

**Exercise capacity outcomes:**
- VO₂peak: **17.1 → 19.4 mL/kg/min** — clinically meaningful aerobic improvement driven by peripheral muscle adaptation
- Leg press strength: **92 → 129 kg** (+40%) — substantial lower limb strength gain

**Safety:** No adverse events reported.

**Limitations:** Small pilot (n=22); primary endpoint not formally pre-stated in abstract; short duration (12.5 weeks); no long-term follow-up; single-centre.

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

Exercise intolerance in HFpEF involves not only central haemodynamic limitation (elevated PCWP) but also peripheral skeletal muscle dysfunction — reduced oxidative capacity, fat infiltration, muscle atrophy. This pilot shows resistance training can address the peripheral component: reducing intramuscular fat, increasing lean mass, and improving both aerobic capacity and strength. The fat/lean selectivity parallels the HuMAIN trial [[pandey2025humain]] comparison: resistance training achieves fat-specific changes without lean mass loss concern.
