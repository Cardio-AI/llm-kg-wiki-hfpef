# 2023 ACC Expert Consensus Decision Pathway — HFpEF

> Operational companion to the AHA 2022 HF guidelines, providing practical HFpEF-specific algorithms for diagnosis, treatment initiation, comorbidity management, and referral; elevates SGLT2i to near-Class I status (green/yellow Figure 9), gives the first sex-stratified treatment algorithm (ARNI/MRA preferenced for women and men with LVEF <55–60%), introduces CHECK-IN and INHALE referral acronyms, and flags SUMMIT and STEP-HFpEF GLP-1RA trials as ongoing.

## Core Arguments

### Relationship to existing guidelines
This ECDP explicitly "operates under the framework of recommendations published in the recent HF guidelines" (Heidenreich 2022, [[heidenreich2022aha]]). It fills the HFpEF-specific operational gap: where AHA 2022 gives class recommendations, this document gives algorithms, dose tables, contraindication lists, and referral criteria.

---

### Section 6 — Diagnosis

**HFpEF definition (Section 3.2):**
- Clinical diagnosis of HF + LVEF ≥50% + not attributable to infiltrative CMP, HCM, valvular disease, pericardial disease, or high-output HF
- HFpEF mimics must be actively excluded before diagnosis is made by default

**Diagnostic scoring (Section 6.3):**
The ECDP prioritises H₂FPEF over HFA-PEFF for routine clinical practice:
- H₂FPEF is validated against invasive CPET, uses only 6 clinically accessible variables (no NP threshold), and does not require echocardiography for scoring
- HFA-PEFF is endorsed but step F1 (diastolic stress testing) often impractical; step F2 (exclusion of other causes = etiology) is invaluable
- Critically: a low H₂FPEF score should NOT be used to exclude HFpEF when clinical context is high (obese patients may have low NPs despite invasively confirmed HFpEF)
- Practical recommendation: in obese patients with dyspnea, initiate GDMT empirically rather than pursue invasive testing if functional diagnosis is otherwise plausible

**HFpEF mimics (Table 1) — diagnostic clues and testing:**

| Mimic | Clinical Clues | Testing |
|---|---|---|
| Cardiac amyloidosis | Increased LV wall thickness, CTS/lumbar stenosis, neuropathy | Monoclonal protein screen; Tc-pyrophosphate scan; endomyocardial biopsy if MGUS positive |
| HCM | Unexplained LV hypertrophy, LVOTO, family history | CMR if uncertain |
| Cardiac sarcoidosis | Extracardiac disease (pulm/ocular/derm), high-grade AV block <60y, VT | CMR; FDG-PET |
| Hemochromatosis | Family history, frequent transfusions, DM, erectile dysfunction | Ferritin/transferrin; HFE genetics; CMR T2* |
| Fabry disease | Angiokeratomas, neuropathy, proteinuria, X-linked | α-galactosidase (men); GLA genetics; biopsy |
| High-output HF | 4-chamber enlargement, increased LVOT VTI | Investigate: anemia, AV malformations, cirrhosis, fistulas, thiamine |
| Myocarditis | Viral prodrome, troponin without CAD, heart block/VT | CMR; endomyocardial biopsy |
| Pericardial disease | Prior cardiac surgery/radiation/pericarditis, right-sided HF | CMR; R+L heart cath (LV/RV discordance) |

**Diagnostic flow (Figure 8 — stepwise):**
1. Assess primary noncardiovascular entity (kidney failure, liver disease, chronic venous insufficiency) → "congestion from"
2. Assess for HFpEF mimics → "HF attributed to" (specific disease-directed therapy exists)
3. If neither → HFpEF by exclusion → identify contributing comorbidities → "HFpEF associated with"

**Sex-specific differences in diagnosis (Section 6.3.1; Figure 6):**
- Women have greater attributable risk from HTN, DM, obesity
- Women have unique risk factors: pre-eclampsia, central obesity/visceral adiposity
- Women: more concentric LV remodeling, more impaired diastolic relaxation, worse dyspnea and health status
- Sex-neutral EF thresholds underestimate LV dysfunction in women: LVEF 50–55% may be abnormal in a woman (smaller chamber → apparent high EF); this is the mechanistic basis for the sex-specific treatment algorithm

### Section 7 — Management

**Treatment algorithm (Figure 9 — the key clinical tool):**

```
HFpEF Treatment

SGLT2i (ALL patients — dapagliflozin or empagliflozin 10 mg daily)
       ↓
Four parallel add-on paths:
1. Loop diuretic → for fluid retention, NYHA II-IV (titrate)
2. MRA → for women (all EF) + men with EF <55-60% + fluid retention
3. ARNI → for women (all EF) + men with LVEF <55-60%
4. ARB → for ARNI-eligible patients who cannot take ARNI (cost/intolerance)
```

Color coding: SGLT2i = green border/yellow (Class 2a but may become Class I); loop diuretic = green (Class I); MRA/ARNI/ARB = orange (Class 2b)

**Table 3 — Starting and Target Doses:**

| Drug | Starting Dose | Target Dose |
|---|---|---|
| Dapagliflozin | 10 mg daily | 10 mg daily |
| Empagliflozin | 10 mg daily | 10 mg daily |
| Spironolactone | 25 mg daily | 50 mg daily |
| Sacubitril/valsartan | 24/26 mg twice daily | 97/103 mg twice daily |
| Candesartan | 4–8 mg daily | 32 mg daily |

**Table 4 — Key Contraindications and Cautions:**

| Drug | Contraindications | Key Cautions |
|---|---|---|
| SGLT2i | Type 1 DM; on dialysis; lactation; known hypersensitivity | eGFR <25 (dapa) or <20 (empa); DKA risk; mycotic genital infections; Fournier's gangrene (rare) |
| MRA | K ≥5.0 mmol/L; Addison disease; pregnancy | eGFR <30 → half dose; creatinine ≥2.5 mg/dL; concomitant K+ agents (ACEi, ARB, ARNI, NSAIDs, trimethoprim); gynecomastia (use eplerenone) |
| ARNI | Within 36h of ACEi; history of angioedema; pregnancy; severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C); aliskiren in DM | Half dose if not already on ACEi/ARB; moderate hepatic impairment; renal artery stenosis; hypotension |
| ARB | Pregnancy/lactation; concomitant ACEi/aliskiren/ARNI | History of angioedema; hyperkalemia; hypotension; AKI |

**SGLT2i section (7.1.1):**
- Meta-analysis (5 trials): CV death/HF hosp HR 0.80 (0.73–0.87) pooled; HR 0.88 (0.81–0.95) across all EF subgroups; effect additive to MRAs and ARNIs
- SOLOIST-WHF: sotagliflozin (SGLT1+2, not FDA-approved) reduced HF deaths/hospitalizations/urgent visits across EF spectrum
- EMPULSE: empagliflozin in hospitalized acute decompensated HF; more rapid decongestion; safe and effective at hospital initiation
- PRESERVED-HF: dapagliflozin; improvements in health status and QoL; greatest benefit in those with worst baseline impairment
- In-hospital GDMT initiation is safe and effective (STRONG-HF trial supports this approach)

**MRA section (7.1.2):**
- ALDO-DHF (N=422): spironolactone significantly improves diastolic function and exercise capacity (source: Table 2 footnote — referenced as ref 70)
- TOPCAT Americas: HR 0.82 (0.69–0.98); canrenone undetectable in 30% Russia vs. 3% US/Canada — regional contamination explains overall null
- Benefit most evident in: lower NP tertile (NT-proBNP <682 pg/mL, BNP <166 pg/mL); women; LVEF <60% (Solomon 2016 TOPCAT EF subgroup analysis)
- Most HFpEF patients benefit for diuresis, hypertension control, and HF hospitalizations — EVEN if primary composite was null

**ARNI section (7.1.3):**
- PARAGON-HF: HR 0.87 (0.75–1.01) overall; women HR 0.73 (0.59–0.90); LVEF 45–57%: HR 0.78 (0.64–0.95)
- FDA expanded label (February 2021): "benefits most clearly evident in patients with LVEF below normal"
- Women with LVEF 50–55% are effectively in the abnormal LVEF range; thus sex + EF drive the recommendation
- Sacubitril/valsartan associated with lower creatinine elevation and hyperkalemia vs. valsartan despite RAAS augmentation (same safety paradox as in PARAGON vs. I-PRESERVE; mechanism unclear)

**Sex-specific GDMT differences (Section 7.1.5):**
- Women (all EFs): consider ARNI and MRA — LVEF 50–55% may be functionally reduced; PARAGON and TOPCAT show benefit across EF strata in women
- Men: ARNI/MRA mainly for LVEF <55–60%
- The higher apparent LVEF in women is a measurement artifact of smaller LV chamber size — not evidence of preserved systolic function

### Section 7.2 — Nonpharmacological Management

**Table 5 — Exercise and device studies:**
- Edelmann (N=64): 3 months endurance/resistance training → peak VO₂ +3.3 mL/kg/min + improved QoL + E/e' improvement (Ex-DHF trial)
- Kitzman (N=100): caloric restriction + aerobic exercise → peak VO₂: exercise +1.2; diet +1.3; both +2.5 mL/kg/min (additive effect)
- Brubaker (N=88): resistance training added to CR + exercise → leg strength/muscle quality improved; NO additive VO₂ or QoL benefit
- CardioMEMS (CHAMPION trial, N=119): 50% reduction in HF hospitalization at 17.6 months; GUIDE-HF (N=795): no reduction overall (COVID confounding); GUIDE-HF pre-COVID: ~20% HF hospitalization/urgent visit reduction

**CardioMEMS recommendation:** Class 2b; useful subset — ≥1 HF hospitalization + persistent NYHA III despite optimal GDMT + cardiorenal syndrome + obesity/lung disease complicating diagnosis

**Exercise:** ≥150 min/week aerobic physical activity (≥30 min most days); SGLT2i benefit is additive to exercise training

### Section 7.3 — Comorbidity Management

**Figure 11 summary — comorbidity management algorithm:**

**Hypertension:**
- Target SBP <130 mmHg (SPRINT evidence extrapolated)
- Higher goal in symptomatic orthostasis, labile BP, or impact on renal function
- Preferred agents: diuretic + ARNI or ARB or MRA (all have modest HF endpoint evidence)
- Beta-blockers: generally avoid for rate control (chronotropic incompetence); use only for specific indications (post-MI ≤3y, angina, AF)
- Most will require ≥2 agents; investigate secondary hypertension causes if refractory to 4 agents

**Obesity:**
- Prevalence 60–80% in HFpEF; obesity paradox (U-shaped mortality with nadir at BMI 32–34 kg/m²)
- Caloric restriction + aerobic exercise first-line
- GLP-1RA semaglutide (STEP-1 trial: >15 kg sustained weight loss at 68 weeks) and tirzepatide (SURMOUNT-1: up to 20% weight reduction) referenced
- **CAVEAT:** lean muscle mass loss is a concern in HF; SUMMIT (NCT04847557, semaglutide) and STEP-HFpEF (NCT04788511, tirzepatide) trials ongoing at time of ECDP — results will define GLP-1RA role
- Bariatric surgery for BMI ≥35 kg/m² when medical/surgical/nutritional expertise available (Mikhalkova: gastric bypass improved MLHF score and diastolic relaxation)

**Diabetes mellitus:**
- SGLT2i first-line for HFpEF+T2DM (and also for HFpEF without T2DM per HF indication)
- GLP-1RA for T2DM + obesity OR high ASCVD risk (liraglutide, semaglutide, dulaglutide — CV trials showed 10–23% HF hosp reduction)
- Metformin: first-line for glycemic control (eGFR ≥30); safe; 20% relative risk reduction in mortality (observational)
- HbA1c targets: <7% to 7.5% in lower comorbidity burden; <8% to 8.5% in advanced HF with polypharmacy/hypoglycemia risk
- **Avoid:** saxagliptin and alogliptin (DPP4 inhibitors — saxagliptin HR 1.27 for HF hospitalization, FDA contraindicated)
- **Contraindicated:** thiazolidinediones (TZDs) — fluid retention, weight gain, HF events

**Atrial fibrillation:**
- AF coexists in ~35–40% HFpEF; higher-risk phenotype (worse outcomes, pulmonary hypertension, RV dysfunction)
- Anticoagulation: nearly all HFpEF+AF patients have CHA₂DS₂-VASc ≥2; anticoagulate unless contraindicated
- Rate control: beta-blocker or non-dihydropyridine CCB first-line; aggressive rate control (<80 bpm rest, <110 bpm exercise) avoided due to low stroke volume reserve
- Rhythm control: individualized; in persistent AF with HFpEF symptoms despite rate control, rhythm strategy is reasonable
- Catheter ablation (CABANA): neutral overall; CABANA HF subgroup (mostly HFpEF 56%) showed greater benefit — ablation may be considered for symptomatic, drug-refractory AF in HFpEF
- Digoxin: add-on strategy if BB/non-DHP CCB inadequate/contraindicated
- Dronedarone: pharmacological rhythm control (ATHENA: reduced CV events in paroxysmal/persistent AF)
- Nitrates: NOT recommended for HFpEF (NEAT-HFpEF evidence)

**Coronary artery disease:**
- CAD in >50% HFpEF, microvascular dysfunction in up to 75%
- Revascularization: no prospective RCT data in HFpEF specifically; observational data suggest benefit; revascularize if symptomatic/refractory angina or ACS
- COMPASS (rivaroxaban 2.5 mg BID + aspirin): modest CV benefit in HF subgroup, excess bleeding — role unclear in HFpEF+CAD
- Nitrates: not recommended for HFpEF exercise intolerance per NEAT-HFpEF; acceptable for angina symptoms

**Sleep apnea:**
- Prevalence 55–80% in HFpEF
- OSA: CPAP improves daytime sleepiness, quality of life, sleep quality; no definitive mortality benefit in large HF trials
- Screen for OSA in: resistant hypertension (≥3 drugs), AF (CPAP may reduce incident AF), severe obesity, nocturnal bradyarrhythmia, nocturnal hypoxia
- Central sleep apnea: marker of HF severity; CPAP, ASV (avoid ASV in HFrEF EF <45% per SERVE-HF); CPAP preferable for combined OSA/CSA in HFpEF

**Chronic kidney disease:**
- CKD in ~50% HFpEF; 3-fold increased mortality; higher NP cutoffs needed for diagnosis
- SGLT2i: slow CKD progression (EMPEROR-Preserved; PARAGON-HF renal HR 0.50)
- RAAS + SGLT2i preferred — both nephroprotective and HF-directed
- ARNI if eGFR ≥30; SGLT2i if eGFR ≥20
- Finerenone (nonsteroidal MRA) for diabetic CKD (FIGARO-DKD, FIDELIO-DKD: reduced HF hospitalization + CV events at eGFR down to 25 mL/min)
- Thiazide + loop diuretic combination for diuretic resistance in CKD
- Small eGFR dip on RAAS/SGLT2i initiation: expected; do NOT attribute to AKI without other evidence

### Section 8 — Multidisciplinary Care

**CHECK-IN acronym (Figure 12) — when primary care should refer to cardiology:**
- **C**ollaboration: management of AF, HTN, DM, CAD requiring specialist co-management
- **H**igh-risk features: HF hospitalizations, pulmonary hypertension, RV dysfunction
- **E**xtensive evaluation needed: exercise testing, invasive hemodynamics, CMR
- **C**ardiorenal syndrome
- **K**nowledge of HFpEF mimics: valvular disease, HCM, amyloid, pericardial disease
- **I**ncreased need for diuretic agents
- **N**YHA Class III or IV symptoms

**INHALE acronym (Figure 13) — when cardiology should refer to HF specialist:**
- **I**n need of diagnosis: atypical presentation, exercise-intolerance-only phenotype, no classic risk factors
- **N**onresponsive to diuretics or medical therapy; NT-proBNP >3,000 pg/mL, BNP >1,000 pg/mL
- **H**ospitalized ≥2 times for HF in past year
- **A**cute or chronic end-organ dysfunction: worsening kidney/liver function, cardiac cachexia
- **L**ow blood pressure: SBP <100 mmHg
- **E**vidence of HFpEF mimics: management of rare or unusual cardiomyopathies

**STRONG-HF evidence for GDMT titration at discharge:**
- 8% absolute reduction in HF readmission/death at 180 days (15% vs 23%) with high-intensity GDMT up-titration vs. usual care
- High-intensity group: GDMT initiated at ½ target dose before discharge; titrated to target over 2 weeks post-discharge with frequent follow-up
- This approach is safe, feasible, and substantially improves outcomes — strongly supports in-hospital GDMT initiation

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

1. **Most clinically actionable document in wiki:** While AHA 2022 and ESC 2021 give recommendation classes, this ECDP provides the actual algorithm (Figure 9), dose tables (Table 3), contraindication list (Table 4), HFpEF mimic diagnostic testing (Table 1), referral criteria (CHECK-IN/INHALE), and comorbidity management flowchart (Figure 11). It is the primary reference for "how to actually treat HFpEF."

2. **SGLT2i promotion to near-Class I:** Figure 9 treats SGLT2i as a green-bordered yellow box — consensus committee's judgment that evidence exceeds Class 2a despite the formal guideline classification. The note that "SGLT2i may receive a stronger class of recommendation in future guidelines" is an explicit forward-looking statement.

3. **Sex-stratified treatment algorithm is new:** No prior guideline gave a sex-specific treatment pathway for HFpEF. The biological rationale (LVEF 50–55% may be abnormal in women; PARAGON women HR 0.73 vs. men HR 1.03) justifies the different ARNI/MRA recommendations. This directly challenges the assumption that HFpEF is a homogeneous pharmacological target.

4. **GLP-1RA for obesity in HFpEF:** This ECDP is the first major guideline document to explicitly reference semaglutide and tirzepatide in HFpEF management, while appropriately flagging SUMMIT and STEP-HFpEF as the definitive evidence that will be needed. This represents a significant shift in the field's attention toward cardiometabolic mechanisms.

5. **HFpEF mimics framework:** Table 1 provides the most comprehensive diagnostic testing framework for HFpEF mimics in the wiki. Cardiac amyloidosis in particular (with CTS/lumbar stenosis clues → Tc-pyrophosphate scan → endomyocardial biopsy pathway) is clinically high-stakes given tafamidis availability.

## Contradictions
- SGLT2i described as "may receive Class I in future guidelines" (Kittleson 2023 ECDP) vs. AHA 2022 Class 2a and ESC 2021 no recommendation. The evidence base (DELIVER + EMPEROR-Preserved) did not change between 2022 and 2023 — the committee's confidence threshold in interpreting existing evidence increased. See [[contradictions]] and [[guideline-comparison]].
- Sex-specific ARNI/MRA recommendations (women benefit across all EF; men primarily EF <55–60%) are based on PARAGON and TOPCAT subgroup analyses — post-hoc and not pre-specified sex-stratified trials. The biological rationale is strong (sex-specific EF physiology) but prospective sex-enriched trial evidence does not exist. See [[contradictions]].
