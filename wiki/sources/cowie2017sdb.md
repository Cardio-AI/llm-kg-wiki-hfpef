# Cowie 2017 — Sleep-Disordered Breathing in Heart Failure (State of the Art Review)

> SDB affects 50–75% of HF patients; CSA and OSA have distinct mechanisms and respond differently to treatment — adaptive servo-ventilation (ASV) is contraindicated in HFrEF+CSA (SERVE-HF mortality signal) but remains under-investigated in HFpEF where OSA predominates.

## Core Arguments

### Epidemiology and Classification
- SDB prevalence in HF (combined HFpEF + HFrEF): **50–75%**; up to 40% in general cardiovascular disease
- Two main subtypes: **central sleep apnea (CSA)** — central respiratory signal pauses, airflow cessation ≥10s, driven by cardiac dysfunction and Cheyne-Stokes respiration; **obstructive sleep apnea (OSA)** — upper airway collapse, partial or complete obstruction ≥10s
- CSA predominates in HFrEF and is more strongly related to degree of cardiac dysfunction; OSA predominates in HFpEF, especially the obese phenotype
- Both can coexist; AHI ≥15 events/h (regardless of symptoms) defines clinically significant SDB

### SERVE-HF (Adaptive Servo-Ventilation in HFrEF+CSA)
- SERVE-HF (N=1,325 HFrEF, LVEF ≤45%, AHI ≥15, predominantly CSA): ASV significantly **increased CV mortality** (HR 1.28; P=0.01) and all-cause mortality (HR 1.17; P=0.048)
- Mechanism uncertain: suppression of CSA may eliminate a compensatory respiratory drive; increased mean airway pressure may reduce LV preload, causing harm in low-output state
- **Contraindication established: ASV is contraindicated in HFrEF + predominantly CSA**

### CAT-HF and Treatment Landscape
- CAT-HF (adaptive servo-ventilation in acute decompensated HF): **stopped early** due to SERVE-HF findings; no HFpEF-specific ASV data from large RCTs
- CPAP reduces apnea/hypopnea index and improves sleepiness, BP; modest effects on cardiac function in OSA
- Treatment of OSA in HFpEF may reduce blood pressure, reduce cardiac remodelling, and potentially slow HFpEF progression — **large-scale trials lacking**

### HFpEF-Specific Considerations
- HFpEF patients, especially "obese phenotype" (BMI >30), have predominantly OSA
- SDB in HFpEF: intermittent hypoxia/reoxygenation → increased sympathetic tone, RAAS activation, systemic inflammation → contribute to diastolic dysfunction progression
- SDB often unrecognized in HFpEF; symptoms (daytime sleepiness, snoring) can be attributed to cardiac disease
- Diagnostic overlap: Cheyne-Stokes breathing and orthopnea are common in both HF and CSA

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

This review establishes that SDB is highly prevalent in HFpEF and mechanistically intertwined with its pathophysiology. The SERVE-HF lesson (ASV harmful in HFrEF+CSA) limits treatment options. OSA-targeted treatment in HFpEF is a plausible intervention but lacks large-scale RCT evidence.
