# CHAMPION Trial: Primary Results (Abraham 2011)

> CHAMPION primary RCT (N=550; NYHA III HF): haemodynamic-guided management via daily wireless PA pressure monitoring reduced HF hospitalisation rate by 28% (HR 0.72; P=0.0002) over 6 months — the first device-based remote haemodynamic monitoring trial to demonstrate HF hospitalisation reduction across EF subtypes including HFpEF.

## Core Arguments

**Trial:** CHAMPION (CardioMEMS Heart Sensor Allows Monitoring of Pressure to Improve Outcomes in NYHA Class III Heart Failure Patients). Primary 6-month results. NCT00531661. N=**550** patients enrolled at 64 US sites (280 active, 270 control). Inclusion: NYHA III HF with at least one HF hospitalisation in the prior year; LVEF-unrestricted (37% with LVEF ≥40% = HFpEF subgroup).

**Device:** CardioMEMS PA pressure sensor — permanently implanted wireless transducer in a distal pulmonary artery branch. Patients transmit daily PA pressure readings; clinicians adjust diuretics, vasodilators, and medications remotely using a web-based telemonitoring platform in response to PA diastolic pressure excursions.

**Primary endpoint:** Cumulative rate of HF hospitalisations over 6 months (person-year event rate, randomised-access period).

**Primary results:**
- Active group: 0.32 HF hospitalisations per patient-year
- Control group: 0.44 HF hospitalisations per patient-year
- **HR 0.72** (95% CI 0.60–0.87), **P=0.0002** — **28% reduction**
- All-cause hospitalisation rate also reduced
- No significant 6-month mortality difference

**Mechanism:** Daily PA diastolic pressure data detects subclinical haemodynamic congestion (rising PAD >18 mmHg) 1–2 weeks before symptomatic decompensation. Pre-emptive outpatient medication adjustment (primarily diuretic up-titration, vasodilator adjustment) prevents escalation to hospitalisation. This approach treats the elevated filling pressure state directly rather than modifying underlying myocardial biology — a mechanism-appropriate strategy for HFpEF.

**Safety:** Acceptable sensor failure rate; device-related serious adverse events occurred in <1% of patients.

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

CHAMPION is the foundational evidence for device-based haemodynamic monitoring as an HFpEF treatment approach. The 2011 primary paper establishes proof-of-concept; [[abraham2016champion]] (2016) extends follow-up to 18 months with an open-access phase showing 33–48% hospitalisations reductions. The 2014 HFpEF subgroup analysis ([[adamson2014champion]]) confirms benefit specifically in HFpEF.

This trial succeeds where most pharmacological HFpEF strategies had failed (RELAX, I-PRESERVE, CHARM-Preserved) — not by modifying myocardial biology but by real-time haemodynamic surveillance enabling personalised decompensation prevention. FDA approved CardioMEMS for NYHA III HF in April 2014 based on CHAMPION evidence.
