# Giannitsi 2019 — 6-Minute Walking Test: A Useful Tool in the Management of Heart Failure Patients

> Narrative review of the 6-minute walking test (6MWT) in heart failure (HF): summarises the standardised ATS/ERS test protocol, correlations with CPET-derived peak VO₂, clinical/biochemical/echocardiographic determinants of 6MWD, prognostic role, and use as an intervention-response endpoint — concluding 6MWT is a simple, inexpensive, well-tolerated submaximal alternative to CPET where the latter is unavailable or infeasible, but cannot substitute for CPET's mechanistic decomposition of exercise intolerance.

## Summary

Narrative review (Second Department of Cardiology, University of Ioannina Medical School, Greece). Reviews the value, methodology, correlates, determinants, prognostic role, and interventional use of the 6-minute walking test (6MWT) in heart failure populations, contrasting it throughout with cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET). Predominantly synthesises evidence from HF with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) literature, with occasional explicit reference to HFpEF populations. (source: giannitsi2019sixmwt)

## Rationale for 6MWT vs. CPET

- Three categories of functional-capacity assessment in HF: (1) self/physician-reported status (e.g., NYHA class), (2) CPET (gold standard, direct gas-exchange measurement of peak VO₂), (3) submaximal field tests including 6MWT
- **CPET limitations cited as rationale for 6MWT use:** CPET is "an expensive method that demands special equipment and trained personnel, while its availability in many hospital settings is limited"; patients "often find it difficult to cooperate with the test either due to the complexity of the test or the severe impairment of their functional status"
- **6MWT advantages stated:** "a simple test that requires no specialized equipment or advanced training for physicians"; "widely available and well-tolerated"; may serve as an indicator of maximal exercise capacity in severely impaired advanced-HF patients unable to complete a maximal test
- 6MWT explicitly framed as providing prognostic information that "can complement or substitute the information given by peak VO2 in the case when CPET is unavailable"
- Despite significant correlation with peak VO₂, changes in 6MWD are **not** a reliable predictor of changes in peak VO₂ within individual patients and "cannot be considered as a substitute of CPET" for mechanistic assessment

## 6MWT Protocol (Methodological Issues)

- First standardised by the American Thoracic Society (ATS) in 2002; updated jointly by ATS/European Respiratory Society (ERS) subsequently — "although a standard methodology has been proposed, there are many versions of the protocol used in various centers"; using the same strictly standardised protocol matters most for within-center/within-patient comparisons
- **Setting:** performed preferably indoors, on a flat, straight, hard-surfaced corridor **usually at least 30m long**
- **Pre-test:** patient told to be calm, to have taken medications, and to wear comfortable clothing and shoes; supervisor records baseline oxygen saturation, heart rate, brachial arterial blood pressure, and Borg scale rating for dyspnea and fatigue
- **Course marking:** walking course marked every 3m; cones placed at turnarounds advised
- **During the test:** patient walks at a rate suitable to their condition; may stop or slow down and resume as soon as possible; supervisor remains present throughout
- **Standardised encouragement phrasing:** supervisor gives encouragement using standard phrases such as **"You are doing well," "Keep up the good work"**; encouragement has been shown to affect distance covered, particularly in pediatric populations
- **Post-test:** Borg scale for dyspnea/fatigue repeated; blood pressure, heart rate, oxygen saturation optionally re-measured; number of laps plus additional distance recorded to calculate 6MWD
- **Learning effect:** two measurements proposed at initial assessment to ensure accuracy; may be less important in older patients with severe respiratory impairment and severe HF
- **Reproducibility:** "very good" when performed regularly by the same trained operator(s) following an established protocol
- **Contraindications — absolute:** acute MI or unstable angina, uncontrolled symptomatic/haemodynamically significant arrhythmias, acute myocarditis/pericarditis, uncontrolled acutely decompensated HF (acute pulmonary oedema), acute pulmonary embolism, suspected dissecting aneurysm, severe resting hypoxaemia or acute respiratory failure, acute noncardiopulmonary disorder affecting/aggravated by exercise (e.g., infection, renal failure, thyrotoxicosis), or inability to cooperate
- **Contraindications — relative:** resting HR >120 bpm, systolic BP >180 mmHg, diastolic BP >100 mmHg
- **Immediate stop criteria:** chest pain, intolerable dyspnea, leg cramps, diaphoresis, or any report of not feeling well
- Reference 6MWD equations exist for healthy adults (multiple studies, R² typically 0.2–0.8) but are not standardised across populations due to methodological variation between studies; 6MWD in healthy adults reported to range 400–700m

## Correlation with Other Functional Measures

- Only mild-to-moderate inverse correlation between NYHA class and 6MWD; one systematic review reported mean 6MWD ~400m (NYHA II), ~320m (NYHA III), ~225m (NYHA IV), with overlap between NYHA I and II
- Moderate-to-strong correlations reported between 6MWD and peak VO₂ across numerous HF studies (r ranging ~0.28–0.81 across cited cohorts)
- In HF patients evaluated for transplantation, 6MWD <350m had 71% sensitivity/60% specificity for predicting peak VO₂ <14 ml/kg/min
- **HFpEF-specific note:** in a randomized trial of older HFpEF patients, 6MWD did **not** correlate with CPET-derived measures, "challenging" its usefulness as a functional-capacity test in this population (citing Maldonado-Martin et al. 2017)
- 6MWT and CPET are described as assessing two distinct domains of functional capacity — 6MWT evaluates the integrated response of all systems involved in exercise but does not decompose which system (cardiac, pulmonary, musculoskeletal) is limiting, unlike CPET

## Determinants of 6MWD in HF

- Older age, female sex, low BMI, anaemia, elevated resting heart rate, and diabetes associated with reduced 6MWD
- Depression independently associated with reduced 6MWD
- Reduced estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) an independent determinant of shorter 6MWD
- NT-proBNP a significant but comparatively weak predictor of 6MWD (weaker than for peak VO₂)
- In HFrEF: lower LVEF, longer total isovolumic time, higher Tei index, larger left atrial dimensions/LV mass, higher mitral E/A and E/E′ ratios associated with reduced 6MWD; on multivariate analysis only E/E′ and total isovolumic time were independent predictors
- In hypertensive heart disease with preserved systolic function: increased E/E′ and decreased E-wave deceleration time independently predicted abnormal 6MWD
- Peripheral vascular function (ventricular-arterial coupling, aortic elastic properties) also affects submaximal exercise capacity independent of cardiac function

## Prognostic Role

- 6MWD ≤300m generally indicative of poor prognosis; 6MWD <200m identifies markedly increased mortality risk in stable HF
- Reduced 6MWD associated with increased mortality, nonfatal cardiovascular events, and HF hospitalisations, mainly in NYHA II–III HFrEF, with prognostic accuracy similar to peak VO₂
- A stable 6MWD over 1 year associated with improved survival
- A meta-analysis of HF trials found only a **low level of association** between 6MWD improvement and mortality/hospitalisation, and only moderate correlation between 6MWD and quality of life; the magnitude of 6MWD improvement needed to show a QoL benefit (~80m) is higher than the 30–50m increase typically seen in trials showing morbidity/mortality benefit
- In acutely decompensated HF, decreased 6MWD near discharge is among the strongest independent predictors of long-term mortality and HF hospitalisation (not consistent across all studies)
- Variation in 6MWD in stable HF patients over 6–12 months has been reported to be as low as ~36m, relevant to interpreting clinically important change

## Use in Assessing Interventions

- Contradictory results across studies for ACE inhibitors and beta-blockers (mostly no significant 6MWD improvement); cardiac resynchronization therapy (CRT) improved 6MWD in the majority of studies, and baseline 6MWD <350m identified patients deriving the greatest mortality benefit from CRT
- IV iron supplementation and sacubitril/valsartan associated with ~35–40m 6MWD improvement, though not directly linked to prognosis in the cited studies
- Transcatheter mitral/aortic valve interventions improved 6MWT performance; MitraClip meta-analysis reported a mean ~100m increase in 6MWD
- Contrasted with pulmonary arterial hypertension, where 6MWT is an established regulatory endpoint that has led to therapy approvals — the authors describe its role in HF trials as comparatively "questionable"

## Limitations Noted by Authors

- No standardisation on whether 6MWD change should be reported as absolute value, percentage change, or percent-predicted
- Numerous non-standardised reference equations for predicted 6MWD exist, with substantial cross-study variation
- Authors explicitly call for future studies to standardise 6MWT methodology and to establish its prognostic role in HF with **either reduced or preserved** ejection fraction
