# Sex-Specific Effects of Sac/Val in PARAGLIDE-HF

> Pre-specified sex analysis of PARAGLIDE-HF (n=466; 52% women, 48% men): Sac/Val produced numerically similar NT-proBNP reduction in women (ratio 0.86, 95% CI 0.69–1.07) and men (ratio 0.84, 95% CI 0.67–1.05) with no sex-treatment interaction (P=0.908); win ratio also similar (women 1.17, men 1.22); but women had significantly more symptomatic hypotension with Sac/Val (OR 2.29, 95% CI 1.20–4.40, P=0.012).

## Core Arguments

**Study:** Pre-specified sex subgroup analysis of PARAGLIDE-HF (NCT03988634). N=**466** (242 women [52%], 224 men [48%]).

**Baseline sex differences:**
- Women: higher LVEF (27% with LVEF >60% vs 19% men; P=0.004), worse renal function (eGFR 46.5 vs 53.5 mL/min/1.73m², P<0.001), less MI history (3.3% vs 8.5%, P=0.017), more atrial fibrillation (55.0% vs 45.9% ongoing, P=0.011)
- NT-proBNP, SBP (~128 mmHg both), and medications were similar between sexes

**Primary endpoint — time-averaged NT-proBNP change (weeks 4&8):**
- Women (Sac/Val vs Val): ratio = **0.86 (95% CI 0.69–1.07)**
- Men (Sac/Val vs Val): ratio = **0.84 (95% CI 0.67–1.05)**
- **P-interaction = 0.908** — no sex-treatment interaction; benefit consistent across sexes
- Week 4 specifically: women showed significant reduction (ratio 0.74, CI 0.60–0.93, P=0.009); men NS (0.81, P=0.063)

**Secondary — hierarchical win ratio:**
- Women: unmatched WR = **1.17 (95% CI 0.83–1.63)**, P=0.369
- Men: unmatched WR = **1.22 (95% CI 0.86–1.75)**, P=0.265
- Homogeneity test P=0.842 — no sex-treatment interaction

**Safety — symptomatic hypotension (SH):**
- Women (Sac/Val vs Val): **OR 2.29 (95% CI 1.20–4.40), P=0.012** — significantly more SH
- Men (Sac/Val vs Val): OR 1.26 (0.65–2.48), P=0.494 — non-significant
- Sex-by-SH interaction P=0.213 — formally not significant, but numerically important sex-specific safety signal

**Renal function:**
- Men with Sac/Val: significantly lower worsening renal function vs Val (OR 0.52, CI 0.28–0.95, P=0.034)
- Women: trend toward benefit (OR 0.71, CI 0.40–1.26), non-significant

**Mechanism for women's SH excess:** Women had higher LVEF (smaller, stiffer LV) and worse baseline renal function — both risk factors for SH identified in [[fudim2024paraglide]]. High-LVEF patients cannot tolerate PCWP reduction without LV underfilling.

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

PARAGLIDE-HF sex analysis shows sex-consistent Sac/Val benefit, contrasting with PARAGON-HF where women (especially LVEF ≤57%) appeared to benefit substantially more than men. This difference likely reflects population distinctions: WHF vs stable HFpEF, LVEF distribution (PARAGLIDE-HF median LVEF 55%), and baseline BP differences. The symptomatic hypotension excess in women (OR 2.29) is clinically actionable: careful pre-treatment SBP and LVEF assessment is indicated, especially for women with LVEF >60%.
