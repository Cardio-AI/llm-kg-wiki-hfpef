# Exercise Hemodynamics Enhance Diagnosis of Early HFpEF

> In patients with exertional dyspnea, normal BNP, and normal resting hemodynamics, invasive exercise testing unmasked HFpEF in 58% — a population missed entirely by all noninvasive diagnostic criteria.

**File:** `raw/2010-CircHeartFail-Borlaug-Exercise_Hemodynamics.pdf` · **Authors:** Borlaug BA, Nishimura RA, Sorajja P, Lam CSP, Redfield MM · **Year:** 2010 · **Journal:** Circ Heart Fail  
**Study type:** Retrospective observational · **N:** 55 · **Population:** Exertional dyspnea, EF ≥50%, normal BNP, normal resting hemodynamics; Mayo Clinic catheterization laboratory, Aug 2005–Aug 2009  
**Follow-up:** Single-session hemodynamic assessment  
**Intervention/Exposure:** Invasive right ± left heart catheterization with supine cycle ergometry or arm exercise  
**Primary outcome:** Exercise PCWP ≥25 mmHg (HFpEF classification)

---

## Key Findings

- 32/55 patients (58%) classified as HFpEF by exercise PCWP ≥25 mmHg at peak exercise
- All resting hemodynamics were within normal limits in both groups (HFpEF and noncardiac dyspnea [NCD])
- The hemodynamic gap between groups emerged within the first 1.5 minutes of exercise at 20W; 80–90% of peak PCWP rise occurred at this low workload
- Exercise-induced pulmonary hypertension (mean PAP >30 mmHg) was present in **88%** of HFpEF patients; caused by pulmonary venous hypertension (PVRI dropped equally in both groups), not pulmonary vascular disease
- Exercise PCWP was strongly correlated with exercise PASP (R²=0.76, P<0.0001)
- **Exercise PASP ≥45 mmHg** identified HFpEF with **96% sensitivity and 95% specificity** (AUC 0.99)
- All noninvasive markers (BNP, E/e' ratio, ESC algorithm) had AUC <0.70 — none adequate for distinguishing HFpEF from NCD

## Methods (brief)

Exclusion criteria: elevated BNP/NT-proBNP (>200/>220 pg/mL), significant CAD (≥50% stenosis), valvular disease, HCM, constrictive pericarditis, exercise-induced PH from pulmonary vascular disease, or radiographic congestion. Resting PCWP <15 mmHg and mean PAP <25 mmHg required. Exercise: supine cycle ergometry (20W increments) or arm adduction (4-lb weights). High-fidelity micromanometer pressures used in most patients.

## Results

| Parameter | NCD (n=23) | HFpEF (n=32) | P |
|---|---|---|---|
| Age, y | 47±17 | 65±13 | <0.001 |
| BMI, kg/m² | 27.3±5.5 | 32.0±5.9 | 0.004 |
| Resting mean PAP, mmHg | 15±4 | 19±4 | 0.001 |
| Resting PCWP, mmHg | 9±3 | 11±2 | 0.002 |
| Exercise PCWP, mmHg | 13±5 | 32±6 | <0.00001 |
| Exercise mean PAP, mmHg | 23±5 | 43±7 | <0.00001 |
| Exercise CI, L/min/m² | 6.7±1.4 | 4.9±1.0 | 0.0001 |
| Exercise-induced PH, % | — | 88 | — |

Cardiac output augmentation with exercise was blunted in HFpEF (lower heart rate response, lower CI increase). Greater hypertensive BP response and less systemic vasodilation in HFpEF. Passive leg elevation before exercise showed immediate PCWP rise: +7±3 mmHg in HFpEF vs. +2±3 mmHg in NCD (P<0.0001) — consistent with inability to use Frank-Starling mechanism.

## Limitations

Retrospective single-centre referral cohort; not randomly selected. Testing was supine — results may not apply to upright exercise. Exercise type and cardiac output measurement method not uniform. Resting PCWP 9–11 mmHg both groups — subtle group difference within normal range. Sensitivity analyses using PCWP ≥15, ≥18, ≥20 mmHg as HFpEF threshold all showed similar between-group differences.
