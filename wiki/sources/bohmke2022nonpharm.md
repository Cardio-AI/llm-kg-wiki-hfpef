# Nonpharmacological Strategies for Heart Failure With Preserved Ejection Fraction

> Comprehensive narrative review of exercise training modalities (MCT, HIIT, combined resistance/aerobic, IMT) and dietary interventions (caloric restriction, sodium restriction, Mediterranean diet, DASH, malnutrition management) in HFpEF; introduces IMT (inspiratory muscle training) as a modality distinct from standard aerobic training with a unique mechanism operating on respiratory muscle oxygen demand.

**Full citation:**
Bohmke NJ, Billingsley HE, Kirkman DL, Carbone S. Nonpharmacological Strategies in Heart Failure With Preserved Ejection Fraction. *Cardiol Clin.* 2022;40(4):491–506. doi:[10.1016/j.ccl.2022.06.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ccl.2022.06.003)

## Core Arguments

### Exercise Training Modalities

**MCT (Moderate Continuous Training)**
- Kitzman 2010: first RCT of MCT in HFpEF; 16 weeks; VO₂peak +2.7 mL/kg/min; primary mechanism: peripheral skeletal muscle (A-VO2 improvement), not cardiac
- SECRET trial (Kitzman): 20-week MCT arm; VO₂peak +1.2 mL/kg/min (above caloric restriction arm); combination MCT + caloric restriction additive; −7% body weight in CR arm; CR-mediated VO₂ improvement driven by fat mass and LV mass reduction
- HEART Camp: 18-month supervised MCT; HFpEF subgroup n=59; 6MWT +63.25 m; adherence-dependent effects

**HIIT (High-Intensity Interval Training)**
- Head-to-head vs. MCT: two small-to-moderate RCTs
  - Small pilot: HIIT +1.8 mL/kg/min vs. MCT −0.1 mL/kg/min (small N, likely underpowered)
  - 12-week trial: HIIT +3.5 vs. MCT +1.9 mL/kg/min — both improved; HIIT numerically greater but no statistical difference
- **OPTIMEX-CLIN** (N=180, 3-arm HIIT vs. MCT vs. control; 3-month and 12-month follow-up): HIIT = MCT at both time points — no superiority established; largest head-to-head trial to date
- Conclusion: HIIT not superior to MCT overall; no identified subgroup definitively benefits from HIIT; MCT maintains better evidence base and adherence profile

**Combined Resistance + Aerobic Training**
- EX-DHF pilot (Germany): 12-week combined resistance + aerobic training; VO₂peak +2.6 mL/kg/min; improved LAVI (echocardiographic diastolic marker); no pharmacological comparison arm
- 12-month progressive trial: VO₂peak +5.3 mL/kg/min; LV end-systolic volume improved; largest SET VO₂ gain in literature — may reflect longer duration and progressive loading protocol

**IMT (Inspiratory Muscle Training) — new modality for wiki**
IMT trains respiratory muscles specifically, reducing the fraction of cardiac output diverted to diaphragm and respiratory musculature during exertion; potentially redirects perfusion to locomotor muscles.
- **Palau 2014** (crossover RCT, n=26): IMT at 25–30% MIP (maximum inspiratory pressure) for 12 weeks; VO₂peak +2.9 mL/kg/min (P=0.02); no significant changes in echocardiographic parameters (LAVI, E/e'); mechanism is peripheral (respiratory muscle efficiency), not cardiac
- **Kinugasa 2020** (at-home programme, n=17): 30% MIP × 24 weeks; improved anaerobic threshold and % predicted MIP; accessible format — no supervised facility required
- IMT magnitude of VO₂ benefit (~+2.9 mL/kg/min) comparable to standard MCT; standout feature is home delivery without specialist supervision

### Dietary Interventions

**Caloric Restriction (CR)**
- SECRET trial: combined MCT + caloric restriction vs. MCT alone vs. CR alone; CR arm: −7% body weight; VO₂peak +1.3 mL/kg/min; KCCQ improved (secondary endpoint); LV mass decreased; effects driven by reduction in fat mass (including intermuscular fat) → improved skeletal muscle oxygen utilisation
- CR mechanism: reduction in adipose-related inflammation + reduced intermuscular fat infiltration → skeletal muscle metabolic efficiency

**Malnutrition Management**
- PICNIC trial (nutritional supplementation in hospitalised HF): HR 0.45 for composite clinical endpoint at 12 months; applicable to HFpEF subgroup
- EFFORT trial HF subgroup: adjusted OR 0.44 for 30-day mortality with nutritional intervention; signals that undernutrition correction has near-term mortality benefit
- Caveat: malnutrition trials are not HFpEF-specific; LVEF-stratified data sparse

**Sodium Restriction**
- SODIUM-HF (NCT02012179, N=806, LVEF ~36% — predominantly HFrEF): neutral on primary composite; limited HFpEF-specific interpretation
- One inpatient HFpEF-specific sodium restriction trial (N=53): neutral on weight change; weak evidence
- Evidence base for sodium restriction in HFpEF remains insufficient to support strong recommendations

**Dietary Patterns**
- Mediterranean diet (MedDiet): observational signals in HFpEF (reduced HF incidence); no HFpEF RCT; mechanistic rationale via anti-inflammatory pathway
- DASH + low sodium: small trials (not HFpEF-specific) showed ↓BP, ↓arterial stiffness, improved diastolic function markers — mechanistically plausible but no powered HFpEF RCT
- **UFA-Preserved pilot** (omega-3/unsaturated fatty acid supplementation, N=9): exploratory; precursor to UFA-Preserved 2 (NCT03966755)

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

- Adds **IMT** as a distinct fourth exercise modality not previously in wiki; has RCT evidence with VO₂ effect comparable to MCT and home-delivery advantage
- Provides individual trial data supporting the [[supervised-exercise-training]] meta-analytic summary (Sachdev 2023): Kitzman 2010, EX-DHF, HEART Camp, OPTIMEX-CLIN, SECRET are key individual studies
- SECRET trial detail: establishes caloric restriction as an independent, additive intervention to MCT — relevant to [[exercise-intolerance]] peripheral mechanism (fat mass → skeletal muscle function)
- OPTIMEX-CLIN provides the largest head-to-head HIIT vs. MCT data, confirming no HIIT superiority
- Dietary intervention section fills a gap: [[hfpef-treatment-gap]] and [[exercise-intolerance]] previously covered pharmacology and SET; diet was underrepresented
