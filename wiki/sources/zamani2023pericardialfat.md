# Zamani 2023 — Pericardial Fat and LV Eccentricity Index in Obese HFpEF

> In 28 obese HFpEF patients (NCT04068844), epicardial fat correlated strongly with LV eccentricity index (r=0.88; P<0.001) and paracardial fat with LV eccentricity (r=0.91; P<0.001), while BMI, subcutaneous fat, and visceral fat did **not** correlate; 13/28 had abnormal cardio-mechanical interaction (LV eccentricity >1.0), and exercise did not worsen it at 30W.

## Key Findings

- **Epicardial fat (EAT) vs. LV eccentricity index:** r=0.88, P<0.001
- **Paracardial fat vs. LV eccentricity index:** r=0.91, P<0.001
- **NOT correlated with LV eccentricity:** BMI (r≈0), subcutaneous fat (NS), visceral fat (NS)
- **Abnormal cardio-mechanical interaction (LV eccentricity >1.0):** Present in 13/28 patients (46%)
- **Exercise:** LV eccentricity did NOT worsen at 30W exercise intensity — pericardial restraint is present at rest but not exacerbated by low-intensity exercise in this small cohort

### Clinical Interpretation
- LV eccentricity index >1.0 indicates septal flattening due to pericardial restraint — the RV is constrained by pericardium, pushing on LV septum
- Pericardial restraint from EAT specifically (not systemic or subcutaneous fat) is the mechanistic link
- Paracardial fat (outside pericardium) also correlated — both compartments contribute to pericardial constraint

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

This brief but highly cited finding pinpoints pericardial/epicardial fat as the specific adipose depot mechanistically relevant to cardiac compression in obese HFpEF — not BMI or systemic adiposity. Provides direct imaging evidence for the pericardial restraint hypothesis (compression of LV by enlarged pericardial fat). Directly relevant to [[timoteo2024eat]] (comprehensive EAT review) and the pericardiotomy pilot study also published by the same group. The finding that exercise (at 30W) does not worsen LV eccentricity is preliminary and limited by small N.
