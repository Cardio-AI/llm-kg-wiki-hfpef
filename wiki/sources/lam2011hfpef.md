# Epidemiology and Clinical Course of HFpEF (Lam 2011)

> HFpEF constitutes approximately 50% of all heart failure cases with rising prevalence; all-cause mortality approaches that of HFrEF (~20–30% per year), yet no treatment had been proven to reduce mortality at the time of publication.

**Full citation:**
Lam CSP, Donal E, Kraigher-Krainer E, Vasan RS. Epidemiology and clinical course of heart failure with preserved ejection fraction. *Eur J Heart Fail.* 2011;13(1):18–28. doi:[10.1093/eurjhf/hfq121](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurjhf/hfq121)

## Core Arguments

**Prevalence and trends:** HFpEF constitutes approximately 50% of all heart failure cases in community-based studies. Population-based registries (Olmsted County, Framingham Heart Study) demonstrate HFpEF increasing at approximately 1% per year, driven by aging populations and rising rates of hypertension, obesity, and diabetes. HFrEF prevalence remained relatively stable or declined owing to improved post-MI care. The rising prevalence-of-HFpEF relative to HFrEF was already apparent by the mid-2000s.

**Clinical profile:** HFpEF patients are older, more frequently female (~60%), and carry higher rates of hypertension (~70%), atrial fibrillation (~35%), and obesity (~50%) compared to HFrEF. Coronary artery disease and prior MI are less prevalent than in HFrEF. Most patients present with NYHA class II–III dyspnea on exertion.

**Prognosis:** Annual all-cause mortality is approximately 20–29% in hospital-based cohorts — similar to HFrEF. The MAGGIC meta-analysis ([[pocock2013maggic]]) later confirmed overlapping mortality curves across EF subtypes, though community data suggests slightly lower short-term mortality for HFpEF. Quality of life is substantially impaired; HFpEF contributes disproportionately to HF hospitalisation burden due to its higher overall prevalence. Hospitalisation rates of approximately 30% at 6 months are reported.

**Natural history:** Diastolic dysfunction progresses over time — from impaired relaxation (grade I) to pseudo-normal and restrictive filling (grades II–III). Elevated filling pressures drive pulmonary hypertension, ultimately producing right ventricular failure in advanced stages. The disease progression spectrum (LA → PH → RV) described here was later formalised in [[borlaug2023statement]].

**Treatment landscape (2011):** No randomised trial had demonstrated a mortality benefit. Diuretics for symptom control, blood pressure treatment, and rate control for AF were the only evidence-based interventions. CHARM-Preserved and I-PRESERVE were neutral; TOPCAT and RELAX were ongoing.

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

This paper establishes the epidemiological baseline for HFpEF — the scale of the problem and its natural history — as of 2011. It provides the foundational "no proven treatment" framing that motivates all subsequent trial activity in this wiki. The rising prevalence data and the similar mortality to HFrEF are among the most frequently cited justifications for HFpEF research prioritisation.

The clinical profile described (older, female, hypertensive, obese) directly aligns with the [[paulus2013novelparadigm]] comorbidity-driven paradigm and with the two-layer phenotype model in [[hfpef-phenotype-profiling]].
