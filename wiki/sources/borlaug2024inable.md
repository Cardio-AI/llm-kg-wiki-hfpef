# INABLE-Training

> Double-blind RCT (N=73) of inorganic sodium nitrite (40 mg TID) vs. placebo added to 12 weeks of supervised exercise training in HFpEF (NCT02713126); exercise training itself significantly improved peak VO₂, KCCQ-OSS, and 6MWD; nitrite added no benefit on any endpoint (ΔVO₂peak −0.13 mL/kg/min; P=0.77), extending the consistent pattern of NO/cGMP pathway failure in HFpEF.

**Full citation:**
Borlaug BA, Koepp KE, Melenovsky V, et al. Inorganic Nitrite Added to Exercise Training in Heart Failure With Preserved Ejection Fraction: The INABLE-Training Randomized Trial. *Mayo Clin Proc.* 2024;99(2):206–217. doi:10.1016/j.mayocp.2023.08.031

---

## Core Arguments

1. **Exercise training works in HFpEF.** Both the nitrite and placebo groups improved significantly from baseline: peak VO₂ +0.79 mL/kg/min (95% CI 0.34–1.24; P<0.001), KCCQ-OSS +5.5 (P<0.001), 6MWD +34 m (P<0.001). These within-group effects confirm exercise training as an effective intervention even in this high-risk, predominantly NYHA III, rural, older population.

2. **Nitrite augmentation failed completely.** Compared to exercise alone, nitrite produced no additional improvement in peak VO₂ (between-group difference −0.13 mL/kg/min; 95% CI −1.03 to 0.76; P=0.77), KCCQ-OSS, 6MWD, or any secondary endpoint. This null result held regardless of baseline NT-proBNP, prior HHF, sex, or NYHA class.

3. **The NO/cGMP target hypothesis is pharmacologically addressed but clinically inert in HFpEF.** Nitrite is converted to NO in hypoxic exercising muscle — the mechanistic rationale was that augmenting local NO production during exercise would improve vasodilation and O₂ delivery to myopathic skeletal muscle. The failure to improve peak VO₂ despite confirmed NO augmentation (validated by plasma nitrite levels) suggests skeletal muscle myopathy in HFpEF is not primarily NO-limited.

4. **Rural, high-comorbidity population.** 63% rural; 75.3% NYHA III; AF 58.9%; T2DM 42.5%; mean age 72.4 years. This population profile differs from earlier HFpEF exercise trials (e.g., SECRET, Kitzman 2010) and demonstrates that exercise training benefits extend to more severely symptomatic, older, rural patients.

(source: Borlaug2024INABLE)

## Trial Design

**Full title:** Inorganic Nitrite Added to Beneficial L-carnitine Exercise Training in HFpEF  
**NCT:** NCT02713126  
**PI/Sponsor:** Barry Borlaug, Mayo Clinic  
**Design:** Double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-arm RCT  
**Intervention:** Inorganic sodium nitrite 40 mg TID (three times daily) vs. matching placebo, initiated at exercise program start and continued for the 12-week duration; exercise training delivered to both groups (MCT protocol, 3×/week supervised sessions)  
**Population:** HFpEF (LVEF ≥50%), NYHA class II–IV, peak VO₂ <20 mL/kg/min, NT-proBNP ≥100 pg/mL or NP ≥500 at prior HHF  
**Enrolled:** N=92; N=73 in primary analysis set (37 nitrite, 36 placebo); 19 withdrew or did not complete training  
**Primary endpoint:** Change in peak VO₂ from baseline to 12 weeks  
**Key secondary endpoints:** KCCQ-OSS, 6MWD, rest/exercise hemodynamics (invasive subset), NT-proBNP  
**Publication:** Mayo Clin Proc 2024;99(2):206–217

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

INABLE-Training is the most direct test of combined NO augmentation + exercise in HFpEF. It sits at the intersection of two major HFpEF treatment axes:

- **NO/cGMP pathway:** INABLE-Training is the fifth major negative trial targeting this axis in HFpEF (after NEAT-HFpEF isosorbide mononitrate, INDIE-HFpEF KNO₃, RELAX sildenafil, SOCRATES-PRESERVED vericiguat, VITALITY-HFpEF vericiguat). The consistent pattern across mechanistically distinct agents — organic nitrates, inorganic nitrate, PDE5i, sGC stimulators, and now inorganic nitrite with exercise — establishes that NO pathway upregulation is not a sufficient therapeutic strategy in HFpEF regardless of delivery route.

- **Exercise training:** Confirms exercise benefit in a higher-acuity, older, rural HFpEF cohort. The within-group VO₂ and KCCQ improvements are consistent with the broader meta-analysis evidence (Sachdev AHA Scientific Statement 2023: +2.8 mL/kg/min). This extends the evidence base to NYHA III-predominant patients — a population underrepresented in earlier exercise trials.
