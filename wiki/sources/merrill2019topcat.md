# TOPCAT Sex Differences Secondary Analysis (Merrill 2019)

> In TOPCAT (N=3,445), women with HFpEF had higher LVEF (~63% vs. ~58%), smaller LV volumes, more hypertension, and less CAD than men; the spironolactone treatment effect on the primary composite endpoint did not differ significantly by sex (P-interaction=0.26), indicating that MRA response is not sex-stratified in HFpEF.

**Full citation:**
Merrill M, Sweitzer NK, Lindenfeld J, Kao DP. Sex Differences in Outcomes and Responses to Spironolactone in Heart Failure With Preserved Ejection Fraction: A Secondary Analysis of TOPCAT Trial. *JACC Heart Fail.* 2019;7(3):228–238. doi:[10.1016/j.jchf.2018.11.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jchf.2018.11.004)

## Core Arguments

**Population:** Full TOPCAT cohort (N=3,445; Americas + Russia/Georgia sites), approximately 52% women.

**Clinical characteristics by sex:**
- **Women:** mean LVEF ~63% vs. men ~58%; smaller LV end-diastolic volume; more hypertension (~74% vs. ~69%); less CAD (~33% vs. ~53%); more frequently in sinus rhythm; older at enrolment; higher rates of non-ischaemic aetiology
- **Men:** higher rates of prior MI, CAD, and borderline-HFrEF morphology; more CRT; more ICD use

**Outcomes by sex (placebo arm):**
- Women had similar or slightly better event rates vs. men for the primary composite (CV death, HF hospitalisation, aborted cardiac arrest)
- All-cause mortality was higher in men
- Sex did not independently predict the primary endpoint after multivariate adjustment

**Spironolactone response by sex:**
- Sex × treatment interaction: **P=0.26** (not significant)
- Neither sex showed a statistically significant treatment benefit in the full cohort (recall TOPCAT was overall neutral; the Americas subgroup was positive for both sexes)
- No statistically meaningful sex-specific advantage or disadvantage for spironolactone

**Biological context:** Women's higher LVEF in HFpEF reflects greater LV concentricity, more interstitial fibrosis relative to cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, estrogen effects on titin phosphorylation, and smaller LV volumes — a pattern repeatedly demonstrated ([[beale2018sex]], [[beale2019sex]]). This biological distinctiveness does not translate to differential MRA responsiveness.

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

This paper provides the TOPCAT empirical sex-differences characterisation within a major HFpEF RCT. The HFpEF female phenotype (higher LVEF, more hypertension, less CAD) is now incorporated into phenotype profiling ([[hfpef-phenotype-profiling]]) and informs the sex-subgroup signals seen with ARNI therapy ([[solomon2019paragon]], PARAGON-HF). The finding that spironolactone response does not differ by sex removes MRA therapy from the list of sex-differential interventions in HFpEF.

Contrast: PARAGON-HF women showed a directional signal for sacubitril/valsartan benefit not seen in men — a different drug with a different mechanism, and a different trial population.

## Contradictions
PARAGON-HF women showed directional sacubitril/valsartan benefit; TOPCAT shows no sex interaction for spironolactone. Not a contradiction — different drugs, mechanisms, and populations. Both findings can coexist if sex biology specifically modifies ARNI but not MRA response.
