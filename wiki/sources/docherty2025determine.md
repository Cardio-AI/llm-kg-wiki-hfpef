# DETERMINE Accelerometry Substudy

> In the DETERMINE trials, wearable accelerometer measures of physical activity correlated only weakly with KCCQ scores (r=0.06–0.21) and weakly-to-moderately with 6MWD (r=0.20–0.31), indicating that accelerometers, 6MWD, and patient-reported outcomes measure distinct dimensions of functional limitation in heart failure.

## Key Findings

- 319/817 patients (39%) had adequate baseline accelerometer data (≥10 hr/day wear for ≥3 days)
- **Baseline correlations:**
  - Accelerometer vs. KCCQ summary scores: r=0.06–0.21 (weak)
  - Accelerometer vs. 6MWD: r=0.20–0.31 (weak to moderate)
- **Change correlations (baseline to 16 weeks):**
  - Accelerometer change vs. KCCQ change: r=0.00–0.18 (weak/absent)
  - Accelerometer change vs. 6MWD change: r=0.01–0.10 (negligible)
- Patients with lower accelerometer activity had: higher NT-proBNP, worse KCCQ, lower 6MWD, more AF, more T2DM, higher BMI, more NYHA III
- Physical activity was similar across LVEF subgroups (≤40%, 41–49%, ≥50%)
- Accelerometer measures: movement intensity, step count, vector magnitude units (VMU), time in light-to-vigorous physical activity (LVPA)

## Methods (brief)

- Waist-worn triaxial seismic accelerometer (Dynaport MoveMonitor, McRoberts) at 3 time points: baseline, weeks 8 and 14
- Pre-specified minimum wear: ≥10 hr/day for ≥3 days per 7-day period
- 10 countries: Argentina, Brazil, Bulgaria, Canada, Denmark, Italy, Slovakia, Sweden, US, South Africa
- DETERMINE-Pooled accelerometer cohort: mean age 70.1y; 34% female; mean LVEF 44.2%; median NT-proBNP 855 pg/mL; NYHA II 81%

## Implications

- Supports the need for multiple outcome domains in HFpEF trials — no single measure captures the full picture
- Casts doubt on 6MWD as a surrogate for real-world physical activity
- Objective accelerometry and subjective KCCQ capture complementary, not overlapping, information
- Large proportion of enrolled patients (61%) had insufficient accelerometer wear — feasibility remains a challenge
