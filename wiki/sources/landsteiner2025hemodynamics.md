# HFpEF Definitions Unveiled by Rest and Exercise Hemodynamics

> Societal HFpEF guidelines and pivotal clinical trial entry criteria identify overlapping but non-equivalent patient populations; exercise hemodynamics uncover 23–28% of hemodynamically confirmed HFpEF not captured by resting measurements, while trial NT-proBNP thresholds exclude 55–83% of physiologically confirmed HFpEF patients.

**Full citation:**
Landsteiner I, Ikoma T, Ramesh A, Campain J, Cohen LP, Hardin CC, Malhotra R, Lewis GD. Implications of HFpEF Definitions Unveiled by Rest and Exercise Hemodynamics. *Circ Res.* 2025;137(4):357–359. doi:[10.1161/CIRCRESAHA.125.326504](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCRESAHA.125.326504) [DOI unverified]
**Study type:** Retrospective referral cohort · **N:** 872 · **Population:** Exertional dyspnea, LVEF ≥50%, Mass General Brigham referral cohort  
**Follow-up:** Clinical outcomes (combined all-cause mortality and CV events)  
**Intervention/Exposure:** Comprehensive phenotyping: right heart catheterization, invasive CPET with upright exercise, NT-proBNP, echocardiogram  
**Primary outcome:** Prevalence of hemodynamically confirmed HFpEF (HC-HFpEF) under different definitions; prognostic comparison across hemodynamic profiles

---

## Key Findings

**HC-HFpEF definition:** Resting supine PCWP ≥15 mmHg OR exercise PCWP/cardiac output slope >2 mmHg/L/min (derived from 10.4±2.7 minute-by-minute measurements during upright exercise)

**Societal guideline coverage:**
- 74–77% of the cohort met any of 3 societal definitions (AHA, ESC, Universal)
- But only 61–75% of those meeting societal definitions also had HC-HFpEF
- Exercise hemodynamics identified HC-HFpEF in **23–28% of patients not captured by resting hemodynamics alone**

**Four hemodynamic profiles:**
1. Normal rPCWP + normal exPCWP/CO — reference group
2. High rPCWP + normal exPCWP/CO
3. Normal rPCWP + high exPCWP/CO (exercise-unmasked HFpEF)
4. High rPCWP + high exPCWP/CO — highest-risk group

**Prognostic value of exercise PCWP/CO slope:**
- Elevated exercise PCWP/CO slope with normal resting PCWP: HR 1.42 (95% CI 1.08–1.86, P=0.012)
- Elevated exercise PCWP/CO slope with elevated resting PCWP: HR 2.07 (95% CI 1.58–2.71, P=1.5×10⁻⁷)

**Clinical trial enrichment analysis (Figure D):**
- Among all 872 patients: 28% met STEP-HFpEF, 13% FINEARTS-HF, 13% EMPEROR-Preserved, 11% PARAGON-HF criteria
- Among those meeting trial criteria: 87–90% had HC-HFpEF — trials successfully enriched for physiologic HFpEF
- **BUT:** among HC-HFpEF (n=485), 55–83% did NOT meet trial entry criteria
- Most common exclusion: NT-proBNP below trial threshold — excluded **67–71%** of HC-HFpEF from PARAGON-HF, EMPEROR-Preserved, and FINEARTS-HF
- STEP-HFpEF: 35% excluded by BMI <30 threshold
- Trial-eligible patients had lower pVO2 and higher event rates — trials enriched for sicker, more severe end of HFpEF

## Methods (brief)

Upright exercise hemodynamics (distinct from supine protocol in Borlaug 2010). PCWP/CO slope used rather than absolute PCWP threshold for exercise assessment. Cox proportional hazards models adjusted for age, sex, BMI, NT-proBNP.

## Limitations

Referral-based cohort — may not generalise to community HFpEF populations. Upright exercise hemodynamics used (may differ from supine measurements; reclassification issues noted vs. supine protocols). No interventional data — purely observational with outcomes follow-up.

## Contradictions

NT-proBNP thresholds used as trial entry criteria exclude 67–71% of patients with hemodynamically confirmed HFpEF (HC-HFpEF) — particularly problematic given known NP suppression in obesity (STEP-HFpEF used BMI ≥30 + hemodynamic criteria instead, excluding only 35% of HC-HFpEF). Trial-enrolled patients are sicker and more severely affected than the broader HC-HFpEF population, meaning trial results may not apply to the majority of patients with physiologically confirmed disease.
