# Leahy 2025 — Dynamic Hyperinflation and Exercise PCWP in HFpEF

> In 55 obese HFpEF patients (NCT04068844), 62% developed dynamic hyperinflation (DH) at 20W exercise and 85% at peak exercise; DH patients had significantly higher exercise PCWP at 20W (23±8 vs. 16±6 mmHg; P=0.005) and peak (38±11 vs. 28±6 mmHg; P=0.007), and magnitude of hyperinflation (ΔEELV) correlated with ΔPCWP (r²=0.167 at peak; P=0.002) — suggesting obesity-driven ventilatory mechanics contribute to elevated exercise filling pressures.

## Key Findings

### Prevalence of Dynamic Hyperinflation
- 20W exercise: **62% of patients** (n=34) had DH
- Peak exercise: **85% of patients** (n=47) had DH
- DH defined as ΔEELV ≥150 mL from rest
- 75% of patients at 20W and 87% at peak had expiratory flow limitation (EFL) — but EFL severity did NOT correlate with PCWP

### DH vs. Typical Inflation: PCWP Comparison

| Timepoint | DH Group | Typical Inflation | P |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rest | 9±4 mmHg | 7±4 mmHg | NS |
| 20W exercise | **23±8 mmHg** | **16±6 mmHg** | **0.005** |
| Peak exercise | **38±11 mmHg** | **28±6 mmHg** | **0.007** |

### Correlation of ΔEELV with ΔPCWP
- 20W: r²=0.112 (P=0.013)
- Peak: r²=0.167 (P=0.002)
- Also correlates with ΔmPAP and ΔtransPulmonary pressure

### No Association of EFL Severity with PCWP
- EFL severity (% tidal volume) was NOT significantly associated with PCWP at 20W or peak
- DH and EFL are distinct phenomena with different hemodynamic consequences in HFpEF

### DH Patients: Characteristics
- Higher BMI (median 39 vs. 33 kg/m²; P=0.013)
- Lower FVC, FEV₁, TLC, FEF₂₅₋₇₅ — reduced pulmonary function at baseline
- Shorter height
- No difference in age, NT-proBNP, LVEF

## Mechanism
- Dynamic hyperinflation → increased end-expiratory lung volume → increased positive intrathoracic pressure → extracardiac compression of pulmonary vasculature → elevated measured (atmospheric-referenced) PCWP
- This is external cardiac compression by hyperinflated lungs; actual LV transmural pressure (LV-pleural pressure) is less elevated
- Synergistic with poor LV compliance (cardiac cause of elevated PCWP in HFpEF)
- Obesity is the primary driver: excess abdominal/chest wall mass → EFL → EELV increase during exercise → intrathoracic pressure elevation

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

This study introduces a non-cardiac mechanism for elevated exercise PCWP in obese HFpEF: dynamic hyperinflation from obesity-related ventilatory constraints increases intrathoracic pressure and compresses pulmonary vasculature, raising measured PCWP. This reframes the diagnostic challenge: elevated PCWP during exercise is not purely a marker of diastolic dysfunction — in obese patients, ventilatory mechanics confound the measurement. This is mechanistically linked to [[babb2026ventilatorylimit]] (companion paper showing patients stop at the same ventilatory limit before and after NTG). Together these papers challenge the PCWP-centric paradigm of exercise intolerance in obese HFpEF.

## Contradictions

The finding that PCWP elevation in obese HFpEF is partially driven by external ventilatory mechanics challenges the assumption that elevated exercise PCWP is purely a cardiac/diastolic marker. This has implications for exercise catheterisation as a diagnostic test in obese HFpEF — PCWP may be overestimated. Transmural pressures (corrected for pleural pressure) would be lower.
