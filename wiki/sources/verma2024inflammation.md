# Inflammation and the Effects of Semaglutide in Patients With Heart Failure With Preserved Ejection Fraction: The STEP-HFpEF Program

> Semaglutide reduces CRP by 36% in HFpEF with obesity, but its benefits on symptoms, weight, and exercise capacity are independent of baseline inflammatory burden — suggesting CRP reduction is not the primary mechanistic pathway for symptom improvement.

**File:** `raw/2024-JACC-Verma-STEP-HFpEF_study_inflammation.pdf` · **Authors:** Verma S, Butler J, Bhatt DL, et al. · **Year:** 2024 · **Journal:** JACC 84(17):1646–1662

## Core Arguments

**Study:** Pre-specified secondary analysis of pooled STEP-HFpEF (NCT04788511) + STEP-HFpEF DM (NCT04916470). N=1,145 randomised (semaglutide 2.4 mg SC weekly vs. placebo; 52 weeks). Population: HFpEF (LVEF ≥45%), obesity (BMI ≥30), KCCQ-CSS ≤75.

**Inflammation burden at baseline:**
- Mean CRP: **3.7 mg/L**; **71% had CRP ≥2 mg/L** (elevated systemic inflammation)
- CRP correlated with obesity markers (BMI, waist circumference) but not with HF severity (NT-proBNP, KCCQ)

**Semaglutide effect on inflammation:**
- Overall CRP treatment ratio: **0.64 (95% CI 0.56–0.72), P<0.0001** (36% reduction)
- CRP reduction consistent across all baseline CRP categories (P-interaction >0.28 across subgroups)
- CRP reduction independent of magnitude of weight loss (P-interaction = 0.91 for weight loss–CRP interaction)

**HF benefit by baseline CRP level:**
- KCCQ-CSS improvement, weight loss, 6MWD improvement, and hierarchical composite win ratio all consistent **regardless of baseline CRP category** (all P-interaction >0.28)
- No significant treatment effect modification by baseline CRP for any efficacy endpoint

**Key implication:** Semaglutide's clinical benefit in obese HFpEF is NOT driven primarily by inflammation suppression — patients with and without elevated CRP benefit equally. The mechanism of symptom improvement is likely multifactorial (haemodynamic offloading, visceral fat reduction, cardiac remodelling) rather than anti-inflammatory per se.

## Relevance to HFpEF Wiki

Directly addresses the mechanistic debate about whether GLP-1RA benefit in HFpEF operates through anti-inflammatory pathways (per the Paulus-Tschöpe paradigm of comorbidity-driven myocardial inflammation) or through other mechanisms. The CRP-independence of benefit argues against inflammation as the primary target, challenging the dominant mechanistic narrative. Relevant to phenotype-guided therapy: inflammatory phenotype does not enrich for semaglutide response.
