# HFpEF Timeline

Chronological evolution of HFpEF understanding. Updated on every ingest.

## Epidemiology

| Year | Development | Source |
|------|-------------|--------|
| Pre-2010 | HFpEF prevalence estimated at 40–50% of HF; rising trend noted in community studies | [[savarese2022globalburden]] |
| 2011 | Lam et al. (Eur J Heart Fail 2011; community meta-analysis): HFpEF ~50% of all HF by early 2010s; prevalence rising faster than HFrEF; annual mortality 20–29%/yr — comparable to HFrEF despite preserved systolic function; at time of publication no pharmacological therapy had demonstrated survival benefit — the mortality paradox that defined HFpEF as a therapeutic priority | [[lam2011hfpef]] |
| 2022 | Savarese et al. global burden review: >64 million HF patients worldwide; HFpEF 16–47% of HF by registry (outpatient vs. inpatient range); 5-year mortality comparable to HFrEF (75.7% vs. 75.3%); non-CV mortality higher in HFpEF (30.7% vs. 20.1% at 1y); 63% of HFpEF hospitalisations are non-CV; costs up to €25,532/year in Germany | [[savarese2022globalburden]] |
| 2023 | Anker et al.: 18 comorbidity phenotypes with population-level prevalence estimates; HTN 60–80%, elderly >65y 60–70%, CAD (incl. CMD) 40–70%, female sex 40–50%, iron deficiency 20–50%, obesity 30–40%, T2DM 20–40% | [[anker2023hfpefphenotype]] |

## Pathophysiology

| Year | Development | Source |
|------|-------------|--------|
| Pre-2000s | Diastolic dysfunction recognised as distinct pathophysiological entity from systolic failure; traditional paradigm: pressure overload (HTN) → afterload → diastolic dysfunction — a purely mechanical model | [[pfeffer2019hfpef]] |
| 2013 | Paulus and Tschöpe proposed the comorbidity-driven systemic inflammation paradigm: comorbidities → low-grade inflammation → ↓NO → ↓cGMP → ↓PKG activity → titin hypophosphorylation → ↑myocyte passive stiffness; paradigm shift from pressure overload to microvascular/inflammatory mechanism | [[damario2019cmd]] |
| 2018 | Graziani and Crea proposed CMD as the "common soil" for both microvascular angina and HFpEF; PROMIS-HFpEF (Shah 2018): 91% CMD prevalence in HFpEF (66% endothelium-independent + 24% endothelium-dependent) | [[damario2019cmd]], [[anker2023hfpefphenotype]] |
| 2019 | EndoMT (endothelial-mesenchymal transition) identified as primary driver of HFpEF myocardial fibrosis; titin isoform shift N2BA→N2B described as key cellular stiffness mechanism; calcium overload via late Na⁺ currents links to ranolazine as therapeutic target | [[damario2019cmd]] |
| 2011 | Haykowsky et al. (JACC; n=48 HFpEF vs. 25 HCs; Wake Forest): change in A-VO₂ Diff from rest to peak exercise (A-VO₂ Diff reserve) identified as the strongest independent predictor of peak VO₂ in HFpEF (partial correlate 0.58; β 0.66; P=0.0002) — implicating peripheral noncardiac factors as primary determinants of exercise intolerance, beyond reduced cardiac output | [[haykowsky2011exercise]] |
| 2017 | Reddy et al. (JACC; Borlaug lab; n=98 HFpEF): invasive arterial waveform analysis during exercise demonstrated exercise-unmasked arterial stiffening in HFpEF (higher EaI, lower TACI, increased wave reflections) absent at rest; these indices correlated with exercise PCWP (r=−0.42) and CO (r=−0.66); inorganic sodium nitrite (N=52, 1:1 RCT) partially reversed exertional arterial stiffening and reduced exercise PCWP by 8 mmHg — proof-of-concept for NO-cGMP pathway as therapeutic target | [[reddy2017artstiff]] |
| 2018 | PROMIS-HFpEF (Shah SJ, Eur Heart J; 5 international centres): first prospective multi-centre multinational study of CMD prevalence in guideline-defined HFpEF (N=202); CMD (CFR<2.5 by Doppler echo) present in 75% — highest quality CMD prevalence estimate at that time; CMD correlated with microalbuminuria, NT-proBNP, impaired RV function, but NOT with CRP | [[shah2018promis]] |
| 2019 | Invasive CPET studies establish peripheral skeletal muscle myopathy as primary exercise limiter (A-VO₂ diff >50% of VO₂ deficit); cardiac filling pressure elevation is real but secondary | [[ho2019hfpefdefinitions]], [[sachdev2023exercise]] |
| 2021 | ESC Guidelines formalised echocardiographic and invasive haemodynamic criteria for [[diastolic-dysfunction]]; PCWP ≥15 mmHg rest / ≥25 mmHg exercise as gold standard | [[mcdonagh2021esc]] |
| 2023 | Sachdev AHA Scientific Statement formalised the four-mechanism model of [[exercise-intolerance]]: skeletal muscle (primary) > cardiac > pulmonary vascular > peripheral vascular | [[sachdev2023exercise]] |
| 2023 | Borlaug JACC Scientific Statement: 5-phenotype Venn model (obese/cardiometabolic, arterial stiffening, ischaemic/CMD, pulmonary vascular, LA myopathy); disease progression spectrum — exercise-induced LA hypertension → resting → PH → RV failure; 24 knowledge gaps enumerated; SGLT2i first-line | [[borlaug2023statement]] |
| 2023 | Manabe et al. mini-review: sympathetic neural responses to dynamic exercise in HFpEF — paradoxical MSNA increase (opposite to controls); static exercise MSNA normal; excess SVR → reduced LBF → VO₂ limitation; functional sympatholysis gap identified | [[manabe2023sympathetic]] |
| 2022 | DIAMOND-HFpEF (Arnold, JACC Cardiovasc Imaging; N=101 HFpEF, 43 controls; Leicester, UK; NCT03050593): CMR multiparametric — 70% MVD (MPR<2.0) in HFpEF vs 48% controls; MPR independently predicts death/HF hospitalisation across 3 adjusted models (HR 0.69 per 1 SD increase; P<0.04); critically, NO correlation between MPR and ECV fibrosis (r=−0.06; P=0.638) — establishes CMD and diffuse myocardial fibrosis as independent, additive prognostic pathways rather than a serial causal chain | [[arnold2022diamond]] |
| 2023 | Tamaki et al. (JAHA; PURSUIT-HFpEF registry; N=1,026 ADHF-HFpEF; Osaka Japan; 429-day follow-up): combined high NLR (>4.5) + PLR (>193) HR 2.66 (P=0.0008) for cardiac death; CRP not independently associated — cellular innate immune activation (NLR) is a more relevant prognostic marker than hepatic acute-phase response (CRP) in HFpEF; largest single NLR/PLR study in ADHF-HFpEF | [[tamaki2023nlrplr]] |
| 2025 | Fayyaz et al. systematic review (Nat Rev Cardiol; 56 human myocardial tissue studies, 2004–2023): 8-pathway framework for HFpEF tissue pathophysiology — (1) fibrosis, (2) cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, (3) microvascular rarefaction, (4) diastolic dysfunction [titin/SERCA2a/T-tubule], (5) metabolic derangements [↓ATP/NAD⁺], (6) inflammation/oxidative stress, (7) cGMP-PKG impairment, (8) ER stress/DNA damage; comorbidity-dependent heterogeneity (obesity/T2DM/HTN each drive distinct signatures) identified as the mechanistic explanation for monotherapy failure in HFpEF | [[fayyaz2025pathophys]] |
| 2018 | Suzuki et al. (Fukushima; ESC HF 2018; N=221 HF): severe SDB (AHI >30/h) independently predicts higher pulse wave velocity in HFpEF (β=0.234; P=0.005) but NOT in HFrEF (P=0.068) — first evidence that SDB exerts an EF-subtype-specific effect on arterial stiffness; links SDB → arterial stiffening pathway to HFpEF specifically | [[suzuki2018sdb]] |
| 2018 | Duca et al. (Vienna; Sci Rep 2018;8:1080; N=260 invasive hemodynamics + CMR cohort): men with HFpEF more hemodynamically/structurally compromised and die more of cardiac causes (16.5% vs. 6.1%, P=0.008); women die more of non-cardiac causes (10.5% vs. 2.5%, P=0.030) — establishes the "men die of HFpEF, women die with HFpEF" sex-mortality paradox, unresolved as of this ingest | [[duca2018genderdiff]] |
| 2022 | Lau et al. (MGH/Harvard; J Card Fail 2022;28:202–211; N=190 invasive CPET cohort): first invasively-measured demonstration that women with HFpEF have significantly higher resting arterial stiffness than men (augmentation pressure +6mmHg, augmentation index +10%, both P<0.0001), and that arterial stiffness associates with abnormal exercise diastolic reserve more strongly in women (OR 3.16) than men (OR 2.07) — identifies arterial stiffness as a preferentially female HFpEF mechanism | [[lau2022arterialstiffness]] |
| 2025 | Coppi et al. review (J Cardiovasc Dev Dis 2025;12:213): synthesises sex-differences literature with SERCA2a/estrogen-receptor (ERα/ERβ/GPR30) mechanistic detail and proposes sex-specific echocardiographic/CMR diagnostic reference ranges (LA reservoir strain, GLS, ECV) — flags these as not yet guideline-endorsed; reports EMPEROR-Preserved/DELIVER/SUMMIT sex-subgroup trends numerically favouring women but explicitly non-significant in all three trials | [[coppi2025genderdiff]] |
| 2023 | Wester et al. review (Biomedicines; Regensburg/UT Southwestern): CaMKII oxidation pathway identified as mechanistic bridge between SDB and HFpEF; intermittent hypoxia → ↑mitochondrial ROS → CaMKII oxidation at Met281/282 → dysregulated Na⁺/Ca²⁺ → diastolic SR Ca²⁺ leak → atrial triggered activity → AF → HFpEF; obesity amplifies SDB → metabolic phenotype synergy; SDB 58–80% in HFpEF | [[wester2023sdb]] |
| 2023 | Zamani et al. (Circulation research letter; NCT04068844; N=28 obese HFpEF): epicardial fat correlated with LV eccentricity index r=0.88 (P<0.001); paracardial fat r=0.91 (P<0.001); BMI and subcutaneous/visceral fat NOT correlated — pericardial restraint by locally deposited fat specifically compresses the heart, elevating filling pressures independently of total adiposity; 13/28 patients had abnormal eccentricity | [[zamani2023pericardialfat]] |
| 2025 | Leahy et al. (JACC HF 2025;13:102523; NCT04068844; N=55 obese HFpEF): dynamic hyperinflation (ΔEELV ≥150 mL) in 62% at 20W and 85% at peak exercise; DH group had higher PCWP at both timepoints (23±8 vs. 16±6 at 20W; P=0.005); ΔEELV correlated with ΔPCWP (r²=0.167 at peak; P=0.002) and ΔmPAP; EFL severity NOT associated with PCWP — ventilatory mechanics contribute independently to elevated exercise filling pressures in obese HFpEF | [[leahy2025heartlung]] |
| 2026 | Babb et al. (Respir Physiol Neurobiol 2026;341:104546; NCT04068844; N=42 obese HFpEF; single-blind crossover RCT): NTG (400 μg SL) vs. placebo confirmed haemodynamic unloading (PCWP and CO both P<0.01) but did NOT change exercise capacity, breathing mechanics, or lung volumes (EELV R²=0.96 before vs. after NTG); V̇E/V̇CO₂ qualitatively declined from 20W to peak both arms — blunted ventilatory response at mechanical ceiling; paradigm shift: exercise is primarily ventilatory-limited, not cardiac-limited, in obese HFpEF | [[babb2026ventilatorylimit]] |

## Treatment

| Year | Development | Source |
|------|-------------|--------|
| 2010 | Kitzman et al. published the first randomised exercise training trial in HFpEF, demonstrating substantial improvement in cardiorespiratory fitness; established exercise as a legitimate HFpEF therapeutic target | [[pfeffer2019hfpef]] |
| 2011 | CHAMPION primary (Abraham, Lancet 2011; NCT00531661): CardioMEMS PA pressure sensor vs. standard care; N=550 NYHA III HF (all EF); 28% reduction in HF hospitalisations over 6 months (HR 0.72; P=0.0002); first positive haemodynamic monitoring trial in HF | [[abraham2011champion]] |
| 2013 | RALI-DHF (Maier, JACC Heart Fail 2013; N=20 HFpEF; ranolazine crossover): ranolazine reduced exercise LVEDP −4.7 mmHg (P=0.001) — proof-of-concept that late Na⁺ current → Ca²⁺ overload pathway is mechanistically operative in HFpEF; target engaged but functional benefit absent at Phase 2 scale | [[maier2013ralidhf]] |
| 2014 | CHAMPION HFpEF subgroup (Adamson, Circ Heart Fail 2014; N=119 HFpEF from CHAMPION): PA-guided management in HFpEF specifically; ~46% reduction in HF hospitalisations — first positive device evidence specifically in the HFpEF subtype | [[adamson2014champion]] |
| 2017 | AHA/ACC 2017 Focused Update: Class IIb recommendation for ARBs (candesartan) to reduce hospitalisations in HFpEF based on CHARM-Preserved; Class IIb for MRA (spironolactone) to reduce hospitalisations in TOPCAT-eligible patients based on Americas subgroup | [[pfeffer2019hfpef]] |
| 2018 | D-HART2 results (Van Tassell, Circ Heart Fail 2018; N=31 HFpEF; anakinra 100 mg/day SC × 24 weeks vs. placebo): hs-CRP AUC ratio 0.40 (P=0.001) — IL-1 pathway fully suppressed; peak VO₂ +0.32 mL/kg/min (P=0.54) — no exercise improvement; critical null constraining inflammation→exercise hypothesis in HFpEF | [[vantassell2018dhart2]] |
| 2019 | TOPCAT sex analysis (Merrill, JACC Heart Fail 2019; N=3,445): women had higher LVEF (~63% vs. ~58%), less CAD, and fewer CV deaths; spironolactone sex×treatment interaction P=0.26 (NS) — no sex-specific MRA signal identified | [[merrill2019topcat]] |
| 2012 | PARAMOUNT (Solomon, Lancet 2012; Phase 2 RCT; N=301; 65 centres/13 countries; NCT00887588): LCZ696 (ARNi precursor = sacubitril/valsartan) vs. valsartan in HFpEF — NT-proBNP reduced 23% (ratio 0.77; P=0.005) at 12 weeks; LA volume improved at 36 weeks (−4.6 vs +0.37 mL; P=0.003); eGFR decline attenuated; NT-proBNP benefit independent of BP difference; no diastolic function improvement; Phase 2 mechanistic rationale that motivated the PARAGON-HF Phase 3 | [[solomon2012paramount]] |
| 2021 | Only two Class I HFpEF recommendations: comorbidity management + diuretics for congestion — no disease-modifying therapy | [[mcdonagh2021esc]] |
| 2021 | FDA endorsed [[sacubitril-valsartan]] for LVEF "less than normal" based on PARAGON-HF subgroup; ESC did not follow | [[mcdonagh2021esc]] |
| 2021 | EMPEROR-Preserved (empagliflozin in HFpEF) published after ESC guidelines cutoff — first positive major HFpEF trial | [[anker2021emperor]] |
| 2022 | DELIVER (dapagliflozin in HFpEF) positive — together with EMPEROR-Preserved established SGLT2i across the HF EF spectrum | [[solomon2022deliver]] |
| 2022 | AHA 2022 Guideline: **SGLT2i Class 2a for HFpEF** — first pharmacological guideline recommendation above Class 2b for HFpEF; also Class 2b for MRA and ARNI in HFpEF; introduced HFimpEF as 4th EF category; formalised A–D staging | [[heidenreich2022aha]] |
| 2021 | REHAB-HF (Kitzman, NEJM; NCT02196038): transitional progressive multidomain rehabilitation in 349 acute HF patients (any EF; ≥60y; 97% frail/pre-frail); SPPB +1.5 pts (P<0.001); 6MWD +34 m; 60-day rehospitalisation NS (RR 0.93); HFpEF subgroup (n=185): SPPB +1.9 vs. +1.1 in HFrEF; global rank endpoint significant in HFpEF (P=0.04) not HFrEF — establishes post-hospitalisation window as a critical rehabilitation setting | [[kitzman2021rehabhf]] · [[mentz2021rehabhfhfpef]] · [[rehab-hf]] |
| 2021 | OptimEx-Clin (Mueller, JAMA; NCT02078947): HIIT vs. MCT vs. guideline control in HFpEF (5 European sites; N=180); HIIT +1.5, MCT +2.0 mL/kg/min at 3 months (both P<0.05); HIIT NOT superior to MCT (P=NS); neither met MCID 2.5 mL/kg/min; gains lost at 12 months with telemedical supervision — closes the HIIT superiority debate in typical outpatient HFpEF | [[mueller2021optimex]] · [[optimex-clin]] |
| 2022 | AHA/ACC HF Guidelines: Class I, Level A recommendation for [[supervised-exercise-training\|exercise training]] in all HF patients regardless of EF | [[heidenreich2022aha]], [[sachdev2023exercise]] |
| 2023 | **ESC 2023 Focused Update**: SGLT2i upgraded to **Class I, Level A for HFpEF and HFmrEF** — first Class I pharmacological HFpEF recommendation by the ESC; resolves ESC 2021/AHA 2022 divergence. STRONG-HF Class I (rapid in-hospital GDMT intensification → 8% absolute reduction in 180-day readmission/death). Finerenone Class I for CKD+T2DM. Benefit footnote: hospitalisation-driven, not CV mortality. | [[mcdonagh2023escupdate]] |
| 2023 | Sachdev AHA Scientific Statement: meta-analysis 8 RCTs (n=503); SET improves peak VO2 +2.8 mL/kg/min; skeletal muscle as primary mechanism; Class I AHA/ACC 2022 | [[sachdev2023exercise]] |
| 2023 | Anker 2023 HFA/ESC/ESH Scientific Statement: two-layer treatment model proposed — SGLT2i universal + phenotype-guided add-on for 18 comorbidity phenotypes; CMD 71–91% prevalence (PROMIS-HFpEF); iron deficiency 50–75%; cancer HFpEF recognised | [[anker2023hfpefphenotype]] |
| 2023 | ACC 2023 Expert Consensus Decision Pathway (Kittleson): operational Figure 9 treatment algorithm; sex-stratified ARNI/MRA recommendations (PARAGON HR 0.73 women vs. 1.03 men); HFpEF mimics Table 1; CHECK-IN/INHALE referral acronyms; SUMMIT + STEP-HFpEF flagged as emerging GLP-1RA evidence | [[kittleson2023acc]] |
| 2023 | **STEP-HFpEF** (non-DM, Kosiborod; NEJM 2023): Semaglutide 2.4 mg vs. placebo; N=529; LVEF ≥45%, BMI ≥30, no T2DM; KCCQ-CSS +7.8 pts, body weight −10.7 pp, 6MWD +20.3m, CRP ratio 0.61, win ratio 1.72 (all P<0.001); not powered for hard outcomes; first large positive GLP-1RA trial in obese HFpEF; establishes GLP-1RA as evidence-based obese HFpEF therapy | [[kosiborod2023stephfpef]] · [[step-hfpef]] |
| 2024 | DETERMINE RCTs (McMurray): dapagliflozin 16 weeks — improved KCCQ in HFrEF (P=0.022) but not HFpEF (P=0.079); 6MWD neutral in both arms; raises questions about 6MWD validity as HF endpoint | [[mcmurray2024determine]] |
| 2024 | **FINEARTS-HF** (Solomon; NEJM 2024): Finerenone (non-steroidal MRA) vs. placebo; N=6001; HFpEF/HFmrEF LVEF ≥40%; RR 0.84 (95% CI 0.74–0.95; P=0.007) for total WHF events + CV death; first non-SGLT2i pharmacological positive trial in HFpEF; MRA class benefit partially resurrected after TOPCAT controversy | [[finearts-hf]] · [[solomon2024finearts]] |
| 2024/2025 | **SUMMIT** (Packer; NEJM 2025, online Nov 2024): Tirzepatide (GLP-1/GIP dual agonist, LY3298176; Eli Lilly) vs. placebo; N=731; HFpEF (LVEF ≥50%) + obesity (BMI ≥30); T2DM permitted; co-primary KCCQ-CSS change + CV death/worsening HF; HR 0.62 (0.41–0.95; P=0.026); KCCQ-CSS +6.9 pts (P<0.001); body weight −13–15%; hsCRP −35–44%; CMR substudy: LV mass −11 g (P=0.004), paracardiac fat −45 mL (P<0.001); **first HFpEF trial to show event reduction with GLP-1RA class agent** | [[summit]] · [[packer2025summit]] · [[packer2025summit-ckd]] · [[kramer2025summit-cmr]] |
| 2025 | Mirzai state-of-the-art review: ~30 exercise RCTs in HFpEF (2004–2024); MICT best evidence; HIIT not superior to MICT for ΔpVO₂; ~1/3 non-responders; durability and hard outcomes remain open | [[mirzai2025exercise]] |
| 2025 | **Ex-DHF** (Edelmann; Nat Med 2025; n=322; 12-month combined endurance+resistance training): largest exercise RCT in HFpEF — **primary Packer composite NOT MET** (tau-b=−0.073; P=0.17); VO₂ +1.3 mL/kg/min (P=0.003); NYHA improved OR 5.89 (P<0.001); adherence ~53%; ISRCTN86879094 — confirms functional benefit of combined training but leaves event-driven primary outcome question unresolved | [[edelmann2025exdhf]] |
| 2025 | Akerman et al. (Nat Commun 2025): external validation of EchoGo HF v2 commercial echocardiographic AI in prospective HFpEF cohort; AUROC 0.797 vs. H₂FPEF 0.788 (P=0.001 favours AI); AI intermediate classification 9.1% vs. 61.7% H₂FPEF — first external validation of commercial AI for HFpEF; dominant advantage is reduction in indeterminate diagnostic results | [[akerman2025ai]] |
| 2025 | PARAGLIDE-HF sex analysis (Rambarat; AHJ 2025): NT-proBNP benefit with Sac/Val consistent between women and men (P-interaction=0.908); women excess symptomatic hypotension (OR 2.29; P=0.012); contrasts with PARAGON-HF sex-treatment interaction — LVEF, not sex, appears to be the true treatment moderator | [[rambarat2025paraglide]] |
| 2026 | Ferreira et al. (EMPEROR-Preserved secondary): serum Mg predicts outcomes; empagliflozin raises Mg; higher Mg → more benefit — opposite to HFrEF direction | [[ferreira2026emperor]] |
| 2021 | EAST-AFNET4 HF subgroup (Rillig, Circulation 2021; NCT01288352; N=798 HF in main trial including 442 HFpEF [56.3%]): early rhythm control vs. usual care in recently diagnosed AF; primary composite HR 0.74 (95% CI 0.56–0.97; P=0.03); no HF-type interaction (P=0.63); establishes early rhythm control benefit extends to HF patients, and predominantly to those with HFpEF | [[rillig2021eastafnet4]] |
| 2021 | GUIDE-HF (Lindenfeld, Lancet 2021; NCT03387813; N=1,000 all EF; 47% HFpEF): CardioMEMS haemodynamic-guided management vs. sham; overall primary composite HR 0.88 (P=0.16, NS); pre-COVID subgroup HR 0.81 (P=0.049, P=0.049); HFpEF subgroup (LVEF >40%, N=469) HR 0.85 (P=0.28); COVID-19 confounded the trial — lesson for future trial design in an era of competing treatment changes | [[lindenfeld2021guidehf]] |
| 2024 | FAIR-HFpEF (von Haehling, Eur Heart J 2024;45:3789–3800; NCT03074591; N=40, stopped at 20% of target N=200): first RCT of IV ferric carboxymaltose in HFpEF with iron deficiency; FCM +65 m vs. placebo −8 m 6MWT at week 32; 6MWT difference +49 m at week 24 (P=0.029); SAE rate ratio 0.27 (P=0.043); directionally positive signal from underpowered trial — first prospective interventional iron deficiency evidence in HFpEF | [[vonhaehling2024fair]] |
| 2024 | Kittleson 2024 ACC/AHA Performance Measures (JACC 2024;84:1123–1143): PM-2 established as first HFpEF-specific performance measure in US — BP control (SBP <130 + DBP <80 mmHg) in HFpEF with hypertension; QM-1 (SGLT2i for HFmrEF/HFpEF) as quality measure; QM-2 (SDOH screening); formalises preventive care framework for HFpEF disparities | [[kittleson2024accaha]] |
| 2024 | REBALANCE-HF (Fudim, JAMA Cardiol 2024;9(12):1143–1153; NCT04592445; N=80 HFpEF; sham-controlled RCT): splanchnic nerve ablation via SAVM vs. sham; exercise PCWP −5.4 mmHg (P=0.003); KCCQ +12.5 pts; first sham-controlled positive device trial specifically targeting haemodynamic splanchnic congestion in HFpEF | [[fudim2024rebalance]] · [[rebalance-hf]] |
| 2026 | MyoMobile primary results (Zeid, JACC Heart Fail 2026;14(5):102845; NCT04940312; N=185 HFpEF; RCT): app-based physical activity coaching vs. standard care; step count significantly increased; KCCQ and 6MWT improvements — first positive digital health RCT in HFpEF | [[zeid2026myomobile]] · [[myomobile]] |
